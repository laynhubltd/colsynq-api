# Financing of Cooperative Housing Estates in Nigeria (cooperative level and member level)

Research date: 8 October 2026. All naira figures are nominal. Where a rate/cap/tenor has a known effective date it is given; "programme claim" vs "disbursed result" is labelled. Worked affordability examples in Key Question 5 are the researcher's own arithmetic (standard annuity formula) applied to cited programme terms, and are labelled as such.

## Key Question 1: Member equity routes (savings, instalments, share capital, pre-sale, "pay small-small", handling of members who fall behind)

### Takeaway
Nigerian cooperative estates are overwhelmingly financed from members' own pockets: pooled monthly savings and salary-source deductions form the working capital, members buy plots/units on instalment plans (typically 12–36 months, sometimes longer) and the cooperative collects "cash calls" against construction milestones. Published, estate-specific default/penalty/refund rules are scarce; what exists comes from the Co-operative Societies Act (bye-laws must cover default fines recoverable as civil debt) and from press reports of refund delays of 6–12 months.

### Cited Findings
- Akure study (683 house-owners surveyed, 2021): "all the sampled cooperative societies pooled together members' monetary contributions to form their main source of working capital" and "supplemented their working capital from the deductions at the source of payment of the salaries of their members" — [Olujimi et al., African Journal of Estate and Property Management, Sept 2021](https://www.internationalscholarsjournals.com/articles/the-lending-policy-of-cooperative-societies-and-its-effect-on-construction-of-housing-in-akure.pdf)
- The same study found none of the loan-approval criteria used by cooperatives (savings multiples, guarantors, etc.) was statistically related to the number of housing loans granted (all p > 0.05), i.e. the criteria did not deter members from borrowing for housing — [Olujimi et al., 2021](https://www.internationalscholarsjournals.com/articles/the-lending-policy-of-cooperative-societies-and-its-effect-on-construction-of-housing-in-akure.pdf)
- Cooperatives' housing strategies include general loans, housing-construction loans, specific land-purchase loans and renovation loans; collective construction with bulk purchase of materials is used to remove instalment-driven cost increments — [Ogbomoso cooperative housing finance study (ResearchGate abstract)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/235294736_Housing_development_finance_through_cooperative_societies_The_case_of_Ogbomoso_Nigeria)
- Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act: bye-laws must specify "the consequences of default in payment and repayment" and fines are "recoverable by the society as a civil debt"; bye-laws must also address restrictions on transfer of members' shares or interest — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act (FAO Lex copy)](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- FMBN's model for cooperative estates: the cooperative acquires land, designs house types, obtains title and planning permit, then applies for construction finance; members must join the NHF and contribute 2.5% of monthly salary — [ThisDay, 11 Aug 2021](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/); [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/property/fg-launches-national-cooperative-housing-development-scheme/)
- Worked real example of member pricing: Akacare Multipurpose Cooperative Society's Woodhill Estate, Kuje, Abuja — 230 units (2-bed and 3-bed detached bungalows) priced to members at ₦7.5m and ₦9.5m respectively, "fully subscribed", financed by a ₦997m FMBN construction loan, with 77 units completed at the time of the article (2021) — [ThisDay, 11 Aug 2021](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/)
- Secondary-market evidence of member-allocated land later being resold: plots in "NNPC Cooperative Estate", Life Camp, Abuja are advertised at ₦85–95m (750 m²) and ₦100m (1,000 m²) in 2026 listings — [Nigeria Property Centre listings](https://nigeriapropertycentre.com/for-sale/abuja?n=cooperative+estate)
- Refund-delay pattern: police officers reported being unable to access voluntary contributions from the Nigeria Police Cooperative Society for 6+ months (Nov 2022) — [Sahara Reporters, 10 Nov 2022](https://saharareporters.com/2022/11/10/police-officers-accuse-nigeria-police-cooperative-society-fraud-lament-6-months-delay); a Kwara cooperative told a member his ₦2.1m savings would take 6–12 months to refund — [FIJ](https://fij.ng/article/nigerian-in-germany-asked-for-his-n2-1m-to-pay-mothers-hospital-bills-kwara-co-operative-society-tells-him-to-wait-12-months/)
- Labour Act s.5(7): total deductions from a worker's wages in any month "shall not exceed one-third of the wages of the worker for that month" — this caps salary-source cooperative deductions — [Labour Act s.5 (jurist.ng)](https://jurist.ng/labour_act/sec-5); [Labour Act PDF (PLAC)](https://www.placng.org/lawsofnigeria/laws/L1.pdf)
- Loan default in Nigerian cooperatives is driven by irregular income/unemployment and loan diversion; "members severally fail to repay their loans as and when due" — [EKSU Business & Finance Journal, 2023](https://bfjournal.eksu.edu.ng/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/loan-default.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because the main capital source is salary deductions capped at one-third of wages, a cooperative's monthly collection capacity per member is mechanically bounded by payroll, which is why instalment plans stretch to 24–36 months and why estates are phased.
- The Woodhill example (₦7.5m–₦9.5m units, 2021) implies cooperatives price at or near cost; the FMBN construction loan (₦997m / 230 units ≈ ₦4.3m per unit) covered roughly half of unit price, with the balance presumably from member deposits/instalments and land already held.
- Refund delays of 6–12 months on ordinary savings suggest that estate-project refunds to withdrawing members are, in practice, subordinated to project cash flow (i.e. paid when a replacement subscriber is found), though I found no published estate rule saying so.

### Gaps
- No published, estate-specific schedule of deposit %, milestone cash-call percentages, late-payment penalty rates, reallocation rules or refund terms for a named Nigerian cooperative estate was found in accessible sources (estateintel.com article on cooperative-owned developments returned HTTP 403). The report writer should treat typical deposit/instalment structures as unverified unless the cooperative's own subscription form is obtained.
- No data on the share of subscribers who default or are reallocated.

## Key Question 2: Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria — CHDL, NHF mortgages, Rent-to-Own, Home Renovation Loan, track record and problems

### Takeaway
FMBN's Cooperative Housing Development Loan is a construction bridge of up to ₦500m at 10% for 24 months (12-month moratorium) to registered cooperatives with unencumbered titled land and NHF-contributing members, taken out by individual NHF mortgages (now up to ₦50m at 6% over 30 years, effective Feb–Mar 2025; previously ₦15m). Disbursed results are modest relative to the deficit (6,911 units financed across all FMBN programmes in 2025; ₦455bn total disbursed 1992–Oct 2024) and bottlenecks are title, governor's consent, PMB processing and affordability.

### Cited Findings
**CHDL terms (programme claims)**
- CHDL: "up to N500 million at an interest rate of ten percent, with tenors of up to 24 months and a moratorium of 12 months"; cooperative must have "unencumbered land titles"; members must contribute to NHF; the ₦500m cap is "subject to review upon judicious assessment of utilization of funds earlier disbursed" — [ThisDay, 11 Aug 2021](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/); [Housing Cable (reprint)](https://housingcable.ng/2022/01/31/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/)
- CHDL priced at "best market rates of 10 percent" (FMBN MD Dangiwa, March 2020) — [BusinessDay, 8 Mar 2020](https://businessday.ng/real-estate/article/why-fmbn-seeks-state-govts-collaboration-on-affordable-housing/)
- Process: cooperative acquires land, designs houses, gets title and planning permit, then approaches FMBN; on completion FMBN packages member mortgages — [ThisDay, 11 Aug 2021](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/); [ThisDay, 2 Sep 2024](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2024/09/02/fmbn-fulfilling-home-ownership-dream-through-nhf-scheme/)
- National Cooperative Housing Development Scheme (launched by Federal Ministry/FMBN): take-out NHF mortgages to members "of up to N15million ... at nine per cent interest rate per annum with tenors of up to 30 years depending on the age of the contributors"; members must join NHF at 2.5% of salary — [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/property/fg-launches-national-cooperative-housing-development-scheme/). Note: 9% was the cooperative-scheme take-out rate at that time; Woodhill members were quoted 7% on up to ₦9m — [ThisDay, 11 Aug 2021](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/). Current FMBN messaging (2024–26) quotes 6% for member take-out — [ThisDay, 2 Sep 2024](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2024/09/02/fmbn-fulfilling-home-ownership-dream-through-nhf-scheme/). The rate history is therefore 9% → 7% → 6%, but I found no single FMBN circular dating each change.
- Scale (2021): "approximately 40 cooperative housing development loan projects ongoing nationwide", projected ~2,570 units — [ThisDay, 11 Aug 2021](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/)
- Named CHDL deals: Akacare MCS Woodhill Estate, Kuje — ₦997m, 230 units (2021) — [ThisDay](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/); Nigeria Police Multipurpose Cooperative Society — ₦4.74bn approved, >₦2.5bn disbursed, 606 units across Abuja, Kaduna, Nasarawa, Ogun, Abia, Adamawa (disclosed 27 Jan 2026) — [The Economic Times NG, 29 Jan 2026](https://theeconomictimes.com.ng/2026/01/29/fmbn-disburses-over-%E2%82%A67bn-in-mortgages-cooperative-housing-for-police-officers-osidi/)

**NHF individual mortgage (member take-out)**
- Current product page: "up to ₦50 million" through an accredited PMB; FMBN lends to PMBs at 4%, PMBs on-lend at 6%; maximum tenor 30 years; after 6 months' continuous contribution; secured by the mortgaged property — [FMBN NHF Mortgage Loan page](https://fmbn.gov.ng/products/nhf_mortgage_loan)
- Ceiling raised from ₦15m to ₦50m, announced 14–23 February 2025 — [Dataphyte](https://dataphyte.substack.com/p/how-affordable-and-convenient-is-the-new-fmbn-mortgage-ceiling); [BusinessDay, 25 Mar 2025](https://businessday.ng/news/article/fmbn-n50m-loan-treshold-excites-contributors-as-affordability-concerns-deepen/); [Nigeria Housing Market](https://www.nigeriahousingmarket.com/real-estate-news-nigeria/nigerias-federal-mortgage-bank-increases-loan-limit-to-50-million-to-support-homeownership)
- Equity bands: zero equity for loans up to ₦5m; 10% for loans above ₦5m (previously 5% and 20% for the ≤₦5m and ≤₦15m bands) — board approval c. Aug 2018 — [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/business-services/fmbn-begins-zero-equity-for-housing-loan-subscribers/); [BusinessDay, 25 Mar 2025](https://businessday.ng/news/article/fmbn-n50m-loan-treshold-excites-contributors-as-affordability-concerns-deepen/); CAHF Yearbook 2022 records the cut "from 5% to 0% for loans up to ₦5 million ... and from 15% to 10% for loans over ₦5 million" — [CAHF Africa Housing Finance Yearbook 2022, Nigeria](https://housingfinanceafrica.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/V20-Nigeria-Final.pdf). Older 30% equity references are outdated.
- Repayment: maximum "35 years or until the contributor turns 60" per FMBN's Aug 2026 list of reasons loans are delayed; other reasons: <6 months contributions, weak income evidence, PMB delays, CBN single-obligor limits, no interim security (e.g. C of O), incomplete documents, disbursement approval separate from loan approval, wrong channel — [Legit.ng, 20 Aug 2026](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/economy/1726243-fmbn-lists-10-reasons-nhf-mortgage-loan-delayed-denied/). (Note the 35-year figure conflicts with the 30-year maximum on the product page; treat 30 years as the standard product term.)
- Other NHF-linked products (ThisDay, Sep 2024): Individual Construction Loan up to ₦50m at 7% for up to 15 years (titled land required); Diaspora NHF mortgage up to ₦50m at 9% up to 10 years ("yet to be fully operational"); Rental Assistance Loan ₦2m cap for informal-sector contributors (not yet launched) — [ThisDay, 2 Sep 2024](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2024/09/02/fmbn-fulfilling-home-ownership-dream-through-nhf-scheme/)

**Rent-to-Own**
- 7% p.a. on property price, up to 30 years (depending on age/years of service), zero equity, "no upfront fees, down payment, or mortgage perfection fees"; property value ₦4m–₦15m at launch (2021); early liquidation allowed — [ThisDay, 13 Dec 2021](https://thisdaylive.com/index.php/2021/12/13/understanding-fmbns-rent-to-own-housing-product); [CAHF 2022](https://housingfinanceafrica.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/V20-Nigeria-Final.pdf)
- Disbursed result: 367 Rent-to-Own beneficiaries in 2025, ₦7.1bn — [ThisDay, 16 Jul 2026](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/07/16/fmbn-6911-housing-units-funded-in-2025-300-increase-recorded/)

**Home Renovation Loan**
- 2015 terms: up to ₦1m, 8% fixed, max 3 years or remaining service, repayment capped at 33⅓% of monthly salary, once every 5 years; security = employer undertaking, two guarantors, reducing-term assurance — [Daily Trust, 8 Jun 2015](https://dailytrust.com/federal-mortgage-bank-of-nigeria-home-renovation-loan-scheme/)
- 2024 terms: up to ₦1m, single-digit rate, up to 5 years — [ThisDay, 2 Sep 2024](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2024/09/02/fmbn-fulfilling-home-ownership-dream-through-nhf-scheme/). Disbursed: 15,290 beneficiaries, ₦13.8bn in 2025 (+86% y/y) — [ThisDay, 16 Jul 2026](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/07/16/fmbn-6911-housing-units-funded-in-2025-300-increase-recorded/)

**Track record (disbursed results)**
- Cumulative: ₦455.132bn disbursed 1992–Oct 2024; ₦29.506bn Jan–Oct 2024; NHF refunds ₦91.184bn since inception; 5.858m registered contributors (MD Osidi to Senate, 21 Jan 2025) — [Tribune](https://tribuneonlineng.com/housing-loan-fmbn-disburses-n455-132bn-in-42-years/)
- Apr 2017–Mar 2020: 5,026,777 contributors; ₦134bn collections; ₦21bn refunds; 7,740 housing units; 3,749 mortgages; 39,458 renovation loans — [BusinessDay, 8 Mar 2020](https://businessday.ng/real-estate/article/why-fmbn-seeks-state-govts-collaboration-on-affordable-housing/)
- 2025: 6,911 units financed (96% of target) vs 2,165 in 2024; project-loan disbursements ₦79bn (2024: ₦31.5bn); NHF mortgage disbursements ₦8.2bn (+38%); recoveries ₦27.3bn (2024: ₦14bn); NHF refunds ₦15.6bn to 55,068 contributors; Renewed Hope cities financing ₦27bn Ibeju-Lekki, ₦19.9bn Karsana, ₦10bn Port Harcourt, ₦7.8bn Enugu (3,139 units) — [ThisDay, 16 Jul 2026](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/07/16/fmbn-6911-housing-units-funded-in-2025-300-increase-recorded/); [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/fmbn-finances-6911-housing-units-in-2025-seeks-stronger-partnerships-to-tackle-housing-deficit/)
- NHF collections 2025: ₦152bn (+48%) — [NAN, 19 Feb 2026](https://nannews.ng/2026/02/19/fmbn-recorded-%E2%82%A6152bn-nhf-collections-representing-48-growth-in-2025-osidi/)
- Jan 2021: cumulative housing-development loans approved ₦35.78bn, of which ₦10.95bn disbursed (approval-to-disbursement gap ~70%) — [CAHF 2022](https://housingfinanceafrica.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/V20-Nigeria-Final.pdf)

**Problems**
- Title perfection costs 5–10% of value; delays in governors' consent to mortgage; FMBN asked states for a flat 0.5% perfection fee and faster consent (2020) — [BusinessDay, 8 Mar 2020](https://businessday.ng/real-estate/article/why-fmbn-seeks-state-govts-collaboration-on-affordable-housing/)
- Sector-wide: lack of title, low borrower income, and "cumbersome procedures for obtaining governors' consent ... which is also costly" limit the number of NHF contributors who benefit — [ThisDay, 23 Aug 2024](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2024/08/23/fmbn-mitigating-nigerias-housing-crisis-through-new-rental-assistance-loan/)
- Affordability critique of ₦50m ceiling: at 6%/30y the ₦50m loan needs ~₦300,000/month, implying ~₦800,000 monthly salary; a ₦200,000 earner "cannot sustain repayments exceeding ₦80,000"; a ₦70,000 earner qualifies for only ~₦297,170 — [BusinessDay, 25 Mar 2025](https://businessday.ng/news/article/fmbn-n50m-loan-treshold-excites-contributors-as-affordability-concerns-deepen/); [Dataphyte](https://dataphyte.substack.com/p/how-affordable-and-convenient-is-the-new-fmbn-mortgage-ceiling)
- Minister Dangiwa advised cooperatives to ensure land is "secured, certificated and not under any encumbrance before acquisition" — [search summary citing FMINO/Guardian](https://guardian.ng/news/fmbn-will-serve-as-cornerstone-of-affordable-housing-delivery-dangiwa/)

### Inferences
- A ₦500m CHDL at 10% with a 12-month moratorium accrues ~₦50m of interest in year one (researcher's arithmetic); on a 100-unit estate that is ~₦500,000 per unit of carrying cost that must be priced into members' units or absorbed by the cooperative's surplus.
- The 24-month tenor forces the estate to be mortgage-ready within two years; where title/consent delays stretch beyond that, the cooperative faces either restructuring with FMBN or bridging from member cash calls.
- Cooperative-specific disbursements are not separately reported in FMBN's annual numbers; the Police cooperative deal (₦4.74bn/606 units ≈ ₦7.8m per unit) is the best recent benchmark of CHDL cost per unit.

### Gaps
- No FMBN annual report with a year-by-year CHDL series (amount, cooperatives, units) was accessible; figures above are from press statements.
- No primary FMBN document dating the member take-out rate change from 9% to 6% for cooperative scheme members.
- fmbn.gov.ng CHDL product page not fetched (only NHF page fetched); the ₦500m/10%/24-month terms are from 2020–2022 press and may have been revised.

## Key Question 3: Other institutional finance (Family Homes Funds, NMRC/PMBs, MREIF, Shelter Afrique, commercial banks, state schemes, pension-backed equity)

### Takeaway
Beyond FMBN, the institutional menu is: Family Homes Funds' Help-to-Own (10% deposit, FHF funds 40% as a loan, ~9.99% target rate over 20 years); MOFI's MREIF (9.75% fixed, 20 years, 10% equity, up to ₦100m — ₦128–140bn disbursed to ~2,000 borrowers by mid-2026); NMRC refinancing of PMB mortgages (15–20 year tenors, funded by bonds at ~17%); Shelter Afrique and commercial-bank project loans to specific staff cooperatives (Customs $3.9m/90 units; TotalEnergies staff cooperative $40m/157 units from FCMB, 2025); Lagos HOMS at 6% over 10 years with up to 30% equity; and PenCom's 2022 rule letting RSA holders use 25% of their balance as mortgage equity.

### Cited Findings
**Family Homes Funds (FHF)**
- Help-to-Own: buyer puts 10% deposit (vs traditional 20%); "Help to Own contributes 40% of the total cost of new homes for low-middle income individuals as a loan"; partner mortgage banks fund the rest; first-time buyers, 18+, must show affordability; ~15 partner lenders (ITMB, Stanbic IBTC, Wema, Abbey, Homebase, NRMC, CMB, FHA, Living Trust, Gateway, CityCode, AG Mortgage, Lotus, Imperial) — [FHF Help-to-Own page](https://fhfl.com.ng/help-to-own)
- FHF's ₦500bn mandate targets mortgage rates "well under the average 23%, targeting 9.99% payable over 20 years" — [Oxford Business Group](https://oxfordbusinessgroup.com/articles-interviews/moves-to-tackle-affordable-housing-shortage-in-nigeria); targets earners of ₦50,000–₦1.7m per year; AfDB-backed tranche expected to support ≥3,750 Help-to-Own mortgages — [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/property/fhf-mortgage-banks-launch-help-to-own-housing-scheme/)
- Other FHF windows: Affordable Housing Fund, Rental Housing, Land & Infrastructure Development Fund — [FHF](https://fhfl.com.ng/help-to-own)

**MREIF (MOFI Real Estate Investment Fund)**
- 9.75% fixed, up to 20 years, minimum 10% equity, loans up to ₦100m, borrower aged 21–60 at maturity; managed by ARM — [ARM MREIF FAQ](https://www.arm.com.ng/mreif-faq/); [ARM About](https://www.arm.com.ng/mreif-about-mreif/)
- Disbursed: ₦128bn to 1,859 families (State House, June 2026); ₦140bn to >2,000 borrowers across 27 states (later 2026) — [State House](https://statehouse.gov.ng/president-tinubu-hails-mreif-as-fund-delivers-%E2%82%A6128-billion-in-mortgages-to-1859-nigerian-families/); [Housing TV Africa](https://www.housingtvafrica.com/mreif-disburses-n140bn-in-mortgages-to-over-2000-nigerians/); ₦1 trillion Series 2 listed on NGX, Nov 2025 — [Nairametrics](http://nairametrics.com/2025/11/12/mofi-lists-n1-trillion-series-2-mreif-on-ngx-promises-affordable-housing-finance/)

**NMRC and PMBs**
- NMRC provides long-term funds to commercial and primary mortgage banks for mortgages with tenors "up to 15–20 years", selecting loans against Uniform Underwriting Standards; no cooperative-specific window — [NMRC Our Business](https://nmrc.com.ng/our-business/)
- NMRC refinanced ₦17.4bn (2020), ₦21.1bn (2021), ₦30bn (to late 2022); 34 PMBs and 24 commercial banks lend residential mortgages; 32,260 mortgages outstanding (2021); PMB/commercial rates up to 28.3%; LTV 70–90% — [CAHF Yearbook 2022](https://housingfinanceafrica.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/V20-Nigeria-Final.pdf)
- NMRC funding cost: ₦11.50bn 10-year Series 1 bond at 17.25% listed on FMDQ under a ₦440bn MTN programme — [FMDQ](https://fmdqgroup.com/press-release-nigeria-mortgage-refinance-company-plc-lists-%E2%82%A611-50-billion-fixed-rate-bond-on-fmdq-exchange/)
- Market rates 2025–26: government-backed programmes 6–9.75%; PMBs 15–25%; commercial banks 18–28% — [Swiftbankloan guide](https://swiftbankloan.com/mortgage-companies-in-nigeria/); [MKH Properties](https://mkhproperties.com/nigeria-mortgage-rates-2026-access-affordable-financing/). CBN MPR: 27.5% through 2025, 26.5% (Feb 2026), 23% (22 Sep 2026); maximum lending rate 35.17% (May 2026) — [Nigeria Housing Market](https://www.nigeriahousingmarket.com/news/cbn-cuts-interest-rate-23-percent-experts-business-impact); [allAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202605190271.html)

**Shelter Afrique**
- Nigeria Customs Service staff cooperative: $3.9m approved for 90 units with infrastructure; developer Coop Property Development Limited; borrower "Cooperative of the Nigeria Customs Department staff" — [Shelter Afrique project page](https://www.shelterafrique.org/fr/projects/view/346?region=Western+Africa). - Shelter Afrique approved ~₦8bn / $19.5m for Nigerian affordable housing (2022), plus ₦200bn credit scheme with InfraCredit — [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/property/shelter-afrique-approves-n8b-for-nigerias-affordable-housing-projects/); [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/property/shelter-afrique-infracredit-pioneer-nigerias-n200b-affordable-housing-credit-scheme/)

**Commercial bank project finance to a cooperative**
- FCMB: sole financier, $40m facility to TotalEnergies Staff Housing Cooperative Multipurpose Society (TEHC) for Louisville Phase 1, Eko Atlantic — 157 apartments (2–3 bed flats, 4-bed maisonettes, 5-bed penthouses), phased over 36 months; whole mixed-use project $150m; piling complete (July 2025) — [Nairametrics, 18 Jul 2025](https://nairametrics.com/2025/07/18/fcmb-backs-totalenergies-staff-housing-cooperative-multipurpose-society-ltd-flagship-project-louisville-phase-1-with-40m-loan-facility-in-eko-atlantic/); [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/fcmb-backs-totalenergies-staff-housing-with-40m-loan-facility-in-eko-atlantic/). Member pricing/tenor not disclosed.

**State schemes**
- Lagos HOMS: funded by Lagos State; 6% p.a.; applicant equity up to 30%; balance over 10 years capped at 30% of income; allocation by public draw; LBIC processes mortgages as Lagos Mortgage Board's agent — [Nigeria Property Centre](https://nigeriapropertycentre.com/blog/buying-selling/the-lagos-home-ownership-mortgage-scheme-homs-in-detail); [Lagos HOMS Draw Rules](http://www.lagoshoms.gov.ng/Lagos-HOMS-Draw-Rules.pdf); [Vanguard 2014](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2014/10/houses-lagos-mortgage-scheme/)
- LASMECO is an MSME (not housing) cooperative-channel fund: ₦10bn, 9% p.a., up to ₦10m, 6-month moratorium, 36-month tenor; applicant must belong to a registered cooperative which guarantees 25% of each facility — [Sterling Bank](https://sterling.ng/lagos-state-access-to-finance-for-msmes-through-cooperatives-lasmeco/); [MSME Africa](https://msmeafricaonline.com/lagos-expands-msme-financing-with-n10bn-cooperative-fund/). Its 25%-cooperative-guarantee design is a structural idea transferable to housing.
- Lagos 2025 budget: ₦101.6bn for housing and community development — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/03/14/lagos-govt-allocates-n101-6-billion-for-housing-and-community-developments-in-2025/)

**Pension-backed equity (PenCom, Sept 2022)**
- Max 25% of RSA balance at application date, "irrespective of the percentage of equity contribution required by the Mortgage Lender"; if 25% exceeds required equity, only the required equity is released; if less, holder deposits the shortfall first; RSA must have ≥60 months of employer+employee contributions; <3 years to retirement ineligible; once-only access; in person; joint applications by married couples allowed; lender must be CBN-licensed, meet capital and uniform underwriting standards and hold a Pension Clearance Certificate — [PenCom Guidelines PDF](https://www.pencom.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/GUIDELINES-ON-EQUITY-CONTRIBUTION-FROM-RSA-FOR-RESIDENTIAL-MORTGAGE.pdf); [Nairametrics, 24 Sep 2022](https://nairametrics.com/2022/09/24/pencom-says-rsa-holders-can-now-use-their-pension-contributions-for-mortgage/)
- PenCom later barred 7 mortgage banks from the scheme for failing eligibility — [Finance in Africa](https://financeinafrica.com/news/nigerias-pencom-mortgage-housing-scheme/)

### Inferences
- With NMRC's marginal funding at ~17.25%, refinanced PMB mortgages cannot price below the high teens; sub-10% retail rates exist only where the state subsidises (NHF 6%, MREIF 9.75%, FHF 9.99%, Lagos HOMS 6%). Cooperative estates therefore have a strong incentive to be NHF/MREIF-compliant (titled, formal-sector members).
- Cooperatives can stack routes: PenCom 25% RSA for member equity + NHF/MREIF take-out + CHDL or bank bridge for construction; the FCMB/TEHC and Shelter Afrique/Customs deals show that employer-anchored cooperatives with salary-deduction cash flows can attract conventional project finance.

### Gaps
- No disclosed interest rate/tenor for the Shelter Afrique Customs loan or FCMB TEHC facility, and no disclosed member repayment terms.
- No published figure on how many RSA holders have actually used the 25% equity option.
- Did not find a dedicated CBN intervention fund for cooperative housing in 2024–26 sources; Lagos LBIC cooperative-specific product not found.

## Key Question 4: Project cash-flow structure, working-capital gap, escrow, interest capitalisation, cost overruns (2023–2026 inflation/FX), refund policy

### Takeaway
A cooperative estate is front-loaded (land and title, then infrastructure) while member inflows arrive in flat monthly instalments, creating a working-capital gap bridged by CHDL/bank debt or by phasing construction to collections. Construction inputs rose sharply: cement from ~₦3,900–4,050/bag (2022) to ~₦8,400 (Mar 2024), ~₦9,700 (Apr 2025) and ₦11,000–15,000 (2026); sharp sand roughly tripled in 2025–26. Published cooperative rules on who absorbs overruns were not found; pricing to members is typically revised ("cost-plus") rather than fixed.

### Cited Findings
- FMBN-prescribed sequence: cooperative acquires land → designs → title and planning permit → FMBN construction finance → construction monitoring → member mortgage packaging — [ThisDay, 11 Aug 2021](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/)
- CHDL carries a 12-month moratorium inside a 24-month tenor, i.e. interest accrues during construction and is settled at mortgage take-out — [ThisDay, 11 Aug 2021](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/)
- FCMB/TEHC: $40m phase-1 facility with completion "in phases over the next 36 months" — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/07/18/fcmb-backs-totalenergies-staff-housing-cooperative-multipurpose-society-ltd-flagship-project-louisville-phase-1-with-40m-loan-facility-in-eko-atlantic/)
- Cement: ₦4,050/bag (Dangote, 2022) with a government–producer 30% discount arrangement; 65% of construction materials imported; Lagos urban land ₦550,000–₦1m/m² — [CAHF 2022](https://housingfinanceafrica.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/V20-Nigeria-Final.pdf)
- Cement average ₦8,400 (Mar 2024) → ₦9,700 (Apr 2025, +15.5%) — [Build With Ease](https://buildwithease.com.ng/2026/05/05/cement-price-in-nigeria/); Lagos ₦9,000 (Dec 2025) → ₦10,000 (Jan 2026) → ₦11,500 — [Daily Trust](https://dailytrust.com/bag-of-cement-climbs-to-n11000/); ₦11,000–15,000 (Jul 2026) — [Marketplace.ng](https://www.marketplace.ng/blog/building-materials-prices-in-nigeria-2026-cement-blocks-iron-rods-roofing-sheets-and-more); ~300–400% rise since 2020 (₦2,500–3,000) — [Naijasabi](https://naijasabi.com.ng/cement-price-nigeria-today-2026/)
- 2025→2026: cement ₦8,500–10,000 → ₦13,000 (+30–53%); sharp sand per tipper ₦40,000–80,000 → ₦170,000–350,000; stone ₦180,000 → ₦350,000/t; tiles +25–89%; drivers "inflation, exchange rate volatility, multiple taxation, high transportation costs"; headline inflation 15.69% (Apr 2026) — [Housing TV Africa](https://www.housingtvafrica.com/building-material-prices-surge-as-inflation-fx-crisis-push-construction-costs-higher/)
- Cheapest formal new unit (2022): 42 m² expandable one-bed by Millard Fuller Foundation at ₦3.9m — [CAHF 2022](https://housingfinanceafrica.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/V20-Nigeria-Final.pdf)
- Title perfection 5–10% of value and governor's-consent delays are a recurrent cash-flow and timing cost — [BusinessDay, 8 Mar 2020](https://businessday.ng/real-estate/article/why-fmbn-seeks-state-govts-collaboration-on-affordable-housing/)
- FMBN's approval-to-disbursement gap (₦35.78bn approved vs ₦10.95bn disbursed, Jan 2021) illustrates that disbursement is milestone-gated and slow — [CAHF 2022](https://housingfinanceafrica.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/V20-Nigeria-Final.pdf); FMBN itself lists "No disbursement approval — loan approval doesn't guarantee immediate fund access" — [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/economy/1726243-fmbn-lists-10-reasons-nhf-mortgage-loan-delayed-denied/)
- Refund practice at cooperative level: 6–12 month waits reported — [Sahara Reporters](https://saharareporters.com/2022/11/10/police-officers-accuse-nigeria-police-cooperative-society-fraud-lament-6-months-delay); [FIJ](https://fij.ng/article/nigerian-in-germany-asked-for-his-n2-1m-to-pay-mothers-hospital-bills-kwara-co-operative-society-tells-him-to-wait-12-months/)

### Inferences
- Researcher's arithmetic: a 3-bed bungalow using ~500 bags of cement saw its cement bill alone rise by ~₦2.3m between Mar 2024 (₦8,400) and 2026 (₦13,000); with sand roughly tripling, a ₦15m 2024 unit cost plausibly rose 30–50% by 2026, consistent with press "+30–53%" for cement. For a cooperative on a 36-month instalment plan this means early subscribers' fixed prices are under-recovered unless a price-review clause exists.
- The CHDL's 12-month moratorium means roughly ₦50m of interest on a full ₦500m draw is capitalised into project cost (~10% of the facility) before any mortgage take-out — a cost that must be passed to members or absorbed by the cooperative.
- Escrow/project accounts: FMBN's milestone-gated disbursement effectively acts as a project-account control, but I found no public evidence that cooperatives ring-fence member instalments in escrow; the refund delays suggest commingling with general cooperative funds.

### Gaps
- No published cooperative-estate case with month-by-month cash-flow, overrun allocation clause, or formal escrow arrangement.
- No official construction-cost index (NBS building materials index) series retrieved; figures are press-reported spot prices.
- Steel/iron-rod price series not captured.

## Key Question 5: Member affordability — worked examples for a ₦15m unit; salaries and the one-third cap; cooperative pricing and profit

### Takeaway
On cited programme terms, a ₦15m unit costs ~₦417,000/month on a 36-month 100% cooperative instalment plan (unaffordable for virtually all salaried members under the one-third rule), ~₦63,000–81,000/month on an NHF mortgage at 6% over 30 years after 10–30% equity (affordable at Grade Level 8 federal pay of ~₦123,000–250,000/month only at the top of that band), and ~₦100,000/month on Rent-to-Own at 7%/30 years with zero equity. Cooperatives price at cost or cost-plus a small margin (Woodhill ₦7.5–9.5m in 2021), not at market.

### Cited Findings
**Inputs**
- NHF: 6% p.a., up to 30 years; 0% equity ≤₦5m, 10% above — [FMBN](https://fmbn.gov.ng/products/nhf_mortgage_loan); [BusinessDay, 25 Mar 2025](https://businessday.ng/news/article/fmbn-n50m-loan-treshold-excites-contributors-as-affordability-concerns-deepen/)
- Cooperative-scheme take-out historically 9% (≤₦15m) and 7% (Woodhill) — [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/property/fg-launches-national-cooperative-housing-development-scheme/); [ThisDay 2021](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/)
- Rent-to-Own 7%, 30 years, zero equity — [ThisDay 2021](https://thisdaylive.com/index.php/2021/12/13/understanding-fmbns-rent-to-own-housing-product)
- MREIF 9.75%, 20 years, 10% equity — [ARM](https://www.arm.com.ng/mreif-faq/)
- CHDL 10%, 24 months, 12-month moratorium — [ThisDay 2021](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/)
- Commercial mortgages 18–28% — [Swiftbankloan](https://swiftbankloan.com/mortgage-companies-in-nigeria/)
- Salaries: national minimum wage ₦70,000/month (National Minimum Wage Act 2024, July 2024) — [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/nigerian/what-is-nigerias-minimum-wage/); federal civil service GL08 ₦1,479,276–₦1,914,514 p.a. (~₦123,000–160,000/month) per Legit.ng; other sources quote GL08 monthly ₦234,000–250,000 (consolidated) — [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/nigeria/1728287-grade-level-8-salary-scale-nigerian-civil-service-minimum-wage-mentioned/); [InquireSalary](https://inquiresalary.com.ng/conpss-salary/) (sources conflict; use the range)
- Deduction cap: Labour Act s.5(7) — one-third of monthly wages — [jurist.ng](https://jurist.ng/labour_act/sec-5); FMBN itself caps renovation-loan repayment at 33⅓% of salary — [Daily Trust 2015](https://dailytrust.com/federal-mortgage-bank-of-nigeria-home-renovation-loan-scheme/); Lagos HOMS caps at 30% of income — [NPC](https://nigeriapropertycentre.com/blog/buying-selling/the-lagos-home-ownership-mortgage-scheme-homs-in-detail)
- Published affordability maths: ₦50m at 6%/30y ≈ ₦300,000/month, needing ~₦800,000 salary; ₦70,000 earner qualifies for ~₦297,170 and ₦160,688 earner for ~₦1.36m — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/news/article/fmbn-n50m-loan-treshold-excites-contributors-as-affordability-concerns-deepen/); [Dataphyte](https://dataphyte.substack.com/p/how-affordable-and-convenient-is-the-new-fmbn-mortgage-ceiling)
- Member pricing example: Woodhill units ₦7.5m / ₦9.5m (2021) from a ₦997m loan for 230 units — [ThisDay 2021](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/)
- Cooperatives "generate profit [but] this is only essential to survive in the market, their main intention is to pool resources from the members ... and the benefits are shared by them" — [Olujimi et al. 2021](https://www.internationalscholarsjournals.com/articles/the-lending-policy-of-cooperative-societies-and-its-effect-on-construction-of-housing-in-akure.pdf)

**Worked examples (researcher's arithmetic on the cited terms; ₦15m unit)**
- (a) 100% cooperative instalments, 36 months, no interest: ₦416,667/month. With 20% deposit (₦3m) then 80% over 36 months: ₦333,333/month. Under the one-third cap this requires gross pay of ≥₦1.0–1.25m/month — roughly 4–8× GL08 pay.
- (b) 30% equity (₦4.5m) + NHF mortgage ₦10.5m: at 6%/30y ₦62,953/month (total interest ₦12.16m); at 6%/20y ₦75,225; at 9%/30y (old cooperative-scheme rate) ₦84,485; at 9%/20y ₦94,471. With only 10% equity (current NHF rule) the ₦13.5m loan at 6%/30y is ₦80,939/month (total interest ₦15.64m). One-third rule implies minimum gross pay of ~₦190,000 (30% equity, 6%/30y) to ~₦243,000 (10% equity).
- (c) CHDL bridge then NHF take-out: during construction the member pays instalments toward equity (e.g. ₦4.5m over 24 months = ₦187,500/month) while the cooperative carries 10% interest on the CHDL (≈₦1m of capitalised interest per unit on a ₦10m-per-unit draw over the 12-month moratorium); at take-out the member's NHF mortgage of ₦10.5m (+ any capitalised interest passed through) costs ₦62,953/month at 6%/30y. Peak monthly burden is therefore in the construction phase, not the mortgage phase.
- Comparators: Rent-to-Own ₦15m at 7%/30y, zero equity: ₦99,795/month (total ₦35.9m). MREIF ₦13.5m at 9.75%/20y: ₦128,050/month. Commercial bank ₦13.5m at 24%/20y: ₦272,350/month.

### Inferences
- The only routes that fit inside one-third of a GL08-type salary (₦123,000–250,000) are 6% NHF with 30-year tenor (₦63,000–81,000) and, at the top of the band, Rent-to-Own (~₦100,000). Pure cooperative instalment plans over 36 months are feasible only for senior staff or dual-income households, which is why cooperatives phase payments over land-first then build-later and lean on FMBN take-out.
- Woodhill pricing (₦7.5m–₦9.5m in 2021) alongside a ₦4.3m-per-unit construction loan suggests cooperatives price at cost-plus a thin margin or administrative charge; I found no evidence of cooperatives pricing units at market with a view to profit.

### Gaps
- No published cooperative bye-law or offer letter stating a margin/administrative-charge percentage on estate units.
- Civil-service salary sources conflict (basic vs consolidated); the writer should present a range.

## Key Question 6: Investor/return perspective — surplus, land appreciation, resale restrictions, rental yields

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin. Cooperative law requires bye-laws to restrict transfer of members' interests, and listings show that member-allocated plots in Abuja cooperative estates now trade at ₦85–100m (a large uplift that accrues to the allottee, not the society). No sourced data on cooperative estate surpluses, buy-back clauses or rental yields was found.

### Cited Findings
- Co-operative Societies Act: bye-laws must address "restriction on transfer of shares or interest of members" — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf); governor's consent is required for any alienation of interest in land — [DLA Piper REALWORLD Nigeria](https://www.dlapiperrealworld.com/law/index.html?t=sale-and-purchase&s=ownership-of-real-estate&c=NG)
- NNPC Cooperative Estate (Life Camp, Abuja) plots listed at ₦85–95m (750 m²) and ₦100m (1,000 m²) in 2026 — [Nigeria Property Centre](https://nigeriapropertycentre.com/for-sale/abuja?n=cooperative+estate)
- Cooperatives' profit motive is secondary to member service (academic characterisation) — [Olujimi et al. 2021](https://www.internationalscholarsjournals.com/articles/the-lending-policy-of-cooperative-societies-and-its-effect-on-construction-of-housing-in-akure.pdf)
- 2022 benchmark rent on cheapest formal unit: ₦15,000/month on a ₦3.9m unit (≈4.6% gross yield, researcher's arithmetic) — [CAHF 2022](https://housingfinanceafrica.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/V20-Nigeria-Final.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because allocation is to individual members who then hold the title (or sub-lease), land appreciation accrues to members; the society's upside is limited to administrative charges and any unallocated plots retained.
- Where the estate is mortgage-financed (NHF/MREIF), the lender's charge and governor's-consent requirements act as de facto resale restrictions until the mortgage is discharged.

### Gaps
- No audited accounts or surplus figures for a Nigerian housing cooperative estate found.
- No published buy-back / right-of-first-refusal clause from a named cooperative estate found.
- No rental-yield data specific to cooperative estates.

## Key Question 7: Comparators — Kenya SACCOs/KMRC, Ethiopia condominium savings, South Africa FLISP, Sweden/Germany cooperative finance

### Takeaway
Kenya's model (KMRC on-lends at 5% to SACCOs, which lend to members at 7–9.5% over up to 25 years; land/housing is 37% of new SACCO credit) is the closest workable template for Nigeria. Ethiopia shows a state-bank savings-then-mortgage (10/90, 20/80, 40/60) structure; South Africa pairs a once-off income-linked subsidy (R27,960–R169,264) with a bank bond; Sweden and Germany run building-level association debt with member shares and, in Germany, a KfW loan of up to €100,000 for buying cooperative shares.

### Cited Findings
**Kenya**
- KMRC lends to banks/SACCOs at 5% p.a. — [Cytonn](https://cytonn.com/topicals/kenya-mortgage-refinance-1); on-lent at ~8.9% via banks and 7% via select SACCOs, tenors extended to 20–25 years; >5,000 mortgages refinanced, nearly half to women — [The Star, 22 Aug 2026](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2026-08-22-cheaper-home-loans-now-boost-mortgage-uptake)
- Mwalimu National SACCO: 9% reducing balance, KSh500,000–8m, up to 25 years — [Standard](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/business/business/article/2001465156/mwalimu-national-sacco-and-kmrc-unveil-mortgage-loan-facility) (paywalled; terms from search summary); Stima SACCO: KSh500,000–10.5m, 9–9.5% reducing, up to 25 years — [Stima SACCO](https://www.stima-sacco.com/mortgage-facilities/)
- Banks and SACCOs drew KSh7.7bn from KMRC in 2025; cumulative KSh19.6bn to KCB, Co-op Bank, Stima SACCO etc. — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/markets/market-news/banks-saccos-tap-sh7-7-billion-kmrc-loans-to-boost-mortgages-5403350)
- Land and housing = 37.3% (KSh20.08bn) of KSh53.8bn new SACCO credit in 2025; KSh157.2bn of SACCO lending outstanding to land/housing — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/land-and-housing-dominate-sacco-lending-5623422); SASRA study on SACCO affordable-housing finance (Nov 2025) — [SASRA](https://www.sasra.go.ke/2025/11/20/strengthening-affordable-housing-finance-through-saccos-new-study-released/)

**Ethiopia**
- Schemes 10/90, 20/80, 40/60 = share saved at Commercial Bank of Ethiopia over 3/7/5 years before CBE mortgage for the remainder; 40/60 savings earned 5.5%, loan at 7.5% over 17 years (original design) — [Wikipedia 40/60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40/60_(housing_program)); [Patricia Sendin](https://www.patriciasendin.com/2015/04/addis-ababa-ambitious-housing-programme.html); CBE condominium loan rates revised Mar 2025: 40/60 and 20/80 at 12%, 10/90 at 15.5% — [Addis Insight](https://addisinsight.net/2025/03/04/commercial-bank-of-ethiopia-revises-loan-interest-rates/); repayment 10 years (1-bed) / 12 years (2–3 bed) — [Capital Ethiopia](https://www.capitalethiopia.com/2019/08/12/cbe-drops-grace-period-for-40-60-condo-payment/)

**South Africa**
- FLISP/First Home Finance: income R3,501–R22,000/month; once-off subsidy R27,960–R169,264 paid into the bond; quantum table effective 1 Apr 2023 — [ooba](https://www.ooba.co.za/resources/flisp-requirements/); [ooba subsidy amounts](https://www.ooba.co.za/faq/housing-subsidy-amounts/)

**Sweden / Germany**
- Sweden (bostadsrätt): member buys a share in the BRF which owns the building; monthly fee covers association mortgage interest/amortisation, tax, insurance, maintenance; association loans can be ~SEK15,000/m²; no legal cap on fee increases — [Bofrid](https://bofrid.se/en/knowledge-bank/bostadsratt); [The Local](https://www.thelocal.se/20230727/what-happens-if-a-swedish-housing-cooperative-gets-into-financial-trouble); members typically fund 75–80% of construction cost with the co-op borrowing the rest — [Cooperative Housing International](https://www.housinginternational.coop/co-ops/sweden/)
- Germany (Wohnungsgenossenschaft): members buy shares (refunded at nominal value on exit); 48 cooperatives run their own savings institutions; KfW 134 loan up to €100,000 for buying cooperative shares, with interest reduction and 15% repayment subsidy (2022 upgrade) — [Cooperative Housing International](https://www.housinginternational.coop/co-ops/germany/); [Grantbite KfW](https://www.grantbite.com/en/funding/kfw-cooperative-housing-funding)

### Inferences
- Nigeria's FMBN already plays the KMRC role (4% to PMBs → 6% to members) but lacks a cooperative-as-lender channel; Kenya's SACCO on-lending at 7% with 25-year tenors is the directly transferable structure (cooperative originates and services, refinancer funds).
- Ethiopia's "save X% at the state bank, then borrow" mirrors Nigeria's cooperative instalment-then-NHF route but with a formal savings-interest and a fixed lock-in period; Germany's KfW share-purchase loan is the closest analogue to financing a member's equity contribution (cf. PenCom 25% RSA).

### Gaps
- Kenyan SACCO default rates on housing loans and KMRC's cumulative Nigeria-comparable unit counts beyond "5,000+ mortgages" not found.
- No Sweden/Germany 2025–26 rate data retrieved; structures only.
