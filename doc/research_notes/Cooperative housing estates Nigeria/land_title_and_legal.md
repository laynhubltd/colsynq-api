# Cooperative Housing Estates in Nigeria: Land Title, Legal and Regulatory Risk

Scope: the title chain from acquisition to each member's individual document, approvals, taxes/fees, dispute patterns and controls. Nigeria only (Lagos and FCT emphasis). Research date: 8 October 2026. Figures and case outcomes are dated where the source dates them; unresolved matters are labelled.

## KQ1. Land tenure under the Land Use Act 1978 and FCT specifics (R of O, AGIS, mass housing, 2023–2026 revocations)

### Takeaway
All land is vested in the state Governor (FCT: the Minister) under the Land Use Act 1978; a cooperative only ever holds a right of occupancy that can be revoked for breach of the terms of grant (s.28(5)) — and the FCT has, since 2023, used that power aggressively and in bulk against non-payment of ground rent and non-development. A cooperative that acquires FCT land therefore inherits a live, enforced obligation to pay annual ground rent in advance and to develop within two years of the R of O.

### Cited Findings
- Under the Land Use Act 1978 all land in each state is vested in the Governor, who holds it in trust; title is acquired by government allocation, private purchase, inheritance, gift or long possession — [Mondaq: Land Ownership and Title Disputes in Nigeria](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/land-law-agriculture/1623260/land-ownership-and-title-disputes-in-nigeria-understanding-the-land-tenure-system-and-title-verification)
- Lagos title documents a cooperative will encounter: Certificate of Occupancy, Governor's Consent, Deed of Assignment, Deed of Sublease, Registered Conveyance, Gazette, Excision, Court Judgment, Probate/Letters of Administration, Deed of Gift and Survey Plan; a Gazette or Excision shows land has been released from government acquisition to a community/family — [Mondaq: Steps for Real Estate Due Diligence in Nigeria, Lagos focus](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/landlord-tenant-leases/1793552/steps-for-real-estate-due-diligence-in-nigeria-special-focus-on-lagos)
- Charting at the Lagos Surveyor-General distinguishes "committed government acquisition" (land reserved for roads, schools, hospitals, drainage, housing schemes — not available for private use), "general acquisition" (may be regularised on confirmation) and "excised land" (released to communities/families) — [Mondaq due diligence](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/landlord-tenant-leases/1793552/steps-for-real-estate-due-diligence-in-nigeria-special-focus-on-lagos); "free land" = land in which government has no interest; "committed acquisition" = designated for public projects — [Isochukwu: Excision in process](https://isochukwu.com/2023/01/21/the-dangers-of-buying-an-excision-in-process-land-in-lagos/)
- Excision workflow (Lagos): community–government agreement → Surveyor-General maps/delineates → survey plan separating government and community portions → beacons → Governor approval (described as "the greatest challenge") → Gazette publication recording size, location, dates, beacons — [Isochukwu](https://isochukwu.com/2023/01/21/the-dangers-of-buying-an-excision-in-process-land-in-lagos/); an excision application goes to the Technical Committee of the Land Use Allocation Committee within 7 days of receipt and site inspection follows ~14 days after the Surveyor-General's report, but in practice excision "takes many years and money" and "80% of approvals fall by the wayside" — [Landsng blog](https://www.landsng.com/Blog/Details/12) via search summary (industry blog; treat the 80% as anecdotal)
- "Excision in progress" land has no valid title during processing, cannot be properly documented, and is a common fraud tactic; buyers should insist on completed excision with Gazette publication and Governor approval, not a file number — [Isochukwu](https://isochukwu.com/2023/01/21/the-dangers-of-buying-an-excision-in-process-land-in-lagos/)
- FCT residential grants are for a maximum of 99 years; other uses 35–70 years depending on value of improvements; AGIS (established 2003) now handles all land allocation processes, C of O issuance and title verification — [AGIS FAQ](https://agis.fcta.gov.ng/faq/) via search summary; [AGIS](https://agis.fcta.gov.ng/)
- FCT Mass Housing Programme (PPP, started 2000): 360 private developers allocated 12,691 ha across 22 districts in mass housing zones; the FCT provides land and primary infrastructure, developers build houses and tertiary infrastructure — [Appraisal of the Mass Housing Programme in FCT (academic)](https://aijcr.thebrpi.org/journals/Vol_4_No_4_April_2014/27.pdf); [FCDA Mass Housing/PPP](https://fcda.gov.ng/mass-housing-ppp/)
- Only 2 of 445 mass housing developers have fully complied with the original terms of allocation since 2000 — [Gazette NGR](https://gazettengr.com/2-out-of-445-mass-housing-developers-met-fcta-conditions-official/)
- FCT land reforms effective 21 April 2025: allottees have 21 days from offer to pay all bills/fees/rents and submit acceptance or lose the offer; a two-year development deadline runs from the date of the Statutory R of O; vetted Area Council land documents get 60 days to pay for conversion to statutory title; FCTA decided to commence titling of mass housing estate properties in favour of current occupants, with developers and subscribers initiating title applications — [TheCable](https://www.thecable.ng/wike-enforces-21-day-payment-deadline-two-year-building-limit-in-fct/); [Tribune](https://tribuneonlineng.com/wike-launches-landmark-reforms-in-fct-land-administration/); [Leadership: vetting teams for mass housing/Area Council titling](https://leadership.ng/fcta-sets-up-vetting-teams-on-regularisation-of-area-council-mass-housing-land-allocations/)
- Of 261,914 Area Council land documents submitted for regularisation since 2006, only 8,287 had been vetted and 2,358 regularised to statutory titles (as of April 2025) — [TheCable](https://www.thecable.ng/wike-enforces-21-day-payment-deadline-two-year-building-limit-in-fct/)
- 23 Feb 2026: FCTA nullified 485 Area Council land documents confirmed fake during regularisation vetting (Bwari: Ushafa, Dawaki Ext; AMAC: Kurudu-Jikwoyi, Karu, Nyanya Phase IV, Jikwoyi, Sabon Lugbe, Lugbe I Ext; Kuje: Kuchiyako One layout) — [AllAfrica/Daily Trust](https://allafrica.com/stories/202602240664.html)
- FCT revocations timeline: Sept 2023 (165 plots for non-development in CBD, Maitama, Wuse II, Gudu, Wuye, Jabi, Utako, Katampe Ext, Idu, Asokoro) — [Daily Trust](https://dailytrust.com/wike-revokes-165-plots-of-land-in-abuja/); Oct 2023 (600+ titles) — [SaharaReporters](https://saharareporters.com/2025/11/28/fct-minister-wike-revokes-1095-property-titles-abuja-over-unpaid-ground-rent-others); Dec 2024 (762 Maitama 1 C of Os over unpaid statutory fees) — [PUO Reports](https://puoreports.ng/2024/12/19/wike-revokes-plots-belonging-to-buhari-onnoghen-abbas-akume-others/); 18 Mar 2025: 4,794 titles revoked for 10–43 years' ground rent arrears, 8,375 owners owing ₦6.97bn as at end-2024, legal basis s.28(5)(a)-(b) LUA, 21-day grace for 1–10-year defaulters — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/03/18/fct-minister-revokes-4794-land-titles-in-abuja-over-10-years-of-unpaid-ground-rent/); 26–27 May 2025: President Tinubu paused enforcement and gave defaulters 14 days — [FCTA](https://myfctagov.ng/index.php/2025/05/27/fct-ground-rent-defaulters-get-14-days-to-pay-as-president-tinubu-intervenes-amid-enforcement/); 2 June 2025: FCTA published 3,383 allottees (arrears 2014–2024) with a 14-day ultimatum; ground rent is payable in advance from 1 January each year under the R of O/C of O terms — [Premium Times](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/top-news/798538-ground-rent-fcta-threatens-to-revoke-properties-belonging-to-ex-fct-minister-navy-foreign-embassies-others-issues-14-days-ultimatum-full-list-of-3383-allottees.html); 28 Nov 2025: 1,095 titles revoked (835 ground rent; 260 violation/land-use conversion fees) in Asokoro, Maitama, Garki, Wuse after notices May–Nov 2025 and expiry of grace on 25 Nov 2025; institutional defaulters included ONSA, NNPCL, CBN, FHA, Nigerian Navy, several banks and state governments — [Premium Times](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/headlines/839336-wike-revokes-abuja-lands-belonging-to-patience-jonathan-fayose-iyabo-obasanjo-t-y-danjuma-emir-of-ilorin-others.html); [SaharaReporters](https://saharareporters.com/2025/11/28/fct-minister-wike-revokes-1095-property-titles-abuja-over-unpaid-ground-rent-others)
- NFIU Staff Multipurpose Cooperative Society Limited was reported in press coverage of the Nov 2025 revocation list as owing ₦298 million — reported in a search summary of [Guardian NG coverage](https://guardian.ng/news/danjuma-patience-jonathan-cbn-others-lose-abuja-plots-in-wikes-mass-revocation/) (full text could not be fetched; the Premium Times text fetched did not reproduce the PDF list). Treat as press-reported, not independently verified from the FCTA list.
- Revocation litigation (UNRESOLVED): Abuja Technology Village Free Trade Zone investors sued the FCT Minister (FHC/ABJ/CS/1539/2025) over a 13 May 2025 revocation for "non-payment of ground rent and underdevelopment", pleading no prior notice/demand and NEPZA Act s.8 exemption; they claim >₦978bn of committed investment at risk — [Vanguard, Aug 2025](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2025/08/ground-rent-investors-sue-wike-over-revocation-of-plots-at-abuja-free-trade-zone/)
- Notice is a condition of valid revocation: in Olabanji v Hon. Minister FCT & 2 Ors (FCT High Court, unreported, 25 Feb 2025) the court held a right of occupancy "cannot be lost by revocation without being notified first in writing"; a 1993 allocation beat a 1999 C of O over the same Katampe plot on the "first in time" priority rule — [Legal500 / Olabanji case note](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/litigation-mediation-arbitration/double-allocation-of-land-conflicting-titles-and-the-doctrine-of-priority-lessons-from-olabanji-v-the-honourable-minister-federal-capital-territory-2-ors)
- Nationwide: in Oct 2024 the Federal Ministry of Housing gave titled property owners on federal land 60 days to pay ground rent or face C of O revocation — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2024/10/23/fg-gives-titled-property-owners-nationwide-60-days-to-pay-ground-rent-or-face-c-of-o-revocation/)

### Inferences
- A cooperative holding one master R of O/C of O for an estate concentrates the whole membership's exposure in a single revocable title: one missed ground-rent cycle or a two-year non-development breach can wipe out every member's interest at once. This is the structural reason the NFIU-type exposure matters.
- Because FCT enforcement has swung between hard revocation (Mar 2025, Nov 2025) and presidential pauses (May 2025), cooperatives cannot bank on grace periods; the only durable control is a statutory-charges calendar funded from member levies.
- The 2025 mass-housing titling drive means subscribers in FCT estates (including cooperative-developed ones) now have an official route to individual titles, but with only 2 of 445 developers compliant and 2,358 of 261,914 Area Council documents regularised, the queue is long and depends on the developer's/cooperative's own compliance.

### Gaps
- Could not verify the NFIU ₦298m figure against the FCTA's own published list (PDF not accessible), nor confirm whether the NFIU cooperative's title was actually revoked or subsequently settled.
- No source found that specifically describes the cooperative-allocation stream within FCT mass housing (how cooperatives, as opposed to developers, obtain allocation letters), nor compensation outcomes for any cooperative whose land was revoked for "overriding public interest" (s.28(2)) rather than breach.

## KQ2. Acquisition due diligence: searches, charting, vendor verification, community/omo-onile, deed of assignment and perfection — costs and timelines

### Takeaway
Due diligence in Lagos has three mandatory legs — Lands Registry search, Surveyor-General charting, and verification of the vendor's authority (family head/principal members, accredited representatives) — and perfection (Governor's consent, stamp duty, registration) costs about 3% of assessed value in statutory charges plus professional fees, taking roughly 4–9 months; the 2026 Lagos "Blue Book" revaluation raised assessed values so sharply that total perfection outlays in prime areas rose up to 300%.

### Cited Findings
- Lagos Lands Registry search can be run by title number, registration particulars, survey plan, block/plot number and location; it reveals authenticity and adverse interests. Charting at the Surveyor-General reveals government acquisition (committed/general), road alignment, drainage, coastal regulation and excision status. Planning approval status can be verified on the Lagos Electronic Physical Planning Permit system — [Mondaq due diligence](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/landlord-tenant-leases/1793552/steps-for-real-estate-due-diligence-in-nigeria-special-focus-on-lagos)
- For family, community, cooperative or traditional land-owning-family vendors, the buyer must identify the proper representatives and confirm all principal family members or accredited representatives join in the sale — [Mondaq due diligence](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/landlord-tenant-leases/1793552/steps-for-real-estate-due-diligence-in-nigeria-special-focus-on-lagos)
- Legal checks and searches in Lagos typically cost ₦100,000–₦500,000; conveyancing lawyers charge 2–5% of price; agents 3–10% (typically 5%); total closing costs 8–15% of price, of which roughly 4–8% is government transfer/perfection cost — [The African Vestor, 2026](https://theafricanvestor.com/blogs/news/lagos-nigeria-property-taxes-fees) (investor blog; indicative ranges)
- Lagos perfection steps: (1) Governor's consent application to the Director of Lands Services through the Lands Registry portal with certified root-of-title copies, executed deed, 3 survey plans, ID, photos, dated site photos and sketch; charting & endorsement fee ₦10,500; registry inspects and values; (2) stamp duty at 0.5% of assessed value; (3) registration. Fee schedule: consent 1.5%, stamp duty 0.5%, registration 0.5% of assessed value; typical timeline 4–9 months; unperfected transfers are "legally defective" and a later purchaser with consent gets superior legal interest — [Firmus Nigeria](https://firmusnigeria.com/register-title-to-land-lagos-state/)
- Aggregate government charge on a straightforward Lagos sale ≈3% of assessed value (consent 1.5%, CGT 0.5%, stamp duty 0.5%, registration 0.5%) — [The African Vestor: Governor's consent](https://theafricanvestor.com/blogs/news/lagos-get-governor-consent-property) via search summary
- Lagos 2026 Fair Market Value and Governor's Consent charges ("Blue Book") took effect 1 May 2026; increases of up to 300% in prime areas. Worked examples quoted: Lekki Phase 1 perfection ₦12–18m (2015) → ₦40–90m (2026); Ikoyi ₦25–40m → ₦100–250m; Banana Island waterfront ₦700m–₦1bn on ~₦10bn property. Government said valuation benchmarks were unchanged 2005–2015 — [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/industry/1709992-lagos-increases-governors-consent-stamp-duties-land-transaction-fees/)
- Lagos State Properties Protection Law 2016 (signed 15 Aug 2016): s.2–3 forcible entry/takeover — 10 years; s.4 remaining after eviction notice — ₦5m or 5 years; s.8 selling without title/authority — ₦500,000 or 6 months, and double sale — fine up to 100% of property value or 5 years with property reverting to the legal owner; selling family land without the family head's authority or government land without authority — 21 years; s.11 omo-onile construction levies — ₦1m and/or 2 years; s.9 professionals facilitating illegal deals referred for misconduct; s.12 task force with arrest powers; Special Offences Court — [Isochukwu review](https://isochukwu.com/2023/01/21/lagos-state-properties-protection-law-2016-a-detailed-review/); [LawPavilion](https://lawpavilion.com/blog/lagos-state-property-protection-law-2016/); [Legalnaija](https://legalnaija.com/lagos-state-properties-protection-law/)
- FCT: before any transaction, confirm the layout has ministerial approval — "All layouts in the city or Area Councils must have ministerial approval before the plots are allocated" — [Daily Trust FCT building plan guide](https://dailytrust.com/guide-to-obtaining-building-plans-approval-in-fct/)
- FCT double allocation is "persistent": competing titles appear as C of O, allocation letter, offer of statutory R of O, acceptance letter, deed of assignment or irrevocable power of attorney; the first-in-time rule applies and the second allottee's remedy is against the allocating authority for alternative land or refund — [Legal500 Olabanji note](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/litigation-mediation-arbitration/double-allocation-of-land-conflicting-titles-and-the-doctrine-of-priority-lessons-from-olabanji-v-the-honourable-minister-federal-capital-territory-2-ors)
- Academic: in Osogbo, "difficulty in land acquisition" (factor loading 0.74) and "difficult land-registration process" (0.626) were significant impediments to cooperative housing; C of O requirements "often delay and add significant costs" — [Ajayi 2022, Acta Structilia](https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S2415-04872022000200003)
- Lagos cooperatives face a "land titling catch-22": FMBN's Cooperative Housing Development Loan requires unencumbered titled land, but cooperatives lack capital to acquire titled land — [Adewole 2026, IJRISS systematic review](https://rsisinternational.org/journals/ijriss/view/factors-affecting-housing-affordability-and-housing-cooperatives-performance-within-lagos-state-a-systematic-literature-review)

### Inferences
- The most dangerous Lagos acquisition for a cooperative is cheap "excision in progress" community land at Ibeju-Lekki/Epe: no registrable title exists until gazette, so the cooperative cannot perfect, cannot sub-assign, and cannot pledge to FMBN — while also being exposed to the 176-estate "illegal layout" enforcement (see KQ3).
- Because consent/stamp/registration are ad valorem on assessed value, the 2026 Blue Book materially raises the cost of a cooperative perfecting a master title and then issuing dozens of sub-deeds; budgeting at 3% statutory + 2–5% legal on each layer is now the floor.

### Gaps
- No official Lagos Lands Bureau published fee for a registry search or charting beyond the ₦10,500 charting/endorsement figure; no official statutory timeline for consent (the 4–9 months is practitioner experience).
- No FCT-specific perfection cost schedule (consent fee, deed registration) was found in the sources fetched.

## KQ3. Approvals: layout, building plan, EIA, infrastructure permits; Lagos LASPPPA and FCT Development Control durations

### Takeaway
A cooperative estate needs a layout approval before any plot is allocated or sold, then individual planning/building permits (and fencing permits) — in Lagos this is enforced by the Ministry of Physical Planning, which declared 176 estates illegal in August 2025 for lacking layout approval and has demolished unapproved estate buildings; in the FCT, layout needs ministerial approval and building approval is statutorily due within 90 days.

### Cited Findings
- Lagos planning regime: Lagos State Urban and Regional Planning and Development Law and the Physical Planning Permit Regulations 2019; LASPPPA grants planning permits; the e-Planning Permit portal is the application channel — [LASPPPA Regulations 2019 (PDF)](https://epp.lagosstate.gov.ng/regulations/REVISED_LASPPPA_REGULATION_2019_1.pdf); [e-Planning Permit](https://www.epp.lagosstate.gov.ng/Home/PlanningPermitApproval)
- LASPPPA building permit timeline 4–12 weeks from full submission; permits valid 2 years; fast-track available for extra fee; indicative government fees: residential bungalow/1–3 bed ₦150,000–₦500,000, 2–3 storey duplex ₦400,000–₦1.2m, small commercial ₦600,000–₦2m, high-rise ₦2m+; add-ons include layout fee, fencing fee, local planning office fee, LASEMA levy, spatial enhancement fee and infrastructure development charge; small fixed items cited: fencing permit ₦10,000, application fee ₦10,000, layout fee ₦5,000, registration ₦5,000, certificate of fitness levy ₦10,000 — [Build With Ease 2026](https://buildwithease.com.ng/2026/04/24/building-plan-approval-in-lagos-2026/); [Massodih Plans](https://massodihplans.com/lagos-building-plan-approval-complete-guide-to-fees-documents-application-process-timeline-and-eppps/); [Koriat Law](https://koriatlaw.com/how-to-obtain-a-building-permit-approval-in-lagos-state/) (practitioner/consultant figures)
- 4 Aug 2025: Lagos Ministry of Physical Planning and Urban Development listed 176 estates in Eti-Osa, Ajah, Ibeju-Lekki and elsewhere as illegal for lacking layout approvals (also planning and fencing permits); 21-day ultimatum to regularise; the list includes "FTC Yaba Staff Housing Estate" (Oribanwa, Ibeju-Lekki) — a staff-scheme estate; demolition threatened where developments encroach on committed lands; this followed an April 2025 Epe-corridor crackdown and a 90-day amnesty — [Premium Times (full list)](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/regional/ssouth-west/811991-lagos-govt-lists-176-illegal-estates-issues-developers-ultimatum-to-obtain-approvals-full-list.html)
- 4 Dec 2025: Lagos issued a "final warning" to unapproved estates, threatening demolition and fines — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/12/04/lagos-govt-issues-final-warning-to-unapproved-estates-threatens-demolition-and-fines/); Lagos demolished 17 buildings in Ikota estate — [Premium Times](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/top-news/827710-why-lagos-govt-demolished-17-ikota-estate-buildings-official.html); 22 Nov 2025 LASBCA razed unapproved buildings in the Ikate-Elegushi corridor; 31 Dec 2024 was the deadline to regularise before demolitions began 31 Mar 2025 — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/03/31/lagos-govt-begins-demolition-of-unapproved-buildings-as-amnesty-period-ends/); [PropertyAccess](https://propertyaccess.ng/lagos-govt-begins-demolition-of-illegal-structures-in-ikate-elegushi/)
- FCT Development Control process: application forms on payment of fees; documents: C of O or R of O with payment receipts, Title Deed Plan (TDP), site plan 1:500, floor plans 1:200/1:100, structural/mechanical/electrical drawings sealed by professionals, Site Analysis Report, EIA where required; construction to commence within 2 years or apply for validation; setting-out approval before excavation; layouts need ministerial approval before allocation — [Daily Trust](https://dailytrust.com/guide-to-obtaining-building-plans-approval-in-fct/)
- FCT statutory approval period is 90 days under the Urban and Regional Planning Law; Development Control is considering 60 days — [Abuja Digest](https://abujadigest.info/stories/news/dev-control-mulls-90-days-for-building-plan-approval/) via search summary; indicative FCT costs: processing fee ₦500,000–₦1m; architectural drawings ₦200,000; structural/M&E ₦150,000; soil test/EIA ₦250,000 — [ICA.ng guide](https://ica.ng/comprehensive-guide-on-requirements-and-cost-of-processing-building-plan-approval-from-fct-development-control-department/) (consultant estimates)
- FCT demolitions of unapproved estate buildings in 2025: AMMC ordered demolition of 24 two-storey buildings by Cityscape Estate on Plot 4851 Guzape built on a waterway without approval — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/news/article/fcta-orders-demolition-of-24-storey-buildings/); duplexes on a green area in Wumba District demolished after stop-work orders were ignored; FCTA to investigate officials conniving with developers over green areas — [Vanguard](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2025/05/fcta-to-demolish-over-10-buildings-on-green-areas-waterways/); [Daily Trust](https://dailytrust.com/fcta-officials-fingered-in-green-areas-disappearance/); Development Control demolished 56+ duplexes, rendering families homeless — [AIT](https://ait.live/abuja-development-control-demolishes-over-fifty-six-duplexes-renders-families-homeless/)
- Approvals as a shield: in Royal Niger Properties Ltd & 7 Ors v Sunrise Estate Development Ltd, Setraco, Hon. Minister FCT & Ors (FCT/HC/CV/1705/14, Kutigi J, 4 Oct 2021), neighbours' claims that an Asokoro estate sat on a "flood plain/green area" in breach of the Abuja Master Plan and Development Control Manual 2007 failed because the developer proved an EIA (Exhibits D11) and that "all necessary approvals were obtained ... before construction work began"; all 16 reliefs dismissed — [FCT High Court judgment PDF](https://www.fcthighcourt.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/ROYAL-NIGER-PPTIES-LTD-VS.-SUNRISE-ESTATE-DEV-LTDALLOCATION-OF-LAND.pdf)
- 2025 FCTA imposed ₦5m fines for illegal land-use conversion — [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202509030266.html)

### Inferences
- "Staff/cooperative" branding gives no immunity: the Lagos 176-estate list included a staff housing estate, and Lagos frames layout approval, not title, as the gating document — a cooperative can hold perfected title and still be "illegal" for planning purposes.
- Sequence matters: layout approval should be obtained before plots are allocated to members, because both Lagos and FCT treat pre-layout allocation as the defect, and member-built houses are the collateral damage in demolition.

### Gaps
- No official LASPPPA fee schedule specific to estate layout approval (as distinct from building permits) or official processing time for layout approval was found; figures are consultant estimates.
- No source found quantifying how many of the 176 Lagos estates regularised versus were demolished by 2026.

## KQ4. Transferring title to members: sub-lease/sub-assignment, individual C of O, strata/sectional title for flats, costs, consequences of non-perfection

### Takeaway
The standard route is a perfected master title in the cooperative's name followed by individual Deeds of Sublease/Sub-assignment (Lagos) or, in the FCT, individual statutory titles via the 2025 mass-housing/estate titling drive; each layer attracts its own consent, stamp duty and registration at ad valorem rates, and a member holding only an allocation letter has no registrable legal estate and ranks behind any later purchaser who perfects. No Lagos or federal sectional/strata title statute was found — flats in Nigeria are typically held by sublease plus management arrangements, not strata title.

### Cited Findings
- Deed of Sublease is a recognised Lagos title document alongside Deed of Assignment and C of O — [Mondaq due diligence](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/landlord-tenant-leases/1793552/steps-for-real-estate-due-diligence-in-nigeria-special-focus-on-lagos); the Lagos perfection process expressly covers an "executed Deed of Assignment/Sublease/Gift" with the same consent (1.5%), stamp duty (0.5%) and registration (0.5%) charges; "double consent" cases require a Letter of Indemnity, affidavit and newspaper publication — [Firmus Nigeria](https://firmusnigeria.com/register-title-to-land-lagos-state/)
- Transfers without Governor's consent are legally defective; possession alone does not establish legal ownership; a later purchaser who obtains consent acquires superior legal interest — [Firmus Nigeria](https://firmusnigeria.com/register-title-to-land-lagos-state/); under s.2 Lagos State Land Registration Law 2015 every instrument of interest or title to land must be registered — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=b98a29b3-6734-4f90-8b7d-5367cafe2b59)
- The Governor's consent requirement means a buyer who "paid, built and lives there" may still not own the land in law — [Opinion Nigeria essay](https://www.opinionnigeria.com/you-paid-you-built-you-live-there-but-do-you-actually-own-that-land-the-governors-consent-requirement-and-its-consequences-for-nigerian-property-owners-by-echoga-caleb-and-benedicta-sosu/)
- FCT: the administration approved issuance of titles on properties purchased in mass housing estates; from 21 April 2025 developers and subscribers initiate applications processed by the Department of Land Administration; vetting teams were set up so "all subscribers of unit houses in the Mass Housing Programmes will be captured and be given titles for their individual housing units"; vetted allottees have 60 days to pay or forfeit — [TheCable](https://www.thecable.ng/wike-enforces-21-day-payment-deadline-two-year-building-limit-in-fct/); [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/fcta-sets-up-vetting-teams-on-regularisation-of-area-council-mass-housing-land-allocations/); [HousingTV Africa](https://www.housingtvafrica.com/fct-begins-issuance-of-statutory-rights-of-occupancy-for-regularised-area-council-lands/)
- The Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act and state Co-operative Societies Laws restrict transfer of a member's shares/interest (state law s.36) and provide for devolution on death (s.37), give the society priority of claim over members' property (s.20), make shares non-attachable (s.21), and restrict dealings with non-members (s.17; federal Act: "transactions of a registered society with persons other than members ... shall be subject to such prohibitions and restrictions as may be prescribed") — [Law Nigeria: Co-operative Societies Law](https://lawnigeria.com/2020/03/27/co-operatives-societies-law/); [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act (FAO text)](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Academic case (Enugu): the NEPA District Co-operative Thrift and Loan Saving Association scouted, purchased, surveyed, sub-divided and allocated plots to beneficiaries, which the authors say "ensured tenure security" — [Ibem & Odum, UK Society for Co-operative Studies](https://hubble-live-assets.s3.eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/uk-society-for-co-operative-studies/file_asset/file/452/s4-IbemOdum132.pdf)
- Search for a "Lagos Sectional Titles Law" returned only South African and Botswana sectional title statutes and no Nigerian equivalent — [search results](https://www.privateproperty.co.za/advice/property/articles/sectional-title-schemes/3648); the only Lagos hits were the Land Registration Law 2015 and LASPPPA regulations.

### Inferences
- Cost per member is roughly 3% of the assessed value of the plot/unit in Lagos statutory charges on the sub-deed (plus survey and legal fees), on top of the cooperative's own ~3% on the master title — i.e. the chain is taxed twice. Who bears the second layer should be fixed in the subscription agreement; silence usually means members pay later at a higher Blue Book valuation.
- Where the cooperative never perfects the master title, members' sub-deeds cannot be consented or registered; members hold equitable interests only, cannot mortgage to FMBN or banks, and the whole estate is exposed to a single revocation or adverse claim against the society.
- For blocks of flats, absent a strata statute, the workable structure is long subleases of each flat from the cooperative (or a management company it incorporates) with common areas retained — meaning the cooperative/management company remains a permanent title-holder with its own ground rent/LUC liabilities.

### Gaps
- No primary source on whether Lagos has enacted (or drafted) a sectional/strata titles law as at 2026; the absence in search results is not proof of absence.
- No published FCT fee schedule for converting a mass-housing subscriber's sub-lease into an individual statutory R of O/C of O (the R of O bill contents were reformed in April 2025 but amounts were not in sources fetched).
- No source quantifying the typical time from member sub-deed execution to registration in Lagos or FCT.

## KQ5. Allocation and member agreements: allocation letters, subscription agreements, bye-laws, default/forfeiture, refunds, resale restrictions, inheritance

### Takeaway
Nigerian cooperative bye-laws (as exemplified by the NFIU staff cooperative's 2020 bye-law) deal with withdrawal, refund charges, nominees and arbitration before the Director of Cooperatives, but the fetched examples do not contain estate-specific allocation, forfeiture or resale clauses — those typically sit in separate subscription/allocation documents, and when they are weak, courts fall back on general contract law (e.g., failure to pay the full price can nullify the sale).

### Cited Findings
- A registered society "may make bye-laws for things necessary or desirable for the purpose for which the society is established"; the Act regulates admission, withdrawal/expulsion and "payments to be made to members who withdraw or are expelled"; on liquidation surplus is distributed per the bye-laws — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act (Jet Lawyer summary)](https://djetlawyer.com/nigerian-co-operative-societies-act/); [FAO text](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- NFIU Staff Multipurpose Cooperative Society Bye-Law (2020) — a real Nigerian staff cooperative bye-law — provides: non-refundable application fee ₦2,000; withdrawal of membership on one month's written notice provided the member is not indebted, with a charge "on all refund to the withdrawing member"; a deceased member's indebtedness is recoverable from his/her estate; re-admission fees for members who withdrew; cl.13.1 shares withdrawable but not more than 5% of withdrawal applications in a year (per society's position); cl.13.2 restriction on transfer of shares/interest; cl.14.3 members may withdraw up to 60% of ordinary savings only after all loans are repaid and not while standing surety; the register records each member's Nominee; the Management Committee's functions include "acquisition, lease, transfer and disposal of properties"; disputes are referred to the "Registrar of Co-operatives for settlement by arbitration as provided for in the Act" and the Chief Registrar/Director of Co-operative Societies, Abuja — [NFIU Cooperative Bye-Law PDF](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf) (text extracted locally; the document contains no estate-specific allocation/forfeiture clauses)
- State Co-operative Societies Law: s.42 settlement of disputes, mediation and enforcement; ss.47–48 Director's inspection and inquiry; s.53–54 dissolution and caretaker committee; ss.57–62 liquidation — [Law Nigeria](https://lawnigeria.com/2020/03/27/co-operatives-societies-law/)
- A Nigerian academic commentary argues that land-ownership disputes arising from a cooperative's business (purchase of land for members) fall within the cooperative tribunal/arbitration jurisdiction and that "it is unsustainable for tribunals to renege jurisdiction" where members' investments through the society are contested — [LASJURE article (abstract via search; full text 404)](https://www.nigerianjournalsonline.com/index.php/LASJURE/article/download/2240/2184)
- Instalment default: a Nigerian appellate decision reported by Lexology holds that failure to pay the full purchase price nullifies a land sale contract — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=98f22f99-c281-4e61-8bbe-579a0c63512f) (full text behind 403; case name not captured)
- Apo-Tafyi scheme terms: workers paid 10%–100% upfront in 2013/14 expecting FMBN mortgages for the balance; 160 identified subscribers paid ₦406,034,000; no land, house or refund by 2023 — [Daily Trust](https://dailytrust.com/housing-subscribers-call-for-help-over-stalemate-with-brains-hammers-others/); [LinkedIn/press repost](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/subscribers-vow-sustain-agitations-over-failed-)
- LASRERA: Lagos Real Estate Regulatory Law 2021; practising real estate (agent, realtor, developer, "any individual or organisation involved in real estate transactions") without LASRERA registration declared a criminal offence on 28 June 2025; agency fees capped at 0–10%; unlawful to collect payment without delivering the agreed service — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/06/28/lagos-govt-declares-real-estate-practice-without-lasrera-registration-illegal/); [HousingTV Africa](https://www.housingtvafrica.com/lagos-real-estate-regulation/)

### Inferences
- Because the model bye-law is a thrift-and-credit document, estate projects run by such societies often rest on an ad hoc "allocation letter" with no written default, forfeiture, refund-timing, resale or inheritance terms; that gap is what turns a stalled scheme into a decade of protest rather than a contractual claim.
- A cooperative selling plots to the public (non-members) in Lagos is likely within LASRERA's "any organisation involved in real estate transactions" and should register; it also risks losing cooperative tax status (KQ6) and breaching s.17 non-member restrictions.
- Disputes between a member and the society are first routed to the Director of Cooperatives' arbitration under the Act/state law; members cannot assume direct High Court access on allocation disputes.

### Gaps
- No published Nigerian model "estate allocation/subscription agreement" or any court decision construing a cooperative's forfeiture or right-of-first-refusal clause was found.
- No source on whether LASRERA has in practice required registration of cooperative societies selling to members only.

## KQ6. Taxes and fees on the chain; cooperative tax exemption limits

### Takeaway
Statutory charges on each Lagos transfer are ad valorem (consent 1.5%, stamp duty 0.5%, registration 0.5%, CGT 0.5% as assessed; CGT on sale gains otherwise 10%), annual Land Use Charge applies to the estate and to each member's plot (0.0394% owner-occupied; vacant land now carries a 100% surcharge from 1 Jan 2026), FCT ground rent is due in advance each 1 January and is revocation-enforced; the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 preserves the cooperative income/gains exemption only for gains not derived from assets acquired for a trade or business and applied solely to the society's purposes — trading plots to non-members falls outside it.

### Cited Findings
- Lagos perfection charges: charting & endorsement ₦10,500; consent 1.5%; stamp duty 0.5%; registration 0.5% of assessed value — [Firmus Nigeria](https://firmusnigeria.com/register-title-to-land-lagos-state/); plus CGT 0.5% in the consent bundle (aggregate ≈3%) — [The African Vestor](https://theafricanvestor.com/blogs/news/lagos-get-governor-consent-property) via search summary; 2026 Blue Book raised assessed values and hence all ad valorem charges from 1 May 2026 — [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/industry/1709992-lagos-increases-governors-consent-stamp-duties-land-transaction-fees/)
- Capital gains tax on sale: 10% of chargeable gain (pre-NTA regime as described); rental income taxed progressively up to 24% — [The African Vestor](https://theafricanvestor.com/blogs/news/lagos-nigeria-property-taxes-fees). Under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, companies' chargeable gains are taxed at the corporate rate (30%) — [PwC/EY summaries via search](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/nigeria-tax-act-2025-has-been-signed-highlights)
- Lagos Land Use Charge consolidates ground rent, tenement rate and neighbourhood improvement charge; rates: owner-occupied residential 0.0394%; vacant/undeveloped land 0.0394%; agricultural 0.01%; industrial 0.132%; let residential 0.394% of assessed value; Land Use Charge (Amendment) Law 2025 effective 1 Jan 2026 raised the undeveloped-land penalty surcharge from 50% to 100%; 2026 bills carry a 15% early-payment discount — [Nigeria Real Estate Blog: LUC 2026](https://nigeriarealestateblog.com/lagos-land-use-charge-luc-amendments-2026/); [Arbiterz](https://arbiterz.com/lagos-state-government-releases-2026-land-use-charge-bills-15-early-payment-discount-available); indicative LUC 0.05–0.5% of value (₦50,000–₦500,000 on a ₦100m property) — [The African Vestor](https://theafricanvestor.com/blogs/news/lagos-nigeria-property-taxes-fees)
- FCT ground rent: payable in advance from 1 January each year under R of O/C of O terms; rates vary by district and plot size and FCTA does not publish a flat percentage; non-payment is enforced by revocation under s.28(5) LUA (see KQ1) — [Premium Times](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/top-news/798538-ground-rent-fcta-threatens-to-revoke-properties-belonging-to-ex-fct-minister-navy-foreign-embassies-others-issues-14-days-ultimatum-full-list-of-3383-allottees.html); other FCT statutory bills: C of O bills, penalty/violation fees, land-use conversion fees (₦5m fine for illegal conversion, Sept 2025) — [SaharaReporters](https://saharareporters.com/2025/11/28/fct-minister-wike-revokes-1095-property-titles-abuja-over-unpaid-ground-rent-others); [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202509030266.html)
- Cooperative exemption: registered cooperative societies are among eight organisations exempt from Companies Income Tax — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/business-economy/article/eight-organisations-exempt-from-companies-income-tax-in-nigeria/); under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (s.163 exempt income), gains from disposal of chargeable assets by a registered cooperative are exempt "provided that such gains do not arise from the disposal of assets acquired in connection with any trade or business ... and are applied purely for the purposes of the cooperative society" — [Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (text)](https://irs.gm.gov.ng/docs/national/NIGERIA_TAX_ACT_2025.pdf); [PwC Tax Summaries](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/income-determination); practitioners warn the exemption is "not a blanket waiver" and commercial income may be taxable — [AO2 Law](https://ao2law.com/taxation-of-small-companies-under-the-new-tax-regime-as-2026-approaches-overview-of-key-areas-of-interest/)
- Federal Act: a society's transactions with non-members are "subject to such prohibitions and restrictions as may be prescribed" — [FAO text](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)

### Inferences
- A cooperative that buys a large parcel, subdivides and sells surplus plots to the public is at risk of being characterised as carrying on a trade; the gain on those plots would fall outside the s.163 exemption and the society would also be in LASRERA/s.17 territory. Ring-fencing non-member sales in a separate company (taxable) is the clean structure.
- Undeveloped member plots in Lagos now attract LUC at double surcharge; the society should decide who pays LUC/ground rent during the pre-development period and bill it, because unpaid state charges on a master title are exactly how FCT revocations arise.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm VAT treatment of construction services to a cooperative or withholding-tax rates on estate contractors under the 2025 Acts from sources fetched (general NTA 2025 texts found, but no cooperative-estate-specific guidance).
- No official FCT ground-rent tariff per district was found.

## KQ7. Dispute and fraud patterns with cases, and the controls that worked

### Takeaway
The recurring failure modes are (i) paying a vendor with no authority to sell (NLNG: ₦700m lost, 10-year trial, ₦580m restitution ordered but recovery unknown), (ii) upfront collections against promised but unallocated government land (Apo-Tafyi: ₦406m from 160 workers, nothing delivered after 10+ years), (iii) master-title revocation for unpaid statutory charges (FCT 2023–2025 waves), (iv) double allocation (Olabanji) and forged Area Council documents (485 nullified Feb 2026), and (v) building without layout/planning approval (Lagos 176 estates; FCT green-area demolitions). The controls evidenced in the sources are title-and-authority verification before payment, government-allocated or gazetted land only, approvals before allocation, and a funded statutory-charges calendar.

### Cited Findings
- NLNG case: EFCC v Jonathan Wallang (FCT High Court Maitama, Hamza Muazu J, judgment 31 July 2025). Wallang, MD of Swift and Hay Ltd, received ₦520m (20 Oct 2011) and ₦130m (3 Nov 2011) through Dinijocs Nigeria Ltd from NLNG Staff Bonny Cooperative Investment and Credit Society Ltd purportedly for Plot No.1 Idu-Sabo District, Cadastral Zone D04 (643+ ha) which he had no authority to sell; convicted after a 10-year trial on conspiracy/forgery (2 years or ₦200,000 fine on counts 1–3) and obtaining by false pretence (7 years, no fine option, counts 6–7); ordered to restitute ₦580m; two co-defendants died during trial, one acquitted — [TheNigeriaLawyer](https://thenigerialawyer.com/%E2%82%A6580m-restitution-ordered-after-10-year-trial-court-convicts-and-sentences-wallang-for-%E2%82%A6700m-land-scam-targeting-nlng-staff-cooperative/); [EFCC via Daily Nigerian](https://dailynigerian.com/abuja-court-jails-fraudster-for-defrauding-nlng-cooperative-society-n700m/). RESOLVED (conviction); recovery of restitution UNKNOWN. Separately, EFCC in 2015 arraigned the former president of the NLNG cooperative and four others for a ₦207m fraud — [EFCC](https://www.efcc.gov.ng/efcc/news-and-information/news-release/1424-n207bn-scam-efcc-to-re-arraign-former-president-of-nlng-cooperatives-others-sept-15) (outcome not found; UNRESOLVED in sources)

- Forged documents: 485 FCT Area Council land documents nullified 23 Feb 2026 after AGIS/Land Administration vetting found forged signatures, double allocations and fraudulent survey plans — [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202602240664.html)
- Revocation waves and the NFIU cooperative exposure — see KQ1; the Free Trade Zone investors' suit against revocation (FHC/ABJ/CS/1539/2025) is pending — [Vanguard](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2025/08/ground-rent-investors-sue-wike-over-revocation-of-plots-at-abuja-free-trade-zone/). UNRESOLVED.

### Inferences
- In both NLNG and Apo-Tafyi the fatal step was releasing bulk funds before any government title instrument (allocation letter/R of O in the cooperative's name) existed; an escrow conditioned on a registered title or AGIS-verified allocation would have blocked both losses. Note: the sources describe the failures, not a documented Nigerian cooperative that used escrow successfully — the escrow control is inferred, not evidenced.
- Criminal conviction is a slow and partial remedy: NLNG waited 14 years from payment (2011) to judgment (2025) and restitution was ₦580m against ₦700m, with recovery uncertain.
- The FCT's 2025–2026 cleanup (revocation lists, 485 forged documents, mass-housing vetting) is simultaneously the biggest risk and the best control for cooperatives: those with clean, paid-up titles can convert to statutory titles for members; those relying on Area Council papers or in arrears face loss.

### Gaps
- No Court of Appeal or Supreme Court judgment specifically on a cooperative society's estate allocation to members was located (the LASJURE article on a Supreme Court decision about a registered cooperative's land tribunal jurisdiction returned 404).
- No documented Nigerian example was found of a cooperative estate that used escrow/phased collection and completed titling — the "controls that worked" are inferred from failure analysis and from government-allocated schemes.
- Outcome of the EFCC ₦207m case against the former NLNG cooperative president and whether NLNG recovered the ₦580m are unknown.

## KQ8. Regulatory bodies: Lands Bureaus, Surveyor-General, planning authorities, Director of Cooperatives, REDAN, LASRERA, FCCPC

### Takeaway
A cooperative estate sits under at least five regulators: the state Lands Bureau/FCT Department of Land Administration-AGIS (title), the Surveyor-General (charting/survey), the physical planning authority (LASPPPA/LASBCA; FCT Development Control/AMMC), the Director/Registrar of Cooperatives (registration, inquiry, arbitration, dissolution), and in Lagos LASRERA (registration of anyone transacting in real estate); FCCPC and REDAN appeared in no cooperative-estate source found.

### Cited Findings
- Lagos Lands Registry/Director of Lands Services processes consent and registration; Surveyor-General charts survey plans — [Firmus Nigeria](https://firmusnigeria.com/register-title-to-land-lagos-state/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/landlord-tenant-leases/1793552/steps-for-real-estate-due-diligence-in-nigeria-special-focus-on-lagos)

- Lagos planning: Ministry of Physical Planning and Urban Development; LASPPPA (permits); LASBCA (building control/demolition) — [Premium Times](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/regional/ssouth-west/811991-lagos-govt-lists-176-illegal-estates-issues-developers-ultimatum-to-obtain-approvals-full-list.html); [PropertyAccess](https://propertyaccess.ng/lagos-govt-begins-demolition-of-illegal-structures-in-ikate-elegushi/)

### Inferences
- The Director of Cooperatives is the only regulator with a mandate over the society itself (inquiry, caretaker committee, dissolution) — committee members of a failing estate scheme face that route before any court.

### Gaps
- No source found on FCCPC action involving cooperative housing schemes, or on REDAN's role in cooperative estates.
- No source confirming whether LASRERA has registered or prosecuted any cooperative society.
