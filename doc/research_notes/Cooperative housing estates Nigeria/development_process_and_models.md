## Key Question 1: Delivery models, when each is used, ownership split and risk allocation

### Takeaway
Nigerian cooperatives overwhelmingly default to the land-only / site-and-services model (buy land in the cooperative's name, lay out plots, allocate to members who self-build); turnkey and developer-financed models are concentrated among (a) oil-company and federal-agency staff cooperatives with large contribution bases and (b) FMBN Cooperative Housing Development Loan (CHDL) projects, where a developer builds on cooperative-titled land and FMBN converts member balances to NHF mortgages. Joint ventures with developers follow the generic Nigerian landowner–developer JV pattern (landowner keeps title, developer funds, units split 30:70 to 50:50).

### Cited Findings

**Prevalence of the land-only / site-and-services model**
- Cooperative association estate schemes in Nigeria "are usually in the form of site and service estate scheme where large expanse of land is acquired in the name of the business organization's workers association and thereafter divided into different units/plots" and allocated to staff — [Real Estate Surveyor blog, 6 types of residential estate housing schemes in Nigeria (2014)](http://realestatesurveyor.blogspot.com/2014/02/6-types-of-residential-estate-housing.html)
- A Lagos study (IOP Conf. Series 588, 2020) found "cooperative societies are most active in land acquisition activities but their capacity to convert these lands to housing units is limited" — [IOPscience: Cooperative societies and the urban housing supply, a study of Lagos](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1755-1315/588/5/052064)
- In Osogbo (survey Aug 2019–Jan 2020, 110 valid cooperative societies out of 437 identified), cooperatives delivered housing through direct land acquisition and title processing, construction loans to members, collaboration with built-environment professionals, home-ownership and rental-loan schemes, and collective land purchases; 85% of societies grew their capital from members' contributions and loan interest — [SciELO SA: Factors militating against cooperative societies' contributions to housing development in Osogbo (2022)](https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S2415-04872022000200003)
- For cooperatives in Lagos tertiary institutions, "cost of materials of construction and unnecessary bureaucracy were identified as the most significant constraints" to housing provision — [Academic Journals, J. Geography & Regional Planning: Constraints of affordable housing through cooperative societies in tertiary institutions in Lagos State](https://academicjournals.org/journal/JGRP/article-abstract/BB43BB662960)

**Model (a) in practice: CEMCS Nicon Town (Lagos) — cooperative buys a laid-out estate and allocates plots for member self-build under a design code**
- Chevron Employees Multipurpose Cooperative Society (CEMCS) assumed ownership of Nicon Town Estate, Ikate, Lekki, after Nicon Lekki Limited sold the majority of its plots to CEMCS in 2001; the 57-hectare estate along the Lekki-Epe Expressway was completed in 2007. CEMCS then developed Melrose Park Estate, 14 ha on the Lekki corridor — [Estate Intel: Real estate developments owned by cooperative societies in Nigeria (search snippet; page returned 403)](https://estateintel.com/insights/real-estate-developments-owned-by-cooperative-societies-in-nigeria)
- Nicon Town's 57 ha parcel was designed for over 400 plots: 46 plots of 1,200 m², about 200 plots of 1,000 m², and the remainder 800 m² each; infrastructure includes underground electricity cabling and a central water treatment plant — [OwnAHome: What is so special about Nicon Town Estate Lekki](https://ownahome.ng/blog/what-is-so-special-about-nicon-town-estate-lekki)
- Nicon Town is "a low-density community with wide, paved roads and a strict architectural code that prevents the over-development common in other parts of Lekki," with strict residential zoning (no shops, schools or event centres inside residential clusters) — [ExpertListing: Best estates in Nicon Town (2026)](https://www.expertlisting.ng/blog/estate-guide/lagos/nicon-town)

**Model (b)/(d): cooperative as developer-owner with a contractor/developer building turnkey units — COOPLAG Gardens (Lagos)**
- Shell Lagos Staff Cooperative (COOPLAG) began construction of its first major estate, COOPLAG Gardens, in 2015; it is a serviced, gated estate at the tail end of Orchid Road, Lekki (by the Chevron toll gate) of detached, semi-detached and terrace duplexes (5-bedroom detached/semi-detached per Estate Intel), with 24-hour manned security. COOPLAG has since completed The Autograph (Banana Island Road, Ikoyi) and in March 2026 unveiled Oyster Towers in Eko Atlantic with Veritasi Homes — [CoopLag home page](https://cooplag.coop.ng/); [Estate Intel directory: the Shell Staff Cooperative (snippet)](https://estateintel.com/app/directory/the-shell-staff-cooperative)
- Shell, Mobil and Total staff cooperatives were at one point jointly developing 200 housing units (Estate Intel, snippet only) — [Estate Intel](https://estateintel.com/insights/co-operative-societies-of-shell-mobil-and-total-are-currently-developing-200-housing-units)

**Model (c): FMBN Cooperative Housing Development Loan — cooperative holds land title, developer builds, FMBN finances construction then converts to member mortgages (Akacare Woodhill, Kuje, Abuja)**
- CHDL terms: cooperative society must hold an unencumbered land title; members must be NHF contributors; loan up to N500 million per cooperative; 10% p.a.; tenor up to 24 months with a 12-month moratorium. After completion FMBN packages NHF mortgages of up to N15 million at 9% p.a. for up to 30 years (age-dependent) through primary mortgage banks — [ThisDay: How FMBN is leveraging co-operatives to make homes possible for Nigerians (Aug 2021)](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/)
- Woodhill Estate, Kuje, Abuja: N997 million CHDL to Akacare Multipurpose Cooperative Society; developer Perfect Estate Developers Ltd; 230 units — 2-bedroom semi-detached bungalows at N7.5 million and 3-bedroom detached bungalows at N9.5 million (2020 prices); fully subscribed, commissioned 2020; beneficiaries paid N3.5 million equity with FMBN financing the balance via salary deduction to retirement. FMBN separately financed a 2 km access road. About 40 CHDL projects were ongoing nationally (2021) with 2,570 units projected — [ThisDay (Aug 2021)](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/); [FMBN funded projects, FCT](https://www.fmbn.gov.ng/funded_projects/fct.html)
- CHDL documents: cooperative registration certificate, bye-laws, detailed profile of the society, and a Board of Trustees resolution signed by chairman and secretary authorising borrowing; land title must be in the society's name, the society acting as facilitator for members — [ThisDay: Understanding FMBN's mortgage loan products (2020)](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2020/05/27/understanding-fmbns-mortgage-loan-products/); [BusinessDay: Understanding how FMBN's loan products enable access to affordable housing](https://businessday.ng/features/article/understanding-how-fmbns-loan-products-enable-access-to-affordable-housing/)
- Scale in 2025: FMBN project-loan disbursements rose from N31.5 billion (2024) to N79 billion (2025); 6,911 units funded in 2025; over 500 cooperative societies onboarded into the NHF scheme — [ThisDay: FMBN 6,911 housing units funded in 2025 (Jul 2026)](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/07/16/fmbn-6911-housing-units-funded-in-2025-300-increase-recorded/)
- A 430-home scheme for NMDPRA staff (Abuja, Kano, Lagos) was delivered through a partnership of the Federal Ministry of Housing, FMBN, Family Homes Funds, the NMDPRA Staff Cooperative Society, CityCode Mortgage Bank and private developers (Sept 2026) — [allAfrica: FMBN allocates 430 houses to NMDPRA staff](https://allafrica.com/stories/202609290400.html); [Leadership: FMBN backs 430-home scheme for NMDPRA workers](https://leadership.ng/fmbn-backs-430-home-scheme-for-nmdpra-workers/)

**Model (c)/(d) at scale: NIMCOS (Nigeria Immigration Service cooperative), Abuja**
- NIMCOS's estate at Kutunku Extension, Gwagwalada, FCT, comprises 150 two- and three-bedroom bungalows (commissioned July 2026); a 1,000-unit scheme has commenced at Kuchiyako, Kuje, toward a 1,500-unit target; NIMCOS previously facilitated housing for members at Dei-Dei, Abuja, through a partnership with developer Brains and Hammers, and has a phase underway in Minna — [BusinessDay: FG inaugurates 1,500-unit housing estate for immigration personnel](https://businessday.ng/uncategorized/article/fg-inaugurates-1500-unit-housing-estate-for-immigration-personnel-in-abuja/); [Leadership: NIS unveils nationwide housing drive](https://leadership.ng/nis-unveils-nationwide-housing-drive-inaugurates-new-staff-estates/); [Blueprint](https://blueprint.ng/welfare-fg-targets-1-500-houses-for-immigration-staff-through-nimcos-in-gwagbalada/)

**Model (c): generic landowner–developer JV structure (applies when a cooperative contributes land)**
- Most common structure: landowner contributes land; developer provides capital, design, approvals, construction and marketing; landowner retains legal title throughout construction while the developer holds a contractual right to enter, build and market; completed units are split by a pre-agreed ratio such as 40:60 or 30:70 reflecting "market value of the land against the projected cost of construction, professional fees, and financing." Alternatives: profit-sharing JV (share proceeds, not units), equity JV via an SPV under CAMA 2020, and lease-and-develop (25–99-year lease). Transaction documents: MoU/LoI, JV Agreement, narrowly-scoped Power of Attorney, Deed of Sublease/Transfer (needs Governor's Consent and registration), Shareholders' Agreement for SPVs. Landowner protections cited: minimum guaranteed returns and the right to revert undeveloped land if the developer fails to commence/complete on time — [Better Call Keyz: Understanding joint venture development structures in Nigeria's real estate sector](https://bettercallkeyz.substack.com/p/understanding-joint-venture-development)
- In Lagos JV listings, common splits are 60:40, 70:30 or 50:50 between developer and landowner depending on location and development type — [Nigeria Property Centre: Joint venture land listings](https://nigeriapropertycentre.com/joint-venture/land/lagos/showtype)
- Family/communal land JVs must be executed by the family head "together with the principal members" with a family resolution or deed of family arrangement, or they are voidable — [Better Call Keyz](https://bettercallkeyz.substack.com/p/understanding-joint-venture-development)

**Model (e)/(f): cooperative as capital investor / bulk off-taker into a developer's scheme**
- CEMCS partnered with Metrospeed Property Development Ltd to invest in Metro Smart City, off Freedom Way, Lekki Phase 1 (2025) — [BusinessDay: Metrospeed, Chevron Cooperative align to accelerate Metro Smart City](https://businessday.ng/news/article/metrospeed-chevron-cooperative-align-to-accelerate-metro-smart-city-project-in-lagos/)
- In July 2026 COOPLAG signed on to the same Metro Smart City (2,000+ units, smart-home technology, CCECC and Huawei as partners); Metrospeed handles EIA, planning permit and Governor's Consent; COOPLAG contributes investment capital with members getting "flexible acquisition opportunities and potential capital appreciation" (no land or firm off-take disclosed) — [ThisDay: Metrospeed, Shell COOPLAG partner on Lagos smart city project (Jul 2026)](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/07/17/metrospeed-shell-cooplag-partner-on-lagos-smart-city-project/)
- Prime Assets Housing Cooperative Society (initiated by developer Realty Point Ltd) offers members a 10–20-year micro-mortgage scheme — an example of a developer-sponsored cooperative used to sell into its own estates — [Nigeria Web Success: How to fund a house using cooperatives](https://www.nigeriawebsuccess.com/fund-house.html)

**Smaller/professional and teachers' cooperatives**
- Oluyole NUT Cooperative Investment and Credit Society (CICS) commissioned a teachers' housing estate at Omiyale, Aba-Alfa, Olomi, Ibadan (2026), attributed to regular salary payment enabling consistent cooperative saving — [National Insight News: Olatunji commissions Oluyole Teachers' Housing Estate](https://nationalinsightnews.com/olatunji-commissions-oluyole-teachers-housing-estate-credits-makindes-workers-welfare-policies/)
- Caleb University Staff Cooperative Estate exists at Imota, Ikorodu, Lagos (plots listed on property portals) — [Nigeria Property Centre: cooperative estate listings, Lagos](https://nigeriapropertycentre.com/lagos?n=cooperative+estate)

### Inferences
- Risk allocation by model (inferred from the above): in (a) land-only, the cooperative bears title, infrastructure-cost and omo-onile risk; members bear construction cost and completion risk individually, so estates fill slowly and unevenly. In (b)/(d) the cooperative carries developer-default and cost-escalation risk unless shifted by fixed-price contracts and bonds. In the FMBN CHDL variant of (c), FMBN carries construction finance and mortgage risk, the cooperative carries land-title and subscription risk, and the developer carries delivery risk under FMBN supervision. In landowner JVs, the cooperative gives up 50–70% of units in exchange for zero construction cash outlay; the key protections are title retention and reversion clauses. In (e)/(f) the cooperative is a passive investor with market and developer-counterparty risk.
- Oil-company cooperatives (CEMCS, COOPLAG, TEHCOOP) have progressed over 20 years from buying serviced plots (2001) to building turnkey luxury estates (2015+) to investing as capital partners in third-party smart-city schemes (2025–26) — a maturity path driven by balance-sheet size.
- The CHDL route effectively standardises model (c) for salary-earning cooperatives: cooperative buys/holds land, developer builds, FMBN funds, members pay roughly one-third equity (N3.5m of N7.5–9.5m at Woodhill) and mortgage the rest.

### Gaps
- No source gave an explicit "50/20/30" or similar three-way split involving a state housing agency; the only documented splits are two-party landowner–developer ratios (30:70 to 50:50).
- No documented Nigerian example of a cooperative-held rent-to-own or build-to-rent estate was found; the model is referenced generically (rental-loan facilities in Osogbo) but not with a named estate.
- Estate Intel's full case material (TEHCOOP, Melrose Park, 200-unit joint scheme) could not be fetched (403).
- No documented "group discount" percentage for cooperatives buying into existing estates.

## Key Question 2: Step-by-step process from demand survey to handover

### Takeaway
The process is well-documented at the land and title stages (due diligence → purchase → perimeter survey → excision/consent → layout approval) and at the finance stage (FMBN CHDL), but cooperative-specific internal steps (demand survey, allocation method, bye-laws) are documented only in generic terms; the publicly described Lagos excision and consent workflows, LASPPPA permit steps and developer practice fill in the external steps.

### Cited Findings

**Step 0 – Member subscription and savings (pre-land)**
- Cooperative members typically pay a registration fee of N5,000–25,000 and share capital of N10,000–100,000, then make monthly contributions from N2,000–10,000+; 6–12 months of consistent savings is usual before loan eligibility — [KudiCompass: How to join a cooperative society in Nigeria (2026)](https://kudicompass.com/how-to-join-cooperative-society-nigeria/)
- For FMBN routes, members must contribute 2.5% of monthly salary to the NHF — [MyWorkPay: National Housing Fund in Nigeria](https://www.myworkpay.com/blogs/national-housing-fund-in-nigeria)

**Step 1 – Land search and due diligence (Lagos focus)**
- Steps: (1) verify seller identity and authority (individual, company, family); (2) review title documents (C of O, Governor's Consent, Deed of Assignment, Survey Plan); (3) search at the Lands Registry — "one of the most important steps" — to confirm registration and encumbrances; (4) chart the survey plan at the Office of the Surveyor-General to see whether the land falls within government acquisition or committed acquisition; (5) physical inspection including flooding/drainage check; (6) possession investigation; (7) planning-compliance check via Lagos' electronic planning permit system; (8) litigation search; (9) prepare/review sale agreement and deed of assignment before paying; (10) perfect title (Governor's Consent, stamping, registration); (11) engage lawyer, registered surveyor, engineer — [Mondaq: Steps for real estate due diligence in Nigeria, special focus on Lagos](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/landlord-tenant-leases/1793552/steps-for-real-estate-due-diligence-in-nigeria-special-focus-on-lagos)
- Where land is family-owned, "the buyer must identify the proper representatives and confirm that all necessary principal family members or accredited representatives are involved" — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/landlord-tenant-leases/1793552/steps-for-real-estate-due-diligence-in-nigeria-special-focus-on-lagos)
- Common Lagos title documents: C of O, Governor's Consent, Deed of Assignment, Deed of Sublease, Registered Conveyance, Gazette, Excision, Court Judgment, Probate/Letters of Administration, Deed of Gift, Survey Plan — [Omonile Lawyer: Know your land documents](https://omonilelawyer.com/category/know-your-land-documents/)
- A Gazette/Excision shows land has been released from government acquisition to a community or family; a Gazette "is a step in the right direction but still needs further documentation" (i.e., C of O/consent still needed) — [BellaNaija / Dennis Isong: What is excision land (Apr 2026)](https://www.bellanaija.com/2026/04/dennis-isong-what-is-excision-land-and-is-it-safe-to-buy-in-nigeria/)
- Omo-onile (land-owning families/local youths) "often demand informal fees before you begin construction, even if you've legally acquired the land" — [The African Vestor: Buying property in Lagos, risks, scams and pitfalls (2026)](https://theafricanvestor.com/blogs/news/lagos-nigeria-risks-pitfalls)

**Step 2 – Excision (if buying un-excised family land) — Lagos LUAC workflow**
- An excision application goes to the Lagos State Land Use Allocation Committee (LUAC) on the family head/community head/Baale's letterhead with historical background, population, major occupation, perimeter survey, purpose and justification; within 7 days it is forwarded to LUAC's Technical Committee; the perimeter survey goes to the Surveyor-General who charts it against government acquisition; a site inspection follows about 14 days after the Surveyor-General's report; the Technical Committee checks conformity with the area Master Plan — [Otic Surveys: How to obtain land excision in Lagos](https://oticsurveys.com/how-to-obtain-land-excision-lagos/); [Lagos Lands Bureau: Land Use and Allocation Committee](https://landsbureau.lagosstate.gov.ng/2017/05/16/land-use-and-allocation-committee-2/)
- The full excision sequence runs to about 14 steps: application → technical committees → perimeter survey to Surveyor-General → site inspection and master-plan check → report to Governor → survey/layout by state planners → indemnity agreement → publication in state gazette and newspapers → C of O issued within 30 days of publication → filing at Lands Bureau; the process is "very time-consuming and costly" with no official fee published — [Mixta Africa: Excision of land, meaning, cost and how to process it](https://mixtafrica.com/excision-of-land-in-nigeria/)
- Lagos land regularisation (for land inside acquisition) fee structure reported as 25% of Fair Market Value as premium, 1% annual ground rent, 2% development charge, 0.5% stamp duty, 0.5% registration — reported in search results (source schedule year not confirmed; likely pre-2026) — [Lagos Lands Bureau workflows](https://landsbureau.lagosstate.gov.ng/category/workflow/)

**Step 3 – Purchase and title perfection (Deed of Assignment → Governor's Consent)**
- The seller signs a Deed of Assignment at completion; this is the document on which Governor's Consent is processed — [Resolution Law: Procedure for obtaining Governor's Consent in Lagos](https://www.resolutionlawng.com/procedure-for-obtaining-governor-consent-in-lagos-nigeria/)
- Expected duration for Governor's Consent is 30 days "but the reality is that it could extend for as long as 6 months or even 1 year" — [Resolution Law](https://www.resolutionlawng.com/procedure-for-obtaining-governor-consent-in-lagos-nigeria/); another guide: ~3% of property value in fees and 3–6 months — [The African Vestor: How to get Governor's Consent for Lagos property](https://theafricanvestor.com/blogs/news/lagos-get-governor-consent-property)
- Fee components quoted by Lagos consent guides: consent fee 1.5% of assessed value, CGT 0.5%, stamp duty 0.5%, registration 0.5% — [Vala Homes: Governor's Consent, how to process in Lagos](https://valahomes.com/process-governors-consent-on-property-in-lagos-state); requirements list at [Estate Intel: Requirements for processing Governor's Consent](https://estateintel.com/insights/requirements-for-processing-governors-consent)
- Any assignment, mortgage or sublease requires Governor's Consent; the transfer is "inchoate" until consent is obtained — [Fieldco: Title to land in Nigeria](https://www.fieldcolimited.com/2020/05/14/title-to-land-in-nigeria/)

**Step 4 – Perimeter survey, layout design and planning approval**
- Massodih (town-planning consultancy) lists the estate sequence as topographic/boundary survey → certified survey plan → master/layout plan → planning approval → infrastructure → phased plot release, and states planning approval takes 3–24 months depending on state and application completeness — [Massodih Plans: How much does it cost to develop an estate in Nigeria](https://massodihplans.com/how-much-does-it-cost-to-develop-an-estate-in-nigeria-a-town-planners-honest-breakdown/)
- Lagos building permit (LASPPPA): 4–12 weeks from full submission; realistic end-to-end 6–14 weeks (drawings 2–6 weeks; submission to first assessment 2–3 weeks; payment and site inspection 1–2 weeks; LASBCA technical review 2–4 weeks; issuance 1–2 weeks); permits valid 2 years, renewable once — [Build With Ease: Lagos building plan approval in 2026](https://buildwithease.com.ng/2026/04/24/building-plan-approval-in-lagos-2026/)
- Abuja: FCT land is zoned Rs1 (low density), Rm2/Rm3 (medium), Rh (high), C, MX, institutional; "AGIS and FCDA will not approve a development that conflicts with the plot's zoning" — [Cabans: How to buy land in Abuja, FCT land buyer's guide 2026](https://cabans.ng/how-to-buy-land-in-abuja); Abuja building approval steps at [Build With Ease: Abuja building approval process (2026)](https://buildwithease.com.ng/2026/07/10/abuja-building-approval-process-guide/)

**Step 5 – Infrastructure and phasing**
- Developers should phase: build primary access, sell/allocate Phase 1 plots, then fund later phases from receipts — "a financial survival mechanism for larger developments"; 10–15% construction contingency recommended — [Massodih Plans: Estate development cost breakdown](https://massodihplans.com/estate-development-cost-breakdown-what-developers-should-budget-for/)
- A development fee (levy) covers roads, drainage, electricity, water and street lighting and is typically charged per m² — e.g., N3,000/m² = N1.8 million on a 600 m² plot — [Real Estate Best Deal: How real estate companies calculate the development fee in Nigeria](https://realestatebestdeal.com/how-real-estate-companies-calculate-development-fee-nigeria/); [Hush Homes: What does the development fee cover](https://hushhomesng.com/blog/what-development-fee-cover-nigerian-real-estate/)

**Step 6 – Developer/contractor finance and construction (FMBN route)**
- CHDL: 24-month tenor with 12-month moratorium at 10%; FMBN also funds off-site items (e.g., 2 km access road at Woodhill) — [ThisDay 2021](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/)

**Step 7 – Allocation**
- Plot balloting is "a transparent and random process" in which applicants apply with a fee and a lottery draw determines the allotment — [Union Developers: Understanding the balloting process](https://www.uniondevelopers.com/blog/understanding-the-balloting-process-and-why-it-matters/) (generic/South-Asian source; no Nigerian cooperative-specific description found)
- In Lagos government schemes, an ordinary allocation letter does not identify the specific plot; after payments are confirmed a Letter of Confirmation is issued — [Nairaland thread on letters of land allocation](https://www.nairaland.com/2883036/letter-land-allocation-does-anyone)

**Step 8 – Member title documentation**
- A Deed of Sub-Lease is granted by the holder of a C of O who carves out part of the property to a new owner; it is valid for the unexpired term of the head C of O and must be perfected (Governor's Consent + registration) at the state registry — [Baay Realty: Understanding deeds and titles in Nigeria](https://baayrealty.com/deed-and-title-nigeria-property-deeds/); [Fieldco](https://www.fieldcolimited.com/2020/05/14/title-to-land-in-nigeria/)
- Global C of O "covers several parcels of lands owned by different people which was applied for as a joint application," versus an individual C of O for one plot — [CW Real Estate: Types of land titles and documents in Lagos](https://cwlagos.com/blog/types-of-land-titles-documents-in-lagos-the-importance-how-to-obtain-them-and-all-you-need-to-know)
- NHF mortgage conversion at handover: up to N15m, 9%, up to 30 years via PMBs — [ThisDay 2021](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/)

### Inferences
- A business-analyst sequence consistent with the sources: (1) member demand survey + subscription + savings build-up (6–12 months); (2) land search, title/charting/litigation search, physical inspection, family/omo-onile verification; (3) negotiate, pay, execute Deed of Assignment (and excision/regularisation if required); (4) perimeter survey and beaconing; (5) layout/master plan by town planner and layout approval (Lagos: LASPPPA; Abuja: FCDA Development Control/AGIS); (6) Governor's Consent on the cooperative's title (3–12 months in Lagos); (7) infrastructure in phases, funded from a per-plot development levy; (8) for turnkey models: unit design, BoQ, developer procurement, CHDL or member cash-calls, construction 12–24 months; (9) allocation (ballot or payment-order), allocation letters, estate bye-laws; (10) handover, Deed of Sub-lease per member under the cooperative's global C of O, individual perfection; (11) residents' association/facility management.
- Cooperative title should be perfected before members are allocated or before CHDL application (CHDL requires unencumbered title), which puts the 3–12-month consent process on the critical path.

### Gaps
- No Nigerian source describing a cooperative's internal demand-survey methodology or formal allocation criteria (ballot vs seniority vs payment order); this appears in bye-laws that are not public.
- No published template of cooperative estate bye-laws or design codes (Nicon Town's code is described qualitatively only).
- No source quantifying omo-onile "settlement" amounts for cooperative-scale parcels.
- The Guardian article on the FG's "national cooperative housing development scheme" (Family Homes Funds) returned 403; scheme mechanics could not be verified.

## Key Question 3: Cost build-up per plot/unit (land, title, infrastructure, construction, fees, taxes) and member price vs market

### Takeaway
As of 2025–2026, a serviced plot in a cooperative estate is roughly land (N1–8m on the Lagos/Ogun fringe; N3–25m in Lugbe/Kuje Abuja; N20–60m in Ibeju-Lekki) plus N1.5–3m of infrastructure levy, N0.3–1.6m survey, N0.3–1m legal/documentation and 3%+ of value in consent/stamp/registration; a 3-bedroom bungalow build then adds roughly N25–45m at N220–430k/m² (2026), making a finished cooperative unit on the fringe roughly N30–60m — versus FMBN-financed Woodhill units at N7.5–9.5m in 2020, illustrating how fast costs have moved.

### Cited Findings

**Land cost — Lagos (asking prices)**

## Key Question 4: Timelines by stage and sources of delay

### Takeaway
Documented durations: 6–12 months of member saving before loans; Governor's Consent 30 days nominal but 3–12 months in practice (Lagos); layout/estate planning approval 3–24 months; LASPPPA building permit 6–14 weeks; CHDL construction 24 months (12-month moratorium); real estates ran 6 years (Nicon Town 2001 → 2007). Delays cluster around title/consent, approvals, subscriber cash flow and material-price shocks.

### Cited Findings
- Governor's Consent: 30 days expected, 6–12 months reality (Lagos) — [Resolution Law](https://www.resolutionlawng.com/procedure-for-obtaining-governor-consent-in-lagos-nigeria/); 3–6 months — [The African Vestor](https://theafricanvestor.com/blogs/news/lagos-get-governor-consent-property)
- Excision: 7 days to Technical Committee, ~14 days to site inspection after Surveyor-General report; C of O within 30 days of gazette/newspaper publication; overall "very time-consuming" — [Otic Surveys](https://oticsurveys.com/how-to-obtain-land-excision-lagos/); [Mixta Africa](https://mixtafrica.com/excision-of-land-in-nigeria/)
- Estate planning approval: 3–24 months — [Massodih Plans](https://massodihplans.com/how-much-does-it-cost-to-develop-an-estate-in-nigeria-a-town-planners-honest-breakdown/)
- Lagos building permit: 4–12 weeks from full submission; 6–14 weeks end-to-end — [Build With Ease](https://buildwithease.com.ng/2026/04/24/building-plan-approval-in-lagos-2026/)
- CHDL construction window: 24 months tenor, 12-month moratorium — [ThisDay 2021](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/)
- Nicon Town: plots bought 2001, estate completed 2007 (6 years) — [Estate Intel (snippet)](https://estateintel.com/insights/real-estate-developments-owned-by-cooperative-societies-in-nigeria); COOPLAG Gardens construction started 2015 — [CoopLag](https://cooplag.coop.ng/)
- NIMCOS Kutunku (150 units) commissioned July 2026; 1,000-unit Kuje phase "commenced" at the same time — [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/nis-unveils-nationwide-housing-drive-inaugurates-new-staff-estates/)
- Cash-flow delay mechanism: estates that "depend heavily on off-plan subscriber payments to finance infrastructure without maintaining adequate capital reserves" stall when payments slow, which reduces buyer confidence further — [Nigeria Housing Market: Why some Nigerian estates stall and leave buyers with trapped funds](https://www.nigeriahousingmarket.com/news/ghost-estates-leave-nigerian-property-buyers-trapped-cash)

- Off-plan contracts in Abuja typically penalise buyer payment default within days but are "usually silent on penalties" when the developer misses construction milestones or handover — [Tribune: Off-plan property risks in Abuja, contract loopholes 2026](https://tribuneonlineng.com/buying-off-plan-properties-abuja-contract-loopholes-2026/)

### Inferences
- Land-only estate critical path (inferred): 6–12 months savings → 2–4 months due diligence and purchase → 3–12 months consent (parallel with 3–6 months survey/layout and 3–24 months layout approval) → 6–18 months phase-1 infrastructure → allocation; i.e., ~18–36 months to allocate serviced plots, after which member self-build spreads over many years (Nicon Town took 6 years to "complete").

### Gaps

## Key Question 6: Procurement, developer selection and payment protection

### Takeaway
Nigerian sources describe the protective instruments (performance bonds from banks/insurers, escrow tied to measurable milestones, title retention and reversion rights in JVs) but there is no published cooperative-specific procurement protocol; REDAN membership is a common screening marker and cooperatives themselves join REDAN.

### Cited Findings
- A performance bond is "a financial guarantee provided by a surety (usually a bank or an insurance company) on behalf of the contractor to the project developer" guaranteeing contract performance — [BusinessDay: The impact of performance bonds in project finance contract disputes in Nigeria](https://businessday.ng/news/legal-business/article/the-impact-of-performance-bonds-in-project-finance-contract-disputes-in-nigeria/)
- Nigerian real estate financing commonly uses "phased payment structures tied to project milestones," but contracts tend to protect developers, not buyers, when milestones slip — [Tribune 2026](https://tribuneonlineng.com/buying-off-plan-properties-abuja-contract-loopholes-2026/)
- JV protections: narrowly-scoped Power of Attorney, landowner title retention, minimum guaranteed returns, reversion of undeveloped land on developer failure — [Better Call Keyz](https://bettercallkeyz.substack.com/p/understanding-joint-venture-development)
- REDAN is "the principal agency and umbrella body of the organized private sector… responsible for housing development in Nigeria"; its financial-member list includes cooperative societies (e.g., Abegyi Housing Cooperative Society; Nigerian Defence Academy Civilian Staff Multipurpose Cooperative Society) — [REDAN: About us](https://web.redanonline.org/about-us/); [REDAN financial members (PDF)](https://web.redanonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/REAL-ESTATE-DEVELOPERS-ASSOCIATION-OF-NIGERIA-FINNACIAL-MEMBER-2.pdf)
- FMBN, REDAN and Shelter Afrique signed an MoU for a $2bn affordable-housing fund targeting 10,000 homes/year — [Oxford Business Group: Moves to tackle affordable housing shortage in Nigeria](https://oxfordbusinessgroup.com/articles-interviews/moves-to-tackle-affordable-housing-shortage-in-nigeria)
- FMBN's CHDL effectively acts as the cooperative's procurement/payment-protection mechanism: FMBN lends against the cooperative's unencumbered title, requires registration certificate, bye-laws, profile and BoT borrowing resolution, and disburses to the developer over a 24-month tenor — [ThisDay 2020](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2020/05/27/understanding-fmbns-mortgage-loan-products/); [ThisDay 2021](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians/)
