# Cooperative housing estates in Nigeria — documented case studies (completed, ongoing, stalled, failed) and lessons

Research date: 8 October 2026. Nigeria-first; comparators brief. Every case carries at least one date, a size/unit figure, a money figure and a source. Items marked **[snippet]** rest on a search-result snippet rather than a fetched article; **[single source]** means only one outlet was found; **[unresolved]** means the case is still open or the outcome is unknown. Several key pages (Estate Intel, Guardian Property, Proshare, COOPLAG blog, Premium Times RCCG investigation) returned HTTP 403/404 to the fetch tool, so those facts rest on search snippets and are flagged.

## Key question 1 — Successes to document in depth (timeline, land, size, units, price, financing, developer, title, current value)

### Takeaway
Nigeria's unambiguous cooperative-estate successes are almost all oil-and-gas or federal-agency staff cooperatives (Chevron CEMCS, Shell COOPLAG/CoopEast, ExxonMobil EMCOOP, TotalEnergies TEHC, Immigration NIMCOS, NMDPRA, Police, Customs) that combined (a) stable payroll-deducted member savings, (b) a professional developer or partner bank under contract, and (c) land with a secured, government-approved title — and that delivered in 1–6 years. Member unit prices are rarely published; current open-market values show very large capital gains (Nicon Town land now ~₦1.3–1.5m/sqm).

### Inferences
- Every completed case above had a *single accountable delivery partner* (CMB/Coop Property, Brains & Hammers, Abiskoly, Aircom/Union Homes, Imani & Sons, Renewed Hope SPV) rather than a members' committee acting as its own developer.
- The oil-company cooperatives moved up the value chain: land banking (Nicon Town 2001) → plot estates (Chaplin Court, Northern Foreshore) → built luxury product (Imani 2016, CoopEast 2022, Autograph 2024) → bank-financed high-rise (Louisville 2025). Capital gains on early land (Nicon Town ~₦1.4m/sqm today) likely funded later equity.
- Public-service cooperatives (Police, Customs, NIS, NMDPRA, Akacare) depend on FMBN/NHF or CMB wholesale finance; delivery is slower and publicly reported as approvals rather than completions — these should be treated as "ongoing" unless a commissioning report exists (NIMCOS Gwagwalada and Akacare Woodhill do; Police/Customs do not).
- Employer equity support (NMDPRA's 30% down payment) and regular salary (Oluyole teachers) are decisive affordability enablers.

### Gaps
- Member purchase prices for Nicon Town, Melrose, Autograph, Imani, CoopEast, Northern Foreshore, Louisville and NIMCOS are not published; only open-market asking prices exist.
- Title status (C of O vs Governor's Consent vs excision) is documented only for Chaplin Court (Governor's Consent in listings) and Northern Foreshore (Lagos State approval; family land).
- The "236 units / ₦3bn" Police figure, Shell East (Port Harcourt) estate figures beyond a CMB mention, NLNG-built cooperative estates, OAU/UI/UNILAG staff cooperative estates and ASUU/NASU cooperative estates were not found in any searchable source.

## Key question 2 — Failures, stalls, frauds and disputes

### Takeaway
Nigerian cooperative housing failures cluster around one mechanism: money is collected from members *before* unencumbered land title is secured, and is then paid to a vendor/developer without escrow or independent verification. Outcomes range from decade-long limbo (Apo-Tafyi, RCCG Abuja scheme) to criminal fraud with partial restitution after 10-year trials (NLNG ₦700m) to administrative revocation for unpaid title fees (NFIU ₦298m).

### Inferences
- The three fraud cases (NLNG, NNPC, RCCG) share a pattern: a small executive/committee with signatory power transacted land purchases without independent title verification, escrow or a bonded developer; discovery came years later from members or EFCC, not from internal audit.
- Government-partnered "workers' housing" (Apo-Tafyi) failed where the land allocation itself was never perfected before collection — the same root cause as the NLNG and RCCG cases, but with a political SPV rather than a fraudster.
- Administrative revocation (NFIU) shows a quieter failure mode: a cooperative that wins an allocation but cannot fund C-of-O fees (₦298m) loses the land entirely — carrying costs of title must be budgeted before members are mobilised.
- Litigation is slow (10 years NLNG) and recovery partial (₦580m of ₦700m, payment uncertain); prevention beats remedy.

### Gaps
- No documented case was found of a Nigerian *cooperative-built* estate being demolished; the demolished estates found are community or church developments.
- No published FMBN CHDL default list by cooperative; FMBN reports aggregate arrears only.
- No university or ASUU/NASU cooperative estate stall case with figures was found; the pmworldlibrary paper on SE Nigeria federal-university staff housing financing (2024) exists but was not fetched — [PM World Journal](https://pmworldlibrary.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/pmwj144-Aug2024-Nnametu-et-al-financing-for-staff-housing-in-fedral-universities-in-SE-Nigeria.pdf)
- Ballot/allocation disputes and mid-project price escalation within named cooperatives were not found in press; the only allocation-by-ballot report is a state scheme (Lagos, 698 civil servants).
- Court judgments other than the Wallang conviction and the Kaba forfeiture were not located.

## Key question 3 — Governance in each case (leadership, escrow, phasing, developer contracts, title timing, communication, audit, dispute resolution)

### Takeaway
Published sources rarely describe internal governance explicitly, but the successes consistently show (i) a contracted professional developer or bank-structured deal, (ii) land already approved/titled before build, and (iii) phased, milestone-based disbursement by a lender; failures show executive-controlled funds, no escrow, land paid for before title verification, and disputes resolved (if at all) through EFCC/courts over 7–10 years.

### Cited Findings
- **CoopEast**: cooperatives took equity stakes (50/20/30) in a developer-led scheme and credited "a partnership built on trust"; delivered ahead of a 24-month schedule — [Vanguard 2022](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2022/06/developer-delivers-n5-6bn-residential-estate-for-exxonmobil-shell-cooperatives/)
- **COOPLAG/CMB**: bank-structured ₦8bn project with CMB's in-house developer Coop Property Development Co., 2-year build; repeat partnership after a Port Harcourt estate — [CMB](https://cmbankng.com/cmb-shell-in-n8bn-housing-deal/)
- **TEHC/FCMB**: single-lender $40m facility, 36-month phased delivery — [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/fcmb-backs-totalenergies-staff-housing-with-40m-loan-facility-in-eko-atlantic/)
- **FMBN CHDL**: lender requires *unencumbered title before loan*, NHF membership of beneficiaries, 24-month tenor with moratorium; FMBN cites "quality assurance improvements, phased disbursements, and professional oversight" as departures from past practice — [ThisDay 2021](https://thisdaylive.com/index.php/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians)
- **Kaba/Good Earth**: full disbursement without milestone control → zero output; remedy now a joint ICPC–FMBN monitoring committee — [TheWill 2026](https://thewillnews.com/icpc-reclaims-abandoned-65m-abuja-housing-estate-returns-forfeited-land-to-fmbn-for-completion-of-962-homes/)
- **NMDPRA**: employer funded 30% equity; allocation via staff cooperative under a government SPV — [Nigeria Housing Market](https://www.nigeriahousingmarket.com/news/430-nmdpra-workers-housing-allocations-fmbn-n8-3bn-mortgages) **[snippet]**
- **NIMCOS**: delivery in <1 year with a single developer; management imposes maintenance obligations on allottees — [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/nis-unveils-nationwide-housing-drive-inaugurates-new-staff-estates/)
- **Oluyole NUT**: 28 years of AGMs; savings-funded; leadership succession at AGM — [Tribune](https://tribuneonlineng.com/oluyole-lg-boss-commissions-teachers-housing-estate/)
- **NLNG**: president and VP as signatories allegedly drew ₦1.2bn for ₦830m land; vendor lacked title; resolution via EFCC prosecution over ~10 years — [EFCC](https://www.efcc.gov.ng/efcc/news-and-information/news-release/1424-n207bn-scam-efcc-to-re-arraign-former-president-of-nlng-cooperatives-others-sept-15); [TheNigeriaLawyer](https://thenigerialawyer.com/%E2%82%A6580m-restitution-ordered-after-10-year-trial-court-convicts-and-sentences-wallang-for-%E2%82%A6700m-land-scam-targeting-nlng-staff-cooperative/)
- **NNPC coop**: members alleged "questionable withdrawals by executive members and signatories" and sought EFCC findings before elections — [Vanguard 2017](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2017/08/alleged-400m-fraud-nnpc-cooperative-society-members-demand-outcome-efccs-investigation/)
- **RCCG scheme**: committee-run, no land secured, church withdrew; subscribers resorted to a lawyer's petition — [Sahara Reporters 2017](https://saharareporters.com/2017/07/14/how-redeemed-church-officials-allegedly-duped-members-millions-housing-scheme)
- **Apo-Tafyi**: political SPV + union + FCTA MoU; collection via three banks before land perfected; dispute "resolution" = protests, no refund after 9 years — [Daily Trust 2023](https://dailytrust.com/housing-subscribers-call-for-help-over-stalemate-with-brains-hammers-others/); [Vanguard 2015](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2015/05/revisiting-the-nlc-good-homes-partnership-in-housing-delivery/)
- **NFIU coop**: publishes bye-law and audited financial statements online (governance transparency) yet lost a plot for ₦298m unpaid fees — [NFIU Coop audited FS](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/FS.pdf); [Guardian revocation list](https://guardian.ng/news/full-list-wike-revokes-568-land-allocations-over-unpaid-c-of-o-fees/)

### Inferences
- No Nigerian source explicitly documents use of a formal escrow account or performance bond in a cooperative estate; the functional equivalent in successful cases is lender-controlled, milestone-based disbursement (FMBN CHDL, FCMB, CMB) — i.e. the bank acts as escrow agent.
- "Title first" is the clearest dividing line: Nicon Town (approved layout bought), Northern Foreshore (state approval), Akacare (unencumbered title as loan condition) succeeded; NLNG, RCCG, Apo-Tafyi paid before title.
- Internal audit and AGM accountability appear to prevent slow failure (Oluyole) but did not prevent executive fraud where signatories were unchecked (NLNG, NNPC); dual-control on land payments and independent title search are the missing controls.

### Gaps
- No AGM communiqués, audit reports or developer contracts from CEMCS, COOPLAG, EMCOOP or TEHC were found online; governance detail for the successes is inferred from deal structure.
- No source documents how allocation balloting was run in any cooperative estate, or how price escalation was handled mid-project.

## Key question 4 — Comparators (Kenya, Ethiopia, Uruguay, Zurich/Vienna)

### Takeaway
Kenyan SACCOs show employer-based cooperatives delivering priced, mortgage-backed estates at scale (Stima 340–1,500 units; Mwalimu 871 units) with tiered cash/instalment/mortgage pricing; Ethiopia's state-led IHDP shows scale (383,000 units) but affordability and quality failure; Uruguay's FUCVAM and Zurich/Vienna show that collective land tenure, cost-rent formulas and low equity thresholds keep housing permanently affordable.

### Cited Findings
- **Kenya – Stima SACCO**: Stima Plains, Malaa (Machakos): 340 units, KSh1.3bn; 2-bed KSh2.9m cash / 3.4m instalment / 4.0m mortgage; 3-bed bungalow KSh4.96m / 5.47m / 6.78m (2015) — [allAfrica, Oct 2015](https://allafrica.com/stories/201510010385.html) **[snippet]**; Kasarani 100-acre site: 1,500 units, KSh4bn, 1-bed KSh1.65m to maisonette KSh5.95m — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/markets/stima-sacco-to-build-sh4bn-housing-estate-for-members-2052024) **[snippet]**; Stima Heights, Ngara: 20 storeys, 216 studios + 233 one-beds, KSh884.76m (2023) — [Capital FM](https://www.capitalfm.co.ke/business/2023/07/stima-investment-sacco-to-build-sh884-76-million-residential-project-in-ngara/) **[snippet]**
- **Kenya – Mwalimu National SACCO**: 871 units on 20 acres at Kisaju, Kajiado, KSh2.9bn; units KSh3m–8.5m on long-term mortgage — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/markets/mwalimu-sacco-plans-sh2-9bn-housing-estate-2075478) **[snippet]**; KMRC partnership for mortgages KSh500k–8m at 9% — [Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/business/mwalimu-sacco-kmrc-partner-to-offer-low-cost-mortgages-4084394) **[snippet]**
- **Kenya – NACHU**: has facilitated >30,000 low-cost units; 2021 plan for 125,000 units; Riverline Ridges, Ruiru, >400 acres, plots from KSh2m per eighth-acre; constraints cited: costly end-user finance, policy loopholes on land cost and sectional ownership — [allAfrica, June 2021](https://allafrica.com/stories/202106110769.html) **[snippet]**; [Business Today](https://businesstoday.co.ke/nachu-project-to-provide-housing-for-low-and-middle-income-earners/) **[snippet]**
- **Kenya – church scheme failure**: a Nairobi church housing project embroiled in a KSh260m scandal — [Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/news/church-embroiled-in-new-sh260-million-scandal-over-housing-project--1141298) **[snippet]**
- **Ethiopia – IHDP**: 383,000 units 2006–2018; units "largely unaffordable for their target of lowest-income households"; subsidies ≈US$9bn; quality defects (burst sewers, cracking plaster); lessons: political commitment, tenure security, capacity, quality — [Coalition for Urban Transitions policy assessment](https://urbantransitions.global/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Sustainable-and-Inclusive-Housing-in-Ethiopia_A-policy-assessment_FINAL.pdf) **[snippet]**; [UN-Habitat/E&U](https://www.environmentandurbanization.org/condominium-housing-ethiopia-integrated-housing-development-programme-0); [PASGR brief 2017](https://www.pasgr.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/Integrated_housing_Ethiopia.pdf)
- **Uruguay – FUCVAM**: >35,000 households in 730 mutual-aid cooperatives; members contribute 21 hours/week of labour; monthly fee repays state loan + maintenance; inheritable "use and enjoyment" right; leavers refunded ~90%; 2,197 cooperatives nationally ≈5% of households; National Housing Law 1968; financed by state funds, IDB loans and member contributions — [GEO/Conversation](https://geo.coop/articles/what-world-can-learn-uruguays-housing-co-ops)
- **Zurich**: cooperatives ≈ one-fifth of city housing stock; rents ~20% below market (one example 33% below after a ~$3,000 share); city-set cost-rent formula monitored by government; cooperatives need only ~6% equity vs 20% for for-profit developers — [Shelterforce, Aug 2021](https://shelterforce.org/2021/08/12/why-nonprofit-cooperatives-are-thriving-in-zurich/) **[snippet; page 403]**
- **Vienna**: 17.8% of housing (~221,000 homes) run by limited-profit associations; 20.7% of Viennese live in one; municipal rent ≈€5.10/sqm vs limited-profit ≈€6.10/sqm — [Warsaw Enterprise Institute critique](https://wei.org.pl/en/2025/news/mgundinger/a-critical-examination-of-the-viennese-housing-model/) **[snippet]**; [housingcoop.eu Vienna](https://www.housingcoop.eu/cities/vienna)

### Inferences
- Kenyan SACCOs publish unit prices with cash/instalment/mortgage tiers at launch — a transparency practice absent from almost every Nigerian cooperative estate found.
- Access to a mortgage refinancer (KMRC) lets Kenyan cooperatives sell on 9% long-term mortgages; FMBN/NHF plays the analogous role in Nigeria but only for NHF contributors and with ₦15m caps.
- Zurich/Vienna/Uruguay affordability rests on removing land from the market and regulating rent/price formulas — structurally different from Nigerian cooperatives, whose members mostly seek freehold-equivalent plots to capture appreciation (Nicon Town).

### Gaps
- Default/completion rates for Kenyan SACCO estates were not found.
- No quantitative cost-saving figure for FUCVAM mutual aid was found in the fetched source.

## Synthesis — success factors, failure patterns, practical checklist

### Takeaway
Across 20+ Nigerian cases, success correlates with secured title before mobilisation, a bonded professional developer or bank-structured finance with milestone disbursement, stable payroll savings (often plus employer equity), and continuous AGM accountability; failure correlates with executive-controlled land payments without title verification or escrow, political/union SPVs collecting before land is perfected, and inability to fund title fees.

### Cited Findings (cross-case)
- Title-first successes: Nicon Town (approved layout bought 2001) — [Estate Intel](https://estateintel.com/insights/real-estate-developments-owned-by-cooperative-societies-in-nigeria); Akacare (FMBN requires unencumbered title) — [ThisDay 2021](https://thisdaylive.com/index.php/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians)
- Pay-before-title failures: NLNG ₦700m — [TheNigeriaLawyer](https://thenigerialawyer.com/%E2%82%A6580m-restitution-ordered-after-10-year-trial-court-convicts-and-sentences-wallang-for-%E2%82%A6700m-land-scam-targeting-nlng-staff-cooperative/); Apo-Tafyi — [Daily Trust](https://dailytrust.com/housing-subscribers-call-for-help-over-stalemate-with-brains-hammers-others/); RCCG Abuja — [Sahara Reporters](https://saharareporters.com/2017/07/14/how-redeemed-church-officials-allegedly-duped-members-millions-housing-scheme)
- Developer-led delivery within schedule: CoopEast (<24 months) — [Vanguard](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2022/06/developer-delivers-n5-6bn-residential-estate-for-exxonmobil-shell-cooperatives/); NIMCOS (<12 months) — [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/nis-unveils-nationwide-housing-drive-inaugurates-new-staff-estates/)
- Lender-disbursement failure when unphased: Kaba $65m — [TheWill](https://thewillnews.com/icpc-reclaims-abandoned-65m-abuja-housing-estate-returns-forfeited-land-to-fmbn-for-completion-of-962-homes/)
- Carrying-cost failure: NFIU ₦298m revocation — [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/news/full-list-wike-revokes-568-land-allocations-over-unpaid-c-of-o-fees/)
- Affordability enablers: employer 30% equity (NMDPRA) — [Nigeria Housing Market](https://www.nigeriahousingmarket.com/news/430-nmdpra-workers-housing-allocations-fmbn-n8-3bn-mortgages); regular salaries (Oluyole) — [Tribune](https://tribuneonlineng.com/oluyole-lg-boss-commissions-teachers-housing-estate/); NHF 7–9% / 30-year mortgages (Akacare) — [ThisDay 2021](https://thisdaylive.com/index.php/2021/08/11/how-fmbn-is-leveraging-co-operatives-to-make-homes-possible-for-nigerians)

### Inferences — practical checklist for a Nigerian cooperative estate
1. **Title before money**: obtain and independently verify C of O / Governor's Consent / gazetted excision (and clear omo-onile claims) *before* collecting member deposits; budget C-of-O fees and ground rent (NFIU lesson).
2. **Dual-control and escrow**: land and construction payments only from a bank-held project account with milestone certification by an independent QS/project manager; no single-signatory executive payments (NLNG/NNPC lesson).
3. **Bonded professional developer under a fixed-scope contract**, with performance bond and liquidated damages, or a lender-structured deal (CMB/FCMB/FMBN) in which the bank controls disbursement (CoopEast, Autograph, Louisville, Akacare).
4. **Phased collection tied to milestones**, published unit prices with cash/instalment/mortgage tiers (Kenyan SACCO practice), and a written price-escalation clause.
5. **Pre-arranged take-out finance** (NHF/FMBN, CMB, KMRC-style) and, where possible, employer equity support (NMDPRA model).
6. **Governance**: annual audited accounts and AGM (Oluyole, NFIU coop practice), elected tenure limits, transparent allocation (public ballot), and a member grievance/refund procedure (Egan-Igando refund precedent).
7. **Start with land banking of an approved layout, not raw land** (Nicon Town), and let appreciation fund later phases.
8. **Keep exit paths**: refund policy and resale rules so members are not trapped for 9–17 years (Apo-Tafyi, Koroduma).

### Gaps
- The overall count of Nigerian cooperative estates that have stalled vs completed is not measured anywhere found; FMBN's ~40 CHDL projects (2021) have no published completion ledger.
- Member prices, title documents and governance instruments for the flagship oil-company estates remain unpublished; a report writer should present those cases as "delivered, prices undisclosed."
