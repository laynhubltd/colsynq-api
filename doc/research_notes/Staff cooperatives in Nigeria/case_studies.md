# Nigerian Staff (Workplace) Cooperatives: Case Studies of Successes, Failures and Disputes

Scope note: Nigeria primary; 15 Nigerian cases (9 "worked", 6 "failed/disputed") plus 4 comparators. Every case carries a date, a figure and a source. Several Nigerian outlets (Guardian NG, Estate Intel, Proshare, Total Upstream coop site) block automated fetching, so some figures rest on search-snippet text or secondary outlets; these are flagged. Cases still in court or unresolved are labelled UNRESOLVED.

## KQ1. Successes: staff cooperatives that built estates or ran large, solvent balance sheets

### Takeaway
The cooperatives that delivered estates are overwhelmingly oil-and-gas (Chevron, Shell, ExxonMobil, Total, NLNG) or security/paramilitary (Immigration, Police, Customs) bodies with (a) a stable, high-earning payroll deducted at source, (b) land bought outright early or secured through the employer/government, and (c) delivery through a professional developer or bank-financed SPV rather than in-house construction. Disclosed balance sheets range from N0.6bn savings (a 355-member PPMC branch) to N23bn (NLNG, 1,315 members) and ~N66bn (Total Upstream, ~1,400 members).

### Cited Findings

**Case 1 — Chevron Employees Multipurpose Cooperative Society (CEMCS), Chevron Nigeria Ltd**
- CEMCS assumed ownership of Nicon Town Estate, Ikate, Lekki after Nicon Lekki Ltd sold the majority of its plots to CEMCS in 2001; the estate covers 57 hectares on the Lekki-Epe Expressway and was completed in 2007 — [Estate Intel](https://estateintel.com/insights/real-estate-developments-owned-by-cooperative-societies-in-nigeria); [Nicon Town review](https://www.neighbourhoodreview.com/a-comprehensive-review-of-nicon-town/)
- Nicon Town was laid out as 431 plots: 46 at 1,200 sqm, 204 at 1,000 sqm and the balance at 800 sqm — [Neighbourhood Review](https://www.neighbourhoodreview.com/a-comprehensive-review-of-nicon-town/)
- Plots reportedly started at about N5m and are now valued at N160m–N200m (appreciation >2,000%); current listings put plots at ~N450m and 5–7 bedroom houses at N600m–N1bn (low-grade property-blog sources; treat as indicative) — [NaijaProperty](https://naijaproperty.ng/one-of-the-best-estate-in-lagos-nicon-town-estate/); [Realty Nub](https://realtynub.com/expensive-estates/)
- CEMCS then developed Melrose Park Estate, 14 hectares bordering VGC on the Lekki corridor; mandatory tree-planting, no high perimeter fences — [Estate Intel](https://estateintel.com/insights/real-estate-developments-owned-by-cooperative-societies-in-nigeria); [Venturanna](https://venturanna.com.ng/inside-lagos-top-20-gated-estates-everyone-wants-to-live-in/)
- CEMCS also runs a licensed microfinance bank (Chevron Employee Co-operative MFB) and business lines in travel, e-commerce, loans, commodities and insurance; HQ at 6 Udeco Medical Road, Chevyview Estate, Lekki — [BusinessList](https://www.businesslist.com.ng/company/100557/chevron-employee-co-operative-mfb); [JobGurus](https://www.jobgurus.com.ng/companies/view/chevron-employees-multi-purpose-cooperative-society)

**Case 5 — NLNG Staff Bonny Cooperative Investment and Credit Society**

**Case 9 — Nigerian Shippers' Council (NSC) Staff Multipurpose Cooperative and NNPC/PPMC Port Harcourt branch (small-parastatal benchmarks)**

**Other smaller "worked" examples**

### Inferences
- Oil-sector cooperatives hold assets of N23bn–N66bn with only 1,300–3,000 members (N15m–N47m per member) — a wholly different economic class from civil-service cooperatives where N4m–N200m in arrears is crippling.
- The successful estate model is consistently: cooperative buys land (often from a distressed corporate seller or via government allocation) → engages a developer/bank (Cooperative Mortgage Bank, FCMB, FMBN, Shelter Afrique) → sells plots/units to members, retaining a land-value uplift for the society.
- Published figures are mostly from AGMs reported in the press; few cooperatives publish full accounts online (NLNG, PPMC PH and NSC are exceptions).

### Gaps
- No disclosed member plot prices or allocation timeline for Nicon Town/Melrose Park from CEMCS itself; only property-blog estimates.
- No AGM figures (savings, loan book, dividend %) found for CBN, FIRS, NPA, NDIC, Customs or Police cooperatives; no figures for UI/UNILAG/OAU staff cooperative estates.
- Estate Intel's directory pages (CEMCS, Shell, TEHCOOP) and the Total Upstream site returned 403; unit counts there come from search snippets.

## KQ2. Failures and disputes: fraud, stalled estates, unremitted deductions, liquidity crises, court cases

### Takeaway
The documented failure modes are (1) land-acquisition fraud by outsiders (NLNG, N700m, 2011) and insider-enabled payments (NLNG ex-president, 2015 charges); (2) governance breakdown with regulator-imposed caretakers freezing withdrawals for years (NNPC Lagos, >4,000 members, N806m loss, no AGM since 2022); (3) employers deducting but not remitting — chronic in state governments (Ogun 17–21 months; Oyo Water Corp N200m; Kwara N70m) and federal IPPIS (NBS Kwara N4.4m held 2+ years); (4) stalled worker housing schemes (Apo-Tafyi, 1,000 civil servants, no land 10 years on); and (5) liquidity squeezes when funds are tied up in real estate (Police, 2022–23).

### Cited Findings

**Case 10 — NLNG Staff Bonny Cooperative: N700m land fraud (2011) and ex-officers charged (2015)**
- The cooperative paid N520m (20 Oct 2011) and N130m (3 Nov 2011) through Dinijocs Nigeria Ltd for a 643-hectare plot in Idu-Sabo District, Cadastral Zone D04, Abuja, which Jonathan Wallang (MD, Swift and Hay Ltd) had no authority to sell; arrested 2015 on the cooperative's petition; convicted after a 10-year trial by Justice Hamza Muazu (FCT High Court, Maitama): 7 years without option of fine on counts 6–7, 2 years/N200k option on counts 1–3, and N580m restitution ordered to the cooperative; two co-defendants died during trial, one acquitted — [TheNigeriaLawyer](https://thenigerialawyer.com/%E2%82%A6580m-restitution-ordered-after-10-year-trial-court-convicts-and-sentences-wallang-for-%E2%82%A6700m-land-scam-targeting-nlng-staff-cooperative/); [EFCC on X](https://x.com/officialEFCC/status/1950938960202473627)
- EFCC arraigned the cooperative's former President Julius Ola Peters and Vice-President Emeka Iloegbunam with Alanza, Dinijocs and Wallang on 26 May 2015 on a 12-count charge; the charge alleged N1.22bn obtained, N830m of it "in excess" — [PRNigeria](https://prnigeria.com/2015/05/28/efcc-arraigns-ex-nlng-cooperative-chief-four-others-for-n207m-fraud/); the headline figure is reported inconsistently as "N207m" by [PRNigeria](https://prnigeria.com/2015/05/28/efcc-arraigns-ex-nlng-cooperative-chief-four-others-for-n207m-fraud/), "N207bn" by [SaharaReporters](https://saharareporters.com/2015/06/30/n207bn-scam-efcc-re-arraign-former-president-nlng-cooperatives-others-sept-15) and [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/news/nigeria/metro/efcc-to-re-arraign-nlng-co-operative-societys-former-president-vice-over-alleged-n207-billion-scam/), and "N2.70bn" in the [EFCC release title](https://www.efcc.gov.ng/efcc/news-and-information/news-release/1424-n207bn-scam-efcc-to-re-arraign-former-president-of-nlng-cooperatives-others-sept-15). N207bn is implausible for a cooperative then far smaller than N23bn; treat the exact charged sum as uncertain (N0.2bn–N2.7bn).
- Outcome: Peters died during trial; Iloegbulam discharged and acquitted; only the outside vendor was convicted (2025) — [PRNigeria/EFCC via search](https://prnigeria.com/2015/05/28/efcc-arraigns-ex-nlng-cooperative-chief-four-others-for-n207m-fraud/)

### Inferences
- Insider theft at scale is rarer in the record than (a) outside vendor/land fraud facilitated by weak due diligence and (b) governance paralysis; the latter (NNPC Lagos) freezes far more member money (4,000+ members, 6 years) than any single conviction.
- Non-remittance is structurally an employer-credit risk: a cooperative whose deductions flow through a state treasury or IPPIS is effectively an unsecured lender to that payer.
- Real-estate concentration converts a liquid thrift society into an illiquid developer; both Police (2022) and NNPC Lagos (N806m 2022 loss) show the symptoms.

### Gaps
- No specific cooperative-society-vs-omo-onile court judgment was found; no case of a staff cooperative collapsing outright on employer privatisation (PHCN/NITEL/Nigeria Airways) — the documented losses there are severance/pension and union funds, not cooperative savings.
- Membership counts, loan-book sizes and audited numbers for NNPC Lagos are only what aggrieved members state; the caretaker committee's side is unreported.
- Exact charged sum in the NLNG 2015 case is contradictory across sources.

## KQ3. Governance and management lessons: what separated builders from stallers

### Takeaway
Builders combined early outright land purchase (Nicon Town 2001; Northern Foreshore 141 ha) or government land grants (KASU-ASUU; NIMCOS/FCT) with a professional developer and institutional lender, fixed-term elected committees with an oversight council, external audit, and digital member access; stallers show absent AGMs, no audited accounts, regulator-imposed caretakers, concentration into illiquid property, and unmonitored vendor payments.

### Cited Findings
- Term limits and oversight: NLNG committee two-year terms, max two consecutive, plus a Council of Inspectors; IFRS accounts — [NLNG Coop](https://nlng.coop.ng/About). PPMC PH has a 3-member elected Council of Scrutiny and external auditors — [PPMC PH AGM](https://www.ppmcphcoop.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/AGM-Report-compressed.pdf). COOPLAG: 7-man elected Management Committee under federal Department of Cooperatives supervision — [COOPLAG](https://cooplag.com.ng/About)
- Developer/lender model: NIMCOS–Abiskoly; COOPLAG/NNPC–Cooperative Mortgage Bank; TEHCOOP–FCMB US$40m; Customs–Shelter Afrique US$3.9m and FMBN; Police–FMBN N2.1bn/N3bn — [Vanguard](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/07/immigration-targets-nationwide-housing-for-personnel/); [CMBank](https://cmbankng.com/financed-projects/ongoing-projects/autograph-banana-island/); [Premium Times](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/promoted/807980-fcmb-backs-totalenergies-staff-housing-cooperative-multipurpose-society-ltd-tehc-flagship-project-with-40m.html); [Shelter Afrique](https://www.shelterafrique.org/fr/projects/view/346?region=Western+Africa); [Housing TV Africa](https://www.housingtvafrica.com/nigeria-police-housing-scheme-igp-egbetokuns-vision/)
- Direct-to-bank payouts and e-applications: NSC pays dividends straight to accounts — [Anchor News](https://anchornewsonline.com/nsc-cooperative-pays-retirees-%E2%82%A6102-4m-declares-%E2%82%A690-6m-dividend-as-new-leadership-takes-over/); PPMC PH introduced online loan disbursement and e-applications — [PPMC PH AGM](https://www.ppmcphcoop.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/AGM-Report-compressed.pdf); Police automated platform Nov 2022 — [TheFact](https://thefact.ng/police-digitalizes-cooperative-society-operations/)
- Failure markers: no AGM since 2022, no financial reports, caretaker changed signatories, N180m disputed transaction (NNPC Lagos) — [SaharaReporters](https://saharareporters.com/2025/04/17/nnpc-cooperative-members-accuse-lagos-ministry-enabling-misappropriation-savings); land paid for in two tranches to an intermediary for 643 ha the seller did not own (NLNG) — [TheNigeriaLawyer](https://thenigerialawyer.com/%E2%82%A6580m-restitution-ordered-after-10-year-trial-court-convicts-and-sentences-wallang-for-%E2%82%A6700m-land-scam-targeting-nlng-staff-cooperative/)
- Member behaviour risks named by a cooperative itself: low individual savings, heavy withdrawal demand, pressure to breach loan rules, failure to repay upfront deductions, mass redeployment of members — [PPMC PH AGM](https://www.ppmcphcoop.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/AGM-Report-compressed.pdf)
- Lagos has >16,000 registered cooperatives supervised by its Department of Cooperative Services, which has the power to audit and (as NNPC shows) to dissolve committees — [Lagos MCCTI](https://mccti.lagosstate.gov.ng/departments/cooperatives)

### Inferences
- Risk controls that the record supports: title verification/escrow before land payments (NLNG lesson); cap on property share of assets and a liquidity reserve for withdrawals (Police/NNPC/PPMC lesson); mandatory annual AGM with audited accounts filed to the registrar (NNPC lesson); term limits plus an independent inspection council (NLNG/PPMC practice); direct-debit remittance tracking with escalation when the payer is a state treasury or IPPIS (Ogun/Oyo/NBS lesson); phased allocation tied to developer milestones (Apo-Tafyi lesson).
- Regulator intervention is double-edged: Lagos' caretaker in NNPC is accused of worsening the crisis, suggesting that internal dispute-resolution and court-supervised elections should precede dissolution.

### Gaps
- No Nigerian source found describing use of a formal escrow account for cooperative estate payments; no public evidence on whether CEMCS/COOPLAG used phased allocation.

## KQ4. Interaction with the employer: land, grants, guarantees, interference, restructuring

### Takeaway
Employers help most through payroll deduction-at-source, land/office space and endorsement (Immigration CG, Interior Minister, Police Force Order), and harm most by failing to remit deductions (states, IPPIS) or, in restructuring, by not settling terminal benefits that would otherwise flow into savings; direct employer capture of a cooperative's funds was not documented, but regulator (state ministry) capture was (NNPC Lagos).

### Cited Findings
- Employer sponsorship: Police cooperative created by Force Order No. 130 (1977) — [TheFact](https://thefact.ng/police-digitalizes-cooperative-society-operations/); NIMCOS estates commissioned by the Interior Minister and CGI, i.e., state endorsement of a cooperative-owned asset — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/uncategorized/article/fg-inaugurates-1500-unit-housing-estate-for-immigration-personnel-in-abuja/); PPMC PH thanks NPSC management for premises and a filling-station concession on company land — [PPMC PH AGM](https://www.ppmcphcoop.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/AGM-Report-compressed.pdf)
- Government land: Kaduna State allocated land to KASU-ASUU cooperative (land value framed as a 15% discount on N42m units) — [21st Century Chronicle](https://21stcenturychronicle.com/asuu-commends-uba-sani-over-land-allocation/); Lagos State plans an umbrella Cooperative Housing Association to channel Family Home Fund housing to cooperatives — [Lagos MCIC](https://mcic.lagosstate.gov.ng/2021/06/05/lagos-set-to-establish-housing-cooperative-association/)
- Employer as payer-risk: Ogun (17–21 months), Oyo Water Corp (N200m), Kwara Water Corp (N70m), IPPIS/NBS Kwara (N4.4m, 2+ years) — sources in Case 12
- Independence: CBN Coop states it "is not a subsidiary of the Central Bank of Nigeria" — [CBN Coop](https://www.thecbncoop.com/about); NLNG registered as an "independent association" — [NLNG Coop](https://nlng.coop.ng/About)
- Restructuring: Shell sold SPDC (2025) — [Shell](https://www.shell.com/news-and-insights/newsroom/news-and-media-releases/2025/shell-completes-sale-of-spdc.html); PPMC PH lost members through redeployment (385→355) — [PPMC PH AGM](https://www.ppmcphcoop.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/AGM-Report-compressed.pdf); NNPC members called on NNPC Ltd management to intervene in the cooperative crisis, which it has not publicly done — [Vanguard](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2025/05/nnpc-retirees-cry-out-over-possible-loss-of-savings-with-staff-cooperative/)

### Inferences
- Where the employer is itself the paymaster (state civil service), the cooperative's biggest counterparty risk is the employer; where the employer is a cash-rich corporate, the main risks shift to internal governance and vendor fraud.
- Employer restructuring reduces membership and increases withdrawal pressure (redeployment, divestment), which argues for liquidity buffers sized to exit scenarios.

### Gaps
- No documented case of an employer guaranteeing a cooperative's bank loan or giving a cash grant; no public account of what happened to Shell East/COOPLAG membership rules after the SPDC sale.

## KQ5. Comparators: Kenya (Mwalimu, Stima), Ghana, and a US employer credit union

### Takeaway
Kenya's employer-based SACCOs dwarf Nigeria's: Mwalimu (teachers) KSh76bn assets, Stima (power workers) KSh75bn with 220,650 members — yet Mwalimu's Spire Bank venture shows that even a well-regulated SACCO can lose ~KSh10bn through a poorly evaluated non-core investment; Ghana's largest credit union is a university-staff body (GH¢321m), and the US benchmark Navy Federal holds US$197bn.

### Cited Findings
- Mwalimu National DT SACCO (est. 1974; >120,000 members): 2024 total assets KSh68.9bn, loans KSh47.1bn, 10% interest on deposits, 13% dividend; 2025 assets KSh76bn, deposits KSh56.5bn, loans KSh56bn (+19.3%), surplus KSh1.27bn, 13% dividend — [Mwango Capital](https://x.com/MwangoCapital/status/1904794285024924038); [Sacco Review](https://saccoreview.co.ke/mwalimu-dt-sacco-reports-strong-2025-growth-as-assets-hit-ksh-76billion/); [Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/business/mwalimu-national-sacco-membership-requirements-savings-loan-options-and-interest/)
- Mwalimu's Spire Bank failure: paid KSh2.4bn in Oct 2014 for 75% of Equatorial Commercial Bank; seller withdrew KSh1.7bn in deposits (one-fifth of total) soon after; the bank accumulated ~KSh10bn losses, cost the SACCO ~KSh1.1bn a year, and was transferred to Equity Bank on 1 Feb 2023; chairman admitted "we didn't calculate properly"; a 6-year (2022–27) capital restoration plan writes off ~KSh8bn — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/data-hub/teachers-learn-sh10bn-loss-lesson-in-spire-deal--4108192); [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/companies/merali-sh1-7bn-withdrawal-that-sank-teachers-spire-bank-3381014); [The EastAfrican](https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/business-tech/mwalimu-sacco-foregoes-62m-put-in-spire-bank-4982672)
- Stima DT SACCO (power-sector origin): 2024 assets KSh66.5bn (+12.4%), loans KSh50.2bn, net surplus KSh2.2bn (+50%), 16% dividend and 11% interest rebate (KSh4.6bn payout), members 200,145→220,650, core capital KSh13.4bn; absorbed a KSh108m KUSCCO hit; 2025 assets KSh75.3bn — [Mwango Capital](https://x.com/MwangoCapital/status/1895203418211598383); [The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/business/kenya/2025-02-28-stima-sacco-declares-16-per-cent-dividend); [Kenyan Wall Street](https://kenyanwallstreet.com/stima-sacco-shrugs-off-ksh-108-million-kuscco-hit-as-profits-assets-surge-in-2024); [Sacco Review](https://saccoreview.co.ke/stima-sacco-assets-hit-ksh75-3-billion-post-ksh10-8-billion-revenue/)
- Kenyan counties owed SACCOs KSh1.88bn in unremitted deductions — the same employer-remittance problem as Nigeria — [People Daily](https://peopledaily.digital/business/counties-owe-saccos-ksh1-88-billion-in-unremitted-deductions)
- Ghana: 490 credit unions, GH¢2.68bn assets, 984,034 members (Dec 2021), supervised by CUA under LI 2225 (2015) on behalf of Bank of Ghana; the University of Ghana Co-operative Credit Union is the largest in West Africa with assets GH¢308.64m (2023) → GH¢320.94m (2024) and a GH¢20m surplus — [MoF Ghana](https://mofep.gov.gh/adverts/2022-11-09/%20%20request-for-expressions-of-interest-ghana-financial-sector-development-project-gh-mof-fsd-cs-75-indv); [Graphic Online](https://www.graphic.com.gh/business/business-news/ug-co-operative-credit-union-posts-ghc20m-surplus.html)
- US: Navy Federal Credit Union (military/DoD employer base) had US$197.1bn assets and 15.3m members at 31 Dec 2025, net-worth ratio 11.36% — [Navy Federal 2025 Annual Report](https://www.navyfederal.org/content/dam/nfculibs/pdfs/financial-reports/2025-annual-report.pdf)

### Inferences
- Kenya's scale rests on a dedicated regulator (SASRA) with prudential ratios and public annual results, which Nigeria's state-registrar model lacks; but Spire Bank shows regulation does not prevent governance-driven strategic errors — relevant to Nigerian cooperatives moving into microfinance banks (CEMCS) and large property.
- Nigerian oil-sector cooperatives are already comparable per-member to Kenyan SACCOs; the gap is in civil-service and teacher cooperatives, where remittance risk suppresses growth.

### Gaps
- SASRA supervision report figures were not fetched directly; Mwalimu's current member count comes from secondary sources.
