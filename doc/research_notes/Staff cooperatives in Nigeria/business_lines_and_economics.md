# Staff (Workplace) Cooperatives in Nigeria: Business Lines and Their Economics

Scope note: Nigeria first; Kenya/WOCCU only as benchmarks. All figures carry a year. "Self-reported" marks figures published by the cooperative itself (website, bye-laws, AGM press release) rather than audited statements seen directly. Several key primary pages (estateintel insights, Guardian property, Total Upstream coop share-sale FAQ, academia.edu paper) returned HTTP 403 and could only be used via search-result snippets; these are labelled "(snippet)".

## 1. Thrift & credit (the core line): savings rules, loan menu, rates, tenors, guarantors, payroll recovery, default, and how the spread becomes surplus and dividend

### Takeaway
Nigerian staff cooperatives run a very standard model: compulsory monthly savings (N5,000 is the recurring floor), loans capped at 2x–3x savings (up to 5x for short-term/special products at the CBN coop), member-guarantor security, and payroll deduction with a 33–45% payroll "headroom" test. Interest ranges widely: 5–12.5% p.a. at the CBN staff coop (2020 schedule) versus 18–21% p.a. (and 2–2.5% flat per month on short-term loans) at Shell's CoopEast; universities sit at 10–19%. Published AGM figures show surpluses of N34m (INEC 2024) to N181m (Shippers' Council 2025) with roughly 50–64% of surplus paid out as dividend; the legal structure requires at least 25% of net surplus to the reserve fund.

**Payroll recovery and legal charge on salary**
- Members "endorse a letter of authority for the deduction of their contribution from monthly salary" (snippet, search summary of staff-coop bye-law content) — [FRCN Lagos MCS membership page](https://www.frcnlagoscms.org/web/home/manu.php?id=9)
- Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act gives a society a first charge "upon the shares or interests in the capital and on the deposits of a present or past or deceased member" and "upon any dividend, bonus or profits payable" — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act (FAOLEX)](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Act s.30: a society "shall not... grant a loan to a person who is not a member" except as provided in s.32 — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)

**Default**
- Ekiti State study (EKSU Business & Finance Journal, 2023): "Loan default has remained a dominant issue that has discouraged most cooperative societies from granting loan to members"; loan default rate defined as "Non-performing loan/total loan advance"; loan management procedure, loan diversion and loan size explained "about 76% of the systematic variation in the growth of cooperative societies"; loan diversion exerted a negative effect (coef -0.479, p=0.052) — [EKSU BF Journal PDF](https://bfjournal.eksu.edu.ng/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/loan-default.pdf)
- Anambra university-worker cooperatives study: loan default had "significant positive effect on net profit, liquidity, and return on equity" (snippet; direction counter-intuitive, likely because penal interest is booked as income) — [Zap Journals IJARE](http://zapjournals.com/Journals/index.php/ijare/article/view/2114)
- Kwara CTCS study (search summary): "Loan default rates significantly reduced the net surplus and reserves fund accumulation" (snippet) — [ResearchGate figure/study listing](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Distribution-according-to-loan-default-effects-on-university-worker-cooperative-liquidity_tbl1_388221875)
- CoopEast: "MANAGEMENT RESERVES THE RIGHT TO WITHOLD LOANS TO MEMBERS WITH BAD CREDIT RECORDS" — [CoopEast Loan Schemes](https://www.coopeastngr.com/loanscheme.asp)
- ASUU Ojo bye-laws require the supervisory committee to report "How many loans are delinquent?" and "How many loans are overdue (a) within 1 year (b) over 2 years?" — [ASUU Ojo Coop Bye-Laws](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)

**Surplus appropriation rules (how spread becomes dividend)**
- Act s.34: "At least one fourth of the net profits... shall be paid into a fund to be called the 'reserve fund'"; thereafter up to "ten per cent of the remainder of the net surplus to an education fund"; no dividend/bonus may be distributed "until the proposal payment or distribution has been approved by the committee"; non-compliance is an offence — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Act (liquidation section): surplus on liquidation may go to "a dividend at a rate not exceeding five per cent per annum" — this 5% cap applies in liquidation, not to ordinary annual dividends — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- ASUU Ojo bye-laws: "At least one-fourth shall be carried to the Reserve Fund"; "2½% to the Education Fund"; "Dividend on members' savings, Share Capital and loans, shall not be less than 50% of the net surplus"; Management Committee honorarium "shall not be more than 7.5% of the net surplus"; share capital held by one member capped at "20% of the total paid up share capital"; dividend on savings "determined after the surplus has been ascertained and necessary reserves created" — [ASUU Ojo Coop Bye-Laws](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- UNIZIK paper: "No registered society shall pay dividend on shares at a rate in excess of twenty percent per annum" (snippet; appears to quote a state cooperative law/regulation) — [UNIZIK JFMS](https://journals.unizik.edu.ng/index.php/jfms/article/download/2577/2084/6199)
- UNN FAQ distinguishes "bonus" (share of surplus "in proportion to the volume of business done") from "dividend" (share of profit on shares) — [UNN Cooperative FAQs](https://www.unn.edu.ng/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/FAQs.pdf)

- Total E&P Nigeria Staff Multipurpose Coop: "Profit After Tax exceeded N1 billion for 2-3 years; 2017 PAT was N2.25 billion" (self-reported) — [stories.coop](https://stories.coop/a-growing-coop-in-nigeria/)
- Aggregator claim: dividends "typically paid annually at 5-15% of savings" (unsourced aggregator; treat as indicative only) — [KudiCompass](https://kudicompass.com/how-to-join-cooperative-society-nigeria/)
- RUN University survey (480 respondents, departmental cooperatives): 58.8% said dividend paid "was not enough"; 79.2% opposed raising loan rates to fund dividends; 75.8% opposed outside investments; withdrawing members "are paid only the balances in their savings and shares" with no share of investments — [RUN Repository paper](https://repository.run.edu.ng/bitstreams/3761bb58-bfef-410e-a2d5-2ec08850af24/download)

### Inferences
- Two pricing regimes coexist: (a) "subsidised" coops with large cheap deposit bases and cross-subsidy from investment income (CBN coop 3–12.5% p.a.), and (b) oil-company coops that price close to or above commercial bank rates (CoopEast 18–21% p.a., 2–2.5% flat/month short-term) but offer large multiples and long tenors; the second model generates far larger surpluses per member (Total E&P, N2.25bn PAT 2017 on ~1,500 members ≈ N1.5m/member).
- The 2x–3x savings multiple plus the 33–45% payroll headroom test is the universal credit-risk engine; the member-guarantor and payroll first charge explain why default is treated as a governance rather than an underwriting problem in the literature.
- Payout ratios of ~50–64% of surplus (INEC, NSC) match the ASUU-Ojo bye-law floor ("not less than 50%") after the 25% statutory reserve; a coop earning 10–15% gross on loans and paying 50–65% out plausibly yields a 5–10% dividend on average savings, consistent with the aggregator's 5–15% range — but no cooperative in these sources publishes its dividend as a percentage of savings.
- NNPC's swing from N96m surplus to N806m loss in one year with no AGM is the clearest Nigerian illustration of governance (not credit) risk destroying a thrift-and-credit line.

### Gaps
- No Nigerian staff cooperative publishes a dividend rate expressed as % of savings in the sources found; only naira totals (INEC, NSC, NIMCOS). The uniqueunilagstaffcoop.org site did not resolve and the Total Upstream coop share-sale FAQ was 403.
- No published loan default/NPL ratios for named Nigerian staff cooperatives; academic studies are survey-based and give coefficients, not default rates.
- The 20% dividend-on-shares cap (UNIZIK snippet) could not be traced to a specific statute section.
- Could not access EFInA's 2012 "Understanding Co-operatives in Nigeria" qualitative report text (image PDF).

## 2. Housing and land: cooperative estates, acquisition and allocation, member vs market pricing, instalments and FMBN/NHF tie-ins; successes and stalled schemes

### Takeaway
The showcase cases are oil-company cooperatives in Lekki (Chevron's CEMCS/Nicon Town, Shell's COOPLAG Gardens, CoopEast/ExxonMobil Beach Resort, TotalEnergies' Louisville), which bought land or partnered developers and sold plots/units to members, with Nicon Town plots rising from ~N5m to N160–200m. Public-sector coops (NIMCOS, NMDPRA, Akacare) use FMBN's cooperative windows: CHDL development loans at 10% p.a. up to N500m and member NHF mortgages at 9% p.a., up to 30 years, with equity of a few million naira and salary deduction to retirement. Stalled/disputed estates were not documented in accessible sources beyond NMDPRA's >1,000 applicants for 430 units.

### Inferences
- Two acquisition models: (i) bulk purchase of titled land from a parastatal/insurer then plot allocation to members at cost (Nicon Town 2001; plots ~N5m then), which produced extraordinary member capital gains; (ii) developer joint ventures where the coop takes a 50% equity stake and members buy finished units (CoopEast Beach Resort 2019–22).
- Public-sector coops without oil-sector balance sheets rely on FMBN's CHDL (10% p.a. construction finance) plus NHF member mortgages (9% p.a., 30 years, payroll deduction) — member cash equity in the N3.5m range (2021).
- Over-subscription (NMDPRA 1,000+ applicants for 430 units) is the typical allocation stress point; allocation is by batch/eligibility rather than lottery in the sources found.

### Gaps
- No accessible source gave member price vs open-market price for the same unit at allocation date (except Nicon Town's historical ~N5m).
- No documented stalled or disputed staff-cooperative estate was retrievable (Guardian "N15b Mobil-Shell estate" and Estate Intel pieces returned 403); Punch/Guardian searches did not surface a named stalled staff-coop estate. Mobil, NNPC, CBN, teachers' and Lagos State staff estates were not covered by retrievable sources.

## 3. Consumer and commodity services: shops, bulk food purchase, household goods on instalment, fuel/gas, car/phone/laptop schemes — margins and pricing

### Takeaway
Consumer lines are structured either as a "commodity loan" (CBN coop: 12 months at 5%; CoopEast: N2m, 21% p.a. reducing, 12 months) or as a cost-plus mark-up on goods supplied (UNN: 7% mark-up). Oil-company coops run full retail arms (Coop Mart, Coop Café, laundry, lube bay, travel, guesthouse at Total E&P; online stores and commodity sales at NLNG). Car loans at Shell's CoopEast run to N20m over 4 years at 19–21% p.a. Margin data beyond the UNN 7% mark-up was not published.

- ASUU Ojo bye-laws list "Direct loan, as obtainable in the Civil Service, e.g. Car Loan, Compassionate Loan" among loan categories — [ASUU Ojo Coop Bye-Laws](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- Members' reasons for joining (RUN survey): 10.4% cited cheaper "prices" of goods, 8.3% educational needs — [RUN Repository paper](https://repository.run.edu.ng/bitstreams/3761bb58-bfef-410e-a2d5-2ec08850af24/download)
- Non-coop comparator: Oyo State civil servants can access a "salary-deducted food credit plan where repayments are automatically collected on the 25th of each month" (FoodBank.ng, 2026) — [FoodBank.ng](https://www.foodbank.ng/)

### Inferences
- Pricing is set either as a loan rate (commodity loan priced like a normal loan, 5–21% p.a.) or as a mark-up on landed cost (7% at UNN), with recovery through payroll; the economics are therefore identical to thrift-and-credit with goods substituted for cash.
- Only the very large oil-sector coops have the scale to operate owned retail outlets; for most staff coops the "shop" is a bulk-buy-and-deduct scheme without inventory risk.

### Gaps
- No source gave gross margins, festive-hamper pricing, or fuel/cooking-gas scheme terms for a named Nigerian staff cooperative.
- No published volumes (naira value of commodities supplied) for any cooperative.

## 4. Investment of pooled funds: permitted instruments, actual portfolios, and surplus allocation

### Takeaway
The Act permits deposits in cooperative/approved banks, FGN-guaranteed securities, and "any other manner approved by the Committee" — an open-ended clause that lets coops hold fixed deposits, real estate, shares and even operating businesses. Actual portfolios seen: CBN coop's 4-year 14% p.a. "Cooperative Investment" notes (N5m–N50m), UNILAG's fixed deposits and landed property, Total E&P's treasury/real-estate departments, and UNILAG's 2019 N1bn share issue (500m units at N2). At least 25% of surplus must be reserved before distribution.

### Cited Findings
- Act s.33: a society "may invest or deposit its funds (a) in a Co-operative Bank, or any other bank approved... (b) in any securities issued and guaranteed by the Federal Government; and (c) in any other manner approved by the Committee of the society" — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- ASUU Ojo bye-laws: funds not needed "may be invested in any manner permitted by Law and Regulations and as approved by the Director"; reserve fund to be held in "Current or Savings Accounts or cash, Term Deposit or Federal Government Treasury [bills]"; objects include "investing in stocks but guided by the decision of" the general meeting — [ASUU Ojo Coop Bye-Laws](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- UNN bye-law objects include "To invest in Agricultural products. This will include crop and animal farming" — [UNN Cooperative FAQs](https://www.unn.edu.ng/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/FAQs.pdf)
- CBN COOP "Cooperative Investment" product for serving staff: "Minimum of 5 million and Maximum of N50 million, with a Tenor of 4 Years and an interest rate of 14% per annum"; interest may be paid into COOP savings "which also qualifies for dividend, providing a two-fold return" (self-reported) — [CBN COOP FAQ](https://www.thecbncoop.com/faq)
- CBN COOP offers "financial advisory and wealth management" as a service line — [CBN COOP services](https://thecbncoop.com/service/view/5)
- Unique UNILAG coop: income from "returns on Investment in Fixed Deposit, and landed properties and dividend gains" (snippet, self-reported) — [Unique UNILAG Staff Coop services](https://www.uniqueunilagstaffcoop.org/service/)
- Unique UNILAG coop share issue (3 June–30 Nov 2019): "Five Hundred Million (500,000,000) Units of Ordinary Shares at a Nominal Price of N2.00 per share" payable by cash, salary deduction, equipment loan, savings transfer or dividend set-off — [UNILAG news](https://unilag.edu.ng/?p=4868)
- Total E&P Staff coop reportedly "committed to paying 10% annualized interest on invested funds" in a share sale (snippet; FAQ page returned 403) — [Total Upstream coop shares FAQ](https://www.totalupstreamng.coop/shares-sale-faq/)
- Total E&P coop departments: "Financial Services, Business Development, and Real Estate"; "Financial services & Treasury (loans, investments, financial instruments)" (self-reported) — [stories.coop](https://stories.coop/a-growing-coop-in-nigeria/)
- NLNG coop pays "Annual dividend on share subscriptions" and "Annual interest on cumulative monthly savings" and runs "Collective Investments" (self-reported) — [NLNG COOP About](https://nlng.coop.ng/About)
- INEC coop president pledged to "invest smartly and return huge profits" (2024) — [Gazette Nigeria](https://gazettengr.com/inec-staff-cooperative-records-n34-million-surplus-for-2024/)
- Member attitudes: 75.8% of surveyed members opposed cooperative investment activity; 79.4% were not interested in what executives did with resources "most especially in" investments (RUN survey) — [RUN Repository paper](https://repository.run.edu.ng/bitstreams/3761bb58-bfef-410e-a2d5-2ec08850af24/download)
- Nigeria in WOCCU 2024 data: "40 credit unions, 327,216 members, $41,497,819 in shares/savings" with loans/reserves "NA" (snippet; reflects reporting coverage, not the sector) — [WOCCU 2024 Statistical Report](https://www.woccu.org/documents/2024_Statistical_Report_EN)

### Inferences
- Because s.33(c) is permissive, the binding constraints on investment are the bye-laws, the state Director of Cooperatives' approval, and the management committee — hence the wide dispersion from treasury-only coops to Total E&P's conglomerate.
- The CBN coop's 14% 4-year note (2020-era pricing) and Total's 10% share return are member-facing "deposit" products that fund the loan book and are priced below the 12–21% lending rates, locking in a positive spread.
- Dollar assets, FGN bonds and commercial paper were not mentioned by any named staff coop; the open "any other manner" clause means they are not prohibited, but the ASUU-Ojo reserve-fund clause confines reserves to cash, term deposits and treasury bills.

### Gaps
- No named Nigerian staff cooperative discloses portfolio composition (T-bills vs bonds vs property vs equities) or investment yields in retrievable sources.
- Treatment of real-estate rental income and farming projects within surplus was not documented.

## 5. Member welfare and insurance: group life/burial, HMO, education, retirement payouts — and how funded

### Takeaway
Welfare is mostly embedded in bye-laws (nominee payout on death within two years; waiver of savings during loan repayment; retiree payouts of savings plus dividend) and in dedicated savings sub-accounts (UNN education and retirement savings at 4–5%), rather than in insured products. A 2018 Ibadan survey found only 30.8% of cooperatives had any health budget; large coops (NSC) paid retirees N102m in 2025. Explicit HMO or group-life schemes run by a named Nigerian staff coop were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Act s.18: on death "a registered society may transfer the shares or interest of the deceased" to the nominee; shares/interest "not liable to attachment or sale" (s.17) — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- ASUU Ojo bye-laws: nominee paid "the value of the share or interest, less any sum due to the society" on death/insanity; committee may suspend "ordinary savings during the currency of a loan" where repayment "may impose more hardship"; surety covers "unpaid balance of the loan in the event of death, retirement or termination" — [ASUU Ojo Coop Bye-Laws](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- UNN: "Education Savings (Withdraw-able at the end of each year) - 5%"; "Christmas Savings... - 4%"; Retirement savings (≥10 years) earn "minimum of 4% annual interest"; "Charges on Retirement Savings - 10%" — [UNN Cooperative FAQs](https://www.unn.edu.ng/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/FAQs.pdf)
- NSC coop paid retirees "N102,397,433.39... without deductions" in 2025 and runs a "Children's Education Loan Scheme" — [AnchorNewsOnline](https://anchornewsonline.com/nsc-cooperative-pays-retirees-%E2%82%A6102-4m-declares-%E2%82%A690-6m-dividend-as-new-leadership-takes-over/)
- CBN COOP exit: member receives "his savings balance in the COOP and the accumulated dividend less administrative charge of N5,000" after clearing debts — [CBN COOP FAQ](https://www.thecbncoop.com/faq)
- CBN COOP membership includes "Pensioners, and Retirees" — [CBN COOP About](https://www.thecbncoop.com/about)
- Ibadan study (480 members, data 2018, published 2021): "Only 30.8% of respondents reported their cooperatives had any budget for members' healthcare"; "79.4% had never benefited from cooperative grants for health treatment"; cooperatives "traditionally provide burial funds"; proposed funding split "60% for the government and 40% for the cooperative members" — [IAR Consortium SRJM](https://iarconsortium.org/srjm/47/202/35_Cooperative_Societies_as_Sources_of_Health_Insurance_among_members_in_Ibadan_Nigeria/)
- Group Life Assurance "open to cooperatives and other social organisations" (insurer product page) — [IGI Nigeria](https://www.iginigeria.com/products/life-products/group-life/group-life-assurance/)
- Total E&P coop lists "educational services" among business lines (self-reported) — [stories.coop](https://stories.coop/a-growing-coop-in-nigeria/)
- NNPC retirees: unable to access savings "for approximately 6 years" despite the coop's stated purpose of "assistance, immediate or emergency solutions" (2025) — [Vanguard](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2025/05/nnpc-retirees-cry-out-over-possible-loss-of-savings-with-staff-cooperative/)

### Inferences
- Welfare is financed from (a) the member's own ring-fenced savings pots, (b) the Education Fund (statutory up to 10% of post-reserve surplus; 2.5% at ASUU-Ojo), and (c) loan-write-off via sureties on death — not from insurance premiums; this keeps costs low but leaves retirees exposed to liquidity failures (NNPC case).
- The absence of HMO products in staff coops is consistent with employer-provided HMO cover in formal workplaces, making the coop a secondary welfare layer.

### Gaps
- No Nigerian staff cooperative with a documented HMO group plan or group-life/burial benefit schedule (naira amounts) was found.

## 6. Enterprise ventures: cooperatives owning operating businesses (transport, bakeries, water, schools, event centres, agro-processing, filling stations)

### Takeaway
Operating ventures are concentrated in oil/gas staff cooperatives: Total E&P's coop runs a mart, café, laundry, travel agency, guesthouse, lube bay, event management, e-commerce, educational services and a logistics base; CoopEast and EMCOOP co-own a 101-unit resort estate; NLNG runs real estate and online stores. Bye-laws (UNN, ASUU-Ojo) authorise farming, production, and "new ventures", but no public-sector or university staff coop with a documented transport fleet, bakery, water factory, school, event centre or filling station — with figures — was found.

### Cited Findings
- Total E&P Nigeria Staff Multipurpose Coop: "Coop Mart, Coop Café... Coop Laundry, Coop Travels, Coop Guesthouse, Coop Lube Bay... La Definition (mixed-use development), Logistics Base (warehouse/accommodation)... E-commerce, event management, educational services, supply/procurement"; "Best management team" award; "hired managers... with dedicated revenue departments" (self-reported, c. 2018) — [stories.coop](https://stories.coop/a-growing-coop-in-nigeria/)
- CoopEast Beach Resort (CoopEast 50% / EMCOOP 20% / individuals 30%): N5.6bn, 101 units with "Gym, water treatment plant, six transformers, swimming pool, recreation area, mini-mall, and 24-hour clubhouse" (2022) — [Vanguard](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2022/06/developer-delivers-n5-6bn-residential-estate-for-exxonmobil-shell-cooperatives/)
- NLNG coop has a "Manager, Real Estate & COOP Life" role and "Online stores" (self-reported) — [NLNG COOP About](https://nlng.coop.ng/About)
- UNN bye-law objects: acquire "producer goods and let out these facilities to interested parties"; "invest in Agricultural products... crop and animal farming"; "undertake and invest in new ventures" — [UNN Cooperative FAQs](https://www.unn.edu.ng/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/FAQs.pdf)

### Inferences
- Enterprise ventures appear only where the coop has professional hired management and a captive high-income member market on a company campus/estate (Total, Shell, NLNG); elsewhere "ventures" remain bye-law aspirations.
- Where ventures exist they are member-serving services (retail, laundry, guesthouse) rather than third-party-facing manufacturing, which limits execution risk but also caps surplus.

### Gaps
- No Nigerian staff cooperative with a documented bakery, water factory, school, event centre, filling station or transport fleet (with revenue/profit figures) was retrievable; FIRS, NDIC, NPA, PHCN/DisCo and Lagos State coop searches returned nothing relevant.

## 7. Benchmarks: Nigerian staff-coop economics vs Kenyan employer-based SACCOs and WOCCU credit unions

### Takeaway
Kenyan employer-based SACCOs lend at ~1%/month (≈12% p.a.) reducing balance on 3x–7x deposits and paid 10% interest on deposits plus 13% (Mwalimu) or 16% (Stima) dividends on shares for 2024; the regulated sector averaged a 10.46% share dividend with 8.2% NPLs and 12.5% core capital. Nigerian staff coops lend at anything from 5% (CBN coop) to 21% (CoopEast) on 2x–3x savings, and publish dividends in naira rather than rates; NNPC's collapse shows weaker supervision. Nigeria is almost invisible in WOCCU data (40 reporting credit unions, 327k members, $41m savings) versus Kenya's 3.8m members and $8.3bn assets.

### Cited Findings
- Mwalimu National SACCO 2024: net interest income +35% to KSh3.2bn; loans +4.8% to KSh47.1bn; total assets +3.7% to KSh68.9bn; "Interest on Member deposits: 10% [2023: 10%]"; "Dividends Rates on Member's Share Capital: 13% [2023: 11.5%]" — [Mwango Capital on X](https://x.com/MwangoCapital/status/1904794285024924038)
- Mwalimu BOSA loan pricing: "Normal Loan: 1% per month, equivalent to 12% per annum; Development Loan: 1.25% per month"; deposits historically earned "10.5% and 11.5% per annum" — [PayBillKE guide](https://paybillke.com/guides/mwalimu-national-sacco-loans-kenya-2026); [The Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/business/mwalimu-national-sacco-membership-requirements-savings-loan-options-and-interest/)
- Stima SACCO FY2024: "dividend of 16% per share on fully paid-up shares and an 11% interest rebate"; total payout KSh4.6bn (2023: KSh4.1bn) — [Terryanne Chebet on X](https://x.com/TerryanneChebet/status/1896556814801444983)
- Kenyan SACCO norm: "lend 3x to 7x your share deposits at around 1% per month on a reducing balance" — [PesaCalc](https://pesacalc.co.ke/calculators/sacco-loan); [HavenKenya](https://havenkenya.com/10-best-saccos-in-kenya-with-the-lowest-loan-interest-rates/)
- SASRA 2024 sector report: total assets KSh1.08trn (+10.1%); lending KSh380bn (+10%); NPL 8.2% (2023: 7.5%); average share-capital dividend 10.46% (2023: 10.92%; DT-SACCOs 10.54%, NWDT 10.37%); core capital ratio 12.5% vs 10% minimum; liquidity ratio 18% (from 22%); membership 6.84m–7.39m; average cost of borrowings 9.8% — [Sacco Review](https://saccoreview.co.ke/unpacking-sasra-supervision-report-2024-a-sector-in-resilient-expansion/); [The Cooperator News](https://thecooperator.news/sacco-supervision-report-2024-unveiled-in-nairobi-as-total-assets-hit-ksh-1-08trn/)
- WOCCU 2024: 412.7m members, 67,137 credit unions, >$3.8trn assets in 101 countries; Africa: 27 countries, 29,181 credit unions, 46.4m members, $12.5bn savings, $13.1bn loans, $13.6bn assets; Kenya ~3.8m members, $8.34bn assets, $5.83bn loans; Nigeria "40 credit unions, 327,216 members, $41,497,819 in shares/savings" (snippet) — [WOCCU 2024 Statistical Report](https://www.woccu.org/documents/2024_Statistical_Report_EN); [The Star infographic](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/infographics/2026-09-29-global-country-performance-of-credit-unions-saccos-2024)
- Nigerian comparators (from sections above): CBN coop 3–12.5% p.a. (2020), CoopEast 18–21% p.a. plus 2–2.5% flat/month short-term, UNN 11–19%, UNILAG 10%, ASUU-Ojo 5–15% band, FRCN 8.5–20%; multiples 2x–3x (5x short-term at CBN coop) — [CBN COOP](https://www.thecbncoop.com/service/view/1); [CoopEast](https://www.coopeastngr.com/loanscheme.asp); [UNN](https://www.unn.edu.ng/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/FAQs.pdf); [ASUU Ojo](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf); [FRCN Lagos](https://www.frcnlagoscms.org/web/home/update2.php?idupdate=2)

### Inferences
- Kenyan SACCOs are more leveraged on member deposits (3x–7x vs Nigeria's 2x–3x) and price loans tightly around 12% p.a. because deposits are remunerated at ~10%; Nigerian coops either under-price loans (CBN coop, cross-subsidised) or price at bank-like 18–21% (CoopEast) in a 2024–26 environment of 25–30% policy rates, so the real return on savings is probably negative for many Nigerian members, whereas Kenyan members earned 10% on deposits plus 13–16% on shares against ~5% inflation.
- Prudential disclosure (NPL 8.2%, capital 12.5%, liquidity 18%) exists in Kenya because of SASRA; Nigeria's state-level Director-of-Cooperatives model produces no comparable published ratios, which is why no Nigerian default or cost-to-income figures were found.

### Gaps
- Cost-to-income ratios for Mwalimu/Stima and for any Nigerian coop were not found.
- Harambee and Unaitas SACCO figures were not retrieved.
- Nigeria-specific WOCCU loan/reserve data is "NA" in the 2024 report.
