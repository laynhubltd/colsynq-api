# Market size and economic value of staff/workplace cooperatives and cooperative finance in Nigeria, with African and global benchmarks

Research date: 8 October 2026. All naira figures nominal. Where a figure is repeated across years without a new primary release it is flagged as STALE/REPEATED. Nigeria has no consolidated prudential regulator for cooperatives (36 states + FCT register and supervise; the Federal Department of Cooperatives sits in the Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security), so almost all national totals are ministerial estimates rather than audited aggregates.

---

## Key Question 1 — Nigeria: how many registered cooperatives, members, savings/assets exist, who publishes it, how reliable?

### Takeaway
The only national headline numbers are federal ministerial estimates — "over 370,000 registered cooperative societies, more than 30 million members, cooperative assets of about ₦1.7 trillion" (Minister of State for Agriculture, 2025–2026) — and they are not backed by any published audited aggregate; the government itself concedes no national registry exists and is only now (Aug 2026 MoU) building one. State-level counts exist for a few states (Lagos 16,000+; Oyo 7,865) and the historical federal CODAS database captured just 19,786 societies, illustrating how far official headline numbers diverge from verified counts.

### Inferences
- The ₦1.7trn "cooperative assets" figure is ~0.6× the microfinance-bank sector's ₦2.8trn assets (June 2024) and ~45% of banks' ₦3.78trn outstanding consumer credit (2025) — i.e. cooperatives are plausibly the second-largest retail credit channel after banks, but the figure is unaudited.
- The jump from 19,786 (CODAS) and ~30,000 (aggregator) to 370,000 societies most likely reflects summing cumulative state registers (including dormant societies) rather than active, filing cooperatives; Lagos' own 2,359 "revalidated" vs 16,000 "registered" ratio (~15%) suggests the active share nationally could be a small fraction of 370,000.
- If 30m members and ₦1.7trn assets are both taken at face value, average assets per member are ~₦57,000 (~US$35–40 at 2026 rates) — far below Kenyan SACCO averages (see KQ5), consistent with most Nigerian cooperatives being small thrift groups.

### Gaps
- No published national time series 2015–2026 for number of societies, members, savings or assets; the federal government has not published a statistical bulletin on cooperatives.
- No state-level figures found for Kano, Rivers or FCT (Rivers and Kano ministry sites list functions only; FCT only via ABCOF's 60 member unions).
- The ₦1.7trn figure's methodology (assets vs turnover, year, coverage) is undisclosed; VON article that carried it is now offline.
- Specific EFInA percentage for "co-operative" usage in 2023 could not be reliably read from the chart text (values garbled in extraction); EFInA questionnaire does ask about cooperatives, so the micro-data at a2f.ng/dataset could yield it.
- No NBS publication on cooperatives found.

## Key Question 2 — Staff/employee cooperatives: number, membership, assets; disclosed balance-sheet figures of named cooperatives

### Takeaway
There is no official count or aggregate for staff/workplace cooperatives in Nigeria; evidence is confined to self-disclosed figures from a handful of large oil/gas and public-sector cooperatives. The largest disclosed are TotalEnergies' staff cooperative (₦66.3bn assets, ~1,400–1,500 members) and NLNG's (₦23bn, 1,315 members, 2024); public-sector staff cooperatives (CBN, NNPC, Immigration, Shippers' Council, NAN) are much larger in membership but disclose only surpluses in the tens or hundreds of millions.

### Cited Findings

**Oil & gas staff cooperatives (largest per-member balance sheets)**
- TotalEnergies E&P Nigeria Staff Multipurpose Cooperative Society: "total assets of over ₦66.3 billion"; business segments banking (savings and member loans) and real estate; most recent dividend ₦0.92 per share (2022); "over 1,400 members" — [totalupstreamng.coop (via search)](https://www.totalupstreamng.coop/about-us/our-history/). Earlier disclosures: assets over ₦32.8bn and 1,427 members (Dec 2017); 2017 profit ₦2.25bn; profit after tax above ₦1bn for 2–3 consecutive years; NPL 0.157% over five years; later "over N45 billion" assets and 1,496 members; registered 24 Mar 1988 (LSCS 589), operations since 6 June 1984 — [stories.coop](https://stories.coop/a-growing-coop-in-nigeria/). Trajectory: ₦32.8bn (2017) → ₦45bn (c.2019–20) → ₦66.3bn (c.2022–24).
- NLNG Staff Cooperative: "total asset base of more than N23 Billion as at year-end 2024"; 1,315 members (Feb 2025); registered 28 Aug 2003; pays annual dividend on shares and interest on cumulative monthly savings; IFRS reporting — [NLNG Coop](https://nlng.coop.ng/About)
- NNPC Staff Cooperative Multipurpose Society (Lagos): founded 1979 with ~20 members; current membership 7,606; 40th AGM annual report exists but figures not on site — [NNPC Coop](https://nnpccoop.coop/)

**Public-sector staff cooperatives**
- CBN Staff Multi-Purpose Cooperative Society: "over 9,000 members spread across all the States"; began 1982, re-registered 29 Oct 1997 (FCT reg. 1001), re-certified 2019 (reg. 0015); no financials disclosed on site — [CBN Coop](https://www.thecbncoop.com/about)
- Nigerian Shippers' Council Staff Multipurpose Cooperative Society: net surplus ₦181.22m (FY2025) vs ₦156.41m (FY2024); dividend ₦90.61m; retirement savings benefits paid ₦102.4m; 30th AGM — [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/shippers-council-cooperative-posts-n181-2m-surplus-declares-n90-6m-dividend/); [Tribune](https://tribuneonlineng.com/nsc-cooperative-declares-n181-2m-surplus-unveils-new-leadership/)

- FRCN Lagos Multipurpose Cooperative, LASCOFIS, Lion Multipurpose (UNN staff), Niger Delta University Workers Cooperative exist with websites but publish no aggregate figures — [FRCN Lagos](https://www.frcnlagoscms.org/); [UNN](https://www.unn.edu.ng/lion-multi-purpose-cooperative-society-nigeria-limited/); [NDU Coop](https://nducoop.com/AGM)

**Housing by staff cooperatives (indicative of balance-sheet scale)**
- FMBN Cooperative Housing Development Loan: Kuje, Abuja estate of 154 units (first FMBN-funded cooperative estate); WoodHill estate via ₦997m loan to Akacare Multipurpose Cooperative Society, first phase 77 units — [Guardian Property](https://guardian.ng/property/fg-launches-national-cooperative-housing-development-scheme/)
- Abuja "Cooperative City" project: 10,000 affordable homes planned for federal workers — [Nigeria Housing Market](https://www.nigeriahousingmarket.com/news/10000-affordable-homes-planned-for-federal-workers-under-abuja-cooperative-city-project)

### Inferences
- Per-member assets: TotalEnergies ≈ ₦44–47m/member; NLNG ≈ ₦17.5m/member; NAN ≈ ₦0.5bn savings total (members not disclosed). Oil & gas staff coops are outliers; typical civil-service coops have savings per member likely in the ₦100k–₦1m range (NAN's ₦438.69m savings over a newsroom-sized staff implies roughly ₦0.5–1m per member).
- Kenya's SASRA data show employer-based ("government-based") SACCOs hold ~65% of regulated SACCO assets with 37% of members (see KQ5); by analogy, staff/payroll-deduction cooperatives probably account for a majority of Nigerian cooperative financial assets despite being a small minority of the 370,000 societies — but this is inference, not measured.

### Gaps
- No source gives the number of staff/employee cooperatives in Nigeria, their membership, or aggregate assets; no regulator distinguishes employer-based from community cooperatives.
- No balance-sheet figures found for FIRS, NPA, Chevron, Shell/SPDC, Customs, or teachers' cooperatives, nor for CBN Coop or NNPC Coop (annual reports exist but are not online in extractable form).
- TotalEnergies' ₦66.3bn asset figure is undated on the site (likely FY2022–2024).

## Key Question 3 — Context: microfinance banks, bank consumer lending, digital lenders

### Takeaway
Nigeria's ~900 microfinance banks held ₦2.8trn assets, ₦1.3trn deposits and ₦1.3trn net loans at June 2024 (up 91% y/y, largely due to fintech-owned MFBs), while banks' outstanding consumer credit fell 19.9% to ₦3.78trn in 2025; the ministerial ₦1.7trn cooperative-asset estimate therefore sits between the MFB sector and bank consumer credit in scale, and well above the formal lending that most households actually access (consumer credit is only 6.6% of private-sector credit).

### Cited Findings
- MFB industry (CBN data cited by Agusto): total assets ₦2.8trn at 30 June 2024, +91.3% from ₦1.5trn a year earlier; deposit liabilities ₦1.3trn (+168%), funding 44.7% of assets; net loans and advances ₦1.3trn (+27.5% y/y); "macroeconomic headwinds especially inflation significantly constrained growth"; MPR 27.25% (Feb 2025) — [Agusto 2025 Microfinance Industry Report](https://www.agustoresearch.com/report/2025-nigerias-microfinance-industry-report/)
- NDIC: 880 insured MFBs at 31 Dec 2022; MFB total deposits ₦507.63bn, insured deposits ₦299.66bn (Dec 2022); current MFB coverage limit ₦2,000,000 per depositor — [NDIC facts (via search)](https://ndic.gov.ng/resources/facts-and-figures/facts-about-deposit-insurance-for-mfbs-pmis/). NOTE: deposits ₦507.63bn (Dec 2022) → ₦1.3trn (June 2024) implies the sector more than doubled in 18 months, consistent with fintech MFBs (Moniepoint, OPay, PalmPay etc.) booking deposits.
- "Over 900 licensed MFBs" at end-2024 — [Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/0307-57031-nigeria-revokes-46-microfinance-bank-licenses-over-regulatory-breaches); CBN revoked 46 MFB licences effective 1 July 2026 (insufficient assets, inactivity, failure to meet minimum capital) — [Channels TV](https://www.channelstv.com/2026/07/01/cbn-revokes-operating-licences-of-46-microfinance-banks-full-list/); NDIC began paying up to ₦2m per depositor of the 46 banks — [Pulse](https://www.pulse.ng/story/ndic-begins-paying-up-to-naira2-million-to-customers-of-46-failed-microfinance-banks-2026072305304484998)
- CBN credit data (Sept 2024): MFBs accounted for 95.66% of new debtor records (6,253 of 6,537) vs 4.34% from deposit money banks; total value ₦118.73bn — [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/money/1634486-microfinance-banks-get-95-loans-2024-other-banks-left-with-5-cbn/)
- Bank consumer credit (CBN 2025 Annual Report): outstanding consumer credit ₦3.78trn at end-2025, -19.89% from ₦4.72trn (2024), first decline since Dec 2019; personal loans ₦1.85trn (48.84%), retail loans ₦1.94trn (51.16%, +63.77%); consumer credit = 6.60% of private-sector credit (7.98% in 2024); MPR 26.50%; CBN attributed the fall to "the dynamic interest rate environment" — [Nairametrics, 29 July 2026](http://nairametrics.com/2026/07/29/nigerias-consumer-credit-falls-to-n3-8-trillion-first-decline-in-six-years/); [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/business-services/banks-flag-13117-bvns-for-fraud-as-consumer-credit-falls-to-n3-8-trillion/)
- CONFLICT/definition note: CBN monthly data put "personal loans" at ₦7.52trn in March 2024 (+329% y/y) — [Kolaking/Punch summary](https://kolaking.substack.com/p/lending-apps-surge-80-as-personal) — which is inconsistent with the ₦4.72trn year-end 2024 consumer-credit total in the Annual Report; different CBN series/definitions, flag when using.
- Private-sector credit ₦84.55trn (Aug 2026) — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/09/21/nigerias-private-sector-credit-rises-for-third-straight-month-to-n84-55-trillion-in-august/)
- Digital lenders: FCCPC-approved digital lenders rose to 425 by May 2025 (362 full, 42 conditional) from 320 a year earlier; lenders reported Nigerians "quadrupled" loan applications in 2024 — [Nairametrics, May 2025](https://nairametrics.com/2025/05/15/approved-digital-lenders-in-nigeria-surge-to-425-amid-concerns-over-rising-indebtedness/); regulation suspended by a Federal High Court interim injunction of 15 Apr 2026; reports of a 505-app list were denied by FCCPC (June 2026) — [PM News](https://pmnewsnigeria.com/2026/06/28/fccpc-denies-expanding-nigerias-licensed-loan-app-list/)
- Household savings stress: PiggyVest survey — only 57% of Nigerians saved any money in 2024 vs 64% in 2023 — [FinRemit summary](https://www.finremit.co.uk/navigating-nigerias-financial-landscape-in-2025/)

### Inferences
- Positioning: MFB assets ₦2.8trn (mid-2024) > bank consumer credit ₦3.78trn (2025, loans only) ≈ 2× claimed cooperative assets ₦1.7trn. If cooperatives' claimed 30m members is right, they reach more adults than MFBs' depositor base, but with much smaller balances.
- The 2025 contraction in bank consumer credit at 26–27% MPR increases relative demand for cooperative loans (typically 1–2% per month, payroll-secured), supporting anecdotal reports of rising cooperative loan demand — but no quantified cooperative loan-book series exists to confirm this.

### Gaps
- No NDIC 2024 Annual Report figures for MFB NPLs, number of MFBs, or Dec-2024 balance sheet were retrievable (report exists at ndic.gov.ng but is PDF-only).
- No CBN or NDIC statement found about cooperatives' size relative to MFBs; CBN does not collect cooperative data.

## Key Question 4 — Growth and trends 2015–2026: digitisation, bank/fintech partnerships, CBN policy on financial cooperatives, inflation/devaluation effects

### Takeaway
The dominant 2024–2026 trend is state-led formalisation: a national digital registry (Seamfix MoU, Aug 2026), a proposed ₦200bn Cooperative Bank of Nigeria 65%-owned by cooperatives, and repeal/re-enactment of the Cooperative Societies Act — alongside a crowded private cooperative-software market (SmartTeller, Coopra, Cooperativ, Coopify, CloudCoop/Sterling) and Lagos' ₦10bn LASMECO fund. Inflation (33% in Mar 2024, ~35% at end-2024) is reported by cooperatives themselves as the main headwind to savings, while the data show the sector still posting nominal surpluses.

### Cited Findings

**Policy / institutional**
- Cooperative Bank of Nigeria: ₦200bn share-capital mobilisation targeting 10,000 cooperative societies across 36 states + FCT; larger societies asked for ₦21m–₦50m each — Minister Abdullahi, North-West zonal tour, Kaduna (2026) — [MSME Africa](https://msmeafricaonline.com/fg-launches-n200bn-capital-mobilization-drive-for-proposed-cooperative-bank/); [FMINO](https://fmino.gov.ng/fg-launches-n200-billion-mobilization-campaign-for-cooperative-bank/)
- Proposed ownership: 65% controlling equity for cooperative societies/unions/federations/individual cooperators, 30% non-controlling for private institutional investors, 5% employees — [AllAfrica, 18 May 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202605180292.html); [Paradigm News](https://paradigmnews.ng/36553-2/); [VON](https://von.gov.ng/nigeria-launches-cooperative-digitalisation-bank-reform-initiative/)
- Share subscription for the bank targeted to begin end-October 2026 — [The Cooperator](https://thecooperator.news/nigeria-moves-to-digitalise-cooperative-sector-as-government-launches-national-identity-system/)
- CBN's 2005 Microfinance Policy already provided a "Government Sponsored Co-operative Model" for a state to help a cooperative set up a Unit MFB (75:25 state:cooperative shareholding, repaid within 5 years) — [CBN Microfinance Policy](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/OUT/PUBLICATIONS/GUIDELINES/DFD/2006/MICROFINANCE%20POLICY.PDF). Under BOFIA 2020, deposit-taking from the public without a CBN licence is illegal; cooperatives may take member savings only — [Legal500 summary](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/technology/cbn-guidelines-on-digital-lending-in-nigeria-legal-obligations-for-fintechs-and-lenders)
- NPF Microfinance Bank (ex-Police cooperative-linked) and LAPO MFB are historical examples of cooperative/NGO lenders converting to CBN-licensed MFBs — [Wikipedia NPF MFB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPF_Microfinance_Bank); [Wikipedia LAPO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAPO_Microfinance_Bank)

**Digitisation / software / bank partnerships**
- Sterling Bank partnered with Lagos State Cooperative Federation (LASCOFED), CloudCoop Technologies and the Corporate Affairs Commission to digitise cooperatives across 28 states: cooperatives open a Sterling corporate account, host data on a cloud Cooperative Management Platform and integrate with NIBSS — [search summary citing Sterling](https://sterling.ng/about/)

**Macro shock 2023–2026**
- Headline inflation 33.20% in March 2024 vs 21.09% in March 2023; peaked ~35% in Dec 2024 (28-year high) — [Proshare](https://www.proshare.co/articles/nigeria-march-2024-inflation-rises-to-33.20-the-options-for-households?menu=Economy&classification=Read&category=Nigeria+Economy); [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/nigeria-reduces-inflation-rate-but-the-cost-of-living-remains-high-heres-why-251073)

- Research on Lagos cooperatives (2020–2025) finds employment instability and irregular income significantly increase loan-default risk and that defaults "eroded capital reserves and member equity" — [MAJAF](https://majaf.com.ng/index.php/majaf/article/view/306); Awka-South study on inflation and cooperative savings — [IJRISS](https://rsisinternational.org/journals/ijriss/articles/impact-of-inflation-on-savings-and-investments-of-cooperative-societies-in-awka-south-local-government-area/)
- Fraud risk to the sector's brand: Baraza Multipurpose Cooperative (Bayelsa, 2021) — 30,000+ investors, founder absconded with ~₦40bn; SEC says Nigerians lost ₦316bn to Ponzi schemes; schemes are sometimes "dressed as ... cooperative societies"; ISA 2025 imposes 10-year jail/₦20m fine — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/news/article/ponzi-schemes-6-biggest-frauds-that-scammed-nigerians/); [Pulse](https://www.pulse.ng/story/worst-ponzi-schemes-in-nigeria-2025110106084446283)

### Inferences
- Nominal growth: Lagos' reported capital base rose ₦32bn (2019) → ₦33bn (2021), i.e. ~1.5% a year nominal — effectively a real decline given inflation. Named staff cooperatives, by contrast, grew assets fast in nominal terms (TotalEnergies ₦32.8bn → ₦66.3bn, ~2× over c.5–7 years), suggesting bifurcation between payroll-deduction staff coops (resilient) and community thrift coops (squeezed).
- Naira devaluation (₦460→₦1,500+/US$ over 2023–24) means the sector's dollar value roughly halved: ₦1.7trn ≈ US$1.1bn at ~₦1,500/US$, vs Kenya's regulated SACCO assets ≈ US$8.3bn.
- The Cooperative Bank proposal mirrors Rwanda's Umurenge SACCO → Cooperative Bank consolidation (see KQ5) and would, if capitalised, become the first national prudential channel for cooperative liquidity.

### Gaps
- No quantified data on cooperative savings/loan trends 2023–2026 (no regulator series); only anecdotal and single-cooperative evidence.
- No specific Wema or Access Bank cooperative product details found; Sterling is the only bank with a documented cooperative platform partnership.
- No CBN directive found specifically requiring large financial cooperatives to convert to MFBs (the 2005 cooperative-MFB model is voluntary); claims of such a "push" could not be sourced.

## Key Question 5 — Benchmarks: Kenya SASRA, Ghana CUA, Rwanda Umurenge SACCOs, South Africa CFIs, WOCCU global/Africa, Mondragón/Rabobank/Desjardins

### Takeaway
Kenya's 355–357 regulated SACCOs hold KSh1.08trn (US$8.3bn) assets and 7.4m members (2024), rising to KSh1.21trn by Dec 2025, with employer/"government-based" SACCOs holding 64.5% of assets — roughly 7–8× the claimed asset base of Nigeria's entire cooperative sector in dollar terms; Ghana (495 CUs, 2.2m members, US$589m assets) and Rwanda (416 SACCOs, 4–5m members, ~US$390m assets) are smaller but far better measured. ## Key Question 6 — Economic effect: GDP contribution, financial inclusion, housing delivery, employment

### Takeaway
No credible estimate of Nigerian cooperatives' GDP contribution exists — the federal government's own 2026 reform agenda lists "capturing the true value of the cooperative sector in GDP" as a future goal — and the only quantified effects are indirect: informal-provider usage (incl. cooperatives) rose to 46% of adults in 2023 (EFInA), and cooperative housing via FMBN has delivered hundreds (not thousands) of units per scheme, with one study finding only 27% of housing-cooperative members achieved their housing goal.

### Cited Findings
- Government aim to capture cooperatives in GDP computation (implies not currently measured) — [FMINO, Oct 2026](https://fmino.gov.ng/cooperative-digitalisation-fg-reaffirms-commitment-towards-national-cooperative-identity-reform-under-rh-crrp-2030-tasks-states-on-implementation/)
- Academic literature frames cooperatives inside the MSME sector: SMEDAN/NBS — 37,067,416 MSMEs employing 59,741,211 persons (84.02% of labour force), ~48.47% of nominal GDP (2017 survey figures, STALE) — [ResearchGate review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343725915_Cooperative_Societies_and_Microenterprise_Financing_in_Nigeria_A_Literature_Approach); state-level studies (Kogi, Ekiti, Kaduna) report member-income effects but no sector GDP figure — [AJOL](https://www.ajol.info/index.php/ngjsd/article/download/274951/259534/646437); [IIARD Kogi](https://iiardjournals.org/abstract?+Nigeria=&comma=&id=1546&j=IJEBM&pn=Contributions+of+Co-Operative+Societies+to+Economic+Development+in+Kogi+State)
- Financial inclusion: informal uptake 46% of adults (2023) vs 33% (2020); informal-only reliance fell to 10% — [EFInA A2F 2023](https://efina.org.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/A2F-2023-Event-Day-Presentation-Version4-1.pdf); NFIU national risk assessment lists "financial cooperatives, self-help groups, trade associations and credit unions" as non-bank MFIs in the inclusion strategy — [NFIU](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/images/Downloads/downloads/financialinclusion.pdf)
- Housing: FMBN funded 6,911 units in 2025 (vs 2,165 in 2024; +300%), project-loan disbursements ₦79bn (2025) vs ₦31.5bn (2024), NHF collections ₦152bn (2025, +48%); products include Cooperative Housing Development Loans but no cooperative breakdown given — [ThisDay, 16 July 2026](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/07/16/fmbn-6911-housing-units-funded-in-2025-300-increase-recorded/); [NAN](https://nannews.ng/2026/02/19/fmbn-recorded-%E2%82%A6152bn-nhf-collections-representing-48-growth-in-2025-osidi/); FMBN cumulative disbursement ₦455.132bn over 42 years — [Tribune](https://tribuneonlineng.com/housing-loan-fmbn-disburses-n455-132bn-in-42-years/)
- Cooperative housing specifics: Kuje cooperative estate 154 units; WoodHill (Akacare coop) ₦997m loan, 77 units phase 1 — [Guardian Property](https://guardian.ng/property/fg-launches-national-cooperative-housing-development-scheme/); Police cooperative ₦4.74bn approved for 606 units — [Economic Times NG](https://theeconomictimes.com.ng/2026/01/29/fmbn-disburses-over-%E2%82%A67bn-in-mortgages-cooperative-housing-for-police-officers-osidi/); "only 27.2% of cooperative members successfully achieving their housing objectives" (Lagos study) — [IJRISS 2026](https://rsisinternational.org/journals/ijriss/uploads/vol10-iss6-pg3334-3360-202606_pdf.pdf); housing cooperatives "struggle to attract low-income earners" — [Guardian](https://guardian.ng/property/why-housing-cooperatives-struggle-to-attract-low-income-earners/)
- Global reference: ICA/minister — >1bn people belong to cooperatives globally; agricultural cooperatives ≈35% of global cooperative assets/turnover — [Paradigm News](https://paradigmnews.ng/36553-2/); [agriculture.gov.ng](https://agriculture.gov.ng/a-revamped-cooperative-sector-will-ensure-economic-empowerment-and-prosperity-for-nigerians-sen-abdullahi/)

### Inferences
- Even taking the ministerial ₦1.7trn at face value, cooperative assets are roughly 0.5–0.6% of Nigeria's ~₦300trn+ nominal GDP (2025) — not a macro-material sector by balance sheet, though potentially material by headcount (30m claimed members ≈ 25% of adults).
- Cooperative housing output is in the low thousands of units cumulatively against a 20m+ unit housing deficit — a negligible share of delivery.

- No EFInA cross-tab isolating cooperative members' borrowing/saving rates found.
- No cumulative count of housing units delivered by cooperatives nationally.
