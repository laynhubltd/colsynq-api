# Staff (Workplace) Cooperative Societies in Nigeria: Formation, Registration and Governance

Research date: 8 October 2026. All naira figures and rules carry the date of the source they were taken from. "Act" below means the Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act, Cap. N98 LFN 2004 (originally Decree No. 90 of 1993, commenced 26 August 1993). The federal Act text was read directly from the FAO FAOLEX copy; state practice is drawn from state ministry pages and law-firm explainers and is flagged as such.

A note on the legal architecture that frames everything below: the Act lets both the President (Federal Director of Co-operatives) and each State Governor (State Director of Co-operatives) appoint a Director and confer the Act's powers on them by Gazette notice (s.1(1)-(2)), which is why in practice registration is done at state level (Lagos: Ministry of Commerce, Cooperatives, Trade and Investment; FCT: Office of the Chief Registrar of Co-operatives, FCTA) while the Federal Department of Cooperatives (now under the Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security) sets national policy. Several states (notably Lagos, 2022) have enacted their own cooperative laws that operate alongside the federal Act. — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act, FAOLEX](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)

## Key Question 1: Registration steps, documents, fees, minimum members and timelines (Lagos, FCT, other states)

### Takeaway
The federal Act sets a floor of 10 qualified persons for a primary society and a nominal ₦100 fee, but state practice differs sharply: Lagos requires 20 founding members, an inaugural meeting witnessed by the Ministry, trustee induction at the Lagos State Cooperative College and a 3-month monitoring period before the certificate issues; the FCT issues a 3-year "Recognition Letter" (provisional status) before a full Certificate of Registration, with a published ₦25,000 processing fee (Dec 2025). No official fee schedule was found online for any state.

### Cited Findings

**Federal Act text (statutory floor)**
- A primary society may be registered if it "consists of at least ten persons each of whom is qualified for membership under section 22" (s.3(1)); an industrial society needs a minimum of six persons and must be economically viable (s.3(2)); a secondary society needs at least five registered societies as members (s.3(3)). — [Act s.3, FAOLEX](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- The word "Co-operative" must form part of the name and "limited" must be the last word; "bank"/"banking" may not appear in the name except for a central financing society (s.3(4)-(6)). — [Act s.3](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Statutory registration fee: "N100 or such other sum as may be prescribed by the Minister or Commissioner" (s.3(8)) — i.e. states set the actual fee. — [Act s.3(8)](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Application is made to the Director, signed by at least ten individuals qualified for membership (primary society), accompanied by the number of copies of proposed bye-laws the Director prescribes; the Director may alter the proposed bye-laws to bring them into conformity with the Act (s.4). — [Act s.4](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- The Director "shall within 60 days dispose of an application for registration"; refusal may be appealed to the Minister/Commissioner within 60 days of notification (s.5(2)-(3)). — [Act s.5](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Registration makes the society a body corporate with perpetual succession and a common seal, able to hold property, contract and sue (s.6(1)); its affairs are managed by a committee appointed by members (s.6(2)); the certificate of registration signed and sealed by the Director is conclusive evidence of registration (s.7). — [Act ss.6-7](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Membership qualification for a primary society: age 16+, and residence/occupation of land within the area of operation, except that the land requirement does not apply to a credit society; the Director may grant exemptions (s.22). — [Act s.22](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- A registered society must keep a register of members (prima facie evidence of dates of joining/leaving, s.8), a registered address with notice of changes to the Director (s.10), and a copy of the Act, regulations, bye-laws and list of members open for free inspection at its address (s.13). — [Act ss.8, 10, 13](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Registered societies are exempt from stamp duties, instrument-registration fees and companies income tax (s.20). — [Act s.20](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)

**Lagos State (state practice)**
- Lagos State's Department of Cooperative Services (Ministry of Commerce, Cooperatives, Trade and Investment, MCCTI) states eligibility as: members 18+, "good character and sound mental capacity", **minimum 20 founding members**, a shared common bond (profession, trade, employment) and a verifiable secretariat address; the process is (1) formal application to the Permanent Secretary, (2) hold and document inaugural meeting, (3) pay registration fees, (4) trustees attend induction at Lagos State Cooperative College, (5) purchase statutory record books, (6) three-month monitoring period, (7) receive Certificate of Registration and registered bye-laws. The governing law is cited as the "Lagos State Cooperative Societies Law, 2022"; Lagos has "over 16,000 registered cooperative societies" served through 24 Area Offices; the Department has Registration, Inspectorate, Mediation, Insurance and Monitoring/Enforcement units. Fee amounts are not published on the page. — [Lagos MCCTI, Department of Cooperative Services](https://mccti.lagosstate.gov.ng/departments/cooperatives)
- Law-firm explainer (Mondaq, 12 May 2023): at least 20 members aged 18+; application letter to the Permanent Secretary enclosing letter of intent, application for registration, invitation to the inaugural meeting, objectives and a copy of the bye-laws; inaugural meeting held with a Ministry representative present and minutes submitted as proof; payment of registration and trustee-training fees; trustees (President, Secretary, Treasurer) attend induction at Lagos State Cooperative College; 3-month monitoring; certificate issued; post-registration the society must keep statutory books (cash book, general ledger), prepare annual statements of account and returns, and hold regular and annual general meetings. — [Mondaq, Registering a Cooperative Society in Lagos State (12 May 2023)](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1314598/registering-a-cooperative-society-in-lagos-state)
- Pavestones Legal (28 Apr 2023) gives the same sequence and confirms: "the cooperative will be monitored for a period of 3 months by the Ministry, after which, (where satisfied) a certificate of registration will be issued". Fee amounts not disclosed. — [Pavestones Legal](https://pavestoneslegal.com/registering-a-cooperative-society-in-lagos-state/)
- Eko Solicitors (6 Nov 2025) lists different document specifics: four copies of bye-laws signed by the interim management committee, minutes of the inaugural meeting showing the formation resolution and committee election, a register of members (names, addresses, occupations, share contributions), payment receipts for name reservation and registration, and surety forms if required; steps include name reservation/availability check and completion of "Form A and Form B". It cites "Cooperative Societies Law, Cap. C15, Laws of Lagos State" and quotes a 10-member minimum (which matches the federal Act, not the Ministry's stated 20). — [Eko Solicitors](https://ekosolicitors.com/how-to-obtain-registration-of-cooperative-societies-in-lagos-what-you-need-to-know/)
- Governor Sanwo-Olu signed the law "to provide for the registration of cooperative societies in Lagos State" on 7 February 2022; Lagos cooperative capital base was reported at ₦33 billion in 2021 (up from ₦32 billion in 2019). — [Nairametrics, 8 Feb 2022](https://nairametrics.com/2022/02/08/lagos-governor-signs-bills-to-regulate-real-estate-provide-for-registration-of-cooperative-societies/)
- Lagos inaugurated a Cooperative Dispute Resolution Panel in November 2022 "pursuant to section 60 of the Lagos State Cooperatives Societies Law, 2022" (page could not be fetched; relying on search-result text). — [Lagos MCIC, 26 Nov 2022](http://mcic.lagosstate.gov.ng/2022/11/26/lagos-inaugurates-cooperative-dispute-resolution-panel/)
- An older explainer (LawCare Nigeria, 14 Aug 2019) describes a "letter of recognition" that permits operation "for three years" before a certificate issues, with four copies of bye-laws and letters of consent from all members, and a premises inspection. This 3-year recognition model matches the FCT/federal practice rather than the current Lagos 3-month model. — [LawCare Nigeria](https://lawcarenigeria.com/how-to-register-a-cooperative-society/)

**FCT Abuja (state-equivalent practice)**
- Registration office: Directorate of Cooperative Registration, FCDA Premises, Area 11, Garki, Abuja. Documents: certified copy of the formation resolution, feasibility study report, four copies of proposed bye-laws, letters of intent from prospective members, proof of fee payment, one passport photo per trustee. Application signed by at least ten eligible individuals. Fee: **processing fee ₦25,000** (plus ₦150,000–₦250,000 if a law firm is engaged). Process: pay before submission, submit to the Director General, await assessment and a **Recognition Letter valid for three years**, then "after three years, receive Certificate of Registration if requirements met". Eligibility quoted as members 15+, board members 18+. (Article dated 25 Dec 2025.) — [ICA Legal, How to register a co-operative society in Abuja FCT](https://ica.ng/how-to-register-a-co-operative-society-in-abuja-fct-requirements-costs-and-procedure/)
- The FCT Agriculture and Rural Development Secretariat announced (June 2022) that all FCT cooperatives must be recertified at the Chief Registrar's Office, Area 11, citing societies operating without authorisation from the Chief Registrar, poor financial records, no fixed addresses, officials overstaying tenure and "maladministration and sharp practices"; non-recertified societies face de-registration and corrupt officials referral to the EFCC. The recertification exercise began in 2018. — [Abuja Digest, 4 June 2022](http://abujadigest.info/2022/06/why-all-cooperative-societies-in-fct-must-be-recertified-secretary/)
- The NFIU Staff Multi-Purpose Co-operative Society bye-laws (2020) are certified as "registered under Section 5 of the Nigerian Co-operatives Society Act/Regulation Cap. N98, LFN 2004" and "approved under the seal of the Chief Register of Co-operatives, Federal Capital Territory Administration, Abuja" — confirming the FCT registers staff cooperatives directly under the federal Act. — [NFIUCOOP Bye-Law 2020](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf)

**General Nigerian practice / other states**
- A widely-repeated schedule (Fred-Young & Evans, 4 Apr 2017) gives minimum members by type: agricultural 50; savings and credit 20; specialised skills 10; other 10; documents: certified resolution of the first meeting with the cooperative officer, feasibility study, four copies of bye-laws, letters of intent; then "a letter of recognition will be issued which will enable the group to operate as a Cooperative Society for 3 years", after which a Certificate of Registration issues. — [Legal500 / Fred-Young & Evans](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/strategy/procedure-for-registration-of-cooperative-society-in-nigeria)
- Rivers State: application to the "Director or the Department of Cooperative Services" (not CAC); completed application form, constitution and bye-laws, passport photographs and personal details of President, Secretary and Treasurer, prescribed fee; thresholds quoted from the federal Act (10 persons primary, 6 industrial, 5 societies secondary). No fee amounts or timeline published (article 6 Feb 2025). — [Lexpraxis Solicitors, Rivers State](https://lexpraxisng.com/how-to-register-a-cooperative-society-in-rivers-state/)
- A 2026 consumer guide reports typical one-time cooperative registration (joining) fees of ₦5,000–₦25,000 and that cooperative deposits "are generally not insured by the NDIC". — [KudiCompass (Apr/May 2026)](https://kudicompass.com/how-to-join-cooperative-society-nigeria/)

### Inferences
- Two distinct models of "provisional recognition" exist: the FCT/federal 3-year Recognition Letter model (recognition letter first, certificate after 3 years), and the Lagos post-2022 model (3-month supervised probation, then certificate). A staff cooperative's time-to-full-registration is therefore roughly 3-6 months in Lagos but up to 3 years in the FCT, even though the Act itself requires the Director to dispose of an application within 60 days.
- The Lagos 20-member minimum is state policy under the 2022 Law and exceeds the Act's 10-person floor; a workplace with fewer than 20 interested staff could register in the FCT or in a state applying the federal floor, but not in Lagos.
- Because the statutory fee is "such other sum as may be prescribed by the Minister or Commissioner", fees are set administratively and vary by state and by type; a budget line should assume registration fee + trustee induction fee + statutory record books (Lagos) or processing fee ₦25,000 (FCT, Dec 2025), plus professional fees if outsourced.

### Gaps
- No official, dated fee schedule for Lagos was found. A search-result snippet (not verifiable on any fetched page) claimed Lagos fees are "pegged at a minimum fee of 130,000 Naira subject to review"; this figure could not be traced to a primary or dated source and should be treated as unverified.
- The text of the Lagos State Cooperative Societies Law 2022 (including s.60 on the Dispute Resolution Panel) could not be fetched (lawnigeria.com certificate expired; mcic.lagosstate.gov.ng unreachable).
- No published guidance was found for Oyo State specifically; results were generic.
- Lagos State Cooperative College induction fee and duration were not published on any page fetched.

## Key Question 2: Payroll check-off deductions — legal basis, authorisations, employer agreement, remittance, and remedies for non-remittance

### Takeaway
There is no specific "cooperative check-off" provision in the Labour Act; cooperative deductions are lawful only as deductions the worker has agreed to in writing (and Labour Act s.5(7) caps all deductions at one-third of monthly wages). Staff cooperatives operate through (a) a signed deduction authority from each member and (b) a tripartite deduction/remittance arrangement with the employer; where an employer deducts but fails to remit, the National Industrial Court has treated this as breach of a tripartite agreement and of fiduciary duty, awarding the full unremitted sum with interest (Oredo LGC Staff Cooperative v Oredo LGC, NICN/BEN/18/2022, judgment 14 Jan 2025).

### Cited Findings
- Labour Act s.5(1): "no employer shall make any deduction or make any agreement or contract with a worker for any deduction from the wages" except as permitted; s.5(2) permits deductions with the worker's consent for contributions to provident/pension funds or "other schemes agreed to by the worker and approved by the State Authority"; s.5(3)-(4) cover trade-union check-off (automatic for Schedule 3 Part A unions with written opt-out; written opt-in for Part B unions); s.5(7): "the total amount of deductions that may be made from the wages of a worker in any one month shall not exceed one-third of the wages". — [Labour Act s.5 (jurist.ng)](https://jurist.ng/labour_act/sec-5); [Labour Act Cap L1, PLAC](https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/laws/L1.pdf)
- A Nigerian legal commentary lists "cooperatives dues" among deductions that are illegal unless authorised, alongside welfare dues and levies — i.e. the lawfulness of a cooperative deduction depends on authorisation, not on the cooperative's status. — [TheNigeriaLawyer, Fines and deductions from a worker's salary are illegal](https://thenigerialawyer.com/fines-and-deductions-from-a-workers-salary-are-illegal/)
- Payroll-compliance guidance (2026): deductions are restricted to statutory deductions, court-ordered deductions, or those "the employee has agreed to in writing", and cooperative contributions should appear as separately labelled payslip lines. — [AnooreHR, Nigerian payslip legal requirements 2026](https://anoorehr.com/blog/nigeria-payslip-legal-requirements-2026)
- Model bye-law mechanics (NFIU staff cooperative, FCT, 2020): minimum monthly savings "shall be deducted from members' salaries at source, where applicable"; "Every member shall sign a letter of authority for the deduction of his/her contribution from his/her monthly salary/pension"; "Repayment of every Loan defaulter shall be deducted from the Salary of the Member"; where a member's employment is withdrawn, their entitlements in the Society or their guarantor are called upon to repay. — [NFIUCOOP Bye-Law 2020, ss.14.1, 15.6, 6.x](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf)
- Model bye-law mechanics (ASUU Ojo cooperative, Lagos): supervisory checklist asks whether each borrower "entered into a special agreement with the society, authorizing the society to claim from his retiring benefits or gratuities any unpaid balance of the loan in the event of death, retirement or termination of appointment"; loan "repayment of which may impose more hardship" can be relieved by waiving savings during the loan. — [ASUU (Ojo) Cooperative Bye-Laws, s.7.1.5](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- The Act gives the society a statutory charge and set-off on a member's shares, deposits, dividends and bonuses for any debt due (s.16) and makes a member's shares/interest immune from attachment by the member's other creditors (s.17); a claim by the society for a debt from a member is deemed a "dispute touching the business of the society" referable to the Director (s.49(2)). — [Act ss.16, 17, 49](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- **NICN case law**: Oredo Local Government Council Staff Multi-Purpose Cooperative Society Ltd & 3 Ors v Oredo Local Government Council, NICN/BEN/18/2022, judgment 14 January 2025 (Adewemimo J, Benin). Facts: under a "tripartite arrangement" the council was obliged to deduct monthly thrift contributions from staff salaries and transfer them to the cooperative's bank account; remittances stopped after September 2018 although deductions continued, accumulating ₦127.46m by February 2022. Holding: the court had jurisdiction under s.254C(1)(a) of the Constitution as the dispute arose from the workplace and wage conditions; the employer breached the tripartite agreement and a fiduciary duty owed to staff; admitted facts need no proof (Evidence Act s.123). Orders: declaration of breach; ₦112,460,634.52 (after ₦15m part-payment); 10% p.a. pre-judgment interest from 28 Feb 2022; 10% p.a. post-judgment interest if unpaid within 60 days; ₦250,000 costs. — [NICN judgment portal, Oredo LGC Staff v Oredo LGC](https://nicnadr.gov.ng/nicnweb/details.php?+3+ORS+-VS-+Oredo+Local+Government+Council=&id=9622&p=Oredo+Local+Government+Council+Staff+)
- A related NICN pattern: the court ordered NICON Insurance to refund deducted salaries and remit outstanding pension contributions within 30 days, applying the principle that failure to remit sums already deducted is a breach of contract/unfair labour practice and unjust enrichment (search-result summary; judgment not fetched). — [NICN news: NICON Insurance ordered to refund deductions](https://nicnadr.gov.ng/news/2082/-)

### Inferences
- The legally safest architecture for a staff cooperative check-off is: (1) a written, revocable deduction authority signed by each member (bye-laws should make this a condition of membership); (2) a written deduction-and-remittance agreement/MoU between the cooperative and the employer (the "tripartite agreement" language used in Oredo) specifying the monthly deduction file, remittance date (commonly within days of payday), reconciliation and the employer's obligation to remit even where it is in arrears to staff; (3) payslip line-itemisation; and (4) monitoring of aggregate deductions so a member's total deductions (tax, pension, union, cooperative) stay under the one-third cap in Labour Act s.5(7).
- Remedies when an employer deducts but does not remit: the NICN will treat the cooperative and its officers as proper claimants (as in Oredo), and awards have included pre- and post-judgment interest; the Act's s.49 arbitration route does not bind the employer (a non-member), so court action is the realistic remedy against an employer.

### Gaps
- No statutory remittance deadline for cooperative deductions exists in the Labour Act or the Co-operative Societies Act; timelines are purely contractual (MoU). No published model employer-cooperative MoU was found online.
- No Nigerian law-firm article specifically on cooperative check-off deductions (as opposed to union check-off) was found; the Indian RCS Delhi page returned by search is not applicable to Nigeria and was excluded.
- Did not locate the full Oredo judgment text to confirm whether Labour Act s.5 was expressly relied upon; the fetched summary relies on s.254C(1)(a) Constitution, NIC Act 2006 and fiduciary breach.

## Key Question 3: What model staff-cooperative bye-laws contain

### Takeaway
Nigerian staff-cooperative bye-laws follow a near-standard template driven by Act s.11 (mandatory bye-law topics) and s.34 (surplus rules): membership limited to staff of the employer (with variants for seconded, retired and contract staff), compulsory monthly savings via payroll, minimum shareholding, loans capped at 2x savings with 2 guarantors, interest capped (10%–15% p.a. in the examples), a Management Committee of ~7 with 2-year terms (max two consecutive terms), a 3- or 5-member Supervisory/Internal Audit Committee, honoraria capped at 7.5% of net surplus, statutory reserve of at least 25% of net surplus, an education fund, and nominee/next-of-kin and exit provisions.

### Cited Findings

**Statutory content requirements for bye-laws**
- Act s.11(2) requires bye-laws on: name and registered address; objects; area of operation; qualifications for membership, admission terms, admission fees and mode of election; application, custody and investment of funds; nature and extent of members' liability; withdrawal and expulsion and payments to leaving members; transfer of shares; raising share capital; general meetings; appointment, suspension and removal of committee members and their powers; signing authority and seal; disposal of annual surplus. Where the society lends to members, s.11(3) requires additional bye-laws on interest rate, maximum loan per member, maximum repayment period, extension, permitted purposes and consequences of default. Bye-laws may impose fines recoverable as civil debts (s.11(4)). — [Act s.11](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Bye-law amendments are invalid until registered by the Director (three copies forwarded); refusal appealable to the Minister/Commissioner within two months (s.12). — [Act s.12](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- No member other than a registered society may hold more than one-fifth of share capital (s.27); each member has one vote, chairman has casting vote (s.25); no person may belong to more than one loan-granting society without consent (s.24); past members and deceased members' estates remain liable for the society's debts for two years (s.29). — [Act ss.24, 25, 27, 29](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Surplus: no dividend/bonus may be paid except as the Act allows and after committee approval (s.34(1)); "At least one fourth of the net profits ... shall be paid into a fund to be called the 'reserve fund'", though the Director may reduce or exempt limited-liability societies (s.34(2)); with the Director's approval, up to 10% of the remainder may go to an education fund (s.34(3)); wilful breach of s.34(1)-(2) is an offence (fine ₦1,000 or six months' imprisonment or both) (s.34(4)). — [Act s.34](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Loans may not be made to non-members except to another registered society with majority consent (s.30); borrowing requires general-meeting consent (s.31); funds may be invested in a co-operative bank or approved bank, Federal Government securities, or as the committee approves (s.33). — [Act ss.30-33](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- On death, shares/interest transfer to the nominee, else heir/legal representative or administering court (s.18). — [Act s.18](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)

**Example 1 — NFIU Staff Multi-Purpose Co-operative Society Ltd (FCT-registered, 2020 bye-law)**
- Membership (s.6.1): (i) staff of the NFIU with a valid staff ID; (ii) staff seconded to/from the NFIU; (iii) retired/former staff of good character, "provided such former or retired staff shall not vie for or hold any position in the MC"; seconded, retired and former staff may not sit on the Management Committee. Application fee ₦2,000 non-refundable. — [NFIUCOOP Bye-Law 2020](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf)
- Savings (s.14): minimum ordinary monthly savings ₦20,000, deducted at source; split 60% "savings account" (counts for loan eligibility) / 40% "investment account" (does not); six months' non-payment makes the account dormant/suspended; members may withdraw up to 60% of total contributions once a year, only if no loan is outstanding and any member they guarantee has repaid at least 20%. — [NFIUCOOP s.14](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf)
- Shares (s.13): par value ₦1; minimum voluntary shareholding ₦50,000 payable by instalment within three months; no member may own more than 20% of issued shares; not more than 5% of share-withdrawal applications treated in one year without the Chief Registrar's permission. — [NFIUCOOP s.13](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf)
- Loans (s.15): eligibility after six months as a contributing member; Loan Committee of seven (President, VP, Secretary, Treasurer, Financial Secretary + two non-MC members) decides within 14 days; **maximum loan 2x total contribution**, contributions locked until repaid; all loans and interest repayable within one year; **two guarantors**, at least one a regular staff member; seconded staff need two confirmed-staff guarantors with 5+ years to serve plus a parent-organisation undertaking; ex-staff may borrow below 70% of savings with two guarantors having 10+ years to retirement; defaulters' repayments deducted from salary; the General Meeting fixes interest "with the provision that this rate shall not exceed ten (10) per cent". — [NFIUCOOP ss.8.3, 15](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf)
- Governance: Management Committee quorum 5, meets at least monthly; Electoral Committee of at least five; elected officers serve two-year terms, eligible for one further two-year term in the same office; nomination fee ₦10,000; an FCT Chief Registrar representative acts as Returning Officer and witnesses swearing-in. Advisory Committee chaired by an NFIU staff member not below GL15 with two Heads of Department and two MC members (two-year tenure) — the de facto "patron" structure. Internal Audit Committee of five (three ratified at AGM plus Treasurer and Financial Secretary), meets at least four times a year; two External Auditors — one appointed by the Society (two-year renewable) and one from the FCT Cooperative Secretariat, whose statutory audit report "shall be the final audit exercise". MC members are "trustees of the Society's funds"; an MC member absent for more than a set number of meetings, or on study leave over three months, vacates office. — [NFIUCOOP ss.7-10](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf)
- Honoraria (s.7.6.1): "shall not be more than seven and a half (7.5%) per cent of the net surplus"; determined at the AGM. — [NFIUCOOP s.7.6](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf)
- Surplus appropriation (s.11): Reserve Fund 25%; Education and Training Fund 7%; Audit and Supervision fee to the FCT Chief Registrar 1%; Honorarium to MC and staff 7.5%; Retained Earnings 2%; Meeting Expenses 4.5%; Community Development 1%; Dividends 52%. Reserve fund is indivisible and may be used in the business or to meet deficiencies only with the Chief Registrar's approval. — [NFIUCOOP s.11](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf)
- Compliance calendar (s.17): accounts to 31 December; annual accounts published by 31 March; three copies of annual accounts and reports furnished to the Registrar/Director "not later than the 31st of July annually ... before the Annual General Meeting is summoned"; work-plan by 31 October, approved by 30 November; budget by 30 November; annual audit and supervision fees paid to the Chief Registrar's office. Prescribed books: membership & attendance register, cash book, general ledger, personal ledger, loan register and loan bond file, minutes book, register of share payments, bond book. — [NFIUCOOP ss.16-17](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf)
- Exit: withdrawal on one month's notice if not indebted, with a ₦20,000 administrative fee; re-admission after not less than a stated interval with a re-admission fee; next of kin appointed on a prescribed form, failing which a will/letters of administration govern; a deceased member's debts are set off against the estate's entitlements; where employment is withdrawn, entitlements or the guarantor repay outstanding loans. Disputes go MC → Advisory Committee → Chief Registrar arbitration "as provided for in the Act". Fines up to ₦5,000 per breach. Amendments need a 2/3 General Meeting vote and Chief Registrar registration. — [NFIUCOOP ss.6.5-6.8, 19](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf)

**Example 2 — ASUU (Ojo) Cooperative Multipurpose Society Ltd (Lagos-registered; university staff)**
- Membership: 18+, not a member of another loan-granting society; non-refundable entrance fee and minimum monthly savings set by the MC; membership terminates on death, withdrawal (one month's notice, not indebted), expulsion, or "Termination of appointment from the Company"; re-admission after at least 12 months. Nominee must be named in writing and recorded in the register. — [ASUU (Ojo) Bye-Laws ss.4.2-4.7](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- Capital and savings: shares of ₦2.00 each, minimum 25,000 shares (₦50,000) payable within six months; cap 20% of paid-up capital; compulsory thrift savings not below ₦5,000 per month; savings not withdrawable except on exit; share withdrawals in any year capped at 5% of paid-up capital without the Director's permission. — [ASUU (Ojo) ss.8.1-8.7](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- Loans: only to members; not within first six months; maximum credit limit "twice the total savings of the applicant"; interest "shall not exceed 15% per annum nor ... be below 5% per annum", penal interest at twice the normal rate on default; Credit Committee of at least five chaired by the Treasurer. — [ASUU (Ojo) ss.10.1-10.5](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- Governance: general meeting quorum one-quarter of members; MC of at least seven (President, VP, Secretary, Treasurer, Financial Secretary, Assistant Secretary, ex-officio); tenure "a maximum period of four (4) years for a term of two (2) years" and no position for more than two consecutive terms; MC quorum four; honorarium "shall not be more than 7.5% of the net surplus"; removal by 2/3 of members at a special general meeting; related-party transactions over ₦100,000 or 10% of total assets need general-meeting approval; officers handling cash to be bonded/fidelity-insured; bank account operated by at least two of three trustees. Supervisory Committee of three elected at the AGM, meeting at least quarterly, with a 16-point audit checklist and power to recommend suspension of officers or call a special meeting. — [ASUU (Ojo) ss.5-7](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- Surplus: at least one-fourth to Reserve Fund "unless a lower rate is prescribed by the Director"; 2.5% to Education Fund; honoraria/donations as approved by the Director; dividend on savings, shares and loans "shall not be less than 50% of the net surplus"; appropriation "subject to approval by the Director"; audit and supervision fees assessed by the Director paid annually. Disputes referred "to the Director for settlement by arbitration" under the state law; amendments invalid until registered by the Director of Co-operative Services, Lagos State. — [ASUU (Ojo) ss.11-15](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)

**Other examples (search snippets only; PDFs not fully read)**
- God's Treasury Co-operative Thrift and Credit Society bye-laws: not less than 25% to reserve fund (unless a lower rate is prescribed by the Director) and 2.5% to education fund — the same Lagos template as ASUU Ojo. — [GTCS Bye-laws (2023)](https://godstreasury.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/THE-GTCS-BYELAWS.pdf)
- Niger Delta University cooperative bye-laws: withdrawals from thrift savings limited to twice a year provided the member holds above minimum shares. — [NDU Coop Bye-Laws](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf)
- Unique UNILAG Staff Cooperative Multipurpose Society offers car-purchase and compassionate loans repayable by salary deduction and sold shares to members "in compliance with section 8.3 of its Bye-Law and Chapter 14 of the Cooperative Societies' Law of Lagos State". — [UNILAG news](https://unilag.edu.ng/unique-unilag-staff-cooperative-society-announces-sale-of-shares-to-members/)
- Additional published staff-cooperative bye-laws exist at [Accion MFB Staff Cooperative](http://accionmfbcoop.com.ng/assets/files/bye_law.pdf) and [NIFESAF ESUT Cooperative](https://www.nemcos.org/files/shares/17_0323%20NIFESAF%20ESUT%20Cooperative%20%20BYE%20laws%20Submitted.pdf) (not read in this pass).

### Inferences
- The two fully-read bye-laws (one FCT-registered, one Lagos-registered) converge on: 2x-savings loan ceiling, 6-month waiting period, two guarantors who are confirmed staff, 7.5% honoraria cap, 25% statutory reserve, 2-year committee terms with a two-term limit, and Director/Registrar approval of surplus appropriation. These can be treated as de facto "model" terms for a Nigerian staff cooperative; the statutory floor is only s.34 (25% reserve, ≤10% of remainder to education) and s.11's list of topics.
- "Patron role of the employer" is not a statutory concept; it is implemented through an Advisory Committee of senior management (NFIU: chaired by a GL15+ officer with Heads of Department) and through the employer's payroll and audit cooperation. Retired/seconded staff are typically allowed as members but barred from committee office; contract staff are generally not expressly addressed — NFIU admits only staff "issued with valid staff identification card".
- Interest-rate caps differ by society (10% at NFIU vs 5–15% at ASUU Ojo) and are set in bye-laws, not statute.

### Gaps
- No bye-law expressly addressing contract/outsourced staff eligibility was found.
- Could not confirm a model bye-law template published by the Federal Department of Cooperatives or Lagos MCCTI; the convergence above is inferred from registered examples.
- Dividend caps: Act s.44(d) caps dividend at 5% p.a. only in the liquidation waterfall; no general statutory dividend cap for a going concern was found in the Act (bye-laws instead set a floor of ≥50% of surplus to dividends in the examples).

## Key Question 4: Supervising bodies and their powers

### Takeaway
Supervision is two-tier: the Federal Department of Cooperatives (Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security) sets policy and runs the new national digital registry, while registration, audit, inspection, inquiry, arbitration, surcharge and dissolution powers under the Act are exercised by the State Director/Chief Registrar of Co-operatives (Lagos: Director of Cooperative Services, MCCTI; FCT: Chief Registrar, FCTA). Lagos additionally runs the Lagos State Cooperative College for mandatory trustee induction and a statutory Cooperative Dispute Resolution Panel (s.60, 2022 Law).

### Cited Findings
- Appointment: the President may appoint a Federal Director of Co-operatives; the Governor of a State may appoint a State Director and confer the Act's powers on them by State Gazette (s.1). — [Act s.1](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Audit (s.36): accounts must be audited at each financial year-end by an auditor approved by the society, covering overdue debts and valuation of assets and liabilities; auditors can summon officers and members and compel production of books; the report must cover P&L, balance sheet, management, disposal of profit, remuneration in kind of the chief executive, cash and securities verification; the Director or anyone authorised in writing has access at all reasonable times to all books, accounts, papers, securities and cash; the Director "may give directions prescribing the returns to be submitted by a registered society" and their timing (s.36(7)). Obstruction: fine not less than ₦1,000 or six months or both. — [Act s.36](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Inquiry and inspection (s.37): the Director may, and must on application of a simple majority of the committee or at least one-third of members, hold an inquiry into "the constitution, working and financial state" of a society; must inspect on a creditor's application (with security for costs); may apportion costs; and "if an inquiry ... proves that the committee ... is inefficient, the Director may ... take over the management of its affairs for a period of two years". — [Act s.37](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Cancellation/dissolution (s.38): the Director may cancel registration if membership falls below ten (primary) or six (industrial), or after an inquiry/inspection or on application by three-fourths of members; appeal to Minister/Commissioner within two months; "No registered society shall wind or be wound up except by an order of the Director or of a court" (s.38(6)); Director appoints and controls the liquidator (ss.40-42); liquidation waterfall: costs, liabilities, share capital, then dividend up to 5% p.a. if bye-laws permit (s.44); claims barred two years after Gazette notice of closure (s.45). — [Act ss.38-45](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Surcharge and attachment (ss.46-48): after an audit, inquiry or winding-up, the Director may order anyone who organised/managed the society and misapplied or retained its money, or committed misfeasance/breach of trust, to repay with interest or compensate; may order interim attachment of property to prevent dissipation; appeal to the Minister/Commissioner within 30 days. — [Act ss.46-48](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Disputes (s.49): any dispute "touching the business of a registered society" among members, between members and the society/committee/officers, or between societies "shall be referred to the Director for settlement"; the Director may settle it or refer it to an arbitrator; the award is enforceable by any civil court as a judgment; appeal to the Minister/Commissioner within 30 days, whose decision is "final and conclusive"; questions of law may be referred to a High Court (s.50). — [Act ss.49-50](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Offences: misapplication of society property — fine not less than ₦1,000 or six months or both (s.35); non-compliance with surplus rules — same (s.34(4)); the Act also prohibits unregistered use of the word "Co-operative" (s.54). — [Act ss.34, 35, 54](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Lagos: the Department of Cooperative Services handles "registration, supervision, auditing, and general oversight of all cooperative societies in Lagos State" through Registration, Inspectorate, Mediation, Insurance and Monitoring/Enforcement units; trustees must complete an induction course at the Lagos State Cooperative College before certification; there is a mandatory three-month post-registration supervision; over 16,000 societies across 24 Area Offices. — [Lagos MCCTI](https://mccti.lagosstate.gov.ng/departments/cooperatives)
- Lagos State Cooperative College (LASCOCO) runs certificate/diploma programmes and intermediate training programmes for cooperators. — [LASCOCO](https://lascoco.lagosstate.gov.ng/)
- FCT: the Chief Registrar of Co-operatives (Area 11, FCTA, under the Agriculture and Rural Development Secretariat) registers, recertifies, audits (statutory audit report "shall be the final audit") and arbitrates; NFIU bye-laws budget 1% of surplus as "Audit and Supervision fees payable to office of the Chief Registrar". — [Abuja Digest, June 2022](http://abujadigest.info/2022/06/why-all-cooperative-societies-in-fct-must-be-recertified-secretary/); [NFIUCOOP s.11](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf)
- Federal: the supervising minister for cooperative affairs is the Minister of State for Agriculture and Food Security (Sen. Dr. Aliyu Sabi Abdullahi); the Federal Director of Cooperatives Department is Dr. Mohammed Bashir Abdulkadir (May 2026). — [Voice of Nigeria, 12 May 2026](https://von.gov.ng/nigeria-launches-cooperative-digitalisation-bank-reform-initiative/)

### Inferences
- The Director's s.37(7) power to take over management for two years, the s.46 surcharge power and the s.49 compulsory arbitration route together mean a staff cooperative's internal disputes and officer misconduct are primarily administrative (Director → Minister/Commissioner) rather than judicial matters; courts enter mainly to enforce awards or on questions of law.
- Annual "audit and supervision fees" to the state Director/Registrar are a standard recurring cost (1% of surplus in the NFIU model; "rate assessed by the Director" in Lagos).

### Gaps
- Regulations made under s.56 of the Act (which would set arbitration procedure, prescribed forms and returns) were not located online.
- Lagos 2022 Law text (including the Dispute Resolution Panel and any Lagos-specific penalties) not retrievable.

## Key Question 5: National apex bodies and what affiliation gives a staff cooperative

### Takeaway
The apex for financial (thrift/credit) cooperatives is the National Cooperative Financing Agency of Nigeria (CFAN), the April 2015 rebrand of NACCUN (founded Lagos, 1978), affiliated to the Cooperative Federation of Nigeria (CFN), ACCOSCA and WOCCU; the Act itself envisages a state-and-national Cooperative Federation structure (s.26) and model bye-laws require affiliation with the relevant union/federation unless the Director exempts the society.

### Cited Findings
- NACCUN was established in Lagos in 1978 as the apex body for cooperative financing agencies, credit unions and thrift and credit societies, and was rebranded by the Federal Department of Cooperatives on 7 April 2015 as CFAN, aligned with the National Cooperative Development Policy (2002). CFAN claims "over 5.2 million Credit Co-operative Members nationwide" in more than 2,040 cooperative groups; services offered to member cooperatives: financial linkage and affordable credit, development advisory, training and capacity building, insurance, pension and mortgage access, advocacy, technology solutions; affiliations: ACCOSCA (Africa), and it chairs the CBN financial-inclusion Product Working Group. Membership fees not published. — [CFAN, About](https://www.cfan.coop/about-cfan/); [CFAN, Who we are](https://cfan.ng/who-we-are/)
- CFAN is affiliated to the Cooperative Federation of Nigeria (CFN) and is a member of WACCOSCA and ACCOSCA; "National Association of Co-operative Credit Unions of Nigeria Ltd." is listed as a direct WOCCU member. — [WOCCU membership](https://www.woccu.org/member_services/our_network/membership); [NOUN CRD 208 courseware](https://nou.edu.ng/coursewarecontent/CRD%20208%20Nigeria%20and%20International%20Cooperation_0.pdf)
- Act s.26: members of the Board of the Co-operative Federation in each State form committees per activity; committee chairmen represent the State at the National Apex and the State Federation President sits on the Co-operative Federation of Nigeria; no individual may hold office in more than two National/State apex organisations, and apex office is limited to two terms of four years. — [Act s.26](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- The CFN is affiliated to the International Cooperative Alliance and has publicly called for sector reforms including stronger partnerships to expand access to capital, markets and technical support; NIPOST Staff Cooperative's newly elected Management Committee was inducted through a "Cooperative Good Governance training" (Guardian report; page could not be fetched, relying on search summary). — [The Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/news/nigeria/national/cooperatives-seek-reforms-to-unlock-sectors-economic-potential-2/)
- Bye-law practice: NFIU s.19.1 — "Unless exempted by the Registrar (Director), the Society shall apply for membership of a Co-operative Union (if any)" in its region; ASUU Ojo s.14.1 — "Unless there are reasons to the contrary accepted by the Director, the Society shall affiliate" with the relevant union. — [NFIUCOOP s.19.1](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf); [ASUU (Ojo) s.14.1](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- Lagos has a state federation, LASCOFED (Lagos State Cooperative Federation), whose multipurpose hall in Ikeja hosted the federal ministerial tour in May 2026. — [Voice of Nigeria, 12 May 2026](https://von.gov.ng/nigeria-launches-cooperative-digitalisation-bank-reform-initiative/)
- The Abuja Co-operative Federation (ABCOF) exists as the FCT-level apex. — [ABCOF](http://abcof.coop/news_details.php?news_id=18)

### Inferences
- A staff thrift-and-credit cooperative's affiliation path is: state/area cooperative union or financing agency → state federation (e.g. LASCOFED, ABCOF) → CFAN (financial apex) and CFN (general apex). Practical benefits documented by CFAN are wholesale/central-finance liquidity, training for new committees, insurance/pension/mortgage linkages and representation in CBN financial-inclusion policy; affiliation is also effectively expected by Registrars via the standard bye-law clause.

### Gaps
- No published CFAN or CFN affiliation fee schedule, membership criteria or stabilisation/deposit-guarantee fund details were found.
- No evidence found of a NDIC-style deposit protection for cooperative savings; a 2026 consumer guide states cooperative deposits are "generally not insured by the NDIC". — [KudiCompass](https://kudicompass.com/how-to-join-cooperative-society-nigeria/)

## Key Question 6: Recent (2023–2026) reforms — new cooperative law, CBN conversion policy, FCCPC/CBN scrutiny

### Takeaway
The headline 2025–2026 reform is the Federal Government's Renewed Hope Cooperative Reform and Revamp Programme (RH-CRRP 2025–2030), approved by the National Council on Cooperative Affairs in March 2026, which bundles (i) a review/harmonisation of the Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act, (ii) a National Cooperative Digital Architecture Platform with a national register, Cooperative Verification Number and CoopID, and (iii) a proposed cooperative-owned Cooperative Bank of Nigeria. No bill text or National Assembly reading of a new federal cooperative Act was found, and no CBN rule compelling large cooperatives to convert to microfinance banks was found — CBN's microfinance framework permits (not mandates) transformation.

### Cited Findings
- RH-CRRP 2030 was "approved by the National Council on Cooperative Affairs in March 2026" with seven pillars (legal/regulatory reform, digitalisation, cooperative finance, capacity building, enterprise growth, socio-economic inclusion, partnerships); sector statistics cited: over 370,000 registered cooperative societies, more than 30 million members, annual turnover exceeding ₦1.7 trillion, target of ₦5 trillion in assets within five years; NCDAP comprises a National Cooperative Societies Register (NCSR), Cooperative Verifiable Number (CVN) and CoopID; SEAMFIX Limited is technical partner under an MoU signed 12 August 2026 at no cost to federal or state governments; responsible ministry: Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security. (Published 7 Oct 2026.) — [FMINO, Cooperative digitalisation / RH-CRRP 2030](https://fmino.gov.ng/cooperative-digitalisation-fg-reaffirms-commitment-towards-national-cooperative-identity-reform-under-rh-crrp-2030-tasks-states-on-implementation/)
- The FG is "harmonising ongoing reviews of the Nigerian Cooperative Societies Act" to produce legislation recognising digital registration, electronic records, cooperative identity, data governance and financial technology, in consultation with the CFN, National Assembly, State Houses of Assembly, Nigerian Law Reform Commission, Federal Ministry of Justice and Federal Ministry of Industry, Trade and Investment; the three priorities are NCDAP, a Cooperative Bank of Nigeria, and the Act review. — [FMINO, FG to revolutionize cooperative societies through digitalization](https://fmino.gov.ng/fg-to-revolutionize-cooperative-societies-through-digitalization/); [TG News, 19 June 2026](https://tgnews.com.ng/fg-moves-to-modernise-nigerias-cooperative-sector/)
- Proposed Cooperative Bank of Nigeria ownership: 65% controlling equity for cooperative societies, unions, federations and individual cooperators; 30% for private institutional investors and DFIs; 5% for bank employees and affiliated cooperative enterprises; "government-enabled but not government-funded", no TSA involvement; CVN and CoopID to integrate with the NIN architecture; a "CoopTrust" investment society also proposed. Announced at LASCOFED Hall, Ikeja, 12 May 2026. — [Voice of Nigeria, 12 May 2026](https://von.gov.ng/nigeria-launches-cooperative-digitalisation-bank-reform-initiative/)
- The FG "charged 36 states" to be "frontline implementers" of the cooperative digital rollout. — [FMINO](https://fmino.gov.ng/be-frontline-implementers-not-spectators-fg-charges-36-states-on-cooperative-digital-rollout/)
- CBN Microfinance Policy (2005/revised 2011): "An existing NGO-MFI or Financial Cooperative which intends to operate as MFB can either incorporate a subsidiary MFB ... or transform to a MFB" and must obtain a licence and meet the MFB guidelines; the policy also provides a government–cooperative 75:25 PPP model for Unit MFBs. The language is permissive, not mandatory. — [CBN Microfinance Policy Framework (2011)](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2011/publications/dfd/Reviewed%20Microfinance%20Policy%20July%2012%202011.pdf); [CBN Microfinance Policy 2005](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/OUT/PUBLICATIONS/GUIDELINES/DFD/2006/MICROFINANCE%20POLICY.PDF)
- The FCCPC's 2025 consumer-lending guidelines framework recognises "duly registered and licensed cooperative societies operating in accordance with applicable laws", and digital lenders operating community-based models may additionally need cooperative registration. — [FCCPC DEON Consumer Lending Guidelines 2025](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/DEON-Consumer-Lending-Guidelines-2025.pdf); [Legal500, CBN guidelines on digital lending](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/technology/cbn-guidelines-on-digital-lending-in-nigeria-legal-obligations-for-fintechs-and-lenders)
- Historical: a 2008 draft bill at the National Assembly proposed a National Co-operatives Societies Development Agency as an improvement on the current Act (coops4dev country profile). — [coops4dev Nigeria](https://coops4dev.coop/en/4devafrica/nigeria)
- Lagos's own reform was the Lagos State Cooperative Societies Law signed 7 Feb 2022 (see KQ1). — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2022/02/08/lagos-governor-signs-bills-to-regulate-real-estate-provide-for-registration-of-cooperative-societies/)

### Inferences
- For a staff cooperative forming in late 2026, the practical implication of RH-CRRP is that it should expect to obtain a Cooperative Verification Number and member CoopIDs through the state Director once the NCDAP rollout reaches its state, in addition to the state registration certificate; FCT societies already face periodic recertification.
- The Act review is at policy/harmonisation stage, not a bill before the National Assembly as of the sources found (Oct 2026); the 1993/2004 Act remains the operative federal law.
- No evidence was found of a CBN asset- or membership-threshold rule forcing cooperatives to become microfinance banks; conversion remains voluntary under the CBN microfinance framework, and cooperatives remain outside CBN prudential supervision and NDIC cover.

### Gaps
- No text, sponsor or gazette number for a "Nigerian Co-operative Societies Bill" (2023–2026) was found; searches returned Kenya's Cooperative Bill 2024 and India's bills instead.
- No CBN circular (2023–2026) specifically on cooperative societies taking deposits or on conversion thresholds was found; no FCCPC enforcement action targeting cooperatives was found.
- Could not confirm whether the Cooperative Bank of Nigeria has applied for or received a CBN licence.
