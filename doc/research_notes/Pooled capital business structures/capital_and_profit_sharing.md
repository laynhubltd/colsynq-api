# Capital Contributions and Profit/Loss Sharing in Pooled-Capital Businesses: Mechanics, Formulas and Accounting

Scope note: these notes cover the *mechanics* of how groups who pool money (partnerships, LLC-style ventures, syndicates/SPVs, cooperatives, dynamic-equity startups) value and record contributions, allocate profits and losses, handle distributions, admit/exit members, and keep partner capital accounts. Sources are mainly ACCA study material, accounting explainers, USDA cooperative publications, US tax guidance (IRS Pub 541), real-estate/VC waterfall explainers, the Slicing Pie materials, and Nigerian statute/commentary. Figures in worked examples are quoted from sources where available; where I have built an illustrative calculation myself from sourced rules, it is placed under **Inferences** and labelled as such.

---

## Key Question 1: Capital contributions — cash vs in-kind vs sweat equity; valuation; recording; member loans vs equity

### Takeaway
Cash is recorded at face value and non-cash assets at agreed fair market value (not the contributor's cost), each credited to the contributor's capital account; services ("sweat equity") are the exception — standard partnership bookkeeping does not credit capital for unpaid labour, and if a capital interest *is* granted for services the tax rules treat its fair value as taxable compensation to the recipient. Loans from members are liabilities, not capital: they carry interest charged as an expense (default 5% p.a. under the 1890 Act and Lagos law) and are repaid ahead of capital.

### Cited Findings
- Recording a cash contribution: "A contribution will be a credit entry in the capital account and a debit entry in the bank account, and a withdrawal will be a debit entry in the capital account and a credit entry in the bank account." — [ACCA FA2, Accounting for partnerships](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/foundation-level-study-resources/fa2/fa2-technical-articles/accounting-for-partnerships.html)
- Journal entry for a $300,000 cash contribution: Debit Cash $300,000 / Credit Partner Capital Account $300,000; for non-cash contributions, the asset is debited at fair market value with a corresponding credit to capital. "Assets contributed to the business are recorded at the fair market value, not book value or tax basis." Accounts receivable are evaluated for collectibility, equipment is appraised at current market value, real estate requires professional appraisal; client relationships are generally not accepted as capital contributions. — [LeanLaw, Financial mechanics of partner capital contributions](https://www.leanlaw.co/blog/the-financial-mechanics-of-partner-capital-contributions-how-theyre-valued-paid-and-recorded/)
- Non-cash assets are recorded at "market value of the assets" rather than historical cost; if the partnership assumes liabilities attached to the asset, the partner's capital is credited only with the net (asset value less debt assumed). — [Wikipedia, Partnership accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership_accounting)
- Dual accounts per partner: the capital account "typically remains fixed, reflecting the owner's initial investment," while "the current account is the current total of appropriations and the share of residual profit or loss, less drawings." — [ACCA FA2](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/foundation-level-study-resources/fa2/fa2-technical-articles/accounting-for-partnerships.html)
- Current accounts may go negative if draws exceed profits. — [LeanLaw](https://www.leanlaw.co/blog/the-financial-mechanics-of-partner-capital-contributions-how-theyre-valued-paid-and-recorded/)
- Loans from partners are treated as third-party liabilities: "The liability of the partnership will be recorded by the creation of a liability, resulting in a credit balance for the amount of the loan." Interest on partner loans is a business expense debited to profit or loss (i.e., it is *not* an appropriation of profit). — [ACCA FA2](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/foundation-level-study-resources/fa2/fa2-technical-articles/accounting-for-partnerships.html)
- Default statutory rule (Partnership Act 1890 s.24): a partner who makes "any actual payment or advance beyond the amount of capital which he has agreed to subscribe, is entitled to interest at the rate of five per cent. per annum from the date of the payment or advance," while "a partner is not entitled, before the ascertainment of profits, to interest on the capital subscribed by him." — [Partnership Act 1890 s.24 (Irish Statute Book)](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1890/act/39/section/24/enacted/en/html); [LexisNexis s.24](https://www.lexisnexis.co.uk/legal/legislation/uk-parliament-acts/partnership-act-1890-c39/section-24)
- Nigeria mirrors this: under the Lagos State Partnership Law (s.23), partners share equally in capital and profits absent agreement, are not entitled to interest on capital before profits are ascertained, and a partner who advances money beyond agreed capital is entitled to interest at five percent per annum from the date of the advance. — [Lexology, Overview of partnership law & agreement in Nigeria](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=1f0ca982-779b-4c17-bcef-2788c265dc0c); [ǼLEX paper on establishment and management of partnerships](https://www.aelex.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/PAPER-ON-ESTABLISHMENT-MANAGEMENT-OF-PARTNERSHIPS.pdf)
- Sweat equity (US tax view): "The FMV of an interest in partnership capital transferred to a partner as payment for services to the partnership is a guaranteed payment" — ordinary income to the recipient at receipt (unless subject to substantial risk of forfeiture). A *profits* interest for services is generally not taxable on receipt. Partner's initial basis = "the money plus the adjusted basis of any property the partner contributed" (services add nothing to basis). — [IRS Publication 541](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p541)
- Guaranteed payments are compensation "determined without regard to the partnership's income"; the partnership deducts them and the partner reports them as ordinary income. — [IRS Publication 541](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p541)
- Sweat-equity valuation guidance: "Value contributions at their fair market rate—what the work would cost to buy in the market." — [Equity Matrix, Sweat equity valuation](https://equitymatrix.io/blog/sweat-equity-valuation)
- Partner capital contributions may be funded by bank loans (5–10 year terms at prime +1–3%), internal zero-interest firm loans deducted from future distributions, graduated contributions over 3–5 years, or profit-based funding (allocating a percentage of annual profits until the requirement is met); a firm loan to a partner for their contribution is recorded separately as a note receivable. Market data: average capital requirement in the top-100 US law firms is 23% of compensation (up to 49%); a new partner earning $150,000 with a 30–35% requirement contributes $45,000–$52,500. — [LeanLaw](https://www.leanlaw.co/blog/the-financial-mechanics-of-partner-capital-contributions-how-theyre-valued-paid-and-recorded/)
- Return of capital on exit typically follows a schedule: immediate (rare), 1 year (voluntary departures in good standing), 2–3 years (standard), 3–5 years (larger contributions / smaller firms), with possible offsets for undistributed income forfeiture, loan acceleration, firm liabilities and outstanding draws. — [LeanLaw](https://www.leanlaw.co/blog/the-financial-mechanics-of-partner-capital-contributions-how-theyre-valued-paid-and-recorded/)
- Slicing Pie (startup practice) treats unpaid time, unreimbursed expenses, equipment, IP and relationships all as "at-risk contributions" valued at fair market value and converted to "slices" (see KQ2 for multipliers). — [Slicing Pie, Learn the model](https://slicingpie.com/learn-slicing-pie-model/)

### Inferences
- Practical rule for a deed: record *three* numbers for every in-kind contribution — the agreed fair value, who valued it (independent appraiser or unanimous member agreement), and any liabilities assumed — because the capital credit is net of assumed debt and disputes almost always trace back to an unwritten valuation.
- Illustrative journal for a Nigerian 5-member venture (my own arithmetic applying the sourced rules): Member A ₦5,000,000 cash; Member B a truck agreed at ₦3,000,000 subject to a ₦800,000 outstanding vehicle loan the business assumes; Member C a shop fit-out valued ₦1,500,000; Member D ₦2,000,000 cash *plus* a ₦1,000,000 member loan at 10%; Member E contributes services only. Entries: Dr Bank 7,000,000; Dr Truck 3,000,000; Dr Fixtures 1,500,000; Cr Vehicle loan 800,000; Cr Member loan (D) 1,000,000; Cr Capital A 5,000,000; Cr Capital B 2,200,000; Cr Capital C 1,500,000; Cr Capital D 2,000,000; Cr Capital E 0. E is rewarded instead through a partner salary in the appropriation account (KQ2) or a vesting/dynamic-equity arrangement (KQ5). D's ₦100,000 annual loan interest is an expense above the line, paid before any profit share.
- Because the default statutory interest on advances (5%) is far below Nigerian market rates, groups should set the member-loan rate expressly in the deed; otherwise the lending member is under-compensated and may dispute it.

### Gaps
- I found no IFRS for SMEs–specific text on partner capital accounts (the sources above are ACCA/US-practice based); IFRS for SMEs Section 22 treats puttable member interests as liabilities in some cases, but I did not retrieve a source confirming this for partnerships and have not included it.
- The exact text of Lagos Partnership Law s.23 was reported via secondary commentary (Lexology/ǼLEX); the primary statute text was not retrieved.

## Key Question 2: Profit-sharing methods with worked numeric examples

### Takeaway
All methods reduce to an "appropriation" of net profit in a fixed order — salaries/management fees first, then interest or preferred return on capital, then the residual in an agreed ratio — and the only real differences are (a) which steps exist and (b) what ratio governs the residual (capital, fixed deed ratio, patronage volume, or dynamic contribution-based slices). Syndicate/real-estate waterfalls are the same structure with return-of-capital and a GP catch-up inserted before the residual split.

### Cited Findings

**A. Appropriation account order (salary → interest on capital → interest on drawings → residual in PSR)**
- "The appropriation process allocates profits in order: first any salaries, then any interest on capital, and finally the remaining profit in the profit-sharing ratio." Salaries and interest on capital are *not* expenses in the P&L; they appear in the appropriation account. — [ACCA F3, Working in partnership](https://www.accaglobal.com/us/en/student/exam-support-resources/fundamentals-exams-study-resources/f3/technical-articles/working-partnership.html)
- Interest on capital is paid on capital account balances only (current-account balances are excluded even though they are economically part of capital). "Paying interest on capital is a means of rewarding partners for investing funds in the partnership as opposed to alternative investments." — [ACCA F3](https://www.accaglobal.com/us/en/student/exam-support-resources/fundamentals-exams-study-resources/f3/technical-articles/working-partnership.html)
- **Worked example (Amit & Burton, ACCA FA2):** Profit $65,460; PSR 3:2; capital Amit $120,000, Burton $80,000; interest on capital 5%; interest on drawings 8%; salaries Amit $9,000, Burton $5,000; drawings Amit $18,000, Burton $31,000. Interest on capital: Amit $6,000, Burton $4,000. Interest on drawings (charged *to* partners): Amit $1,440, Burton $2,480. Appropriation: $65,460 − salaries $14,000 − interest on capital $10,000 + interest on drawings $3,920 = residual $45,380... *[Note: the source's own arithmetic presents the residual as $37,540 by subtracting the $3,920 interest on drawings; conventionally interest on drawings is added back to divisible profit. I report the source's figures as given below and flag the discrepancy.]* Source's residual $37,540 split 3:2 = Amit $22,524, Burton $15,016. Closing current accounts: Amit $38,443 Cr; Burton $10,465 Dr. — [ACCA FA2](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/foundation-level-study-resources/fa2/fa2-technical-articles/accounting-for-partnerships.html)
- Tiered allocation example (US textbook style): net income $100,000; salary allowances $50,000; interest allowances $30,000; remaining $20,000 split equally. "When allowances exceed net income, losses are allocated according to the same profit-sharing ratio used for gains." — [Wikipedia, Partnership accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership_accounting)
- Hybrid example: $200,000 profit after an $80,000 guaranteed payment; remaining $120,000 split 60/40. — [Philly Small Business Lawyer, Profit, loss and distributions](https://phillysmallbusinesslawyer.com/business-success-tips/how-to-handle-distributions-allocations-and-profitloss-division-in-partnerships/)

**B. Fixed ratios in the deed / default equal shares**
- Default absent agreement: "All partners are entitled to share equally in the capital and profits of the business, and must contribute equally towards the losses." — [Partnership Act 1890 s.24](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1890/act/39/section/24/enacted/en/html); same rule in Lagos Partnership Law s.23 — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=1f0ca982-779b-4c17-bcef-2788c265dc0c)
- Nigerian deeds record profit and loss shares, capital contributions, responsibilities, decision rules, dispute resolution and entry/exit; "Profit and loss sharing need not match capital." Without a deed, default rules impose "equal profit shares regardless of what each partner contributed, equal say in management regardless of effort, and no entitlement to a salary." — [LegalDoc.ng, Partnership deed](https://legaldoc.ng/glossary/partnership-deed)

**C. Preferred return + catch-up + promote (syndicate / real-estate / PE waterfall)**
- Standard tiers: (1) return of capital; (2) preferred return to LPs; (3) GP catch-up; (4) residual split at the promote ratio, commonly 70/30 or 80/20 LP/GP, often with multiple IRR hurdles. "A GP catch-up provision allows the sponsor to receive 100% of available profits after the preferred return is met until they have reached their agreed-upon carried interest percentage." — [Agora, Real estate waterfall models](https://agorareal.com/learn/commercial-real-estate-waterfall-model/); [apers.app, Catch-up provisions](https://apers.app/learn/financial-modeling/waterfall-mechanics/catch-up-provisions-how-80-20-actually-works)
- **Worked example 1 (Agora):** LP equity $4,000,000; 5-year hold; $6,400,000 available to equity at exit; profit $2,400,000; 8% *simple cumulative* pref = $1,600,000.
  - Tier 1 return of capital: $4,000,000 to LP. Tier 2 pref: $1,600,000 to LP. Remaining $800,000.
  - *Structure A (no catch-up):* $800,000 split 80/20 → LP $640,000, GP $160,000. LP total $6,240,000 (1.56x); GP promote = 6.7% of profit.
  - *Structure B (full catch-up to 20% of total profit):* solve C + 0.20 × ($800,000 − C) = 0.20 × $2,400,000 → C = $400,000 to GP. Residual $400,000 split 80/20 → LP $320,000, GP $80,000. LP total $5,920,000 (1.48x); GP total $480,000 = exactly 20.0% of profit. — [Agora](https://agorareal.com/learn/commercial-real-estate-waterfall-model/)
- **Worked example 2 (Agora, multi-hurdle IRR waterfall, annual distributions):** LP equity $4,000,000; distributable cash $7,400,000 over 5 years ($280k, $300k, $320k, $340k, $6,160k). Tier 1: 100% to LP until 8% IRR (compounded); Tier 2: 80/20 until LP reaches 12% IRR; Tier 3: 70/30. Years 1–4 all cash goes to LP in Tier 1. Year 5: Tier 1 $4,378,014; Tier 2 $1,283,851; Tier 3 $498,135. Totals: LP $6,993,789 (1.75x, 13.2% IRR); GP $406,211. — [Agora](https://agorareal.com/learn/commercial-real-estate-waterfall-model/)
- European vs American: "an American waterfall can pay carry after a realized investment satisfies the agreement's capital and return requirements," whereas "a European waterfall generally requires repayment of the specified fund-wide capital and preferred return before carry is paid." Clawback: "can require the GP to repay excess carry after later results establish that it received more than its agreed entitlement." — [Agora](https://agorareal.com/learn/commercial-real-estate-waterfall-model/)
- **SPV carry (AngelList):** carry is "the share of profits that will be paid to the lead after investors have received back their original capital"; 20% is typical; "Carry is calculated only on profits above cost basis." Example: LP invests $5,000; distribution $100,000; carry = 20% × ($100,000 − $5,000) = $19,000; LP nets $81,000. SPV-level example: raise $1M, exit $10M, profit $9M, carry $1.8M, LPs receive $8.2M. — [AngelList Help, Carry](https://help.angellist.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048801431-Carry-Carried-Interest); [AngelList Help, How does carry work for SPVs](https://help.angellist.com/hc/en-us/articles/360047684032-How-does-carried-interest-carry-work-for-SPVs); [AngelList Learn, Carried interest](https://www.angellist.com/learn/carried-interest)

**D. Cooperative patronage dividends (by usage) and limited interest on shares**
- ICA Principle 3: "Members usually receive limited compensation, if any, on capital subscribed as a condition of membership. Members allocate surpluses for any or all of the following purposes: developing their cooperative, possibly by setting up reserves, part of which at least would be indivisible; benefiting members in proportion to their transactions with the cooperative; and supporting other activities approved by the membership." — [ICA, Cooperative identity](https://ica.coop/en/cooperatives/cooperative-identity)
- US definition: "A patronage refund is the net income of a cooperative allocated to a patron in proportion to the value or quantity of business the patron does with the cooperative"; it is paid to patrons "whose business with the cooperative produced the margin being returned." The cooperative must pay at least 20 percent of a qualified patronage refund in cash; the remainder is equity issued in the member's name, "payable at some point in the future." — [USDA RD, Income Tax Treatment of Cooperatives: Patronage Refunds (CIR 44-2)](https://www.rd.usda.gov/files/cir44-2.pdf); [Southern States, Patronage refunds](https://southernstates.com/pages/patronage-refunds)
- **Worked example (Iowa State Extension):** member sells 100,000 bushels; co-op declares a qualified refund of $0.03/bushel, 50% in cash. Allocation $3,000; cash now $1,500; retained as allocated equity $1,500; effective price uplift 1.5¢/bushel. The allocation is taxable income to the member in the year allocated. Historical patronage rates 1.5–5¢/bushel; cash portions typically 30–60% (minimum 20%). — [Iowa State Extension, How patronage affects your net marketing price](https://www.extension.iastate.edu/agdm/crops/html/a2-80.html)
- Example 2: Southern States declared a patronage refund of 4.7% of purchase volume; a member who bought $50,000 of inputs would be allocated $2,350. — [Southern States](https://southernstates.com/pages/patronage-refunds)
- **Nigeria (statute):** Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act s.34: "A registered society shall not pay a dividend or bonus or otherwise distribute any part of its net surplus except as provided under this Act and until the proposal payment or distribution has been approved by the committee"; "At least one fourth of the net profits … shall be paid into a fund to be called the 'reserve fund'"; after that, with the Director's approval, up to ten per cent of the remainder may go to an education fund; unlimited-liability societies need Minister/Commissioner approval to distribute. s.27: "No member, other than a registered society, shall hold more than one fifth of the share capital of a society." s.44(d) (on liquidation): dividend "at a rate not exceeding five per cent per annum." Definitions (s.57): "bonus" = share of surplus "divided among its members in proportion to the volume of business done by them with the society"; "dividend" = share of profits "in proportion to the share capital held by them." Regulations may prescribe "the maximum rate of dividend." — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act (FAO Faolex PDF)](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Secondary Nigerian commentary states bye-laws "may provide for the payment of a dividend at a rate not exceeding five per cent per annum" and that co-operative societies are exempt from companies income tax. — [Mondaq, Establishing a cooperative society for investment purposes in Nigeria](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/996640/establishing-a-cooperative-society-for-investment-purposes-in-nigeria); [UNN FAQs](https://www.unn.edu.ng/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/FAQs.pdf)

**E. Dynamic "Slicing Pie" equity for startups**
- Formula: "Individual's percentage share = Individual's Slices ÷ All Slices." A slice is a normalised unit of at-risk contributions: "time, money, ideas, relationships, supplies, equipment, facilities or anything else someone provides without full payment of its fair market value." Pie freezes at breakeven or when enough capital is raised to pay participants. — [Slicing Pie, Learn the model](https://slicingpie.com/learn-slicing-pie-model/)
- Multipliers: unpaid work 2x fair-market hourly rate; below-market salary 2x on the unpaid portion only; unreimbursed expenses 2x; equipment/supplies 1x fair market (resale) value; IP 1x–2x of development cost; at-risk cash 4x. Hourly rate (GHRR) = annual market salary ÷ 2,000; a $150,000 developer = $75/hr; 20 unpaid hours = 20 × $75 × 2 = $3,000 in slices. Relationships/sales typically 5–10% of resulting deal value. — [Equity Matrix, Slicing Pie guide](https://equitymatrix.io/blog/slicing-pie-guide); [Vestd, Slicing Pie](https://www.vestd.com/blog/slicing-pie-a-tasty-way-to-share-equity)
- **Worked example (Equity Matrix, month 1):** A (dev) 40 hrs/wk × $80 × 2 = $6,400 slices/wk → 15.4%; B (business) 30 hrs/wk × $60 × 2 plus $30,000 cash × 4 = $134,400 total → 81.0%; C (advisor) 5 hrs/wk × $150 × 2 = $1,500/wk → 3.6%. After 6 months: A 38.8%, B 52.1%, C 9.1% — "consistent work compounds over time." On freeze, a 4-year vest with 1-year cliff typically applies. — [Equity Matrix](https://equitymatrix.io/blog/slicing-pie-guide)

### Inferences
- **Pro-rata to capital, illustrative (my arithmetic applying the "share in capital ratio" method):** five members contribute ₦5.0m, ₦2.2m, ₦1.5m, ₦2.0m, ₦0 (total ₦10.7m); profit ₦2,140,000 shared by capital → A ₦1,000,000 (46.7%), B ₦440,000, C ₦300,000, D ₦400,000, E ₦0. This is why pure capital-ratio sharing is unworkable where one member contributes only labour; the fix is a salary step before the residual.
- **Salary + interest + residual, illustrative on the same group:** E (full-time manager) salary ₦600,000; interest on capital 10% = ₦1,070,000 (A ₦500k, B ₦220k, C ₦150k, D ₦200k); residual ₦470,000 split equally ₦94,000 each. Totals: A ₦594k, B ₦314k, C ₦244k, D ₦294k, E ₦694k. Same ₦2.14m profit, radically different outcome — the deed, not the capital, decides.
- The waterfall catch-up algebra generalises: with promote p and total profit P, GP catch-up C satisfies C + p(R − C) = pP where R is cash remaining after pref; so C = p(P − R)/(1 − p) = p × Pref/(1 − p). In the Agora example: 0.2 × 1,600,000 / 0.8 = $400,000, matching the source.
- Cooperatives are structurally a "fixed-interest-on-capital then residual-by-usage" partnership: Nigerian law caps the capital step (≤5%) and forces a 25% reserve step before any patronage bonus.

### Gaps
- The ACCA FA2 Amit/Burton example as fetched appears to *subtract* interest on drawings from divisible profit; ACCA's own convention (and the F3 article) treats it as a credit to the appropriation account. I could not reconcile whether this is a transcription error in my fetch or in the article; the report writer should use the figures with caution or recompute.
- No source gave a worked Nigerian cooperative surplus-appropriation example (25% reserve → ≤10% education → ≤5% dividend → patronage bonus); the sequence is inferred from s.34 and s.57 definitions.
- Co-operatives UK handbook on share interest (section 6.2) and USDA CIR 44-2 full text returned HTTP 403; patronage rules are cited from the search excerpt and Iowa State/Southern States instead.

## Key Question 3: Loss sharing; negative capital accounts

### Takeaway
By default losses follow the profit-sharing ratio (and the appropriation of salaries/interest can itself turn a small profit into a shared loss); a deficit capital account is a debt the member owes the firm, and if the member cannot pay it on dissolution the shortfall is borne by the solvent partners in their *capital* ratio (Garner v Murray), not the profit ratio — unless the deed says otherwise.

### Cited Findings
- Default rule: partners "must contribute equally towards the losses whether of capital or otherwise sustained by the firm" absent agreement. — [Partnership Act 1890 s.24](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1890/act/39/section/24/enacted/en/html); Lagos Partnership Law s.23 identical in effect — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=1f0ca982-779b-4c17-bcef-2788c265dc0c)
- When appropriations (salaries + interest) exceed profit, "the amount to be shared between the partners will be a loss," debited to current accounts in the PSR. — [ACCA FA2](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/foundation-level-study-resources/fa2/fa2-technical-articles/accounting-for-partnerships.html)
- "When allowances exceed net income, losses are allocated according to the same profit-sharing ratio used for gains." — [Wikipedia, Partnership accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership_accounting)
- Garner v Murray: "the deficiency of insolvent partner will be borne by solvent partners in proportion to their capital accounts and not in accordance with the profit sharing ratio." Realisation losses are shared in PSR; the deficiency loss only by solvent partners in capital ratio. Applies only where there is no contrary agreement. **Worked example:** equal-profit partners A ₹60,000 Cr, B ₹40,000 Cr, C ₹20,000 Dr (insolvent). Capital ratio A:B = 3:2 → A bears ₹12,000, B ₹8,000; resulting capitals A ₹48,000, B ₹32,000 [source states ₹40,000 for B, which appears to be a typo — 40,000 − 8,000 = 32,000]. Under fixed capital use last balance-sheet capitals; under fluctuating capital first adjust for reserves, undistributed profits and drawings, excluding realisation losses. — [Testbook, Garner vs Murray rule](https://testbook.com/ugc-net-commerce/garner-vs-murray-rule); [FinanceDictionaryPro](https://financedictionarypro.com/definitions/g/garner-v-murray/)
- US tax side: a partner's basis is "decreased (but not below zero)" by distributions and losses, and partners cannot deduct losses exceeding their adjusted basis in the partnership interest. — [IRS Publication 541](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p541)
- US-style agreements separate *allocations* (paper, K-1) from *distributions* (cash); "Liquidating distributions must be made in accordance with partners' capital accounts," which is why capital accounts should be updated at least quarterly. — [Milestone CPAs, Partnership tax basics](https://milestonecpas.com/partnership-tax-basics/); [ABA, Deciphering tax provisions in an operating agreement](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/real_property_trust_estate/resources/probate-property/2025-march-april/deciphering-tax-provisions-operating-agreement/)
- Cooperatives: USDA publishes dedicated guidance on cooperative handling of losses (CIR 44-5). — [USDA RD, Handling of Losses](https://www.rd.usda.gov/sites/default/files/cir44-5.pdf) *(fetch returned 403; content not verified)*

### Inferences
- A good deed should state three things about losses: (1) the loss ratio (usually = profit ratio, but a sweat-equity member with zero capital is often given a 0% loss share and a capped profit share); (2) whether a negative current/capital account must be restored in cash and by when (a "deficit restoration" or capital-call clause); (3) whether Garner v Murray or an alternative (e.g., deficiency shared in PSR) applies on dissolution.
- Illustrative (my arithmetic): in the 5-member venture, a year with ₦300,000 loss *after* paying E's ₦600,000 salary and 10% interest on capital produces a divisible loss of ₦300,000 + ₦600,000 + ₦1,070,000 = ₦1,970,000 to be debited to current accounts in PSR — members must understand that salaries and interest are still credited even in a loss year unless the deed makes them contingent on profit.

### Gaps
- No Nigerian case law applying Garner v Murray was found; the rule is English and its reception in Nigerian courts was not verified.
- USDA CIR 44-5 (cooperative losses) could not be fetched; whether losses must be allocated by patronage or may be absorbed by unallocated reserves is unverified.

## Key Question 4: Distributions vs retained earnings; drawings; distribution policies and reserves

### Takeaway
Allocation of profit (what each member *earns*) and distribution (what each member *is paid*) are separate decisions; well-run groups fix in writing the order of cash use (reserves → debt → tax distributions → discretionary distributions), set drawing limits (often with interest charged on drawings), and — for cooperatives in Nigeria — are legally forced to retain at least 25% of net profit as reserve.

### Cited Findings
- "A partnership can allocate profits to a partner for the year without making a matching cash distribution. That is where 'phantom income' appears." Rules should be set in advance for when distributions occur, how much cash is held for taxes, operating reserves and reinvestment, and what happens when profit is allocated but cash is constrained. — [Philly Small Business Lawyer](https://phillysmallbusinesslawyer.com/business-success-tips/how-to-handle-distributions-allocations-and-profitloss-division-in-partnerships/)
- Tax-distribution clauses require the entity "to distribute at least enough cash for partners to cover the tax on their allocated income." — [Hawkins Ash CPAs](https://www.hawkinsash.cpa/navigating-tax-complexities-craft-partnership-agreements-and-llc-operating-agreements-with-precision/); [DTN, Key tax provisions](https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/magazine/article/2024/10/01/key-tax-provisions-every-partnership)
- Sample distribution-policy clause: distribute cash "not needed and may not reasonably be expected to be needed for partnership purposes, including repayment of partnership obligations, or establishing reasonable reserves." Some fund LPAs cap reinvested proceeds at the aggregate of organisational expenses, operating expenses and management fees. — [Law Insider, Distribution policy clauses](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/distribution-policy)
- Drawings are debited to the partner's current account; interest on drawings (e.g., 8% p.a. in the ACCA example — Amit $1,440 on $18,000; Burton $2,480 on $31,000) is charged to the partner and credited to the appropriation account, i.e., it penalises early/large withdrawals. — [ACCA FA2](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/foundation-level-study-resources/fa2/fa2-technical-articles/accounting-for-partnerships.html)
- Cooperative retained patronage: at least 20% of a qualified patronage refund must be paid in cash; the rest is retained as allocated equity "payable at some point in the future" (revolving equity). — [USDA CIR 44-2](https://www.rd.usda.gov/files/cir44-2.pdf); [Iowa State Extension](https://www.extension.iastate.edu/agdm/crops/html/a2-80.html)
- ICA Principle 3 explicitly contemplates "setting up reserves, part of which at least would be indivisible." — [ICA](https://ica.coop/en/cooperatives/cooperative-identity)
- Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act s.34(2)–(3): at least one-fourth of audited net profit to the reserve fund (Director may exempt/reduce for limited-liability societies); then up to 10% of the remainder to an education fund with Director approval; distribution without committee approval is an offence (fine or six months' imprisonment, s.34(4)). — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Real-estate syndicates distribute via the waterfall as cash arises (annual distributions in Agora Example 2: $280k, $300k, $320k, $340k then $6.16m at exit), and a clawback protects LPs when early GP distributions exceed final entitlement. — [Agora](https://agorareal.com/learn/commercial-real-estate-waterfall-model/)

### Inferences
- A workable five-member policy, consistent with the sources: (1) monthly drawings capped at an agreed fraction of expected annual share (e.g., 50%), charged interest at the deed rate; (2) after year-end accounts, allocate profit via the appropriation account; (3) transfer an agreed % (Nigerian co-ops: ≥25%) to a reserve; (4) pay a tax distribution; (5) distribute a fixed payout ratio (say 60%) of the remainder pro-rata to allocated profit and credit the balance to current accounts as reinvested capital. Writing the *ratio* and the *trigger* in the deed is what removes the annual argument.
- Phantom income is a real issue in Nigeria for partnerships because partners are taxed personally on their share of partnership profit (Personal Income Tax) whether or not distributed — hence the value of a tax-distribution clause even outside the US. *(The PIT treatment itself is general knowledge not verified by a fetched source — flagged as inference.)*

### Gaps
- No source quantified typical payout/retention ratios for small Nigerian partnerships or cooperatives.
- Co-operatives UK guidance on share interest ("no more than necessary to attract capital") returned 403 and is not cited.

## Key Question 5: Changes in membership — admission, withdrawal, death, buyout formulas, ROFR, vesting

### Takeaway
Admission is accounted for by either the goodwill method (recognise implied goodwill and credit old partners in the old ratio) or the bonus method (no goodwill; the difference between cash paid and book capital shifts between old and new partners); withdrawal is the mirror image. Buy-sell clauses fix price by fixed/agreed value, formula (e.g., multiple of earnings), book value, or independent appraisal, with triggers (death, disability, retirement, divorce), a right of first refusal, and funding via insurance or instalment notes; sweat-equity members are protected against "dead equity" by vesting (4 years, 1-year cliff) or Slicing Pie's recovery rules.

### Cited Findings

**Admission — goodwill method (ACCA style, goodwill created then written off):**
- Andrew & Binta (old PSR 4:3) admit Chen (new PSR 3:2:1); goodwill valued $42,000; capitals Andrew $60,000, Binta $40,000. Step 1 Dr Goodwill $42,000; Cr Andrew $24,000 (4/7), Cr Binta $18,000 (3/7) → $84,000 and $58,000. Step 2 eliminate goodwill in the new ratio: Dr Andrew $21,000, Dr Binta $14,000, Dr Chen $7,000; Cr Goodwill $42,000 → Andrew $63,000, Binta $44,000, Chen $7,000 Dr. Step 3 Chen contributes $7,000 cash to clear his debit. Net effect: Andrew +$3,000, Binta +$4,000 compensated for the share of goodwill given up. — [ACCA FA2](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/foundation-level-study-resources/fa2/fa2-technical-articles/accounting-for-partnerships.html)

**Admission — US-style goodwill vs bonus methods (Double Entry Bookkeeping):** existing capital A+B = 65,000, PSR 70:30; C to receive 20%.
- *At book value:* required capital = 65,000 ÷ 80% = 81,250; C invests 16,250. Dr Cash 16,250 / Cr Capital C 16,250.
- *Goodwill method, investment above book:* C invests 30,000 → implied value 30,000 ÷ 20% = 150,000; goodwill = 150,000 − 95,000 = 55,000; Dr Cash 30,000, Dr Goodwill 55,000; Cr A 38,500, Cr B 16,500, Cr C 30,000.
- *Goodwill method, investment below book:* C invests 14,000 → implied value 65,000 ÷ 80% = 81,250; goodwill 2,250 attributed to C; Dr Cash 14,000, Dr Goodwill 2,250; Cr C 16,250.
- *Bonus method, above book:* total capital 95,000; C's capital 95,000 × 20% = 19,000; bonus to old partners 11,000 → A 7,700, B 3,300. Dr Cash 30,000; Cr C 19,000, Cr A 7,700, Cr B 3,300.
- *Bonus method, below book:* total 79,000; C's capital 15,800; bonus to C 1,800 absorbed A 1,260, B 540. — [Double Entry Bookkeeping, Admission of a new partner](https://www.double-entry-bookkeeping.com/partnership/admission-of-a-new-partner/); bonus-method definition — [AccountingTools](https://www.accountingtools.com/articles/bonus-method)
- Admission requires consent: default rule that no person may be introduced as a partner without the consent of all existing partners (s.24(7)). — [Partnership Act 1890 s.24](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1890/act/39/section/24/enacted/en/html)

**Withdrawal / retirement:**
- Retiring partners may be paid equal to, above or below their capital balance; the difference is allocated among remaining partners per the agreement — a gain to continuing partners if paid below book, a loss (or goodwill) if above. — [Wikipedia, Partnership accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership_accounting)
- Law-firm practice: capital returned over 1–5 years depending on circumstances, net of offsets. — [LeanLaw](https://www.leanlaw.co/blog/the-financial-mechanics-of-partner-capital-contributions-how-theyre-valued-paid-and-recorded/)

**Buy-sell valuation and triggers:**
- Four methods: fixed price (simple, but "not appropriate for transfer-tax purposes" as it goes stale); independent appraisal (carries weight with tax authorities, costs money); formula ("a multiple of earnings"); book value (courts may find it "arbitrary in nature"). Triggers: "death, disability, retirement, and divorce." Structures: mandatory purchase obligation or right of first refusal. Funding: life insurance (immediate liquidity on death) or promissory notes (extends payment, raises risk for remaining members). — [The Tax Adviser, Using a buy/sell agreement to establish value](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2020/feb/buy-sell-agreement-value-business-interest/)
- Book value = (assets − liabilities) × ownership %; suits asset-intensive firms. Fixed value is "agreed upon annually or periodically." Common formulas: multiple of EBITDA (e.g., 3x), multiple of revenue, hybrid. Many agreements "use a formula as the default and allow an independent appraisal if the owners disagree by more than a set percentage." — [Virtue CPAs, Partner buyout valuation](https://virtuecpas.com/business-valuation-partner-buyouts/); [Partnership Agreement Template, Buyout clause](https://partnershipagreementtemplate.com/buyout-clause); [Sofer Advisors](https://soferadvisors.com/insights/blog/business-valuation-for-partner-buyout-complete-transition-guide/)
- Nigerian deeds should cover "Procedures for admitting new partners, retirement, and expulsion," "What happens upon death or incapacity," "How departing partners' shares are valued and paid out," and non-compete/confidentiality. — [LegalDoc.ng](https://legaldoc.ng/glossary/partnership-deed)
- Nigerian partnership cap: general and limited partnerships cannot exceed 20 partners; LLPs (CAMA 2020) have no cap and are separate legal entities. — [Mondaq, Registering business partnerships in Nigeria](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1430552/registering-business-partnerships-in-nigeria-general-partnerships-limited-partnerships-and-limited-liability-partnerships-in-nigeria)
- Nigerian cooperatives: no individual may hold more than one-fifth of share capital (s.27); transfer of a member's share/interest is restricted (s.28); regulations/bye-laws govern "the payments, if any, to be made to members who withdraw or are expelled." — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Cooperative equity redemption (revolving funds, base capital plans, age-of-patron and estate redemption) is covered in USDA RR 124. — [USDA RD, Equity redemption and member equity allocation (RR124)](https://www.rd.usda.gov/files/rr124.pdf) *(fetch returned 403; content unverified)*

**Vesting / sweat-equity protection:**
- Industry standard founder vesting: four years with a one-year cliff — 25% vests at 12 months, remaining 75% monthly over 36 months; unvested shares revert to the company on departure. — [Carta, Vesting explained](https://carta.com/learn/equity/stock-options/vesting/); [Cake Equity, Founder vesting](https://www.cakeequity.com/cliff-notes/founder-equity-founder-vesting); [Equity Matrix, Vesting schedules](https://equitymatrix.io/blog/vesting-schedules-explained)
- Slicing Pie recovery: "If a person is terminated for cause or resigns without cause they will lose slices and forfeit their rights to future equity. If, however, he or she is terminated without cause or resigns with cause the slices stay in the Pie and convert to equity along with everyone else." For-cause/resign-without-cause: unreimbursed expenses repaid "when and if possible." Buyback is triggered when the Pie freezes. — [Slicing Pie, Slices that can survive separation](https://slicingpie.com/slices-that-can-survive-separation/); [Slicing Pie, How to fire a co-founder](https://slicingpie.com/how-to-fire-a-cofounder/)
- "Dead equity" — departed members retaining stakes while others keep contributing — is the main failure mode vesting and recovery rules address. — [Equity Matrix](https://equitymatrix.io/blog/slicing-pie-guide)

### Inferences
- For a small Nigerian venture the practical combination is: *agreed value* reset annually at the AGM (recorded in the minutes), a *formula fallback* (e.g., adjusted book value + N× average profits) if no agreement is reached, and *independent valuation* only when the parties differ by more than a set percentage — matching the "formula default, appraisal on disagreement" pattern in the sources. Payment over 2–3 years by instalment note, with life insurance for death triggers, follows the cited norms.
- Goodwill vs bonus choice matters for later exits: the goodwill method inflates total capital (and the book-value buyout of a later leaver), the bonus method does not. Groups using book value as buyout price should deliberately pick one and state it.
- Dilution arithmetic for admission under the bonus method (illustrative, my arithmetic): if five members hold ₦10.7m capital equally-ish and admit a sixth for 1/6 for ₦2.5m, total capital ₦13.2m, new member capital ₦2.2m, bonus ₦0.3m credited to old members in PSR; old members' percentage falls from 20% to 16.67% each regardless of the method — "dilution" is in percentage terms even when capital accounts rise.

### Gaps
- No source gave a Nigerian-specific statutory default on the valuation of a deceased partner's share (the 1890 Act s.42 allows the estate 5% interest or a profit share on undrawn capital, but I did not retrieve that section and have not cited it).
- USDA RR124 on cooperative equity redemption could not be fetched; typical revolving periods are unverified.

## Key Question 6: Partner capital account bookkeeping; statement of partners' equity; a simple 5-person set of books

### Takeaway
Each member's equity is tracked as: opening balance + contributions + share of profit (salary + interest + residual) − drawings − share of loss = closing balance, presented in a statement of partners' equity; many firms split this into a fixed capital account and a fluctuating current account so that the contribution base for interest stays stable and overdrawn members are visible.

### Cited Findings
- Statement of partners' equity "starts with capital balances at the beginning of the accounting period, and reflects additional investments, made by the partners during the year, net income for the period, and withdrawals." Formula: beginning balance + contributions ± share of net income/loss − withdrawals = ending balance. — [Wikipedia, Partnership accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership_accounting); [Shajani CPA, Statement of partners' equity](https://shajani.ca/the-statement-of-partners-equity/); [Fiveable](https://fiveable.me/financial-accounting-ii/key-terms/statement-of-partners-equity)
- Worked example: Partner A begins $50,000, contributes $10,000, is allocated $15,000 (50%), withdraws $5,000 → ends $70,000; Partner B begins $40,000, contributes $15,000, allocated $15,000, withdraws $8,000 → ends $62,000. — [Shajani CPA](https://shajani.ca/the-statement-of-partners-equity/)
- Fixed capital + current account mechanics; appropriation entries: salaries Dr Appropriation / Cr Current; interest on capital Dr Appropriation / Cr Current; interest on drawings Dr Current / Cr Appropriation; residual to current accounts in PSR. — [ACCA FA2](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/foundation-level-study-resources/fa2/fa2-technical-articles/accounting-for-partnerships.html); [ACCA F3](https://www.accaglobal.com/us/en/student/exam-support-resources/fundamentals-exams-study-resources/f3/technical-articles/working-partnership.html)
- Capital accounts "should be updated at least quarterly, reconciling profits, losses, and debt." — [Milestone CPAs](https://milestonecpas.com/partnership-tax-basics/)
- Partner loans sit as liabilities (not equity) with interest expensed. — [ACCA FA2](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/foundation-level-study-resources/fa2/fa2-technical-articles/accounting-for-partnerships.html)

### Inferences
- **Illustrative 5-member set of books (my arithmetic, applying the sourced mechanics).** Deed: fixed capital accounts; interest on capital 10%; E salary ₦600,000; interest on drawings 5%; residual equal; member loan from D ₦1,000,000 at 10% (liability). Opening capitals A ₦5,000,000; B ₦2,200,000; C ₦1,500,000; D ₦2,000,000; E ₦0. Profit before loan interest ₦2,240,000; loan interest ₦100,000 → net profit ₦2,140,000. Drawings: A ₦400,000; B ₦200,000; C ₦100,000; D ₦200,000; E ₦500,000 (interest on drawings 5%: ₦20,000; ₦10,000; ₦5,000; ₦10,000; ₦25,000 = ₦70,000).

  Appropriation account: Net profit 2,140,000 + interest on drawings 70,000 = 2,210,000; less salary (E) 600,000; less interest on capital 1,070,000 (A 500,000; B 220,000; C 150,000; D 200,000); residual 540,000 ÷ 5 = 108,000 each.

  Current accounts (₦): 
  | | A | B | C | D | E |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Salary | – | – | – | – | 600,000 |
  | Interest on capital | 500,000 | 220,000 | 150,000 | 200,000 | – |
  | Residual | 108,000 | 108,000 | 108,000 | 108,000 | 108,000 |
  | Less drawings | (400,000) | (200,000) | (100,000) | (200,000) | (500,000) |
  | Less interest on drawings | (20,000) | (10,000) | (5,000) | (10,000) | (25,000) |
  | Closing current a/c | 188,000 | 118,000 | 153,000 | 98,000 | 183,000 |

  Check: total credited to partners = 600,000 + 1,070,000 + 540,000 = 2,210,000; total drawings + interest on drawings = 1,400,000 + 70,000 = 1,470,000; net increase in current accounts 740,000 = sum of row (188+118+153+98+183 = 740k). Balance sheet equity = capital ₦10,700,000 + current ₦740,000 = ₦11,440,000; D's ₦1,000,000 loan is shown separately under liabilities.
- A minimal chart of accounts: Bank; Fixed assets (by contributor, for valuation audit trail); Member loan (D); Capital accounts ×5; Current accounts ×5; Drawings ×5 (closed to current at year end); Appropriation account; plus normal trading accounts. The statement of partners' equity is then simply the current-account table above with the capital row added.

### Gaps
- No source showed a published Nigerian small-partnership financial statement to confirm local presentation norms (e.g., whether fixed/fluctuating capital is the common convention in Nigeria).

## Key Question 7: Common disputes and how good agreements prevent them

### Takeaway
The recurring disputes are unequal effort versus equal shares, missed or late capital contributions/capital calls, partners running competing side businesses, and unilateral control of bank accounts; each is prevented by the same deed clauses — explicit profit/loss ratios decoupled from capital, salaries for active members, written contribution deadlines with penalties/dilution, a duty-of-loyalty/non-compete clause, and dual-signatory banking authority.

### Cited Findings
- "Disparities in effort, time, or financial investment can breed resentment… If one partner feels they are contributing more time or effort than the other, they may believe they are entitled to a larger share of the profits." Disputes "commonly stem from disagreements over capital contributions… particularly when one partner fails to provide the agreed-upon funds or questions arise about whether additional contributions are required," and "disputes over non-capital contributions arise just as frequently." — [Barnes PC, Partnership disputes over profit sharing and contributions](https://www.barnespc.com/insights/partnership-disputes-over-profit-sharing-and-contributions/); [Fahl & Donaldson](https://www.fahldonaldson.com/blog/navigating-business-partnership-disputes/)
- "Unequal capital contributions in partnerships create legal exposure by disrupting ownership percentages, profit allocation, and voting rights." — [Aaron Hall, Legal exposure from unequal capital contributions](https://aaronhall.com/legal-exposure-from-unequal-capital-contributions/)
- Capital calls: "A partner who resists contributing more funds might create resentment, particularly if the others feel the investment is necessary." — [Anelya Law, Common partnership disputes](https://anelyalaw.com/2025/02/03/common-partnership-disputes-how-to-prevent-them/)
- Side businesses: "Partners generally cannot operate or invest in a competing business without the knowledge and consent of the partnership. Running a side venture that targets the same customers or markets may violate the duty of loyalty." — [Gross Law Group](https://grosslaw.com/common-causes-of-partnership-disputes-and-how-to-resolve-them-legally/); [Cali Law](https://www.calilawpa.com/blog/when-business-partners-turn-against-you-legal-options-for-partnership-disputes)
- Bank accounts: lock-outs arise when "a partner unilaterally decides to change account access credentials, such as passwords or account signatories, without the consent or knowledge of the other partner"; the agreement "should specify who has the authority to open bank accounts and whether such actions require the consent of all members." — [Aaron Hall, Can you be locked out](https://aaronhall.com/can-you-be-locked-out/); [JustAnswer (illustrative Q&A)](https://www.justanswer.com/business-law/qn5wx-llc-company-managing-partners.html)
- Default statutory rules worsen these disputes: equal shares, equal management say, no salary, unanimous consent to admit, majority for ordinary matters (s.24). — [Partnership Act 1890 s.24](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1890/act/39/section/24/enacted/en/html); Nigerian defaults identical — [LegalDoc.ng](https://legaldoc.ng/glossary/partnership-deed)
- Nigerian deed checklist includes "Which decisions require unanimity versus majority approval," "Banking powers and signing authority," expulsion procedures, exit valuation and non-compete. Partners have unlimited, joint and several liability, so one partner's unauthorised borrowing binds all. — [LegalDoc.ng](https://legaldoc.ng/glossary/partnership-deed); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1430552/registering-business-partnerships-in-nigeria-general-partnerships-limited-partnerships-and-limited-liability-partnerships-in-nigeria)
- Mechanisms that pre-empt effort disputes: Slicing Pie's contribution-tracked equity (ownership "adjusts automatically" with logged hours and cash) and vesting with a cliff. — [Equity Matrix](https://equitymatrix.io/blog/slicing-pie-guide); [Carta](https://carta.com/learn/equity/stock-options/vesting/)
- Interest on drawings (ACCA) is itself a dispute-prevention device: it charges members who draw cash early relative to those who leave it in. — [ACCA FA2](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/foundation-level-study-resources/fa2/fa2-technical-articles/accounting-for-partnerships.html)

### Inferences
- Late contributions: the sources give the problem but not a standard remedy; a defensible deed mechanic consistent with the mechanics above is (a) interest on capital accrues only from the date each tranche is actually paid (the ACCA method computes interest on *opening* capital, so a late payer earns nothing until the next year), (b) unpaid commitments are shown as a debit in the member's current account, and (c) persistent default triggers dilution via the bonus method or expulsion.
- Bank control: require two signatories from different "camps" for payments above a threshold, monthly bank-statement circulation to all members, and a prohibition on any member being sole signatory — this directly addresses the lock-out and unauthorised-withdrawal scenarios in the sources.

### Gaps
- No Nigerian court or survey data on the frequency of specific partnership-dispute types was found; the dispute taxonomy is from US/UK practitioner sources and general Nigerian legal commentary.
- Co-operative-specific governance disputes (committee vs members over surplus distribution) were not covered by any retrieved source beyond the statutory approval requirement in s.34.
