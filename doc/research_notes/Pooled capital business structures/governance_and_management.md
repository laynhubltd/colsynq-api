# Governance and Management of a Jointly Funded Business (3–30 Co-owners): Formation to Wind-up

Scope note: these notes are drawn from (a) the text of real governing documents — the IBA Guide on Shareholders' Agreements for Nigeria (Ajibade & Co), two registered Nigerian cooperative bye-laws (ASUU Ojo Cooperative Multipurpose Society, Lagos; Niger Delta University Workers Multipurpose Cooperative Society, Bayelsa), the Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act (Cap N98) — and (b) practitioner explainers on partnership deeds, LLC operating agreements and shareholders' agreements, plus fraud/control and co-founder-conflict evidence. Where a figure comes from a secondary summary rather than the primary report, that is flagged.

---

## Key Question 1 — Formation process step by step

### Takeaway
Every source that explains why co-owned businesses fail lands on the same cause — no written agreement covering roles, money, decision rights and exit — so the formation sequence is: align on purpose/horizon → fix contributions and equity (with vesting) → pick the legal wrapper → draft and sign the agreement before money moves → open a bank account with a written mandate → register with CAC (or the State Director of Co-operatives) and calendar the annual filings.

### Cited Findings
- Nigerian partnership forms: a general partnership "would be registered with the CAC as a regular business name" and partners have "unlimited liability towards the Partnership"; an LLP is "a Corporate Individual, a separate legal entity from the partners" registered with CAC; an LP needs at least one general and one limited partner. Before CAMA 2020, partnerships were governed by state laws such as the Lagos State Partnership Law 2003 — [Mondaq / Nigerian law-firm explainer](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1430552/registering-business-partnerships-in-nigeria-general-partnerships-limited-partnerships-and-limited-liability-partnerships-in-nigeria)
- LLPs must be registered with CAC "with at least two designated partners, one of whom must be resident in Nigeria" — [Mondaq review of LLPs under CAMA 2020](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/withholding-tax/998568/a-review-of-limited-liability-partnerships-under-the-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020)
- Registering a business name "records the name; it does not create a separate legal person and it does not limit liability" — [LegalDoc.ng, Partnership Deed in Nigeria](https://legaldoc.ng/glossary/partnership-deed)
- A Nigerian shareholders' agreement (SHA) is a private contract; it "must expressly declare that it will supersede the provisions of CAMA and the company's articles of association in the event of a conflict", but shareholders "cannot contract out of some mandatory provisions in CAMA (eg, voting thresholds for the removal of a director)". SHAs "are not typically required to be filed publicly at the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)" unless the parties choose to — [IBA Guide on Shareholders' Agreements – Nigeria (Ajibade & Co)](https://www.ibanet.org/medias/iba-guide-shareholders-agreements-nigeria.pdf)
- Most Nigerian companies "adopt the format of the model articles of association provided by CAMA for ease of administration of the registration process", using the SHA to add private "by-laws" on voting, decision-making and restrictions — [IBA Guide – Nigeria](https://www.ibanet.org/medias/iba-guide-shareholders-agreements-nigeria.pdf)
- New shareholders are bound by requiring, as a condition of any share sale, that the buyer "executes, a deed of accession (in the form attached to the shareholders' agreement)" before the board approves the transfer — [IBA Guide – Nigeria](https://www.ibanet.org/medias/iba-guide-shareholders-agreements-nigeria.pdf)
- Co-operative route: a Nigerian registered society's bye-laws must (by statute) cover, among others, "the purposes to which its funds may be applied and the mode of custody and investment of its funds", "the nature and extent of the liability of members", "the withdrawal and expulsion of members and the payment, if any, to be made to those members", "the transfer of shares or interests of members", "the general meetings, and the procedure for and power of those meetings", "the appointment, suspension and removal of members of any committee", "the authorisation of an officer to sign documents and to use the seal", and "the disposal of annual surplus" — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act, s.11 (FAO text)](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Wasserman's data: "73% of founding teams split equity within the first month—often before anyone has a clear picture of who will contribute what"; equal splits "feel fair because they avoid a hard talk, but if contributions are not equal over time… equal equity can lead to resentment" — [Culta summary of Wasserman research](https://culta.ai/blog/cofounder-equity-salary-split); [Open Forest equity split guide](https://www.openforest.co/articles/founder-equity-split-guide)
- Standard founder vesting: "four years with a one-year cliff" — [Startup Shortcut](https://startupshortcut.com/knowledge-base/how-to-structure-co-founder-equity-fair-splits-vesting-explained); same figure in the Pivo analysis — [PlanetWeb.ng](https://planetweb.ng/co-founder-conflicts-in-nigerian-startups/)
- "Calculate financial investments separately from sweat equity to maintain clarity about what compensates cash versus work" — [iCanPitch sweat equity guide](https://www.icanpitch.com/blog/sweat-equity-guide)
- A diaspora/Africa-focused playbook: every partnership needs a written agreement covering "equity/vesting, roles, salaries, profit distribution, decision authority, exit mechanics, and dispute resolution"; author's own 50-50, no-contract venture in Congo ended after 18 months and "$15,000 on legal dissolution fees" — [Nohemie Co, Diaspora Playbook](https://nohemieco.substack.com/p/diaspora-playbook-why-partnerships)
- Cooperative bank mandate as written in bye-laws: "The Society shall operate a Trustee Bank Account. Before money is withdrawn from the Bank, the cheques or withdrawal slip shall be signed by at least two of the Trustees" (Trustees = President, Secretary, Treasurer) — [ASUU (Ojo) Cooperative bye-laws, cl. 9.4, 6.8](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf); NDU version: cheques "signed by the Treasurer and President. In the absence of any one of these officers the Secretary General signs" — [NDU Workers Cooperative bye-laws, cl. 22](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf)
- CAC annual returns: private companies must file "within 42 days of their Annual General Meeting"; business names (incl. general partnerships) "not later than 30th day of June each year, except the year it was registered"; "Every registered entity is legally required to file annual returns regardless of whether the business traded" — [SmartSMS CAC annual-returns guide](https://smartsmssolutions.com/resources/blog/ng/ng-02-hub-cac-annual-returns); [cacregister.com.ng on partnership returns](https://cacregister.com.ng/blog/annual-returns-for-partnership-business); [Mondaq, annual returns with CAC](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1487672/all-you-need-to-know-about-annual-returns-with-cac)

### Inferences
- A practical formation checklist for 3–30 Nigerian co-investors: (1) one-page purpose/time-horizon memo signed by all; (2) contribution schedule (cash, assets, time) with equity and vesting for active members; (3) structure choice — LLP or Ltd company via CAC if liability protection matters, cooperative society via State Director if the group wants one-member-one-vote and a supervisory committee by law, business-name partnership only if the group accepts unlimited liability; (4) sign the deed/SHA/bye-laws *before* the first transfer of funds; (5) open the account with a board/general-meeting resolution fixing signatories and limits; (6) register, then diarise annual returns (30 June for business names; 42 days post-AGM for companies).
- Because the SHA is private and overrides model articles, Nigerian groups can keep sensitive terms (profit split, vetoes, buyout formula) out of the public CAC record while still binding future members via deed of accession.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve a Nigerian LLP-specific annual-return deadline from a primary source (only "must maintain annual returns").
- No source gave default statutory rules (if no deed) for Nigerian general partnerships post-CAMA 2020; UK Partnership Act 1890 defaults (equal shares, majority for ordinary matters, unanimity for new partners) are the usual reference point in Nigerian practice but I did not confirm from a Nigerian primary source.

## Key Question 2 — Decision rights: ordinary vs reserved matters, voting basis, quorum, proxy

### Takeaway
Real agreements run a three-tier system: day-to-day decisions to the manager/committee; ordinary matters by simple majority; a scheduled list of "reserved matters" (borrowing above a limit, admitting members, selling major assets, changing profit split, amending the agreement, dissolution) by two-thirds/75% or unanimity. Companies vote by capital (CAMA special resolution = 75% of votes cast, 21 days' notice); cooperatives vote one-member-one-vote with a chairman's casting vote and quorum of one-quarter of members.

### Cited Findings
- Reserved matters that "may require unanimity or enhanced majority approval include borrowing, leases, large asset purchases, litigation, guarantees, changes to business lines, and opening/closing sites"; default rule: "ordinary matters are decided by majority, however, changes to the nature of the business, admission of new partners, or changes to the agreement require unanimity" — [PastPaperHero, partnership governance (UK SQE)](https://www.pastpaperhero.com/resources/sqe1-flk1-partnership-governance-common-provisions-in-partnership-agreements); [Holt Law](https://djholtlaw.com/key-considerations-for-partnership-agreements/)
- Under the Partnership Act 1890, "no new person can be admitted as a partner without the consent of all existing partners" unless agreed otherwise — [Moore Barlow guide](https://www.moorebarlow.com/guides/guide-to-partnership-agreements/)
- Partnership deed should "define how routine and major decisions will be made and state whether decisions require unanimous consent, majority approval, or the consent of specific managing partners", with higher thresholds for "borrowing, admitting new partners, changing business activity, or selling major assets" — [MHCO Law, key clauses in a partnership deed](https://mhcolaw.com/insights/key-clauses-every-partnership-deed-must-include/163)
- LLC supermajority norms: "two-thirds (66.7%) to three-fourths (75%)"; examples: "75% supermajority for operating agreement amendments", "two-thirds vote for mergers or acquisitions", "75% threshold for LLC dissolution", "66.7% to 75% for significant capital expenditures" — [Aaron Hall, Supermajority Vote Requirements in LLC Agreements](https://aaronhall.com/supermajority-vote-requirements-in-llc-agreements/)
- Sample clause: "The following actions shall require a [supermajority / two-thirds / unanimous] vote: amendment of this Agreement, dissolution, sale of substantially all assets, admission of new Members, and any transaction between the Company and a Member." Vote counting by units: "if there are 1,000 outstanding LLC Units, 667 affirmative votes are required" — [LegalGPS, voting rights](https://www.legalgps.com/llc-operating-agreement/voting-rights); [LegalGPS, material decisions](https://www.legalgps.com/llc-operating-agreement/material-decisions)
- Nigeria companies: ordinary resolution = "simple majority of votes cast by members… in person or by proxy"; special resolution = "at least three-fourths of the votes cast… at a general meeting of which 21 days' notice, specifying the intention to propose the resolution as a special resolution, has been duly given" — [CAMA 2020 s.258 (LawGlobal Hub)](https://www.lawglobalhub.com/section-258-companies-and-allied-matters-act-cama-2020/); [Tekedia on CAMA 2020 resolutions](https://www.tekedia.com/new-provisions-on-resolutions-under-the-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020-nigeria/)
- Nigerian SHAs commonly contain: "shareholders' meetings, quorum, voting thresholds and veto power", "funding and financing of operations", "shareholder loans and repayment", "suretyships and guarantees", "forced sales", "drag-along and tag-along rights", "disputes and deadlock resolution", "arbitration". Reserved matters in Nigerian SHAs typically include "taking on debt above a specified threshold, making acquisitions, changing the nature of the business, or approving related-party transactions" — [IBA Guide – Nigeria](https://www.ibanet.org/medias/iba-guide-shareholders-agreements-nigeria.pdf); [Enebeli & Partners](https://goenebeli.com/why-every-nigerian-business-needs-a-shareholders-agreement-before-the-first-deal/)
- Minority protection via SHA in Nigeria: "right to nominate the chair of the board… and to have mandatory quorum requirements that includes at least one nominee director of the minority at every meeting", plus "the power to veto certain decisions" — [IBA Guide – Nigeria](https://www.ibanet.org/medias/iba-guide-shareholders-agreements-nigeria.pdf)
- Cooperative statute: "A member of a registered society shall be entitled to have one vote only… provided that (a) in the case of an equality of votes, the chairman shall have a casting vote" — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act s.25](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf); ICA 2nd principle: "In primary cooperatives members have equal voting rights (one member, one vote)" — [ICA Guidance Notes](https://ica.coop/sites/default/files/basic-page-attachments/guidance-notes-en-221700169.pdf)
- Cooperative quorum and resolutions (ASUU Ojo): quorum "at least a quarter of the members"; if no quorum, adjourn "not less than 7 days or more than 15 days" and those present then form a quorum; secret ballot if "demanded by, at least, five members present"; Ordinary Resolution = "simple majority of votes cast… 7 days' Notice"; Special Resolution = "not less than three fourth of the votes cast… in person at a General Meeting of which 21 days Notice"; removal of a committee member and amending bye-laws require special resolution — [ASUU (Ojo) bye-laws cl. 5.2, 5.6](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- NDU cooperative: general body meets "at least once in three months (i.e. quarterly)"; quorum one quarter of members (one tenth if >1,000 members); at AGM "attendance by proxy can also count"; "The President shall have a casting vote in addition to his ordinary vote, if there is a tie" — [NDU bye-laws cl. 15–16, 20](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf)
- Three-tier decision framework recommended after the Pivo collapse: "CEO authority over daily operations, joint founder approval for major strategic moves (fundraising, senior hires), and board sign-off for exits or major pivots" — [PlanetWeb.ng](https://planetweb.ng/co-founder-conflicts-in-nigerian-startups/)
- Shared-decision threshold example: "capital expenditures >$10K require both partners" — [Nohemie Co](https://nohemieco.substack.com/p/diaspora-playbook-why-partnerships)

### Inferences
- A workable reserved-matters schedule for a 3–30 member group: **simple majority** (by capital in a company/LLP, per head in a cooperative) for budget approval, hiring below a salary cap, contracts within budget; **75% / special resolution** for borrowing or guarantees above a naira limit, capex above a limit, related-party transactions, changing the manager, distributions outside the agreed policy, amending the agreement; **unanimity** for admitting a new member, changing the profit-sharing ratio, changing the business, selling substantially all assets, voluntary dissolution. The 75% figure aligns with both CAMA s.258 and cooperative special resolutions, so one threshold serves both wrappers.
- Capital-weighted voting protects large funders; per-head voting (cooperative) protects the many small funders. Groups with wide contribution disparity should pick a wrapper deliberately rather than by default.

### Gaps
- No Nigerian source fixed a *customary* naira borrowing or capex threshold; thresholds are bespoke per agreement.
- Proxy rules in LLC/partnership agreements were not covered by the sources fetched (Aaron Hall article did not address proxy/quorum).

## Key Question 3 — Roles: managing partner vs passive investors; committees; paying active members

### Takeaway
Governing documents separate a governing body (board / management committee elected by members, with fixed tenure and fiduciary duties), an executive (managing partner / GM / officers with defined duties), and in cooperatives an independent supervisory or audit committee reporting to the general meeting. Active members are paid an agreed salary or capped honorarium for work, separately from profit share on capital; vesting and documented roles are the standard antidotes to "I do all the work" resentment.

### Cited Findings
- Cooperative three-organ structure (ASUU Ojo): "The ultimate authority… shall be the General body of members"; "day-to-day administration… vested in the Management Committee" of "at least seven members including the President, Vice President, Vice President, General Secretary, Treasurer, Financial Secretary, Assistant General Secretary, and one or three or more Ex-Officio members"; tenure "a maximum period of four (4) years for a term of two (2) years", no more than two consecutive terms in one position; a "Supervisory committee, which shall comprise of three members, shall be elected at the Annual General Meeting" and "shall meet at least once every three months to make… an examination of the affairs of the society, which shall include an audit of its books and an inspection of the sureties, cash account, loans and stock of goods", "ascertain that all actions of the Management Committee are in conformity with the Law", report in writing after each examination and "Make an Annual Audit and written financial statements and submit same to the Annual General Meeting" — [ASUU (Ojo) bye-laws cl. 5.1, 6.1, 6.1.8, 7.0–7.1](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- NDU cooperative: Management Committee "not more than Nine (9) members"; officers serve two terms and "shall not qualify to stand for third term except in another office"; candidates must have been active members for at least two years and "must not have been a loan defaulter"; standing committees are Audit, Business and Legal; the Audit Committee checks "whether the internal audit control system contained in these rules are functioning effectively" and vouches "all books of account and related records" — [NDU bye-laws cl. 21–24](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf)
- Fiduciary standard written into bye-laws: committee members "stand in a fiduciary relationship towards the Society and shall observe the utmost good faith"; a committee member "shall not allow his personal interests conflict with official duties"; the society "shall not enter into an arrangement" with a committee member or connected person (related-party restriction) — [ASUU (Ojo) bye-laws cl. 6.5.1, 6.6, 6.12](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- Paying the governing body in a cooperative: honorarium "shall not be more than 7.5% of the net surplus of the society for any particular year", determined by the committee and "approved at the general meeting", shared among committee members "in proportions to be agreed within themselves"; plus reimbursement of travel/hotel expenses — [ASUU (Ojo) bye-laws cl. 6.4](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- Supervisory committee has "power to recommend the suspension of any officer or any or all members of the management committee" — [NFIU Cooperative bye-law](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf)
- Partnership deeds should state "entitlements for active management compensation, interest on capital contributions, and drawing procedures… where partners contribute differing capital versus time/expertise" — [MHCO Law](https://mhcolaw.com/insights/key-clauses-every-partnership-deed-must-include/163)
- Founder pay: "Pay modest monthly salaries reflecting workload disparity, reserving equity splits for profit distribution"; split ownership "reflecting capital invested, hours committed, expertise provided, and risk assumed—not default 50-50 splits"; "Assign exclusive decision authority within domains (Operations vs. Sales/Finance)" — [Nohemie Co](https://nohemieco.substack.com/p/diaspora-playbook-why-partnerships)
- Wasserman: 65% of high-potential startups fail "because of problems between the people at the top"; "35% of failures are due to product development, functional management and market problems, while 65%… due to people problems" — [CNN Money on Wasserman](https://money.cnn.com/2014/02/24/smallbusiness/startups-entrepreneur-cofounder/); [HBR, How Cofounders Can Prevent Their Relationship from Derailing](https://hbr.org/2022/04/how-cofounders-can-prevent-their-relationship-from-derailing). Provenance caveat: the 65% traces partly to a 1989 Gorman & Sahlman survey of 49 VCs about 96 troubled portfolio companies, of which 61 (63.5%) ranked team issues top-three — [Cofounder Clarity](https://cofounderclarity.com/founder-therapy-blog/the-truth-behind-8-common-myths-on-cofounder-conflict)
- Nigeria partnership research: "Requiring one partner to take an authoritative, leading role can create imbalance in an existing friendship"; Nigerian partners' "inability to differentiate between personal and business affairs… treating business property as personal property" — [NEXLeaders](https://nexleaders.com/buddies-as-business-partners-failures-and-fruitfulness/); [Nigerian project study on partnership problems](https://projectstopics.com/the-problem-of-partnership-business-in-nigeria/)
- Pivo (Nigerian fintech, $2m seed Nov 2022): CEO "appointed her brother as company secretary without consulting" the COO co-founder while also running another company; "What it destroyed was the assumption that major decisions would be made jointly"; investors issued a warning memo May 2023; shut down Dec 2023. Recommended routines: "Weekly 30-minute founder syncs covering blockers and disagreements, plus monthly relationship-focused conversations", and shared access to "banking, repositories, infrastructure, and domain registrars" — [PlanetWeb.ng](https://planetweb.ng/co-founder-conflicts-in-nigerian-startups/); [WeeTracker](https://weetracker.com/2023/12/06/why-pivo-shutdown/)

### Inferences
- For a 3–30 person group the cooperative template is directly reusable even in a company/LLP: a 5–9 person management committee elected by all members with 2-year terms and a 2-term cap; a 3-person supervisory/audit committee of *non-committee* members that inspects quarterly and reports to the AGM; a paid GM/managing partner with a written job description and domain authority.
- Compensation design: fixed, market-referenced salary (or capped honorarium, e.g. ≤7.5% of surplus) for work; profit distribution strictly by agreed ratio; both approved by the general meeting annually and disclosed to all members — this isolates "pay for effort" from "return on capital" and removes the main resentment driver.

### Gaps
- I found no Nigerian survey quantifying how often active-member compensation disputes cause dissolution; evidence is qualitative/anecdotal.
- No source specified a Nigerian market norm for managing-partner salary vs profit share.

## Key Question 4 — Financial controls and embezzlement red flags

### Takeaway
Small organisations lose a median ~$141k per fraud (ACFE 2024), mostly through asset misappropriation enabled by absent or overridden controls; the control set that real cooperative bye-laws and small-business guides converge on is: separate trustee/company account, two signatories (three named, any two), daily/48-hour banking of cash, voucher for every payment approved by a non-signatory, monthly bank reconciliation reviewed by someone who cannot sign, quarterly independent inspection, annual external audit, and a tip channel.

### Cited Findings
- ACFE 2024 (secondary summaries of the primary report): global median loss "$145,000"; organisations with "fewer than 100 employees had a median loss of $141,000" (second-largest); owner/executive perpetrators "$500,000" vs managers "$184,000" vs employees "$60,000"; median duration "12 months"; "Tips account for 43% of fraud discoveries" (employees 52% of tipsters), internal/external audit 17%, management review 13%; asset misappropriation in "89% of cases"; "lack of internal controls accounted for 32% and override of existing controls accounted for 19% of cases"; organisations lose "approximately 5% of… revenue to fraud"; small businesses more often hit by "check and payment tampering and skimming" — [Porte Brown summary](https://www.portebrown.com/newsblog-archive/acfe-publishes-new-fraud-study); [Schneider Downs summary](https://schneiderdowns.com/our-thoughts-on/spot-and-stop-long-lasting-fraud/); [ACFE 2024 report page (primary PDF, 11MB)](https://www.acfe.com/-/media/files/acfe/pdfs/rttn/2024/2024-report-to-the-nations.pdf). ACFE's current landing page lists red flags with highest median losses: "excessive organizational pressure ($532,000), past legal problems ($400,000), and vendor/customer relationships ($300,000)" — [ACFE Report to the Nations page](https://www.acfe.com/fraud-resources/report-to-the-nations)
- Segregation of duties: "no single person controls all three parts of a transaction: authorizing it, holding the related asset (cash or the checkbook), and recording it"; owner compensating controls — "receiving bank statements directly and looking at the cleared-check images, personally approving new vendors and employees, running surprise cash and inventory counts, and monitoring the bank feed weekly"; owners should "review bank and credit card statements monthly, approve all payments above a defined threshold, review financial statements monthly" — [Beancount SoD playbook](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/05/15/segregation-of-duties-small-business-three-employees-internal-controls-fraud-prevention-compensating-controls-guide); [Zluri](https://www.zluri.com/eye-on-identity/segregation-of-duties-small-business)
- US Trustee small-business control sheet: "Receive bank statements unopened and scrutinize"; "Review canceled checks each month, including payee endorsements"; "Review transfers between bank accounts"; "Make daily (or more frequent) deposits of checks and cash"; "Secure blank check stock"; "Restrict signature authority on company bank accounts"; "Verify the name of each vendor paid"; "Change computer passwords… after terminating someone's employment" — [US DOJ / UST, Internal Control Information for the Small Business Owner](https://justice.gov/sites/default/files/ust-regions/legacy/2011/07/13/sb_internal_control_info.pdf)
- Behavioural red flags: "reluctance of an employee to take time off and an employee living beyond their means"; "embezzlements are perpetrated by someone with unfettered or unmonitored access" — [Adams Brown CPA](https://www.adamsbrowncpa.com/blog/tighten-financial-internal-controls-to-guard-against-workplace-fraud/)
- Cooperative bye-law controls (ASUU Ojo supervisory checklist): "(1) Is a receipt issue for every item of cash received? (2) Is there a payment voucher for every amount paid out? (3) Is too much cash… kept on hand? (4) Are any unused cheques on hand signed in blank (5) Is the cashbook balanced regularly (daily weekly and monthly)? (6) Are all expenses approved by the management committee and vouchers signed by the President? (7) Are the members' balances compiled monthly? (8) Do these members' balances agree with the general control account? (9) Are the minutes signed by the Chairman…" — [ASUU (Ojo) bye-laws cl. 7.1.5](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- NDU cooperative cash rules: Treasurer "shall deposit all funds over the limit fixed by the General Meetings in any bank approved… All cash accrued or left over must be paid into the Cooperative Bank Account within 48 hours"; Treasurer prepares a "Quarterly/Financial Report to the Management" and keeps a daily cash book; "No book or account… shall be kept by the Secretary or the Treasurer except those officially recognized and prescribed"; "All employees of the Cooperative will be required to give two guarantors"; dividend "shall not exceed 5% of the annual surplus and no dividend shall be paid if any overdue claim to a depositor or lender is unsatisfied" — [NDU bye-laws cl. 22, 26, 31, 34](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf)
- Budget approval: the management committee must "prepare and lay before the general meeting a profit and loss account, audited balance sheets and the budget" and "consider the audit and inspections reports of the Auditor and the Director of Cooperative Services" — [ASUU (Ojo) bye-laws cl. 6.5.5–6.5.6](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- Statutory audit for Nigerian cooperatives: accounts "shall be audited at the end of the financial year… by an auditor approved in writing for that purpose by the society", including "examination of overdue debts… and a valuation of the assets and liabilities"; the auditor may "summon… any officer, agent, servant or member" and "require the production of any book or document… or any cash or security" — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act s.36](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Members can force an inquiry: the Director "shall, on the application of a simple majority of the members of the committee, or of not less than one-third of the members of a registered society, hold an inquiry… into the constitution, working and financial state" — [Co-operative Societies Act s.37](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Partnership deed should set "who can sign cheques, who can approve payments, and how expenses will be authorised", plus "record maintenance location, audit frequency (internal or external), and access protocols" — [MHCO Law](https://mhcolaw.com/insights/key-clauses-every-partnership-deed-must-include/163)
- Reserved decisions typically include "borrowing more than a specified amount or granting a lien" — [McE Law](https://www.mcelaw.com/blog/critical-terms-to-include-in-a-partnership-agreement-to-prevent-disputes/)

### Inferences
- Minimum control stack for a 3–30 member venture (derived from the above): (1) business account only, no personal-account routing; (2) three named signatories, any two to sign, with a single-signature ceiling for petty amounts and a higher tier (e.g. above a set naira limit) requiring committee resolution; (3) every payment backed by a voucher approved by someone other than the payer/signatory; (4) cash banked within 48 hours, no blank signed cheques; (5) monthly bank statement + reconciliation + members' balance report circulated to *all* members, reviewed by the supervisory/audit committee; (6) quarterly supervisory inspection with written report; (7) annual external audit approved at AGM; (8) accounting software with read-only member access and audit trail; (9) a confidential tip channel — tips find 43% of frauds and small groups rarely have one.
- Red-flag list specific to co-owned small businesses (synthesised): one member holds bank token/cheque book *and* keeps the books; statements go to a personal address or phone; recurring "rounding" shortages; refusal to take leave or hand over; unexplained lifestyle change; vendors connected to a member (related-party); late or missing monthly reports; related-party loans not approved by resolution.

### Gaps
- The primary ACFE 2024 PDF exceeded the fetch size limit; small-business figures above are from CPA-firm summaries of it and should be verified against the PDF before publication.
- I found no Nigerian-specific fraud-loss statistics for cooperatives or SMEs (ACFE's Sub-Saharan Africa regional cut was not retrieved).

## Key Question 5 — Reporting, transparency, members' information rights, AGM content

### Takeaway
Governing documents fix a rhythm: monthly statements and members' balances, quarterly financial report from the treasurer and quarterly supervisory inspection, an AGM within Q1–Q2 after audited accounts on 21 days' notice that receives the annual report, audited accounts and budget, decides surplus distribution, elects officers and appoints the auditor; members have statutory rights to inspect the bye-laws and (for 1/3 of members) demand an inquiry.

### Cited Findings
- AGM timing/notice: "The Annual General Meeting shall be held within the first or second quarters of the succeeding year after the Annual Statement of Accounts have been prepared and approved by the Director"; notice for all general meetings "shall be twenty-one days" — [ASUU (Ojo) bye-laws cl. 5.3, 5.6](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf); NFIU cooperative: AGM convened "as soon as the Annual audited Accounts have been approved by the Director of Cooperative Societies, with at least twenty-one (21) days notice" — [NFIU bye-law](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf)
- AGM agenda (NDU): "receive from the out-going Management Committee a report on the preceding year's working… with the financial statement"; "decide upon the disposal of the surplus"; "consider any communication received from the Director of Cooperatives"; "elect the President, Vice-President, Secretary General, Assistant Secretary General, Treasurer, Auditor and four (4) Committee members" — [NDU bye-laws cl. 17](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf). NFIU version adds "considering the Management Committee's annual report including the annual budget and annual accounts" and "appointing delegates" — [NFIU bye-law](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng/NFIUCooperative/Files/NFIUCOOP%20BYE%20LAW.pdf)
- Ordinary general meeting powers: "Suspend or remove from office, any Officer or Member of the Committee"; "Confirm the expulsion of member(s)"; "Amend or repeal any existing Bye-Laws"; NDU adds "Fix the Rate of interest to be charged for loans" and "Ratify the appointment/termination/dismissal of any employee" — [ASUU (Ojo) cl. 5.4](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf); [NDU cl. 18](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf)
- Extraordinary general meeting "may be called at any time by a majority of the Management Committee or on a request from the Director or from [members]" — [ASUU (Ojo) cl. 5.7](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- Reporting cadence: Treasurer "To prepare and present Quarterly/Financial Report to the Management"; supervisory committee inspects "at least once every three months" and makes "a written report… following each examination"; "members' balances compiled monthly" — [NDU cl. 22](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf); [ASUU (Ojo) cl. 7.1](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- Statutory inspection right: "Copy of Act, regulations and bye-laws to be kept open for inspection" (s.13); one-third of members can compel a Director's inquiry (s.37) — [Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- UK co-op governance: members "should be provided with a copy of their co-operative's governing document"; "Unless a co-operative is small, an auditor will need to be appointed for each financial year, often done at the AGM by members passing an ordinary resolution"; "The chairs of all board committees should attend the AGM and be prepared to answer questions from members" — [Co-operatives UK, members and participation](https://www.uk.coop/support-your-co-op/governance/what-good-governance/members-and-participation); [Co-operatives UK AGM guide](https://www.uk.coop/sites/default/files/2021-10/AGM_guide.pdf); [Consumer co-op governance code](https://library.uniteddiversity.coop/Cooperatives/governance_code.pdf)
- Company route (Nigeria): annual returns due "within 42 days of their Annual General Meeting" — [SmartSMS guide](https://smartsmssolutions.com/resources/blog/ng/ng-02-hub-cac-annual-returns)
- Partnership deeds should give partners "access protocols" to books and define "audit frequency" — [MHCO Law](https://mhcolaw.com/insights/key-clauses-every-partnership-deed-must-include/163); SHAs may grant minorities information/veto rights — [IBA Guide – Nigeria](https://www.ibanet.org/medias/iba-guide-shareholders-agreements-nigeria.pdf)
- Case law illustration (Kenya): a 33% shareholder excluded from management, "removed… as director without notice, denied… access to company records and funds" — court ordered an independent valuation and buy-out rather than liquidation — [Business Daily Africa, Paradiso Toys](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/companies/court-orders-buy-out-in-fight-over-firm-founded-on-friendship-5604604)

### Inferences
- Reporting pack for 3–30 members: **monthly** (by 10th): bank statement, reconciliation, cash book summary, members' capital/loan balances, variance vs budget; **quarterly**: management accounts, supervisory committee report, loan/debtor ageing; **annually**: audited accounts, committee report, budget, surplus-distribution proposal, officer elections, auditor appointment, 21 days' notice. Write information rights (inspect books on 7 days' notice; receive monthly pack) into the deed/SHA so exclusion is a breach, not a grievance.

### Gaps
- Co-operatives UK AGM guide and governance code PDFs could not be fetched (403); findings are from the search snippets.

## Key Question 6 — Dispute resolution, deadlock, non-compete, non-performing or absent members

### Takeaway
Agreements escalate: negotiation → mediation (cooling-off) → arbitration, with deadlock-breakers for ownership stalemates — casting vote, independent expert/adjudicator choosing among pre-agreed options, or buy-sell devices (shotgun/Russian roulette, Texas shoot-out sealed bids, Mexican shoot-out, deterrence 125%/75% pricing). Buy-sell devices suit two roughly equal, equally liquid owners; for 3–30 members the practical tools are casting vote, supermajority with expert determination, and expulsion/compulsory buyout for defined defaults after a show-cause notice. Nigerian cooperatives must refer disputes to the State Director of Co-operatives for arbitration.

### Cited Findings
- Mechanisms catalogued: Russian roulette — "One party sets a price for a half interest; the other party chooses to buy or sell at that price"; Texas shoot-out — "Each party submits sealed bids. The highest bidder acquires the other's share at their stated price"; Mexican shoot-out/Dutch auction — "sealed bids indicating minimum selling prices. The higher bid wins, and that party purchases at the loser's stated price"; multi-choice procedure — "parties agree on a series of options beforehand; if deadlock persists, a third-party adjudicator selects one"; cooling-off/mediation; deterrence approach — initiator "must either buy all shares at 125% of fair market value or sell theirs at 75%" — [Wikipedia, Deadlock provision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadlock_provision)
- Shotgun = "I cut, you choose"; "rapid and definitive"; but "disadvantages the cash-poor member every time. If one member has access to financing and the other does not, the well-capitalized member can name a below-market price" — [WebLegal on LLC deadlock](https://weblegal.net/article/llc-operating-agreement-deadlock-buyout-clauses); [ECJC guest post](https://www.ecjc.com/guest-post-when-to-use-a-texas-shoot-out-and-resolving-partner-deadlock/); two flaws — "Unequal Information Access" (working owner knows more) and "Liquidity Inequality"; agreements "set a timetable" and "impose stringent penalties for failing to close" — [Business Divorce Law Report](https://www.thebusinessdivorcelawyer.com/shotgun-buy-sell-agreements-solution-to-deadlock/)
- Shotgun triggers: "shareholder disputes, deadlock situations, or material defaults by a shareholder" — [Lexology, Breaking the Deadlock](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=189fc31d-cfcd-4723-aae6-8d3fcc3f93cb)
- Casting vote in cooperatives: chairman has "a casting vote" on equality (statute); "The Chairman shall have a casting vote, if votes are equal or he may call for a second voting" — [Co-operative Societies Act s.25](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf); [ASUU (Ojo) cl. 5.6](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- Cooperative disputes: "Any dispute arising in or concerning the Society and its members or past members shall be referred to the Director for settlement by arbitration" under the state Co-operative Societies Law — [ASUU (Ojo) cl. 14.2](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf); [NDU cl. 37](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf); statute: on reference the Director shall "settle the dispute; or… refer it to an arbitrator"; claims against "a member or nominee, heir or estate of a deceased member" count as disputes — [Co-operative Societies Act s.49](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Nigerian companies: SHAs can include arbitration "with its seat outside Nigeria and/or under a procedural law other than the Nigerian Arbitration and Mediation Act"; the Federal High Court has first-instance jurisdiction over CAMA matters; minorities may sue for "unfairly prejudicial and oppressive" conduct or bring a derivative action with leave — [IBA Guide – Nigeria](https://www.ibanet.org/medias/iba-guide-shareholders-agreements-nigeria.pdf)
- Non-compete/confidentiality: a Nigerian SHA "may regulate any matter between the parties, including… non-disclosure and confidentiality, non-compete clauses, and other restraints against competition" — [IBA Guide – Nigeria](https://www.ibanet.org/medias/iba-guide-shareholders-agreements-nigeria.pdf); partnership deeds should include "restrictions on competing ventures and rules requiring disclosure of personal profits derived from partnership opportunities" — [MHCO Law](https://mhcolaw.com/insights/key-clauses-every-partnership-deed-must-include/163)
- Expulsion grounds in partnership agreements: "insolvency of a partner, misconduct like persistent breaches of agreement, material breach of duties to other partners, dishonesty, and permanent incapacity"; material breach = conduct that "go[es] to the root of the agreement"; sample clause: expulsion "by a unanimous decision of the partners save in good faith and in the interest of the partnership business only after a show-cause notice in writing is served on that Partner giving 7 days time for his response" — [Guildhall Chambers, Exclusions from Partnerships](https://www.guildhallchambers.co.uk/uploadedFiles/ExclusionsfromPartnerships_KG.pdf); [Law Insider expulsion clauses](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/expulsion-of-partner)
- Buyout of an expelled partner: "the expelled partner must be provided with a complete accounting… Then… paid for the value of their partnership interest"; US statutory default price is "the greater of liquidation value or the value of the entire business as a going concern without the dissociated partner" — [Lawyers.com](https://www.lawyers.com/legal-info/business-law/small-business-law/breach-of-partnership-agreements-and-expulsion.html); [Margetas Legal](https://margetaslegal.com/pennsylvania-remove-partner-partnership-buyout/)
- Cooperative expulsion grounds: "Repeated failure to make thrift savings"; "Repeated failure to make up for debts"; "Conviction on a criminal offence involving dishonesty"; "Misconduct or other act contrary to the stated objects"; expelled member is paid "the sum of his credit less any sum due"; expulsion must be confirmed by general meeting — [ASUU (Ojo) cl. 4.7, 5.4](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf); NDU: termination for "consistent failure to make savings, to repay loans, to patronize the Cooperative" — [NDU cl. 8](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf)
- Removing officers: committee member removed "by a majority vote of 2/3 of members present and voting at a special general meeting" for acting "in a manner prejudicial to the interest of the society", becoming a paid servant, or criminal conviction; removal by special resolution with the member's written representations circulated — [ASUU (Ojo) cl. 6.2, 6.11](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- Trust-breakdown dynamic: "Small issues compound: He's late to meeting → I'm annoyed / Money is $200 short → I suspect him" — [Nohemie Co](https://nohemieco.substack.com/p/diaspora-playbook-why-partnerships)

### Inferences
- For 3–30 members, recommended ladder: (1) 30-day negotiation between designated representatives; (2) mediation (Lagos Multi-Door Courthouse or similar) within 60 days; (3) binding arbitration under the Arbitration and Mediation Act 2023; plus (4) for *governance* deadlock, chairman's casting vote on ordinary matters and independent-expert determination on reserved matters; (5) for *member default* (absence >X months, non-contribution, breach, fraud), a show-cause notice (7–14 days), vote by 75% of other members, and compulsory buyout at a formula value with a discount for fault (deterrence-style 75%) paid in instalments.
- Shotgun/Texas shoot-out clauses should be reserved for two-party or two-bloc ownership with comparable means; in a 3–30 group they are impractical and risk a cash-rich member stripping the others.

### Gaps
- No Nigerian court decision specifically enforcing a shotgun or Texas shoot-out clause was found; enforceability in Nigeria is asserted by practitioners generally, not case-confirmed in sources retrieved.
- Typical response/closing windows (e.g., 30–60 days) for shotgun clauses were not confirmed by the source fetched.

## Key Question 7 — Exit and continuity: transfer restrictions, ROFR, tag/drag, death/incapacity, dissolution and payment order

### Takeaway
Nigerian private companies already have statutory pre-emption (CAMA s.142) and board approval of transfers; SHAs add ROFR mechanics, permitted transfers to family/affiliates, tag-along, drag-along and a valuation formula or independent valuer. Cooperatives require every member to name a nominee who inherits or is paid out the member's interest. On winding up the universal order is outside creditors → member loans → member capital → residual by profit ratio; cooperative residue is impounded by the Director rather than shared.

### Cited Findings
- Statutory pre-emption: "Section 142(1) of CAMA restricts shareholders from transferring shares to a third party without first offering said shares to existing members"; shares of private companies "are not freely transferable without the waiver of pre-emption rights by other shareholders and the approval of the board"; "Most shareholder agreements provide an exception for transfers to family and affiliates (permitted transfers)… but will still require board approval" — [IBA Guide – Nigeria](https://www.ibanet.org/medias/iba-guide-shareholders-agreements-nigeria.pdf)
- Tag/drag: tag-along "allows minority shareholders to join a majority shareholder's sale on the same terms"; drag-along "allows majority shareholders to compel minority shareholders to participate in a sale" — [Enebeli & Partners](https://goenebeli.com/why-every-nigerian-business-needs-a-shareholders-agreement-before-the-first-deal/); IBA: these "ensure that minorities and selling majority shareholders are not left behind… or prevented from exiting by minorities that refuse to waive their rights" — [IBA Guide – Nigeria](https://www.ibanet.org/medias/iba-guide-shareholders-agreements-nigeria.pdf)
- Valuation on exit: SHA "may provide a fixed formula… based on published metrics or industry multiples or provide for the appointment of a third-party independent professional"; absent a clause, the seller "may set the price… or may offer the shares to other shareholders on the same basis as an unsolicited offer received from a third party" — [IBA Guide – Nigeria](https://www.ibanet.org/medias/iba-guide-shareholders-agreements-nigeria.pdf)
- Buy-sell valuation methods: "Fixed price (an agreed dollar value, updated annually by the owners) and Formula valuation (book value, a multiple of EBITDA, or capitalized earnings, applied automatically on a triggering event)"; cross-purchase: "surviving business owners (not the entity) purchase… the deceased owner's interest" with each owner holding policies on the others; "If the policy pays out at one amount and the agreed buyout price is another, the surviving owners or the estate absorb the shortfall" — [CPA Journal, Using Buy/Sell Agreements](https://www.cpajournal.com/2026/02/18/using-buy-sell-agreements/); [Thompson Coburn](https://www.thompsoncoburn.com/insights/strategies-for-buy-sell-agreements-using-insurance/); [RateAuthority](https://rateauthority.org/niches/life-insurance-business-owner-buy-sell-2026/)
- Cooperative succession: "Every member shall in writing nominate a person or persons to whom his shares or interest shall be transferred to in the event of such member's death or becoming permanently insane"; nominee recorded in the register; "If not admitted to membership the nominee shall… be paid the value of the share or interest, less any sum due to the society" — [ASUU (Ojo) cl. 4.5](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf); statute s.18 permits payment "to such nominee, heir, legal representative or a court" of "a sum representing the value of such member's share or interest" — [Co-operative Societies Act s.18](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Withdrawal terms: one month's written notice "provided that the withdrawing member is neither indebted to the Society nor surety for an unpaid debt" (ASUU); NDU: no withdrawal "until he or she has been a member for at least three (3) years", then "three (3) months notice"; past members' estates remain liable for society debts existing at exit for two years (ASUU) / one year (NDU) — [ASUU (Ojo) cl. 4.4, 4.6](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf); [NDU cl. 8, 11](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf)
- Partnership liquidation order: "outside creditors first, then partners' loans, then their capital, then their profits"; "Partner's loans… must be paid off first before any distribution of capital is made"; "if the assets fall short, partners may have to contribute more rather than receive anything" — [Business LibreTexts, Dissolution of a Partnership](https://biz.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Accounting/Financial_Accounting_(OpenStax)/15:_Partnership_Accounting/15.05:_Discuss_and_Record_Entries_for_the_Dissolution_of_a_Partnership); [Vivas & Nobles Law](https://vivasandnobleslawoffice.com/answers/in-what-order-are-debts-paid-when-a-partnership-is-liquidated/); [Shaalaa](https://www.shaalaa.com/question-bank-solutions/state-the-following-statement-is-true-partner-s-loans-have-to-be-paid-before-any-payment-is-made-to-any-of-the-partners-as-capital_423530)
- Cooperative dissolution: the Director may cancel registration "on receipt of an application made by not less than three fourths of the members"; members may appeal within two months; the liquidator decides members' contributions, fixes a creditors' claim date, decides creditor priority, may "refer a dispute to arbitration" — [Co-operative Societies Act ss.38, 40–41](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf). Bye-laws: on dissolution funds "shall be applied, first in discharging the liabilities of the society and secondly in the repayment of the share capital paid up. The remainder shall be placed by the Director of Cooperatives in the Bank until a new Cooperative Society with a similar area of operations is registered" — [ASUU (Ojo) cl. 13](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf); [NDU cl. 35](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf). Reserve fund "is indivisible and no member is entitled to claim a specified share in it" — [NDU cl. 32](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf)
- Statutory surplus rules: "At least one fourth of the net profits… shall be paid into… the 'reserve fund'"; up to 10% of the remainder to an education fund with Director's approval; no dividend/bonus "until the proposal payment or distribution has been approved by the committee"; wilful breach is an offence — [Co-operative Societies Act s.34](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf). ASUU Ojo appropriation: ≥25% reserve, "2½% to the Education Fund", honoraria approved by Director, dividend on savings/share capital/loans "not less than 50% of the net surplus" — [ASUU (Ojo) cl. 11](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)
- Partnership deed death clause should "Clarify business continuation, heir compensation" — [MHCO Law](https://mhcolaw.com/insights/key-clauses-every-partnership-deed-must-include/163); exit terms left ambiguous make dissolution "legally expensive without pre-negotiated buyout formulas" — [Nohemie Co](https://nohemieco.substack.com/p/diaspora-playbook-why-partnerships)
- Court-imposed exit when none is agreed: independent valuation by certified accountants, majority owner "gets first right to purchase… at the valuation price; If [he] declines, [the minority] may purchase his shares instead" — [Business Daily Africa, Paradiso Toys](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/companies/court-orders-buy-out-in-fight-over-firm-founded-on-friendship-5604604)

### Inferences
- Exit clause set for a 3–30 member deed/SHA: lock-in period (e.g. 2–3 years, mirroring cooperative 3-year minimum); ROFR to existing members pro rata with 30-day acceptance; permitted transfers to spouse/children/holding company subject to accession deed; tag-along above 50% sale; drag-along at 75% with same-terms protection; valuation = fixed value updated at each AGM, falling back to a formula (e.g., multiple of average 3-year EBITDA or NAV) then an independent valuer nominated by ICAN; payment in instalments (e.g., 25% on completion, balance over 12–24 months) to protect cash flow; compulsory offer on death/incapacity with nominee register and key-person/cross-purchase insurance sized to the formula value.
- Winding-up clause: order of payment (external creditors → member loans with accrued interest → return of capital pro rata → residual by profit ratio), named liquidation committee, and a rule that shortfalls are borne in loss-sharing ratio.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm a Nigerian-market norm for buyout instalment periods or valuation discounts; figures above are inferred from general practice.
- Availability/pricing of key-person or buy-sell life insurance in Nigeria was not researched.

## Key Question 8 — Evidence and lessons: why partnerships and friends'/family businesses fail; Nigerian commentary

### Takeaway
The evidence base is thin on rigorous surveys but consistent in content: people problems (not product/market) drive roughly two-thirds of founding-team failures (Wasserman, with provenance caveats); practitioner and Nigerian commentary cite the same five faults — undefined roles, unequal effort/contribution, unmanaged money, no exit terms, and trust erosion from small unaddressed incidents — and the Nigerian Pivo case shows a single unilateral decision collapsing a funded company within a year.

### Cited Findings
- Wasserman: 65% of high-potential startups fail due to "problems between the people at the top"; based on "three dozen case studies and data on over 10,000 founders" — [Startup Story Media](https://startupstorymedia.com/65-of-high-potential-firms-will-fail-says-noam-wasserman/); [CNN Money](https://money.cnn.com/2014/02/24/smallbusiness/startups-entrepreneur-cofounder/); provenance: Gorman & Sahlman 1989 survey, 61 of 96 troubled companies (63.5%) — [Cofounder Clarity](https://cofounderclarity.com/founder-therapy-blog/the-truth-behind-8-common-myths-on-cofounder-conflict)
- Five failure patterns: "Undefined Responsibilities", "Contribution Imbalance", "Financial Chaos", "Ambiguous Exit Terms", "Trust Deterioration"; diaspora-plus-local-partner ventures reportedly "fail 70% of the time" (author's claim, unsourced) — [Nohemie Co](https://nohemieco.substack.com/p/diaspora-playbook-why-partnerships)
- "Good friendships make terrible partnerships without structure"; "Early trust can hide hard questions about compensation, workload, control, family involvement, risk tolerance" — [MBO Ventures](https://mboventures.com/why-partnerships-fail/); [Bimak Associates](https://www.bimakassociates.com/why-most-business-partnerships-fail-and-how-to-prevent-it/)
- Nigerian partnerships: "Inability to differentiate between personal and business affairs"; partners "treating business property as personal property"; "Poor funding sources and insufficient capital outlay" — [Nigerian project research on partnership problems](https://projectstopics.com/the-problem-of-partnership-business-in-nigeria/); [Nairaproject case study, Warri](https://nairaproject.com/projects/1588.html)
- Nigerian family businesses: "When family disagreements spill into the business, it creates dysfunction with poor governance, nepotism, and emotional decision-making" — [Kolo Nigeria](https://www.kolonigeria.com.ng/2025/11/why-nigerian-family-businesses-fail.html) (page blocked on fetch; from search snippet)
- East Africa press: "Why business partnerships often kill friendship" — [Business Daily Africa](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/Why-business-partnerships-often-kill-friendship/539444-1705260-glk05c/index.html)
- Pivo: founded July 2021; $2m seed Nov 2022 with "98% loan repayment, 400% transaction growth"; unilateral appointment of CEO's brother as company secretary; "communication between the two collapsed"; investor warning memo May 2023; shutdown Dec 2023 — [PlanetWeb.ng](https://planetweb.ng/co-founder-conflicts-in-nigerian-startups/); [WeeTracker](https://weetracker.com/2023/12/06/why-pivo-shutdown/); [Techpoint Africa on co-founder disputes](https://techpoint.africa/brandpress/navigating-co-founder-disputes-in-startups-a-business-law-perspective/)
- Fraud evidence: "More than half of occupational frauds occur due to lack of internal controls or an override of existing internal controls"; small businesses "typically have fewer anti-fraud controls" — [ACFE 2024 summaries](https://www.portebrown.com/newsblog-archive/acfe-publishes-new-fraud-study); [Schneider Downs](https://schneiderdowns.com/our-thoughts-on/spot-and-stop-long-lasting-fraud/)
- Equity-split evidence: "73% of founding teams split equity within the first month" — [Culta summary](https://culta.ai/blog/cofounder-equity-salary-split)

### Inferences
- The lessons map directly onto the playbook: (1) write roles and decision rights down (undefined roles, Pivo); (2) separate pay-for-work from return-on-capital and use vesting (contribution imbalance); (3) two-signatory account, monthly statements to all, supervisory committee (financial chaos, fraud stats); (4) pre-agreed valuation and buyout (exit ambiguity, Paradiso Toys, Nohemie's $15k dissolution); (5) weekly operating sync + monthly relationship check-in + escalation ladder (trust erosion).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or large-sample Nigerian survey on partnership failure rates was found; Nigerian material is law-firm commentary, student project reports and tech-press case studies.
- The "70% diaspora partnership failure" figure is an author's assertion without a cited study.
- HBR 2022 article content beyond the headline statistic was paywalled/unavailable.
