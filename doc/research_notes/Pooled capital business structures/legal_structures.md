# Legal vehicles for pooling money and running a business together — worldwide and Nigeria

Research date: October 2026. Figures are labelled with the year of the source. Nigerian primary law is the Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020 (CAMA 2020), the Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act (Cap N98 LFN 2004, plus state laws), and the Investments and Securities Act 2025 (ISA 2025), which replaced the ISA 2007.

## Key Question 1: General partnership vs. limited partnership (LP) vs. limited liability partnership (LLP)

### Takeaway
A general partnership gives every partner management rights and unlimited, joint personal liability; an LP splits the group into managing general partners (unlimited liability) and passive limited partners (liability capped at their contribution, lost if they manage); an LLP gives all partners limited liability inside a body corporate while keeping partnership-style internal governance. Nigeria had only general partnerships (registered as business names) until CAMA 2020 introduced LPs (Part D, s.795 ff.) and LLPs (Part C, s.746 ff.); CAC began registering both on its portal in 2021.

### Cited Findings

Global comparison
- In a general partnership, partners accept unlimited liability — if the business has legal or financial problems "all partners' personal assets are equally on the line"; all partners typically manage day-to-day operations and each has an equal say — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/legal/learn/general-partnership-vs-limited-partnership)
- In a limited partnership the general partner has unlimited liability and full management control, while limited partners have liability limited to their investment and no management authority — and can lose limited liability if they try to assert management authority — [Delaware Inc.](https://www.delawareinc.com/blog/general-partnership-vs-limited-partnership/); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/legal/learn/general-partnership-vs-limited-partnership)
- An LLP is "a limited partnership without a general partner" — a hybrid of general partnership and LLC; partners are generally not personally liable for the negligence or misconduct of other partners, though they remain liable for their own acts, and the scope of protection varies by jurisdiction — [Wikipedia: Limited liability partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_liability_partnership); [Z-Tax](https://www.ztaxonline.com/business-tax-returns-partnerships-s-corp-c-corp/partnerships/general-partnership-vs-limited-partnership-vs-limited-liability-partnersh)
- UK: LLPs and private limited companies both register at Companies House and pay the same online incorporation fee (£100 in 2026); an LLP needs at least two members with no maximum, while a private limited company can be formed by a single person who is both sole director and sole shareholder — [Binderr (2026)](https://binderr.com/marketplace/uk/cost-of-setting-up-a-uk-company); [The Accountancy Partnership](https://www.theaccountancy.co.uk/limited-company/whats-difference-llp-limited-company-7698.html); [GOV.UK Fees Determination Feb 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/697a0107128d9c1d09a98cb2/Fees_Determination_February_2026.pdf)

Nigeria — general partnership
- A general partnership is registered with CAC as an ordinary business name; it is capped at 20 partners, is not a separate legal entity, partners have unlimited liability ("partners' private property may be sought after by creditors"), and it lacks perpetual succession — a partner's death or resignation can end the partnership — [Mondaq / Registering Business Partnerships in Nigeria (2024)](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1430552/registering-business-partnerships-in-nigeria-general-partnerships-limited-partnerships-and-limited-liability-partnerships-in-nigeria)
- CAC fees (new schedule effective 1 September 2025, postponed from 1 August 2025): name reservation ₦1,000 (was ₦500); business name registration incl. CTC ₦20,000 (was ₦10,000); business name annual return ₦5,000 (was ₦3,000) — [SplashDict summary of 2025 CAC fee schedule](https://splashdict.com/blog/cac/understanding-the-new-2025-cac-service-fees/); the schedule's own PDF is at [cac.gov.ng/api/download-fees](https://www.cac.gov.ng/api/download-fees) (returned HTTP 403 when fetched; figures above are from the secondary source)
- Earlier (pre-Sept 2025) figures still widely quoted: name reservation ₦500, business name registration ₦10,000; DIY total ₦10,500–₦12,000, agent/lawyer ₦15,000–₦30,000 — [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/ask-legit/guides/1709408-everything-budget-cac-registration-fees-nigeria/); [Fbanabena & Co](https://www.fbanabena.com/cac-registration/cac-registration-fees/)

Nigeria — LLP (CAMA 2020 Part C)
- CAMA 2020 was enacted 7 August 2020, repealing CAMA 1990, and introduced the LP and LLP, neither of which existed under the 1990 Act — [DLA Piper Africa (2021)](https://www.dlapiperafrica.com/en/nigeria/insights/2021/cac-commences-registration-of-limited-liability-partnerships-and-limited-partnerships-in-nigeria.html)
- s.746(1): an LLP is a body corporate, a separate legal entity from its partners, with perpetual succession; partners' liability is limited to the amount agreed to be contributed or outstanding on winding up; it can sue and be sued in its own name; a partner's death does not dissolve it; there is no limit on the number of partners — [Mondaq (2024)](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1430552/registering-business-partnerships-in-nigeria-general-partnerships-limited-partnerships-and-limited-liability-partnerships-in-nigeria); [Andersen Nigeria](https://ng.andersen.com/a-review-of-limited-liability-partnerships-under-the-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020/); [Starlion Legal, 50 key points](https://starlionlegal.wordpress.com/2020/09/05/50-key-points-on-the-new-companies-and-allied-matters-act-cama-2020/)
- An LLP must have at least two partners (individuals or body corporates, not of unsound mind or undischarged bankrupts) and at least two "designated partners" responsible for CAMA compliance, who must be individuals with at least one resident in Nigeria — [Pavestones Legal](https://pavestoneslegal.com/procedure-for-registration-of-a-limited-liability-partnership-in-nigeria/); [DLA Piper Africa](https://www.dlapiperafrica.com/en/nigeria/insights/2021/cac-commences-registration-of-limited-liability-partnerships-and-limited-partnerships-in-nigeria.html)
- Registration steps: (1) name availability check and reservation at CAC; (2) complete Form CAC/LLP 01 with the approved name, business description, registered address, partner details and contributions, the designated partners, and persons holding 5%+ interest/control; (3) pay the fee; (4) CAC issues a certificate of incorporation within 14 days stating name, date and registration number — [Pavestones Legal](https://pavestoneslegal.com/procedure-for-registration-of-a-limited-liability-partnership-in-nigeria/)
- LLPs (like companies) must disclose persons with significant control to CAC (s.791 for LLPs; s.119 for companies) — [Starlion Legal](https://starlionlegal.wordpress.com/2020/09/05/50-key-points-on-the-new-companies-and-allied-matters-act-cama-2020/)
- CAC fee (Sept 2025 schedule): LLP name reservation ₦1,000 (was ₦500); LLP change of name ₦20,000 (was ₦10,000) — [SplashDict](https://splashdict.com/blog/cac/understanding-the-new-2025-cac-service-fees/)
- The Companies Regulations 2021 supply the detailed implementation procedures for the LLP/LP regimes — [Pavestones Legal](https://pavestoneslegal.com/procedure-for-registration-of-a-limited-liability-partnership-in-nigeria/)

Nigeria — LP (CAMA 2020 Part D, s.795 ff.)
- An LP "shall consist of one or more persons called general partners ... and one or more persons called limited partners"; it cannot exceed 20 persons; corporate entities may be limited partners — [Mondaq / Registration of LPs under CAMA 2020](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1142692/registration-of-limited-partnerships-in-nigeria-under-cama-2020); [Starlion Legal (s.795)](https://starlionlegal.wordpress.com/2020/09/05/50-key-points-on-the-new-companies-and-allied-matters-act-cama-2020/)
- General partners bear full liability for partnership debts; limited partners' liability is capped at "the amount so contributed or agreed to be contributed", but a limited partner who takes part in management becomes liable "as though he were a general partner" — [Mondaq LP article](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1142692/registration-of-limited-partnerships-in-nigeria-under-cama-2020)
- Partners must contribute (or agree to contribute) capital or property valued at a stated amount on entry; a limited partner may not withdraw contributions without written consent of the partners, and unauthorised withdrawals make the partner liable for debts up to the withdrawn amount — [Mondaq LP article](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1142692/registration-of-limited-partnerships-in-nigeria-under-cama-2020)
- CAC commenced LP registration on 2 September 2021 via the Company Registration Portal — [Mondaq LP article](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1142692/registration-of-limited-partnerships-in-nigeria-under-cama-2020)
- LPs are pass-through for tax: "LPs are not taxed but the partners are taxed as individuals on profits from the Partnership" — [Mondaq LP article](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1142692/registration-of-limited-partnerships-in-nigeria-under-cama-2020)
- CAC fee (Sept 2025 schedule): LP registration incl. CTC ₦30,000 (was ₦15,000) — [SplashDict](https://splashdict.com/blog/cac/understanding-the-new-2025-cac-service-fees/)

### Inferences
- For a group pooling money where a few people run the business and most are passive, the Nigerian LP mirrors the global private-fund structure (GP manages, LPs supply capital) — but the 20-person cap and the loss of limited liability on management participation make it unsuitable for large or hands-on groups.
- The LLP is the natural choice for a professional-services or operating partnership where all partners work and want limited liability and perpetual succession without company-style share capital; the key constitutional document is the LLP agreement (partnership deed), which CAMA says governs transfer of partners' economic rights.
- A general partnership (business name) is cheapest and fastest but exposes every member's personal assets; it is only appropriate where trust is high and the business is low-liability.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the primary CAC fee PDF (HTTP 403); the current (Sept 2025) LLP registration fee itself was not stated in the secondary source summary — only the LLP name-reservation and change-of-name fees.
- No source gave an official LP registration timeline; LLP's "14 days" comes from a law-firm explainer, not CAC.
- Tax treatment of Nigerian LLPs (whether FIRS taxes the LLP as a company or looks through to partners) was discussed in the Andersen/Mondaq LLP review but I could not extract a definitive statement; treat as unresolved.

## Key Question 2: Private limited company (Ltd / LLC)

### Takeaway
A private company limited by shares is the default pooled-capital vehicle worldwide: ownership is recorded as shares in a register of members, liability is limited to unpaid share capital, and governance is by directors subject to statutory duties, with a shareholders' agreement layering in transfer, exit and deadlock rules. Nigeria's CAMA 2020 allows a single shareholder and (for small companies) a single director, requires minimum issued share capital of ₦100,000 for private companies, caps private-company membership at 50, and CAC's stated incorporation timeline is 24 hours (2–10 business days in practice).

### Cited Findings

Nigeria — formation and membership
- s.18(2) CAMA 2020: one person may form and incorporate a private company (single-member company), down from the two-shareholder minimum under CAMA 1990 — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/opinion/article/single-member-companies-and-the-restriction-to-convert/); [Starlion Legal](https://starlionlegal.wordpress.com/2020/09/05/50-key-points-on-the-new-companies-and-allied-matters-act-cama-2020/)
- The Federal High Court has ruled that pre-CAMA 2020 private companies can transition to a single-shareholder structure — [Mondaq (2024)](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1500156/federal-high-court-rules-that-pre-cama-2020-private-companies-can-transition-to-single-shareholder-structure)
- s.22: a private company's membership shall not exceed 50 (excluding current and continuing former employees); subject to its articles a private company may restrict share transfers; it may require a member to offer shares to existing members before selling to a non-member; and a member or group may not sell more than 50% of the shares to a non-member unless that buyer offers to buy all existing members' shares on the same terms — [LawGlobal Hub, s.22 CAMA 2020](https://www.lawglobalhub.com/section-22-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020/); [Baker Tilly Nigeria](https://www.bakertilly.ng/insights/highlights-of-critical-changes-introduced-by-the-amended-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020)
- Statement of compliance (s.40): applicants may sign the compliance statement themselves, replacing the old lawyer/notary declaration — [Starlion Legal](https://starlionlegal.wordpress.com/2020/09/05/50-key-points-on-the-new-companies-and-allied-matters-act-cama-2020/)

Nigeria — share capital and share classes
- CAMA 2020 abolished "authorised share capital" in favour of minimum issued share capital: ₦100,000 for private companies (up from ₦10,000) and ₦2,000,000 for public companies (up from ₦500,000); the whole share capital must be issued (the old 25% rule is gone) — [Aluko & Oyebode](https://www.aluko-oyebode.com/insights/minimum-issued-share-capital-requirement-in-the-cama-2020/); [Mondaq Part 5 — Share Capital](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1023802/the-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020--what-you-need-to-know--part-5-share-capital)
- s.124: companies with unissued shares had six months from commencement (clarified by the Companies Regulations 2021 as 30 June 2021) to issue shares up to the minimum; compliance options included bonus/rights issues, issues to new shareholders, or cancelling unissued shares; CAC applies daily default penalties — [Aluko & Oyebode](https://www.aluko-oyebode.com/insights/minimum-issued-share-capital-requirement-in-the-cama-2020/); [Stren & Blan](https://strenandblan.com/companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020-requirement-for-minimum-issued-share-capital/)
- s.127: to increase issued share capital, the company passes a resolution approving the allotment, notifies CAC within 15 days, and pays stamp duty and filing fees; where a regulatory approval is pending an affidavit may be filed, with a 9-month outer limit before the increase lapses — [Mondaq Part 5](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1023802/the-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020--what-you-need-to-know--part-5-share-capital)
- s.175: shares may be transferred electronically — [Starlion Legal](https://starlionlegal.wordpress.com/2020/09/05/50-key-points-on-the-new-companies-and-allied-matters-act-cama-2020/)
- Stamp duty of 0.75% on share capital is payable on incorporation in addition to CAC fees — [Fbanabena & Co (2025)](https://www.fbanabena.com/cac-registration/cac-registration-fees/)

Nigeria — "small company" concessions
- s.394/395: a small company is a private company with turnover ≤ ₦120m, net assets ≤ ₦60m, no alien or government members, and directors holding at least 51% of equity; a small company may have a single director (s.271), need not appoint a company secretary (s.330), and is exempt from audit (s.402, except banks/insurers); private companies may hold virtual general meetings (s.240) — [Starlion Legal](https://starlionlegal.wordpress.com/2020/09/05/50-key-points-on-the-new-companies-and-allied-matters-act-cama-2020/)
- s.119: companies must disclose persons with significant control (5%+ interest) to CAC — [Starlion Legal](https://starlionlegal.wordpress.com/2020/09/05/50-key-points-on-the-new-companies-and-allied-matters-act-cama-2020/); the LLP form also asks for partners with 5%+ interest/control — [Pavestones Legal](https://pavestoneslegal.com/procedure-for-registration-of-a-limited-liability-partnership-in-nigeria/)

Nigeria — CAC fees and timelines
- Sept 2025 CAC schedule: private/small company incorporation ₦10,000 per ₦1m of share capital (unchanged); public company ₦20,000 per ₦1m; company limited by guarantee ₦40,000 (was ₦20,000); small company annual return ₦5,000; same-day filing surcharge ₦30,000 (small) / ₦50,000 (public); increase of share capital filing ₦10,000–₦20,000 (₦1m–₦500m band); voluntary striking-off ₦50,000 (small); CTC of documents and status report ₦5,000 each; penalties for late filing reduced to ₦100/day (small) and ₦500/day (public) — [SplashDict](https://splashdict.com/blog/cac/understanding-the-new-2025-cac-service-fees/); [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/ask-legit/guides/1709408-everything-budget-cac-registration-fees-nigeria/)
- Conflicting figure: one 2025 explainer states a limited company "starts at ₦25,000 for share capital up to ₦1 million" plus 0.75% stamp duty — [Fbanabena & Co](https://www.fbanabena.com/cac-registration/cac-registration-fees/); the SplashDict table says ₦10,000 per ₦1m. The ₦25,000 figure may bundle name reservation, CTC and agent costs; confirm against the CAC PDF.
- CAC's official service timeline lists name reservation same-day (if submitted before 5:05pm) and private company registration within 24 hours — [CAC Service Timeline](https://www.cac.gov.ng/faq_category/service-timeline/) (page returned 403 on direct fetch; figures via [9jadirectory 2026 guide](https://www.9jadirectory.org/blog/cac-pre-incorporation-guide-nigeria-2026)); practitioners report 2–10 business days in practice — [Lawzana (2026)](https://lawzana.com/articles/nigeria/cac-registration-in-nigeria-step-by-step-guide-2026-881); [Mecer Consulting (2025)](https://mecer.consulting/blog/company-incorporation-nigeria-step-guide-2025/)
- Registration is online at pre.cac.gov.ng, covering business names, private companies and incorporated trustees — [9jadirectory](https://www.9jadirectory.org/blog/cac-pre-incorporation-guide-nigeria-2026)

Shareholders' agreements (global practice)
- A typical shareholders' agreement covers: share structure/cap table, voting and quorum, board composition and reserved matters, dividend policy, transfer restrictions (ROFR, pre-emption), drag-along and tag-along, non-compete/non-solicit, deadlock provisions, and exit/winding-up mechanics — [Linkilaw](https://linkilawsolicitors.com/corporate/key-clauses-in-a-shareholders-agreement/); [VK Legal](https://vklegalassociates.com/shareholders-agreement-demystified-key-clauses-explained/)
- Drag-along lets majority holders (often >75%) compel minorities to sell on the same terms; tag-along lets minorities join a majority sale on the same terms; pre-emption requires offering shares to existing holders first — [Turtons](https://www.turtons.com/blog/tag-along-drag-along-and-other-exit-provisions-in-a-shareholders-agreement)
- Vesting schedules and cliffs can be written into a shareholders' agreement to govern when founders earn their shares and what happens if one fails to deliver — [Medium / E. Japonas](https://medium.com/@electrajaponas/shareholders-agreements-vesting-schedules-and-cliffs-45e86afa6b78)
- A 50/50 split needs an explicit deadlock mechanism (defined trigger, escalation, buy-sell), otherwise "the company freezes" — [Contract Law Miami](https://contractlawmiami.com/shareholder-agreements-business-owners/)

### Inferences
- Nigeria's ₦100,000 minimum issued capital plus ₦10,000-per-₦1m CAC fee and 0.75% stamp duty means a small group can incorporate a Ltd for well under ₦50,000 in statutory fees (2025) if share capital is kept at the minimum; capital can be raised later under s.127.
- The s.22 statutory pre-emption and 50%-sale protections are default-ish private-company rules in Nigeria, so a Nigerian shareholders' agreement should be drafted to interact with (not contradict) the articles and s.22.
- The 50-member cap on private companies (s.22) is the practical ceiling for a Ltd used as an investment club; above that a cooperative or a registered scheme is needed.

Treat specific director-duty sections as unverified.

## Key Question 3: Cooperative societies

### Takeaway
Cooperatives are member-owned, democratically controlled (one member, one vote) enterprises that return surplus in proportion to patronage rather than capital. In Nigeria they are registered by each state's Director of Cooperatives under the Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act and state laws, need at least 10 members (20 for savings & credit, 50 for agricultural, 6 for industrial), must file bye-laws, cap any member at 20% of shares, enjoy company-income-tax exemption, and are outside CAMA — but registration typically takes months and includes a 3-year "letter of recognition" period before a full certificate in some states.

### Cited Findings

Global model
- The International Co-operative Alliance (ICA) is custodian of the co-operative values and principles (1995 Statement); under the democratic-control principle, "in primary co-operatives members have equal voting rights (one member, one vote)" — [ICA Guidance Notes](https://ica.coop/sites/default/files/basic-page-attachments/guidance-notes-en-221700169.pdf); [ICA: What is a cooperative?](https://ica.coop/en/cooperatives/what-is-a-cooperative)
- Members allocate surpluses to developing the co-op, reserves, "benefiting members in proportion to their transactions with the co-operative" (patronage dividends), and other approved activities — [ICA Guidance Notes](https://ica.coop/sites/default/files/basic-page-attachments/guidance-notes-en-221700169.pdf); [NCDC Nebraska](https://ncdc.unl.edu/what-cooperative/)
- Members have an equal say regardless of capital contributed — [Cooperative Network](https://cooperativenetwork.coop/what-is-a-cooperative/)

Nigeria — statute and registration
- Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act s.2: a society eligible for registration must be a "limited liability society" whose objects promote the socio-economic interests of its members in accordance with co-operative principles; s.3: a primary society may be registered if it consists of at least ten persons qualified for membership (industrial societies: minimum six and economically viable); the word "Co-operative" must form part of the name and "limited" must be the last word; s.1: the President may appoint a Federal Director and state governors may appoint state Directors of Co-operatives — [LawGlobal Hub: Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://www.lawglobalhub.com/nigerian-co-operative-societies-act-ng/); full text at [FAO FAOLEX PDF](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)
- Applications must be accompanied by the prescribed number of copies of the proposed bye-laws; the Director registers the society and its bye-laws if satisfied they comply with the Act — [Lexpraxis: Cooperative Societies in Nigeria](https://lexpraxisng.com/co-operative-societies-in-nigeria-everything-you-need-to-know/); [Doing Business in Nigeria](https://doingbusinessinnigeria.org/how-to-register-co-operative-society-in-nigeria/)
- Minimum members by type (as applied by state registrars): agricultural societies 50+; savings and credit (thrift & credit) 20+; specialised-skill and other types 10+; members must be at least 15 (board members 18+) and share a common need/occupation — [Legal500: Procedure for Registration of Cooperative Society in Nigeria](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/strategy/procedure-for-registration-of-cooperative-society-in-nigeria)
- Required documents (per Legal500): certified resolution of the founding meeting, feasibility study report, four copies of proposed bye-laws, letters of intent from prospective members; process: application and fee to the Director, document assessment, letter of recognition (valid 3 years), then Certificate of Registration with certified bye-laws — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/strategy/procedure-for-registration-of-cooperative-society-in-nigeria)
- Lagos State: application to the Director of Cooperatives, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Cooperatives; at least 20 members aged 18+ "of good character and of sound mind"; documents include letter of intent, application form, invitation to inaugural meeting, statement of objectives and a copy of bye-laws; steps include an inaugural meeting, fee payment, a trustee induction course at the Lagos State Cooperative College, and a 3-month monitoring period before certificate issuance — [Pavestones: Registering a Cooperative Society in Lagos State](https://pavestoneslegal.com/registering-a-cooperative-society-in-lagos-state/); [Lagos MCCTI Dept of Cooperative Services](https://mccti.lagosstate.gov.ng/departments/cooperatives)
- Fees/timeline (secondary, undated as to year): registration fee around ₦10,000, with ranges of ₦5,000–₦25,000 depending on type; 2–3 months to receive certificate; letter of recognition allows operation for three years — [Smartteller (Medium) Lagos guide](https://smartteller.medium.com/a-step-by-step-guide-to-registering-a-cooperative-society-in-lagos-state-nigeria-e1843630a9e7); [Resolution Law](https://www.resolutionlawng.com/procedure-for-the-establishment-of-a-cooperative-society-in-nigeria/)

Nigeria — cooperatives as pooled-investment vehicles
- At least ten qualified individuals must sign the application; "no individual member can hold more than 20% of the shares of the society" — [Pavestones: Establishing a Cooperative Society for Investment Purposes](https://pavestoneslegal.com/establishing-a-cooperative-society-for-investment-purposes-in-nigeria/)
- Cooperatives are "exempt from payment of company income tax on the profit or income generated from its activities including shares or interest held in other entities", and avoid stamp duties and registration fees on related instruments — [Pavestones](https://pavestoneslegal.com/establishing-a-cooperative-society-for-investment-purposes-in-nigeria/)
- Cooperatives are "exempt from the provisions of the Companies and Allied Matters Act (CAMA)" — no CAC annual returns or charge registration — but must file returns with the Director of Cooperatives; they have separate legal personality and limited liability and may invest funds in banks, FGN-backed securities, or as their constitution provides — [Pavestones](https://pavestoneslegal.com/establishing-a-cooperative-society-for-investment-purposes-in-nigeria/)
- A cooperative certificate is "limited in its application": lending to non-members or offering foreign-exchange services requires CBN licensing — [Pavestones Lagos](https://pavestoneslegal.com/registering-a-cooperative-society-in-lagos-state/)
- Post-registration: maintain statutory books (cash book, general ledger), annual statements and regular meetings — [Pavestones Lagos](https://pavestoneslegal.com/registering-a-cooperative-society-in-lagos-state/)

### Inferences
- For a Nigerian group of 10–20+ people pooling savings and lending to members (esusu/ajo formalised), the thrift & credit cooperative is the purpose-built vehicle: tax-exempt, cheap to register, limited liability, and outside CAMA — at the cost of one-member-one-vote (capital does not buy control), a 20% per-member share cap, and slower, state-by-state registration.
- A cooperative is a poor fit for a venture where a few investors want control proportional to money contributed, or where outsiders (non-members) are to be served.

### Gaps
- I could not read the full text of the Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act (paywalled on LawGlobal Hub; FAO PDF not parseable), so section numbers for liability, voting, the one-fifth shareholding cap, dividend limits and reserve-fund percentage are not quoted. The 20% share cap and tax exemption are from a law-firm explainer only.
- No authoritative 2025/2026 state fee schedule for cooperative registration was found; fees are state-specific and the figures above are from practitioner blogs.

## Key Question 4: Investment clubs, syndicates and SPVs

### Takeaway
Globally, deal-by-deal pooling is done through an SPV (usually a Delaware LLC or series LLC) sold under a private-placement exemption (Reg D 506(b)/(c)) with investor-count caps (100, or 250 for small VC funds), run by a lead who takes carry; platforms like AngelList charge roughly $8,000 setup plus state filing fees. In Nigeria, an informal club can sit under a business name, Ltd or cooperative, but the ISA 2025 now treats pooled funds (including PE/VC) as collective investment schemes needing SEC registration, criminalises Ponzi/pyramid schemes (₦20m fine and/or 10 years), and penalises unregistered schemes (₦10m minimum plus ₦100,000/day); SEC's 2021 Crowdfunding Rules govern public online raising (₦50m/₦70m/₦100m caps).

### Cited Findings

Global — SPVs and angel syndicates
- An SPV lead creates a syndicate vehicle; LPs commit and wire by a deadline; the SPV makes one consolidated investment and appears as a single line on the company's cap table — [Angel Investors Network (2026)](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/angel-investing/angellist-syndicates-rolling-funds-spv-explained-2026); [Startups.com lexicon](https://www.startups.com/lexicon/spv)
- AngelList (2026): $8,000 SPV setup fee plus ~$2,000 state regulatory filing fee per vehicle; minimum raise $80,000 ($50,000 for follow-ons); setup/regulatory fees capped at 10% of capital raised above minimum thresholds; ongoing admin (legal, filings, tax prep) $5,000–$20,000 per year borne by the SPV — [Angel Investors Network: SPV setup costs 2026](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/capital-raising/spv-setup-costs-what-angel-investors-actually-pay-in-2026); [Allocations vs AngelList pricing 2026](https://www.allocations.com/insights/angellist-pricing-vs-allocations-pricing-(2026))
- Carry: a standard AngelList syndicate lead takes 20% of profits above 1.0x with no hurdle; AngelList takes an additional 5% carry on capital sourced from its own LP network, so platform LPs effectively pay ~25% — [Angel Investors Network](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/angel-investing/angellist-syndicates-rolling-funds-spv-explained-2026); [Capitaly](https://www.capitaly.vc/blog/inside-jason-calacanis-syndicate-spvs-fees-carry-founder-negotiation-tips)
- Delaware series LLC: one master LLC with a thin operating agreement naming the manager; each deal is a separate "series" with its own operating agreement, members, capital commitments, waterfall and investment; if statutory conditions are met (separate records, notice in certificate of formation) each series' assets are shielded from other series' creditors — [Lexology: Delaware Series LLCs for SPV investing](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=82e294b4-5ad5-4833-a70b-b8bdb02e9c34); [Allocations: Delaware Series LLC for SPVs](https://www.allocations.com/insights/delaware-series-llc-for-spvs)
- US securities limits: Rule 506(b) permits up to 35 sophisticated non-accredited purchasers (no general solicitation) and unlimited accredited investors; Section 3(c)(1) of the Investment Company Act caps a private fund/SPV at 100 beneficial owners, or 250 for a "qualifying venture capital fund" with ≤ $12m aggregate contributions and uncalled commitments — [Databento: Rule 506(b)](https://databento.com/compliance/rule-506-b); [Repool: Section 3(c)(1)](https://repool.com/fundpedia/section-3c1/); [Sydecar compliance guide](https://sydecar.io/learn/legal-and-regulatory-compliance); [Syndicately: 506(b) vs 506(c) for SPVs](https://www.syndicately.com/blog/506b-vs-506c-spv/)

Nigeria — investment clubs
- Nigerian investment clubs commonly register as partnerships, limited liability companies or cooperative societies; registration "gives the club a formal identity, protects its name, and provides a legal framework for member agreements and asset ownership"; cooperatives can ease access to government-backed loans, especially for agriculture — [Lexpraxis: How to start an investment club in Nigeria](https://lexpraxisng.com/how-to-start-an-investment-club-in-nigeria/)
- Clubs need member agreements/bye-laws (rights, contributions, profit split, voting), contribution and investment records, and a dedicated bank account; registered clubs file CAC annual returns and must be FIRS-compliant; "most small clubs don't require SEC registration", but larger pools or public solicitation may trigger SEC compliance — [Lexpraxis](https://lexpraxisng.com/how-to-start-an-investment-club-in-nigeria/)
- A business name is available only to Nigerians and is in substance a sole proprietorship/partnership with no separate legal personality; a company creates a separate legal entity distinct from its owners — [Moniepoint: Types of business registration in Nigeria](https://moniepoint.com/blog/types-of-business-registration-in-nigeria); [Metalex Legal](https://metalexlegal.com/publication/article/registering-your-business-name-and-incorporating-a-company-in-nigeria.4)

Nigeria — SEC regime (ISA 2025 and 2021 Crowdfunding Rules)
- ISA 2025 (signed by President Tinubu in 2025) explicitly recognises PE and VC funds as collective investment schemes; s.150 defines CIS to cover open- and closed-ended funds "including those offered only to qualified investors"; s.151 lets SEC approve CIS legal forms including limited partnerships, unit trusts and contractual arrangements; all PE/VC funds are presumed to be CIS requiring SEC registration — [Mondaq / ISA 2025: what is changing for private capital players (June 2025)](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/venture-capital/1638148/nigerias-investments-and-securities-act-2025-what-is-changing-for-private-capital-players); [UUBO analysis (May 2025)](https://uubo.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/UPDATED-DETAILED-ANALYSIS-OF-ISA-2025-WHAT-IS-CHANGING-FOR-PE-AND-VC-FUNDS-1.pdf)
- s.196 and s.357 ISA 2025 prohibit Ponzi and pyramid schemes; promoters face fines of ₦20 million and/or up to 10 years' imprisonment; SEC may seal premises and seek forfeiture of scheme assets via the Investments and Securities Tribunal or Federal High Court — [Mondaq ISA 2025](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/venture-capital/1638148/nigerias-investments-and-securities-act-2025-what-is-changing-for-private-capital-players); [Nairametrics (1 Apr 2025)](https://nairametrics.com/2025/04/01/isa-2025-ponzi-schemes-promoters-in-nigeria-now-face-10-years-jail-term-sec-dg/); [TNP](https://tnp.com.ng/nigerias-investments-and-securities-act-2025-a-new-era-for-capital-markets/)
- s.155(4) ISA 2025: non-registration or unauthorised fund activity attracts a minimum fine of ₦10 million plus ₦100,000 per day of default; foreign CIS marketed to Nigerians without approval face ₦10m or 10% of the gross value offered, whichever is higher — [Mondaq ISA 2025](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/venture-capital/1638148/nigerias-investments-and-securities-act-2025-what-is-changing-for-private-capital-players); [Nairacompare](https://nairacompare.ng/blogs/current-sec-investment-regulations-in-nigeria-what-investors-must-know)
- Before ISA 2025 SEC "had no legal backing to prosecute Ponzi scheme operators"; SEC flagged at least 7–10 platforms as Ponzi/illegal schemes in 2025 and publishes a verification portal — [Nairametrics (July 2025)](https://nairametrics.com/2025/07/21/see-7-investment-platforms-flagged-as-ponzi-scheme-by-sec-so-far-in-2025/); [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/economy/1661085-sec-flags-10-ponzi-illegal-investment-schemes-nigeria-releases-link-check-authenticity/)
- SEC Rules on Crowdfunding (issued 12 Jan 2021, effective 21 Jan 2021 under s.13(a) ISA 2007): investment-based crowdfunding portals may be operated only by SEC-registered Crowdfunding Intermediaries; existing platforms had to register or cease by 30 June 2021 — [ǼLEX](https://aelex.com/the-nigerian-securities-and-exchange-commission-crowdfunding-rules/); [SEC Nigeria Jan 2021 Executed Rules (PDF)](https://sec.gov.ng/documents/15/Jan-2021-Executed-Rules.pdf)
- Eligible fundraisers: MSMEs incorporated in Nigeria with a 2-year operating track record (or a technical partner/core investor with 2+ years); 12-month raise caps ₦100m (medium), ₦70m (small), ₦50m (micro); retail investors capped at 10% of net annual income per calendar year; offers open max 90 days (60 + 30 extension); no secondary trading via the portal — [ǼLEX](https://aelex.com/the-nigerian-securities-and-exchange-commission-crowdfunding-rules/); [Mondaq crowdfunding rules](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/securities/1091098/the-nigerian-securities-and-exchange-commission-crowdfunding-rules--its-impact-on-startups-and-smes)
- TechCabal reported a ₦100m minimum paid-up capital for crowdfunding intermediaries — [TechCabal (Feb 2021)](https://techcabal.com/2021/02/15/crowdfunding-regulations/); ǼLEX's summary instead cites a 30:70 liquid-to-fixed asset ratio and does not state ₦100m — treat the capital figure as reported but not confirmed from the rules text.

### Inferences
- A closed, private Nigerian club of friends (no public solicitation, members know each other, contributions pooled in a Ltd/cooperative they jointly own) is unlikely to be a "collective investment scheme" in SEC's sights, but anything that invites the public, promises returns, or manages third-party money for a fee moves into ISA 2025 territory with severe penalties — so the SPV/syndicate model common in the US needs careful structuring in Nigeria (likely via an SEC-registered fund manager or a registered LP-form CIS under s.151).
- The Nigerian LP (CAMA Part D) is the closest statutory analogue to a US fund SPV (GP + passive LPs), and ISA 2025 s.151 explicitly contemplates LP-form CIS — but the CAMA 20-partner cap limits a single LP vehicle to 19 passive investors.
- For a one-asset real-estate purchase among a handful of people in Nigeria, a Ltd holding the title (shares recorded in the register of members, with a shareholders' agreement setting the waterfall and exit) is the pragmatic SPV, since Nigerian land registries recognise company ownership and the Ltd has no public-offer element.

### Gaps
- I did not find a Nigerian source quantifying when a private pooled vehicle crosses into a registrable CIS (e.g., investor-count or value thresholds, or a "qualified investor" definition in ISA 2025); the Mondaq extract said the Act does not state explicit thresholds.
- No source covered SEC Nigeria's rules for private-placement SPVs or angel syndicates specifically; this remains a regulatory grey area in the literature found.
- Crowdfunding intermediary registration fees and the cooling-off period were not found in the fetched sources.

## Key Question 5: Joint ventures and unincorporated associations

### Takeaway
Operating without a registered entity means the group has no separate legal personality: members (or whoever signs) are personally and often jointly-and-severally liable, assets must be held by individuals or trustees, and the arrangement ends or must be re-papered when members change. Contractual JVs are fine for defined, short-lived projects between parties who each carry their own liability; for pooled money with many contributors, incorporation is the standard advice.

### Cited Findings
- An unincorporated association has no separate legal personality from its members; unlike a company "there is no separate body with limited liability" — [Lexology: Liability in Unincorporated Associations](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=cd347d14-58c3-45e1-80e9-dfcacbbe50bc)
- Where the association's rules authorise a member to sign a contract, that member signs as agent and all members are liable for breach; where the signer was not authorised, he or she may be personally liable — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=cd347d14-58c3-45e1-80e9-dfcacbbe50bc)
- Members of unincorporated organisations are personally liable for the organisation's debts and legal obligations — [FindLaw](https://www.findlaw.com/smallbusiness/incorporation-and-legal-structures/what-is-an-unincorporated-organization.html)
- Members can be jointly and severally liable for each other's acts arising in the association's context — "personally, and each to the full extent of potential damages" — [Wagenmaker & Oberly: Should We Incorporate](https://www.wagenmakerlaw.com/blog/should-we-incorporate)
- Only a few US states have statutes protecting members of unincorporated associations, and those protections typically cover nonprofit associations, with exceptions for for-profit ventures — [TaxShark](https://taxsharkinc.com/who-is-liable-in-an-unincorporated-association/)
- Accounting treats "unincorporated joint ventures" alongside partnerships and LLCs as pass-through vehicles whose investors account for their share (equity method) — [Deloitte DART ASC 323](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/codification/assets/32x/asc323-10/roadmap-equity-method-investments-jv/chapter-2-scope-scope-exceptions/2-2-investments-in-partnerships-unincorporated)
- Nigeria: a general partnership (business name) is likewise not a separate legal entity, partners have unlimited liability and no perpetual succession — [Mondaq (2024)](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1430552/registering-business-partnerships-in-nigeria-general-partnerships-limited-partnerships-and-limited-liability-partnerships-in-nigeria)

### Inferences
- In Nigeria an informal savings/investment group that never registers is, legally, either an unincorporated association or a general partnership at law (if carrying on business for profit) — meaning every member can be pursued for the group's debts and the group cannot hold land or a bank account in its own name without nominees.
- A contractual JV (no new entity) is only sensible where each party is already an incorporated entity carrying its own limited liability, and the JV agreement allocates contributions, control, profit split, IP and exit.

### Gaps
- I found no Nigerian case law or statute specifically on liability of members of unincorporated associations (as opposed to partnerships); the findings above are English/US common-law commentary, which Nigerian courts generally follow but this was not verified.

## Key Question 6: Decision matrix — which structure fits which situation

### Takeaway
Pick by four variables: headcount, who is active vs. passive, liability appetite, and exit/return mechanics. Small active teams → LLP (or Ltd if raising equity); few managers with passive backers → LP (≤20 in Nigeria) or Ltd with a shareholders' agreement; many small savers/borrowers → cooperative; one asset or one deal → SPV Ltd (Nigeria) / LLC-series (US); public or stranger money → SEC-registered scheme or crowdfunding portal, never an unregistered pool.

### Cited Findings
- Headcount limits: Nigerian general partnership and LP max 20 partners; LLP unlimited; private company max 50 members (s.22); cooperative min 10 (20 thrift & credit, 50 agricultural, 6 industrial); US SPV 100 beneficial owners (250 for qualifying VC fund ≤ $12m) and 35 non-accredited under 506(b) — [Mondaq partnerships](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1430552/registering-business-partnerships-in-nigeria-general-partnerships-limited-partnerships-and-limited-liability-partnerships-in-nigeria); [LawGlobal Hub s.22](https://www.lawglobalhub.com/section-22-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020/); [Legal500 cooperatives](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/strategy/procedure-for-registration-of-cooperative-society-in-nigeria); [Repool 3(c)(1)](https://repool.com/fundpedia/section-3c1/)
- Liability: general partnership unlimited; LP limited for limited partners only (lost on management); LLP and Ltd limited for all owners; cooperative limited ("limited liability society", s.2); unincorporated association — members personally liable — [Mondaq LP](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1142692/registration-of-limited-partnerships-in-nigeria-under-cama-2020); [LawGlobal Hub NCS Act](https://www.lawglobalhub.com/nigerian-co-operative-societies-act-ng/); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=cd347d14-58c3-45e1-80e9-dfcacbbe50bc)
- Control vs capital: company — votes follow shares (with s.22 transfer protections); cooperative — one member, one vote regardless of capital, surplus by patronage — [ICA](https://ica.coop/en/cooperatives/what-is-a-cooperative); [LawGlobal Hub s.22](https://www.lawglobalhub.com/section-22-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020/)
- Tax: Nigerian LP is pass-through (partners taxed); Nigerian cooperatives exempt from company income tax; a small Ltd is audit-exempt (s.402) but pays CIT — [Mondaq LP](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1142692/registration-of-limited-partnerships-in-nigeria-under-cama-2020); [Pavestones cooperative](https://pavestoneslegal.com/establishing-a-cooperative-society-for-investment-purposes-in-nigeria/); [Starlion Legal](https://starlionlegal.wordpress.com/2020/09/05/50-key-points-on-the-new-companies-and-allied-matters-act-cama-2020/)
- Cost/speed (Nigeria, Sept 2025 CAC schedule): business name ₦20,000 + ₦1,000 reservation, fastest; LP ₦30,000; Ltd ₦10,000 per ₦1m capital + 0.75% stamp duty, CAC target 24 hours, 2–10 business days in practice; cooperative ~₦5,000–₦25,000 but 2–3 months plus a recognition/monitoring period — [SplashDict](https://splashdict.com/blog/cac/understanding-the-new-2025-cac-service-fees/); [Fbanabena](https://www.fbanabena.com/cac-registration/cac-registration-fees/); [Lawzana](https://lawzana.com/articles/nigeria/cac-registration-in-nigeria-step-by-step-guide-2026-881); [Smartteller](https://smartteller.medium.com/a-step-by-step-guide-to-registering-a-cooperative-society-in-lagos-state-nigeria-e1843630a9e7)
- Regulatory perimeter: pooling from the public or running a fund in Nigeria = CIS under ISA 2025 (registration or ₦10m+ fines; Ponzi = ₦20m/10 years); public online raising = SEC Crowdfunding Rules via registered intermediary with ₦50–100m caps — [Mondaq ISA 2025](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/venture-capital/1638148/nigerias-investments-and-securities-act-2025-what-is-changing-for-private-capital-players); [ǼLEX](https://aelex.com/the-nigerian-securities-and-exchange-commission-crowdfunding-rules/)
- Key documents by structure: partnership deed / LLP agreement (partnerships, governs profit share and transfer of interests); articles + shareholders' agreement with ROFR/pre-emption, drag/tag, vesting, deadlock, reserved matters (company); bye-laws registered with the Director (cooperative); operating agreement per series + subscription agreement (US SPV); member agreement/bye-laws and bank mandate (investment club) — [Mondaq partnerships](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1430552/registering-business-partnerships-in-nigeria-general-partnerships-limited-partnerships-and-limited-liability-partnerships-in-nigeria); [Linkilaw](https://linkilawsolicitors.com/corporate/key-clauses-in-a-shareholders-agreement/); [Lexpraxis cooperatives](https://lexpraxisng.com/co-operative-societies-in-nigeria-everything-you-need-to-know/); [Allocations series LLC](https://www.allocations.com/insights/delaware-series-llc-for-spvs); [Lexpraxis investment club](https://lexpraxisng.com/how-to-start-an-investment-club-in-nigeria/)

### Inferences (proposed matrix)
| Situation | Nigeria | Global analogue | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2–5 people, all working in the business, service/professional | LLP (CAMA Part C) | UK LLP / US LLP | All limited liability, flexible profit split via LLP agreement, perpetual succession, no share capital formalities |
| Small team raising equity now or later, wants investors with proportional votes | Ltd (private company) + shareholders' agreement | Ltd / Delaware C-corp or LLC | Shares are the unit of ownership; s.22 transfer protections; audit-exempt if small; ≤50 members |
| 1–3 managers plus up to ~17 passive money partners for a venture or fund | LP (CAMA Part D) — likely needs SEC registration if it is a "fund" | Delaware LP / LLC fund | GP runs it, LPs capped at contribution; pass-through tax; 20-partner cap |
| 10–50+ people saving monthly and lending/investing for members | Thrift & credit or multipurpose cooperative (state registrar) | Credit union / co-op | Tax-exempt, limited liability, cheap; but one-member-one-vote and 20% share cap; slow registration |
| One building / one deal, 3–15 investors who know each other | SPV Ltd holding the asset, shareholders' agreement with waterfall | Delaware (series) LLC under 506(b) | Separate ring-fenced liability, clean exit by selling shares or the asset; stays outside public-offer rules |
| Raising from strangers / online | Only via SEC-registered crowdfunding intermediary (₦50–100m caps) or registered CIS | Reg CF / registered fund | Unregistered pooled scheme = ISA 2025 penalties |
| Short project between existing companies | Contractual JV (no entity) | Contractual JV | Each party keeps its own limited liability; avoid if individuals are parties |
| Friends' informal club with no registration | Unincorporated association / general partnership at law | Same | Zero cost but every member personally liable; cannot hold assets in group name |

### Gaps
- The matrix is my synthesis; no single source published an equivalent Nigeria-specific decision table.
- Comparative tax figures (Nigerian CIT rate for small vs. medium companies under the 2025 tax reform acts, tax on LLP profits) were outside the sources retrieved and should be verified before relying on the "tax" column.
