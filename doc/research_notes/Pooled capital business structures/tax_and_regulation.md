# Tax, Regulatory and Compliance Side of Pooling Money into a Business (Nigeria primary; US/UK comparators)

Research date: 8 October 2026. Nigeria's four tax reform acts (Nigeria Tax Act 2025 "NTA", Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025 "NTAA", Nigeria Revenue Service (Establishment) Act 2025, Joint Revenue Board (Establishment) Act 2025) were signed 26 June 2025 and took effect 1 January 2026. Everything marked **[2026 CHANGE]** below changed with those acts. Pre-2026 periods remain governed by the repealed CITA/PITA/CGTA/VAT Act/Stamp Duties Act.

Source-quality note: Big-4 explainers (EY, PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries last reviewed 29 May 2026, KPMG, Andersen, Baker Tilly) and law-firm notes (Aluko & Oyebode, Udo Udoma & Belo-Osagie, Banwo & Ighodalo, Mondaq-syndicated articles) were preferred. The gazetted NTA/NTAA PDFs and the SEC Nigeria crowdfunding-rules PDF could not be fetched (403 / image-only PDF), so section numbers are quoted only where a secondary source quotes them. One material conflict is flagged (EY's June 2025 alert reported a NGN50m small-company turnover threshold, which appears to be a pre-final figure; the gazetted Act, PwC and all 2026 sources use NGN100m).

---

## Key Question 1: Nigeria taxation by structure (partnerships, companies, cooperatives, WHT, VAT, CGT, stamp duties)

### Takeaway
From 1 January 2026 Nigeria taxes a pooled-capital vehicle very differently depending on its wrapper: an unincorporated partnership or LLP is fully transparent (partners pay personal income tax at 0–25% on their share plus any salary/interest-on-capital), a "small company" (turnover ≤ NGN100m and fixed assets ≤ NGN250m) pays 0% CIT, 0% CGT and no development levy while any larger company pays 30% CIT + 4% development levy with gains taxed at 30%, and a registered cooperative society is exempt from income tax on its mutual/non-commercial profits. Dividends and interest suffer 10% WHT, which is now the final tax on dividends, and the old minimum tax and excess-dividend tax have gone.

### Cited Findings

**Companies — rates and thresholds**
- Small company definition and 0% rate: CIT is "0% for companies with an annual gross turnover of NGN 100 million or less with total fixed assets not exceeding NGN 250 million"; companies above either threshold pay the standard 30%, assessed on a preceding-year basis — [PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, Nigeria corporate income tax (reviewed 29 May 2026)](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income). **[2026 CHANGE]** (previously small ≤ NGN25m turnover at 0%, medium NGN25m–100m at 20%, large >NGN100m at 30% under Finance Act 2019/2020).
- The small-company definition sits in Section 56 NTA and both conditions must be satisfied simultaneously — [AnooreHR explainer](https://anoorehr.com/blog/small-company-tax-exemption-nigeria-2026); [Techpoint Africa guide](https://techpoint.africa/guide/nigerian-tax-reform-acts/).
- Crossing either threshold "even marginally" moves the company into the full-rate bracket; there is no longer an intermediate "medium company" 20% band in the 2026 sources reviewed — [Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/guide/nigerian-tax-reform-acts/). (PwC's 2026 summary lists only 0% and 30% bands.)
- Conflict: EY's alert on signing (June 2025) describes small companies as "NGN50m or less turnover and NGN250m or less in fixed assets" — [EY Tax Alert, Nigeria Tax Act 2025 signed](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/nigeria-tax-act-2025-has-been-signed-highlights); contradicted by PwC (May 2026), Baker Tilly and every 2026 practitioner source which use NGN100m — [Baker Tilly Nigeria](https://www.bakertilly.ng/insights/nigerias-2025-tax-reform-acts-explained). Treat NGN100m as the operative figure but verify against the gazette.
- Small companies are exempt from CIT, CGT and the Development Levy — [Baker Tilly Nigeria](https://www.bakertilly.ng/insights/nigerias-2025-tax-reform-acts-explained).
- Development Levy: a single 4% levy on assessable profits of Nigerian companies (excluding small and non-resident companies), replacing Tertiary Education Tax, NASENI levy, NITDA IT levy and Police Trust Fund levy — [PwC WWTS](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income); [Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.ng/insights/nigerias-2025-tax-reform-acts-explained). **[2026 CHANGE]**
- Minimum tax (formerly 0.5% of gross turnover for loss-making/low-profit companies) has been abolished under the NTA — [Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/guide/nigerian-tax-reform-acts/); [taxcalc.ng explainer](https://taxcalc.ng/blog/nigeria-tax-act-2025). **[2026 CHANGE]**
- Minimum Effective Tax Rate: companies with turnover ≥ NGN50 billion, or members of MNE groups with ≥ EUR750m consolidated revenue, must bear a 15% effective tax rate with a top-up tax — [PwC WWTS](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income). (EY's earlier alert cited NGN20bn; PwC's later figure of NGN50bn is used here.) Not relevant to small pooled-capital groups.
- Standard CIT is "30% (reducible to 25% via presidential order)" — [PwC WWTS, other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/other-taxes). (Some blogs state a flat 25% rate; this is not what the Act provides — it is an enabling power.)

**Dividends and the end of excess-dividend tax**
- WHT on dividends and interest is 10% for both corporate and individual resident recipients; management/consultancy/professional fees and commission 5%; rent 10%; royalties 10% (corporate) / 5% (individual); directors' fees 15% (individuals). WHT must be remitted by the 21st of the month following payment — [PwC WWTS, withholding taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/withholding-taxes).
- Under Section 19 NTA no further tax is charged on dividends beyond the 10% WHT deducted at source — [taxngr.com, WHT on dividends](https://taxngr.com/wht-on-dividends-in-nigeria/). (Secondary source; the principle that WHT on dividends is final for resident individuals pre-dates 2026.)
- Excess Dividend Tax (old s.19 CITA, which taxed dividends exceeding taxable profit at 30%) no longer applies to dividends paid out of retained earnings already taxed, tax-exempt income, franked investment income or REIT rental/dividend income — [Legal500, Finance Act overview](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/tax/an-overview-of-the-major-changes-introduced-by-the-finance-act-to-nigeria's-tax-regime). (This relief was introduced by Finance Act 2019 and is carried into the NTA.)
- Dividends received by a Nigerian company in the form of shares (scrip) in the paying company are excluded from chargeable profits — [EY Tax Alert](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/nigeria-tax-act-2025-has-been-signed-highlights).
- Dividends distributed by SEC-authorised collective investment schemes are exempt from income tax under s.162(1)(b) NTA for both corporate and individual unitholders; REIT distributions remain subject to WHT as final tax, and the REIT exemption requires ≥75% distribution within 12 months of year end — [Mondaq, Tax treatment of CIS under NTA 2025](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-tax/1819958/an-overview-of-the-tax-treatment-of-collective-investment-schemes-under-the-nigeria-tax-act-2025-and-the-nigeria-tax-administration-act-2025).

**WHT Regulations 2024 (still relevant for 2025 periods and carried forward)**
- The Deduction of Tax at Source (Withholding) Regulations 2024 took effect 1 January 2025; small companies with gross turnover ≤ NGN25m are exempt from WHT deduction where the supplier has a valid TIN and the transaction does not exceed NGN2m in the month; commission/consultancy/technical/management/professional fees 5% (resident) / 10% (non-resident) — [EY Tax News, WHT Regulations 2024](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2024-1347-nigeria-issues-deduction-of-tax-at-source-withholding-regulations-2024); [Mondaq overview](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/withholding-tax/1566828/deduction-of-tax-at-source-withholding-regulations-2024-what-you-need-to-know).
- PwC's 2026 summary states the small-company WHT exemption as "transaction value is less than NGN 2 million and the vendor has a valid TIN" — [PwC WWTS WHT](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/withholding-taxes).

**Capital gains**
- The Capital Gains Tax Act (flat 10%) is repealed from 1 Jan 2026; gains of companies are taxed as part of total profits at 30% (0% for small companies); gains of individuals are taxed within the PIT bands (0–25%) — [EY Tax Alert](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/nigeria-tax-act-2025-has-been-signed-highlights); [PwC WWTS other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/other-taxes); [Mondaq, CGT under NTA 2025](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/capital-gains-tax/1751296/capital-gains-tax-under-the-nigeria-tax-act-2025-no-longer-business-as-usual). **[2026 CHANGE]**
- Share-disposal exemption: gains on Nigerian company shares are exempt where aggregate proceeds in any 12 consecutive months are below NGN150m (up from NGN100m) AND the gain does not exceed NGN10m — [EY Tax Alert](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/nigeria-tax-act-2025-has-been-signed-highlights); [Legal500, non-resident guide](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/tax/nigeria-tax-reforms-for-non-residents-expatriate-foreign-company-guide). **[2026 CHANGE]**
- Indirect disposals of Nigerian shares through offshore holding entities are now within charge, subject to treaties — [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/nigeria-tax-act-2025-has-been-signed-highlights).
- Rollover relief on reinvestment of business-asset proceeds is no longer available — [Mondaq CGT](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/capital-gains-tax/1751296/capital-gains-tax-under-the-nigeria-tax-act-2025-no-longer-business-as-usual). **[2026 CHANGE]**
- Individual exemptions retained: principal private residence, personal chattels under NGN5m, up to two motor vehicles a year, gifts — [Mondaq PIT under NTA](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/capital-gains-tax/1726922/understanding-personal-income-tax-under-the-nigerian-tax-act-2025).
- Compensation for loss of office exemption raised from NGN10m to NGN50m — [Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.ng/insights/nigerias-2025-tax-reform-acts-explained).

**Partnerships and LLPs (pass-through)**
- Partners, not the partnership, are taxed; partners are assessed as individuals under personal income tax on their share of partnership income plus other income — [SSRN, Ademoroti, Taxation of Partners and Partnerships in Nigeria](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/SSRN_ID2965515_code1836193.pdf?abstractid=2965515&mirid=1).
- NTA: a partner's gains or profits are (a) any remuneration, interest on capital, leave passages or other benefits charged to the partnership accounts for that partner, plus (b) the partner's share of partnership profits computed under Chapter Two of the Act — [Nigeria Tax Act 2025 gazette (per search summary)](https://irs.gm.gov.ng/docs/national/NIGERIA_TAX_ACT_2025.pdf).
- For an LLP, all profits are deemed distributed and taxable proportionately in the hands of the partners — [Nigeria Tax Act 2025 gazette (per search summary)](https://irs.gm.gov.ng/docs/national/NIGERIA_TAX_ACT_2025.pdf).
- Where companies carry on business in partnership/JV, each company's share is a separate source of profit taxed in that company — [Nigeria Tax Act 2025 gazette (per search summary)](https://irs.gm.gov.ng/docs/national/NIGERIA_TAX_ACT_2025.pdf).

**Personal income tax bands applying to partners/individual members [2026 CHANGE]**
- First NGN800,000: 0%; NGN800,001–3,000,000: 15%; NGN3,000,001–12,000,000: 18%; NGN12,000,001–25,000,000: 21%; NGN25,000,001–50,000,000: 23%; above NGN50,000,000: 25% — [Mondaq, Understanding PIT under NTA 2025](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/capital-gains-tax/1726922/understanding-personal-income-tax-under-the-nigerian-tax-act-2025); [Safeguard Global](https://www.safeguardglobal.com/resources/blog/nigeria-tax-act-2025/).
- The Consolidated Relief Allowance is abolished and replaced by rent relief of 20% of annual rent capped at NGN500,000; pension, NHF, NHIS, owner-occupied mortgage interest and life-insurance premiums remain deductible; gratuities become taxable — [Mondaq PIT](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/capital-gains-tax/1726922/understanding-personal-income-tax-under-the-nigerian-tax-act-2025); [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/nigeria-tax-act-2025-has-been-signed-highlights).

**Cooperative societies**
- Cooperative societies registered under any State cooperative law are exempt from income tax under the NTA; the exemption does not extend to income from trade or business carried on for profit, which remains taxable — [Pulse.ng, tax-exempt organisations list](https://www.pulse.ng/story/nigeria-tax-exempt-organisations-full-list-2026080411285872358); [BusinessDay, organisations exempt from CIT](https://businessday.ng/business-economy/article/eight-organisations-exempt-from-companies-income-tax-in-nigeria/).
- Profits of registered cooperatives are not taxable "so long as those profits aren't from commercial businesses" — [Lexpraxis, Nigeria's new tax laws](https://lexpraxisng.com/nigerias-new-tax-laws-what-you-should-know/).
- Cooperatives registered under State law are also exempt from Chapter Two provisions dealing with property held in trust — [NTA explanatory memorandum (per search summary)](https://africacheck.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/2025-07/NIGERIA-TAX-ACT-2025.pdf).

**VAT**
- Rate remains 7.5% (since 1 Feb 2020); input VAT on services and capital assets is now fully recoverable; zero-rating expanded to basic food, medical and educational items — [Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.ng/insights/nigerias-2025-tax-reform-acts-explained); [PwC WWTS other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/other-taxes). **[2026 CHANGE: input recovery]**
- Several practitioner guides state the VAT registration threshold rose from NGN25m to NGN50m annual turnover, and that companies within the NTA small-company definition (≤ NGN100m turnover, ≤ NGN250m fixed assets) are relieved from charging VAT — [Pronalytics VAT guide 2026](https://pronalytics.ng/blog/vat-registration-nigeria-2026-guide); [AnooreHR VAT guide](https://anoorehr.com/blog/vat-nigeria-smes-2026). These are secondary sources and the two thresholds are not reconciled between them; see Gaps.
- Input VAT claims must be made within 5 years; refund claims within 12 months, NRS to process within 30 days; false claims attract 100% penalty plus interest at CBN policy rate — [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/nigeria-tax-act-2025-has-been-signed-highlights).

**Stamp duties [2026 CHANGE]**
- Stamping deadline cut from 40 to 30 days of execution (s.26 NTA); "conveyance on sale" ad valorem duty now limited to real property (s.131) and mineral assets (s.133); transfers of intangible assets are outside ad valorem duty; where several instruments cover one transaction only one bears ad valorem duty (s.142(1)); unspecified agreements bear a flat NGN1,000; the person who benefits pays (s.126(2) and Ninth Schedule); unstamped instruments are inadmissible in evidence (s.127); administered by NRS — [Andersen Nigeria, Stamp Duty under NTA 2025](https://ng.andersen.com/signed-sealed-and-taxed-stamp-duty-compliance-under-the-nigeria-tax-act-2025/).
- Lease duty: 0.78% for terms under 7 years, 3% over 7 years; leases under NGN10m or 10x minimum wage exempt — [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/nigeria-tax-act-2025-has-been-signed-highlights).
- PwC still lists 0.75% on authorised share capital at incorporation/increase and the NGN50 electronic money transfer levy on transfers of NGN10,000 or more — [PwC WWTS other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/other-taxes).
- A Legal500 note reads s.131 as meaning share transfers (intangibles) are no longer subject to ad valorem stamp duty — [Legal500 non-resident guide](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/tax/nigeria-tax-reforms-for-non-residents-expatriate-foreign-company-guide). (Pre-2026 practice was 0.75% ad valorem on share transfer instruments; confirm current treatment with the gazette and NRS practice.)

**Loss relief**
- The four-year cap on carrying forward losses was deleted (Finance Act 2019) so companies carry losses forward indefinitely — [Legal500 Finance Act overview](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/tax/an-overview-of-the-major-changes-introduced-by-the-finance-act-to-nigeria's-tax-regime). No source reviewed indicates the NTA reintroduced a time limit.

### Inferences
- For a typical members' pooled business with turnover under NGN100m, incorporating as a private company is now close to tax-neutral at entity level (0% CIT/CGT/levy), with the only recurring tax friction being 10% WHT on dividends paid out and the members' own PIT if they draw salaries.
- Because a partner's "remuneration" and "interest on capital" are expressly folded into their taxable share, re-labelling drawings as salary or interest yields no tax arbitrage inside a Nigerian partnership.
- The abolition of rollover relief and the move to income-rate CGT mean that a group which buys and sells shares in investee companies above the NGN150m/NGN10m thresholds will pay 30% (company) or up to 25% (individual) on gains from 2026, versus 10% before.
- A cooperative that lends only to its own members or buys goods for members stays within the exemption; one that trades with the public or runs a commercial venture for profit will be taxed on that stream and should segregate the accounts.

### Gaps
- Exact NTA section numbers for the cooperative exemption, the partnership provisions and the small-company VAT relief could not be verified because the gazette PDFs returned 403/unreadable; secondary sources are relied on.
- The relationship between the reported NGN50m VAT registration threshold and the NGN100m small-company VAT relief is not clearly explained in any primary source found; the report writer should present it as "≤ NGN50m: no registration obligation; NGN50m–100m: small company relieved from charging VAT per practitioner guidance; confirm with NRS".
- Whether a "medium company" band (20%) survives in any form could not be confirmed from a primary source; 2026 sources list only 0% and 30%.
- Current stamp duty on share transfer instruments post-2026 is inferred (intangibles excluded) rather than confirmed from the Act's schedule.

## Key Question 2: Nigerian regulators — NRS/TIN, CAC, SEC (CIS, crowdfunding, warnings), CBN deposit-taking, State cooperative registrars

### Takeaway
A pooled-money group in Nigeria touches up to five regulators: the Nigeria Revenue Service (TIN and filings, replacing FIRS from 2026), the CAC (incorporation and annual returns, now with director-level penalty enforcement since January 2024), the SEC (any invitation to the public to invest in a managed portfolio is a collective investment scheme requiring registration, and the Investments and Securities Act 2025 now criminalises Ponzi/"prohibited schemes" with 10-year terms), the CBN (taking deposits from the public without a licence is a BOFIA 2020 offence), and the State Director of Cooperatives (registration and annual audited returns for cooperatives).

### Cited Findings

**Tax administration (NRS, TIN, filings, penalties) [2026 CHANGE]**
- The Nigeria Revenue Service replaces FIRS as the central federal tax authority — [Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.ng/insights/nigerias-2025-tax-reform-acts-explained).
- The NTAA requires all taxable individuals and MDAs to register and obtain a TIN; non-residents making taxable supplies or earning Nigerian income (other than passive investment income) must also register — [Afriwise, Tax Administration in Nigeria](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/tax-administration-in-nigeria---a-review-of-the-2025-nigerian-tax-reform-laws).
- Failure to file, or filing incomplete/inaccurate returns, attracts an administrative penalty of NGN100,000 for the first month and NGN50,000 for each subsequent month of default — [Banwo & Ighodalo, taxpayer's guide to compliance, offences and penalties](https://www.banwo-ighodalo.com/grey-matter/nigerias-new-tax-regime-a-taxpayers-guide-to-compliance-offences-penalties-enforcement-and-dispute-resolution/).
- Books and records must be kept for not less than six years after the year of assessment to which they relate — [Banwo & Ighodalo](https://www.banwo-ighodalo.com/grey-matter/nigerias-new-tax-regime-a-taxpayers-guide-to-compliance-offences-penalties-enforcement-and-dispute-resolution/).
- The small-company exemption is not automatic in practice: the company still needs a TIN, proper records and must file annual returns even at nil liability — [Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/guide/nigerian-tax-reform-acts/).
- The reforms mandate digital filing, record-keeping and payment across all major taxes — [Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.ng/insights/nigerias-2025-tax-reform-acts-explained).

**CAC (CAMA 2020)**
- Annual returns must be filed within 42 days of the AGM; single-member companies are exempt from filing annual returns — [FastCAC guide](https://fastcac.com/blog/cac-annual-returns-guide).
- Filing fee NGN5,000 for small and other private companies; late penalty NGN5,000 (small) / NGN10,000 (other private) per source; from 1 January 2024 the CAC applies Companies Regulations 2021 penalties against both company and directors, and s.425 CAMA allows daily default fines with personal liability for officers — [EBC Consults, CAC annual returns 2026](https://www.ebconsults.ng/cac-annual-returns-nigeria/); [Njunkim Legal](https://njunkimlegal.com/how-to-file-your-annual-returns-with-the-cac-and-what-happens-if-you-dont/). Figures vary between secondary sources; the official CAC penalty page could not be fetched (403).
- A company is a "small company" under s.394 CAMA 2020 if turnover ≤ NGN120m, net assets ≤ NGN60m and fewer than 50 employees (and it is private, with no foreign/government members and directors holding ≥51%); small companies and dormant companies are exempt from audit, but not from filing annual returns — [Matog Consulting, audit exemptions](https://www.matogconsulting.com/audit-exemptions-for-small-companies-in-nigeria-who-qualifies/); [UUBO CAMA 2020 review on financial statements, audit and annual returns](https://uubo.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/cama-2020-review-highlights-on-financial-statements-audit-annual-returns-dividends-and-profits.pdf).
- CAMA 2020 Part C creates Limited Liability Partnerships (s.746(1): a body corporate separate from its partners; name must end "LLP", s.757; incorporation document filed under s.753(2)), Part D limited partnerships, Part E business names; CAC began LLP/LP registration on its portal in 2021 — [LawPavilion, LLPs under CAMA 2020](https://www.lawpavilion.com/blog/an-overview-of-limited-liability-partnerships-under-cama-2020); [DLA Piper Africa](https://www.dlapiperafrica.com/en/nigeria/insights/2021/cac-commences-registration-of-limited-liability-partnerships-and-limited-partnerships-in-nigeria.html).

**SEC — collective investment schemes and the ISA 2025**
- The Investments and Securities Act 2025 (s.150) defines a collective investment scheme as any scheme or arrangement "in whatever form" under which members of the public or qualified investors are invited or permitted to invest money or other assets in a portfolio; s.151 lists permitted forms (unit trust, open/close-ended investment company, REIT, specialised/alternative scheme, others approved by SEC) — [Omaplex, licensing and regulation of CIS in Nigeria](https://omaplex.com.ng/an-analysis-of-the-licensing-and-regulation-of-collective-investment-scheme-in-nigeria/).
- Promoters/operators of a "prohibited scheme" (Ponzi/pyramid) face a fine of not less than NGN20m or 10 years' imprisonment or both, plus disgorgement; before ISA 2025 the SEC lacked a clear basis to prosecute — [Nairametrics, ISA 2025 Ponzi penalties](https://nairametrics.com/2025/04/01/isa-2025-ponzi-schemes-promoters-in-nigeria-now-face-10-years-jail-term-sec-dg/); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/news/article/ponzi-operators-risk-10-year-jail-term-n20m-fine-sec/). (One outlet reports NGN40m — [Fintech Magazine Africa](https://fintechmagazine.africa/2025/04/01/new-isa-2025-empowers-sec-to-crack-down-on-ponzi-schemes-with-10-year-jail-terms-and-n40-million-fines/) — a discrepancy between reported figures.)
- Operating an unregistered fund: minimum fine NGN10m plus NGN100,000 per day of default; foreign CIS targeting Nigerians must register — [UUBO, ISA 2025 and PE/VC funds](https://uubo.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/UPDATED-DETAILED-ANALYSIS-OF-ISA-2025-WHAT-IS-CHANGING-FOR-PE-AND-VC-FUNDS-1.pdf); [Mondaq, ISA 2025 for private capital](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/venture-capital/1638148/nigerias-investments-and-securities-act-2025-what-is-changing-for-private-capital-players).
- SEC public notice of 8 May 2026 named unregistered platforms (e.g., "MY SHARE"/UYJ Multitrade, Promiseland Estates) posing as fund managers/advisers, and stressed only SEC-registered operators may offer investment services; earlier notices (March 2025) carried the same warning — [Nairametrics, May 2026 SEC warning](https://nairametrics.com/2026/05/14/sec-warns-nigerians-against-rising-ponzi-schemes-on-whatsapp-tiktok-instagram/); [allAfrica, March 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202503070291.html).

**CBN — deposit-taking**
- BOFIA 2020 makes it an offence to carry on banking business without a CBN licence; penalty of up to 5 years' imprisonment or a fine of not less than NGN50m or twice the deposits collected, or both — [Harlem Solicitors, legal regime for MFBs](https://www.harlemsolicitors.com/2021/07/03/beaming-searchlight-on-the-legal-regime-for-microfinance-banks-in-nigeria/); [KMO Legal, overview of BOFIA 2020](https://www.kmo.legal/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/AN-OVERVIEW-OF-THE-BOFIA-2020.x45260.pdf).
- CBN's Microfinance Policy requires supervision of financial cooperatives and NGO-MFIs with significant member savings/deposit-taking; any such institution reaching total assets of NGN20m and/or 2,000 members/clients must transform into a licensed institution category — [CBN Revised Microfinance Policy 2011](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/OUT/2011/PRESSRELEASE/GVD/REVISED%20MICROFINANCE%20POLICY%20JULY%2012%202011.PDF); [CBN Microfinance Policy 2005](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/OUT/PUBLICATIONS/GUIDELINES/DFD/2006/MICROFINANCE%20POLICY.PDF).
- MFBs are licensed under BOFIA ss.61–63 and the CBN Guidelines for Regulation and Supervision of MFBs (revised 2012, reviewed 2020) — [SPA Ajibade, MFB Guidelines](https://spaajibade.com/guidelines-for-the-regulation-and-supervision-of-microfinance-banks-in-nigeria/); [Templars, 2020 MFB Guidelines](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2020/09/CBN-Microfinance-Bank-Guidelines-2020-and-implications-for-Digital.Banks-08-09-2020-Final.pdf).

**State cooperative registrars**
- Under the Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act a primary society needs at least 10 qualified persons (industrial society: 6; secondary society: 5 registered societies); application is to the Director of Cooperatives with bye-laws attached; bye-law amendments are invalid until registered — [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act (FAO Lex)](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf); [Lexpraxis, Cooperative societies in Nigeria](https://lexpraxisng.com/co-operative-societies-in-nigeria-everything-you-need-to-know/).
- In Lagos, practitioner guidance states a savings and credit cooperative needs at least 20 members and an agricultural cooperative 50 — [Resolution Law, cooperative registration in Lagos](https://www.resolutionlawng.com/procedure-for-the-establishment-of-a-cooperative-society-in-nigeria/).
- Lagos State's Department of Cooperative Services registers, supervises and audits all cooperatives in the State; societies must keep proper books, be audited annually and submit audited returns (accounts, member list, activity report) to the Director, with suspension of the committee as a sanction for default — [Lagos Ministry of Commerce, Dept of Cooperative Services](https://mccti.lagosstate.gov.ng/departments/cooperatives); [Pavestones Legal, registering a cooperative in Lagos](https://pavestoneslegal.com/registering-a-cooperative-society-in-lagos-state/); [Mondaq, registering a cooperative in Lagos](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1314598/registering-a-cooperative-society-in-lagos-state).

### Inferences
- The "collective investment scheme" line is crossed when (a) money is pooled into a portfolio managed by someone other than the contributors and (b) the invitation goes beyond a closed circle of co-owners to "members of the public" — a closed group of co-founders who all hold shares/partnership interests and decide collectively is outside s.150, but advertising a "pool" online for returns is inside it.
- The "deposit-taking" line is crossed when a group takes repayable funds from people who are not its registered members (or from members on a scale CBN treats as banking); a registered thrift/credit cooperative dealing only with its members is the recognised safe harbour until it hits the NGN20m assets / 2,000 members transformation trigger.
- Since January 2024 CAC penalties attach personally to directors, so inactive pooled-capital companies that skip annual returns create personal exposure for the members who sit as directors.

### Gaps
- The CAC's official penalty schedule (per-day amounts under Companies Regulations 2021) could not be retrieved (403); secondary figures conflict (NGN100/day vs NGN5,000–10,000 flat vs NGN50,000 + NGN10,000/day).
- Whether the NGN20m / 2,000-member cooperative transformation trigger has been updated since the 2011 policy could not be confirmed.
- The exact penalty for Ponzi promoters is reported as NGN20m by most outlets and NGN40m by one; the ISA 2025 text was not accessible to resolve this.

## Key Question 3: Crowdfunding and online pooling — Nigeria SEC framework, US Reg CF, UK FCA; equity vs reward vs debt

### Takeaway
Nigeria's 2021 SEC Rules on Crowdfunding let only incorporated MSMEs with two years' history raise up to NGN50m (micro), NGN70m (small) or NGN100m (medium) in 12 months, solely through SEC-registered portals with NGN100m minimum capital, with retail investors capped at 10% of annual income and issuers barred if the money will be on-lent or re-invested; the US caps Reg CF at USD5m per 12 months with tiered non-accredited investor limits (USD2,500 / 5% / 10% / USD124,000), and the UK restricts direct-offer promotions of unlisted securities and P2P loans to certified HNW, sophisticated or "restricted" investors who pledge not to put more than 10% of net investible assets into such products.

### Cited Findings

**Nigeria — SEC Rules on Crowdfunding (released January 2021)**
- Only MSMEs incorporated in Nigeria with a minimum two-year operating track record may raise via a registered crowdfunding portal; entities with less than two years may qualify if backed by a technical partner or core investor meeting the two-year test — [Mondaq, Crowdfunding for SMEs under Nigerian law](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/securities/1138928/crowdfunding-for-small-to-medium-scale-businesses-an-analysis-under-the-nigerian-law).
- Raise caps in any 12 months: micro NGN50m, small NGN70m, medium NGN100m — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/securities/1138928/crowdfunding-for-small-to-medium-scale-businesses-an-analysis-under-the-nigerian-law); [TechCabal, crowdfunding regulations](https://techcabal.com/2021/02/15/crowdfunding-regulations/).
- Excluded issuers: complex structures, public companies and their subsidiaries, entities without a clear business plan, and businesses intending to use proceeds to lend or invest in other companies — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/securities/1138928/crowdfunding-for-small-to-medium-scale-businesses-an-analysis-under-the-nigerian-law).
- Retail investors (not HNW/sophisticated/qualified institutional) may not invest more than 10% of annual income per calendar year — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/securities/1138928/crowdfunding-for-small-to-medium-scale-businesses-an-analysis-under-the-nigerian-law); [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2021/02/15/crowdfunding-regulations/).
- Portals must be run by an SEC-registered crowdfunding intermediary with minimum paid-up capital of NGN100m — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2021/02/15/crowdfunding-regulations/); [SEC Nigeria FinTech rules page](https://home.sec.gov.ng/our-mandate/regulation/rules-and-regulations/sec-rules-for-fintechs/).
- MSME size uses the SMEDAN 2007 definitions: small 10–49 employees / NGN5–50m assets excluding land and buildings; medium 50–199 employees / NGN50–500m — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/securities/1138928/crowdfunding-for-small-to-medium-scale-businesses-an-analysis-under-the-nigerian-law).

**US — Regulation Crowdfunding**
- Offering limit USD5m per 12 months (raised from USD1.07m in March 2021); the SEC has not changed the cap in 2026 despite petitions to raise it to USD20m with inflation indexing; Section 4A(h)(1) requires inflation adjustment at least every five years — [SEC rulemaking petition 4-914 (2026)](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/petitions/2026/petn4-914.pdf); [Crowdfunding Lawyers](https://www.thecrowdfundinglawyers.com/sec-encouraged-to-raise-reg-cf-offer-limit/); [SEC JOBS Act inflation adjustment fact sheet](https://www.sec.gov/files/33-11098-fact-sheet.pdf).
- Non-accredited investor limits per 12 months: if either income or net worth < USD124,000, the greater of USD2,500 or 5% of the greater of income/net worth; if both ≥ USD124,000, 10% of the greater, capped at USD124,000 aggregate across all Reg CF offerings — [SEC Investor Bulletin, Regulation Crowdfunding for investors](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/updated-11); [SEC, pathways to raise capital](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/smallbiz-essentials-what-pathways-are-available-raise-capital-investors).
- Issuer guidance (Form C filings, intermediary requirement) — [SEC, Regulation Crowdfunding guidance for issuers](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/regulation-crowdfunding-guidance-issuers).

**UK — FCA regime**
- Direct-offer financial promotions of non-readily realisable securities (unlisted equity/debt) and, since 9 December 2019 (PS19/14), P2P agreements may only be made to certified HNW investors, certified/self-certified sophisticated investors, or "restricted investors" who certify they will not invest more than 10% of net investible assets in such products in the 12 months after certification; restricted investors with two or more P2P investments in two years may self-certify as sophisticated — [FCA PS19/14](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps19-14.pdf); [Macfarlanes, FCA finalises P2P marketing restriction](https://www.macfarlanes.com/what-we-think/2019/fca-finalises-restriction-on-p2p-marketing-and-other-rules/); [CMS, FCA confirms new P2P rules](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/Crowdfunding-FCA-confirms-new-rules-for-P2P-platforms).
- The FCA regulates investment-based crowdfunding platforms (unlisted shares/debt) and loan-based (P2P) platforms, which require FCA authorisation — [FCA PS14/04](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps14-04.pdf); [Lexology, crowdfunding and FCA authorisation](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=0a4d2358-0916-42a2-b223-726e400ac3fe).

**Equity vs reward vs debt (how returns are shared)**
- Nigeria's rules apply to "investment instruments" (shares, debentures and similar) offered via portals — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/securities/1138928/crowdfunding-for-small-to-medium-scale-businesses-an-analysis-under-the-nigerian-law). Reward/donation crowdfunding (no financial return) is outside the SEC rules by their terms (inference from scope).
- UK: equity crowdfunding returns come as dividends/exit gains on unlisted shares (taxed under dividend/CGT rules); P2P returns are interest to the lender — [FCA crowdfunding review](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/thematic-reviews/crowdfunding-review.pdf).

### Inferences
- A Nigerian members' group that wants to "open the pool" to strangers online has only two lawful routes: raise as an incorporated MSME through a registered crowdfunding portal within the caps, or register a CIS/fund with the SEC. The exclusion of issuers that will on-lend or re-invest proceeds means a "we pool money and invest it in other businesses" club cannot use crowdfunding at all; it must go the CIS route.
- The three regimes converge on the same investor-protection logic: cap retail exposure at roughly 10% of income/assets and force the raise through a licensed intermediary.

### Gaps
- The SEC Nigeria crowdfunding rules PDF was image-only and could not be parsed; details on escrow/custodian, fees, offering period, cooling-off and ongoing reporting are therefore not cited. Reports of 2024–2026 amendments to the Nigerian crowdfunding rules were not found in this research.
- US Reg CF ongoing reporting (Form C-AR) and financial-statement review/audit thresholds (USD124k / USD618k / USD1.235m tiers) were not retrieved from a primary source in this pass.

## Key Question 4: US/UK comparators — pass-through, S-corp, Subchapter T; UK partnerships/LLPs, corporation tax, CICs

### Takeaway
The US and UK both let a pooled group choose transparency (partnership/LLC filing Form 1065 with K-1s; UK partnership/LLP filing SA800 with partners taxed via self-assessment) or a corporate wrapper (S-corp pass-through with mandatory reasonable salary; UK company paying 19–25% corporation tax then dividend tax at 10.75–39.35%), and both have a cooperative-style option (US Subchapter T patronage deduction; UK CIC with a 35% dividend cap and asset lock).

### Cited Findings

**United States**
- A partnership does not pay tax; it files Form 1065 by the 15th day of the third month after year end (15 March for calendar years) and must furnish Schedule K-1 to each partner by that date; multi-member LLCs default to this treatment — [IRS Publication 541 (Rev. Dec 2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p541); [IRS Instructions for Form 1065 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1065.pdf).
- S corporations file Form 1120-S and issue K-1s by the return due date; shareholder-employees must take reasonable compensation before distributions (distributions avoid payroll tax, making low salaries the leading S-corp audit trigger) — [IRS Instructions for Form 1120-S (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120s); [Beancount K-1 (1120-S) guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/04/20/schedule-k1-form-1120s-complete-guide).
- Subchapter T (IRC ss.1381–1388): a cooperative is taxed like a C corporation but deducts patronage dividends paid to patrons in proportion to business done, provided they are paid within 8.5 months after year end (s.1382); it files Form 1120-C and issues Form 1099-PATR to patrons receiving USD10 or more — [IRS Instructions for Form 1120-C (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120c); [The Tax Adviser, qualified patronage dividends](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2017/dec/reducing-taxes-qualified-patronage-dividends/).
- Record retention: records supporting return items must be kept 3 years from the later of due date or filing; 6 years if income under-reported by >25%; 7 years for bad-debt/worthless-securities deductions; employment tax records 4 years — [IRS Instructions for Form 1120-S](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120s).

**United Kingdom**
- General partnerships and LLPs are tax-transparent: no corporation tax; each partner is self-employed and pays income tax (and Class 4 NIC) on their profit share; the nominated partner must register the partnership for Self Assessment before 5 October in the second tax year and file the SA800 partnership return — [A-Wise, corporation tax for partnerships/LLPs](https://a-wise.co.uk/how-to-handle-corporation-tax-for-a-partnership-or-llp/); [Gravita, SA800 obligations](https://www.gravita.com/insights/partnership-tax-returns-and-your-sa800-obligations/).
- Corporation tax FY from 1 April 2026: 25% main rate for profits over GBP250,000; 19% small profits rate under GBP50,000; marginal relief between — [PwC WWTS, UK corporate income tax](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/united-kingdom/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income); [Crunch, corporation tax 2026/27](https://www.crunch.co.uk/knowledge/article/corporation-tax-rates-what-your-business-needs-to-know).
- Dividend tax 2026/27: GBP500 allowance, then 10.75% (basic), 35.75% (higher), 39.35% (additional) — basic and higher rates each rose 2 points from April 2026; dividends carry no NIC — [Taxfly, dividend tax 2026/27](https://www.taxfly.co.uk/guides/dividend-tax-2026-27-rates-and-allowance); [Clive Owen LLP, April 2026 tax changes](https://www.cliveowen.com/2026/03/april-2026-tax-changes-uk/).
- Community Interest Companies: asset lock; aggregate dividend cap of 35% of distributable profits with unused capacity carried forward up to five years; pay corporation tax at normal rates — [Office of the Regulator of CICs information pack](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69b14f4c187a6dea7823316e/13-783-community-interest-companies-information-pack.pdf); [The Committee Room CIC guide 2026](https://thecommitteeroom.org.uk/cic).
- Record retention: self-assessment business records at least 5 years from 31 January following the tax year; company/LLP records (and CT600) 6 years from end of the accounting period — [Go Legal, partnership records checklist](https://go-legal.ai/what-records-does-a-partnership-need-to-keep-uk-legal-checklist/); [Quality Company Formations, LLP self-assessment](https://www.qualitycompanyformations.co.uk/blog/self-assessment-requirements-for-limited-liability-partnerships/).

### Inferences
- Nigeria's new 0% small-company regime is more generous than either comparator: a UK company of the same size pays at least 19% and a US LLC's members pay personal rates on all profit whether or not distributed.
- Nigeria's cooperative exemption (no tax on non-commercial surplus) is broader than US Subchapter T, which only defers tax to the patron via the deduction, and is closer in spirit to the UK CIC's asset lock in that it ties the benefit to member/community purpose.

### Gaps
- UK Class 4 NIC rates for 2026/27 and the GOV.UK primary pages on partnership registration were not fetched directly; practitioner summaries are relied on.
- S-corp eligibility limits (100 shareholders, US persons, one class of stock) were not re-verified from IRS text in this pass.

## Key Question 5: Compliance record-keeping — books, retention, audit thresholds, member statements

### Takeaway
A Nigerian pooled-capital entity must keep accounting records for at least six years after the relevant year of assessment, file CAC annual returns within 42 days of its AGM even if it is a small company exempt from audit (turnover ≤ NGN120m, net assets ≤ NGN60m, <50 employees), remit WHT by the 21st of the following month with evidence for recipients, and — if a cooperative — submit annually audited returns to the State Director of Cooperatives.

### Cited Findings
- NTAA: books and records retained not less than six years after the year of assessment; non-filing penalty NGN100,000 first month then NGN50,000 per month — [Banwo & Ighodalo](https://www.banwo-ighodalo.com/grey-matter/nigerias-new-tax-regime-a-taxpayers-guide-to-compliance-offences-penalties-enforcement-and-dispute-resolution/).
- Digital filing, record-keeping and payment mandated across major taxes — [Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.ng/insights/nigerias-2025-tax-reform-acts-explained).
- CAMA 2020 small company (s.394: turnover ≤ NGN120m, net assets ≤ NGN60m, <50 employees, plus ownership tests) exempt from audit; still files annual returns; dormant companies also exempt — [Matog Consulting](https://www.matogconsulting.com/audit-exemptions-for-small-companies-in-nigeria-who-qualifies/); [UUBO CAMA review](https://uubo.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/cama-2020-review-highlights-on-financial-statements-audit-annual-returns-dividends-and-profits.pdf).
- Annual returns due within 42 days of AGM; single-member companies exempt — [FastCAC](https://fastcac.com/blog/cac-annual-returns-guide).
- WHT remittance by the 21st of the month after payment; for related parties WHT is triggered at the earlier of payment or recognition of the liability — [PwC WWTS WHT](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/withholding-taxes).
- Input VAT may be claimed within 5 years of being incurred (relevant to how long invoices must be retained) — [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/nigeria-tax-act-2025-has-been-signed-highlights).
- Cooperatives: proper books, annual audit and audited returns (accounts, member list, activity report) to the State Director/Registrar; sanction includes suspension of the management committee — [Pavestones Legal](https://pavestoneslegal.com/registering-a-cooperative-society-in-lagos-state/); [Lagos Dept of Cooperative Services](https://mccti.lagosstate.gov.ng/departments/cooperatives).
- Tax-exempt bodies (including cooperatives) should maintain proper accounting records and periodically review income sources to confirm the exemption still applies — [Pulse.ng](https://www.pulse.ng/story/nigeria-tax-exempt-organisations-full-list-2026080411285872358).
- US member statements: Schedule K-1 (Form 1065 / 1120-S) by return due date; Form 1099-PATR for patronage dividends ≥ USD10 — [IRS Pub 541](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p541); [IRS Form 1120-C instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120c).
- UK: 5 years (self-assessment) / 6 years (company) retention — [Go Legal](https://go-legal.ai/what-records-does-a-partnership-need-to-keep-uk-legal-checklist/).

### Inferences
- The Nigerian equivalent of a K-1 is the WHT credit note/receipt the paying company obtains from NRS for each dividend or interest payment, plus a dividend warrant; partners in a Nigerian partnership have no statutory statement form, so the partnership should issue each partner an annual profit-share and drawings statement reconciling to the partnership accounts to support their individual PIT filing with the State IRS.
- Because the audit exemption (CAMA, NGN120m) and the tax exemption (NTA, NGN100m) use different thresholds and bases (turnover/net assets/employees vs turnover/fixed assets), a company can be audit-exempt but tax-paying or vice versa; both tests should be documented annually.

### Gaps
- No Nigerian primary source was found prescribing a statutory "dividend voucher" or partner statement format; the practice is inferred.
- Pre-2026 retention rules under CITA (6 years) versus the NTAA (6 years) appear unchanged, but the gazette could not be checked.

## Key Question 6: Practical tax planning for groups — salary vs dividend vs profit share, double vs single taxation, timing, loss relief

### Takeaway
From 2026 the planning calculus in Nigeria tilts toward a small company: 0% entity tax plus a flat 10% final WHT on dividends usually beats a partnership where every naira of profit is taxed at the partner's marginal PIT rate (up to 25%), while salaries to active members are deductible to the company but taxed at the member's PIT rates (0–25%) and attract pension/NHF costs; above the NGN100m/NGN250m thresholds the company faces 30% + 4% then 10% WHT (an effective ~40% on distributed profit), which is when a partnership or LLP's single layer at ≤25% becomes cheaper.

### Cited Findings
- Small company: 0% CIT, 0% CGT, no development levy — [PwC WWTS](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income); [Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.ng/insights/nigerias-2025-tax-reform-acts-explained).
- Large company: 30% CIT plus 4% development levy on assessable profits — [PwC WWTS](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income).
- Dividends: 10% WHT, no further tax (s.19 NTA) — [taxngr.com](https://taxngr.com/wht-on-dividends-in-nigeria/); dividends from retained taxed profits no longer trigger excess-dividend tax — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/tax/an-overview-of-the-major-changes-introduced-by-the-finance-act-to-nigeria's-tax-regime).
- Individuals/partners: PIT 0% on first NGN800,000 rising to 25% above NGN50m; partner remuneration and interest on capital are part of the partner's taxable share — [Mondaq PIT](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/capital-gains-tax/1726922/understanding-personal-income-tax-under-the-nigerian-tax-act-2025); [NTA gazette per search summary](https://irs.gm.gov.ng/docs/national/NIGERIA_TAX_ACT_2025.pdf).
- Employer on-costs on salary: pension minimum 18% of monthly emolument (employer 10%/employee 8%), NHF 2.5% for employees earning over NGN30,000 p.a., employee compensation 1% of payroll — [PwC WWTS other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/other-taxes).
- Loss carry-forward is indefinite for companies — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/tax/an-overview-of-the-major-changes-introduced-by-the-finance-act-to-nigeria's-tax-regime).
- Capital gains on share exits: company 30% / individual up to 25%, exempt below NGN150m proceeds and NGN10m gain; rollover relief gone — [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/nigeria-tax-act-2025-has-been-signed-highlights); [Mondaq CGT](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/capital-gains-tax/1751296/capital-gains-tax-under-the-nigeria-tax-act-2025-no-longer-business-as-usual).
- Cooperative surplus from member business is exempt; commercial trading income is taxable — [Lexpraxis](https://lexpraxisng.com/nigerias-new-tax-laws-what-you-should-know/).
- US comparator: S-corp distributions avoid payroll tax but require reasonable salary first — [IRS 1120-S instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120s). UK comparator: salary bears income tax + NIC (~32%/42%/47% effective) whereas dividends bear 10.75%/35.75%/39.35% with no NIC after corporation tax of 19–25% — [Taxfly](https://www.taxfly.co.uk/guides/dividend-tax-2026-27-rates-and-allowance); [Probal Global, salary vs dividends 2026/27](https://www.probalglobal.com/blog/dividend-declaration-uk).

### Inferences
- Worked illustration (derived from cited rates): NGN10m of profit in a small company distributed as dividend costs 10% (NGN1m) in total. The same NGN10m earned through a partnership by a single partner with no other income costs roughly NGN1.59m (0% on 800k, 15% on 2.2m = 330k, 18% on 7m = 1.26m) — about 16%. Above the small-company thresholds the company route costs 30% + 4% at entity level then 10% WHT on the remainder, roughly 40% of pre-tax profit if fully distributed, versus a maximum 25% through a partnership — so the wrapper decision flips at scale.
- Salary to an active member is only attractive where the member's marginal PIT rate is below 10% (i.e., very low total income) or where the company is above the threshold and needs the deduction; otherwise dividend (10% final) dominates. Salary also triggers 18% pension contributions and NHF, which dividends do not.
- Timing: because WHT on dividends is final and CIT for a small company is nil, there is no tax reason to delay distributions in a small company; in a large company, retaining profit defers only the 10% WHT, not the 34% entity charge. Watch the NGN100m turnover test each year — one strong year can switch the whole year's profit to 30%.
- Loss relief: indefinite carry-forward favours companies for start-up losses; in a partnership losses flow to partners, who can set them against other income under PIT rules (not verified for the NTA — see Gaps).
- Cooperatives are the cheapest wrapper for member-only savings/lending (exempt surplus) but cannot be used to run a trading business for profit without losing the exemption on that income.

### Gaps
- Whether partners' shares of partnership losses can be offset against their other income under the NTA, and any carry-forward limits for individuals, could not be sourced.
- No primary source confirmed whether the 10% WHT on dividends is final for corporate recipients (franked investment income treatment under the NTA); PwC describes WHT on dividends as "not automatically final".
- Professional-services companies: pre-2026 the small-company 0% band excluded none, but some commentary suggested the NTA excludes professional service firms from the small-company definition; this could not be verified and is omitted.
