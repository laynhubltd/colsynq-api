# Nigeria group-finance platform: bottom-up market-size inputs

Research date: 2026-10-08. Scope: inputs for a TAM/SAM/SOM of a digital platform letting Nigerian groups (ajo/esusu circles, investment clubs, registered cooperatives, staff/workplace cooperatives, cooperative housing estates) pool money with custody, ledgers, payroll integration, escrow and reporting.

Conventions: every figure carries its reference year and a URL. "Claim" = unaudited statement by a company/official; "Survey" = nationally representative survey; "Regulator" = regulator/statistics-office publication. Conflicts are flagged with **CONFLICT**. FX for conversions: NGN 1,331/USD (NFEM, 8 Oct 2026, see Q7).

## Q1. Adult population and use of informal savings groups

### Takeaway
Nigeria has ~111m adults (18+) in 2023 and ~119m implied by the Sept-2026 A2F release. Roughly 46% of adults used some informal financial service in 2023 (up from 33% in 2020), i.e. ~51m adults, of whom only ~10.7m rely on informal services exclusively. Savings groups, thrift collectors, village associations and cooperatives are each used by roughly 4–15% of adults. There is no published national "average monthly ajo contribution"; it must be assumed (anecdotal range N5,000–N50,000/month; market-women examples cluster at N10,000–N20,000).

## Q2. Cooperatives: societies, members, assets; workforce base for staff cooperatives

### Takeaway
Official numbers are claims, not an audited registry. Registered societies: "over 370,000" (Minister of State, Jul 2025) vs "more than 30,000" (same minister, Jan 2026) — **CONFLICT**, likely registry vs active/known count. Members: 20m (Cooperative Federation of Nigeria, 2023) vs 30m+ (CFAN, 2023). Sector savings: N1.8trn mobilised, N1.3trn loans (CRASON, Nov 2024, claim). Lagos: ~16,000 societies, ~N80bn funds / N70.7bn net assets (2022). A National Cooperative Smart Registry is being built (MoU 2025/26) — no CODAS figures found. Staff-cooperative addressable base: ~720k federal IPPIS employees (600k+ on new HR modules), ~2.1m teachers, ~372k police, ~223k armed forces; wage employees ≈ 12m of 84m employed (14.4%), of which public sector ≈ 25.6%; 11.39m RSA holders (Aug 2026) is the best proxy for formal-sector employees.

## Q5. Comparable platforms and willingness to pay

### Takeaway
Nigerian consumer savings apps prove scale: PiggyVest 6m+ users, N1.3trn payouts in 2025 (AUM +110%, absolute undisclosed). Group/ajo digitisers are far smaller (Bankly 2m users/50k agents at 2025 acquisition; LibertyPay 150k ajo cards, ~N5bn/month projected; Alajo N373m saved by Apr 2023). Nigerian cooperative SaaS prices at roughly N200/member/month (SmartCoopHub), N2,000/member/year (SmartTeller), or N300k–N500k/year per society (Prolean) → N600–N1,000/member/year; Coopco from N15k/month/society. Kenya benchmarks: Chamasoft KES 600–1,000/month per group (≤20 members) + KES 30/member/month; Kwara from KES 30,000/month per SACCO (unlimited members; 230+ SACCOs); SASRA-regulated SACCOs: 357 societies, 7.875m members, KES 1.21trn assets (2025). India: chit-fund foreman commission 5% (cap 7%) of pot; Chitmonks charges a flat collection fee.

## Bottom-up TAM / SAM / SOM skeleton (inputs only; assumptions flagged)

Pool-size inputs (naira, annualised flows):
1. Informal group savers: 46% × 111m ≈ 51m adults use informal services (2023). Assume 60–70% of these are in a savings group/thrift/cooperative (~30–36m), consistent with the 20–30m cooperative-member claims. **Assumption:** average contribution N10,000/month → N1.2–N1.4trn/yr... scale with any contribution assumption (N5k → N1.8–2.2trn; N20k → N7–9trn). Cross-check: CRASON claims N1.8trn cumulative cooperative savings stock.
2. Registered cooperatives: 30,000 (active, digitalisation target) to 370,000 (registry claim) societies; Lagos alone 16,000 with ~N80bn funds (2022) → ~N5m per society; scaling N5m × 30k–370k gives N150bn–N1.85trn stock, matching the N1.8trn CRASON claim at the high end.
3. Staff cooperatives: public wage earners ≈ 3.1m (25.6% × 12.1m employees) + 720k IPPIS federal; private formal ≈ 9m (RSA holders 11.39m as upper proxy). Payroll-deduction rails exist for IPPIS (WACS/Remita); unremitted deductions N12bn is a documented pain point.
4. Housing cooperatives: NHF contributors ~5.9m; NHF inflow N152bn/yr; FMBN cooperative/project pipeline N79bn/yr (2025); CHDL caps N15m per member.
5. Investment clubs/proptech: Risevest 600k, Keble 30k, PiggyVest 6m users (N1.3trn payouts/yr) define the digitally-savvy saver pool.

Monetisation inputs:
- SaaS: N2,000–N2,400/member/yr (Nigerian vendors) or $7–10/group/month (Chamasoft-equivalent).
- Float/custody: T-bill 15.8–15.9% (Sep 2026) vs member yield; spread capture on pooled balances.
- Transaction: NIP/PoS rails; typical fintech transfer fees N10–N50; escrow/collection flat fee (Chitmonks model).
- ROSCA management: ≤5% of pot (India foreman benchmark) as upper bound.
