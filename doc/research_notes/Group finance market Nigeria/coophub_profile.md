## Summary for analysts

- CoopHub is an **in-house Wema Bank / ALAT product**. It was built by Wema's Innovation team (IDEAx Labs) and launched on **10 May 2024**, the bank's 79th anniversary. It is not a joint venture and no external vendor has been identified.
- It is a **management layer over a Wema bank account held in the cooperative's name**. It has an admin web portal, a member mobile app, Initiator/Verifier/Approver controls, contributions, a loan lifecycle, announcements/events, reports and bulk data upload. It is linked to Finacle core banking.
- The **only public scale figure is "1,000+ cooperatives" (Jul 2024)**, a company claim made eight weeks after launch, and it has never been updated. **Store evidence points to low member uptake:** Google Play shows **1K+ downloads and 23 reviews**, and the iOS listing has **1 rating**. Neither app has been updated since Oct 2025.
- **The terms of service mention a subscription fee, but the amount is not published.** The cooperative admin must "pay the subscription fee", which renews monthly or biannually.
- In the FY2025 annual report CoopHub is **one line in the sustainability (NSBP) section**. It does not appear in the CEO/strategy narrative, which is about ALAT 2.0, ALAT Pay and ALAT Xplore.

## Q2. Product features

### Takeaway
CoopHub has two surfaces: an **admin web portal** (coophub.alat.ng) and a **member mobile app** (Android and iOS). Functionally it is a cooperative ledger and workflow tool sitting over a Wema account.

What is documented:
- Admin roles (Initiator → Verifier → Approver)
- Member invitation by link only
- Contribution configuration and tracking, with "liquidate contribution" requests that admins approve or decline
- Loan configuration (terms, interest, eligibility, required documents), member loan requests, disbursement and repayment tracking, with direct debit for recovery claimed
- Fund transfers to members, beneficiaries and other banks
- Manual-payment recording and **bulk Excel upload** of contribution and loan histories
- "Incentives" paid to members, which can be withdrawn or applied to contributions or repayments. This is CoopHub's nearest thing to a dividend.
- Announcements (schedulable), events, calendar and chat
- Report analytics and downloadable statements
- Member-level virtual wallet with tiered KYC (Tier-3 upgrade added Apr 2025)
- Biometric login and multiple contribution types (Jul 2025)
- A "combo card" request route

**Not documented:**
- Payroll check-off, IPPIS or Remita integration
- USSD channel
- AGM or voting tools
- Investment or insurance products
- Public API

Recurring contributions were "still under development" according to the FAQ. White-labelling is marketed but no example has been found.

## Q3. Pricing, commercial model, account structure

### Takeaway
CoopHub is a **paid subscription layered on a Wema account in the cooperative's name**, but **no price is published** anywhere. The cooperative must already hold a Wema account. The FAQ says onboarding is "specific to Wema bank cooperative account holders". Under the T&C, members also have to "operate an account with Wema Bank". The bank therefore monetises CoopHub through captured deposits (the cooperative's balance plus members' accounts and wallets), lending and transfer fees, as well as the subscription **[inferred]**. Disputes go to mediation, then arbitration at the Lagos Multi-Door Courthouse under Nigerian law.

## Q5. Licence and regulatory position

### Takeaway
CoopHub is not a separate licensed entity. It is a Wema Bank digital channel, and funds sit in Wema deposit accounts under Wema's commercial banking licence with national authorisation. Wema met the CBN's ₦200bn recapitalisation threshold in 2025. Cooperative deposits at Wema fall under **NDIC cover of up to ₦5m per depositor per bank**, a limit raised in May 2024. A cooperative account is one depositor, so balances above ₦5m are uninsured, and member-level pass-through cover is not documented **[inferred]**. No CBN or state cooperative-department endorsement of CoopHub was found.

## Implications for a competing or partnering platform [inferred]
1. CoopHub shows that a bank will run maker-checker over a cooperative's own account. That makes Wema a natural custody partner, and the T&C forces Wema-only banking on both cooperatives and members, which shapes the competitive design space.
2. Weak signals of member adoption, no update since Oct 2025, recurring contributions still unfinished and no payroll check-off reconciliation together leave the check-off and reconciliation gap open.
3. Before positioning against or with Wema, ask the bank:
   - the subscription price
   - the current cooperative count
   - whether roles bind the core-banking mandate
   - white-label and API availability
