# Payroll check-off for cooperatives: how mature systems work (international reference notes)

Compiled 2026-10-08 from public web sources. Purpose: give the product team design-grade notes on (a) the legal mechanics of employer check-off, (b) the monthly process, (c) file/message formats used for deduction schedules and remittances, (d) reconciliation features in SACCO/credit-union core systems, (e) regulator monitoring of employer arrears, and (f) documented failures. Each section has **Takeaway / Cited Findings / Gaps**. Anything not directly read from a source is marked *[unverified]* or *[inference]*.

Where the Nigerian context is relevant (IPPIS non-remittance, N12bn lenders' claim) it is noted in section 7 because it is the same failure mode.

---

## 0. Executive takeaways (design-relevant)

1. **The law in mature jurisdictions puts the remittance duty, a hard deadline, and a penalty on the employer**, and gives the cooperative or its regulator a fast collection remedy (Kenya: 7 days, >=5%/month compound interest, Commissioner may appoint the employer's bank as collection agent; India: employer liable "as arrears of land revenue", ranking with wages). A product should therefore model **employer-level liabilities with an ageing clock that starts at the deduction date (pay date), not at the date the schedule arrives**.
2. **The monthly process is a three-way match**: (i) the deduction *instruction/schedule* the society expects, (ii) the employer's *payroll schedule* of what was actually deducted, and (iii) the *remittance* (bank credit) and its *advice*. Every mature system (CU*BASE in the US, UK credit unions, Kenyan Sawazi/SACCO vendors) reduces to "balance the file against the money, then post to members, park exceptions in suspense".
3. **Identifiers decide reconciliation quality.** Systems that work use a stable employer-side key (payroll/staff number) mapped to the society's member number, with national ID and name as fallbacks; US child-support ACH even mandates a case ID plus SSN plus pay date in each addenda; ISO 20022 gives a structured creditor reference (ISO 11649) for the same purpose.
4. **Variance taxonomy is standard**: short, over, missing (scheduled but not remitted), unscheduled (remitted but not scheduled), unidentifiable; plus leaver/joiner signals (the US DED segment has an explicit *Employment Termination Indicator*).
5. **Regulators publish employer arrears as a league table by employer category** (Kenya SASRA: counties, universities, state corporations, ministries, private firms, with member counts and the savings/loan split). This is the natural shape of an "employer scorecard".
6. **Affordability caps are universal**: Kenya two-thirds of wages (Employment Act s.19); India 75% when co-op deductions are included (Payment of Wages Act s.7(3)); South Africa 40% cap on discretionary PERSAL deductions and 25% cap on emoluments attachment orders; Zambia 40% affordability threshold; Ghana TPRS computes "affordability" from current net pay.
7. **The structural fix being pursued where employer non-remittance is chronic is to take the employer out of the loop**: Kenya's Treasury plans to pay SACCO deductions directly from a centralised government payroll (2026); Kenyan SACCOs also route members' whole salaries through FOSA accounts so the SACCO deducts first.
8. **Documented abuses** are mostly: employers (especially public bodies) holding deducted money (Kenya, Ghana, Nigeria), unauthorised mandates loaded onto government payroll (South Africa 2024, Zimbabwe 2017/2025), and lenders over-extending on payroll security (Zambia 2026, SA 2000). Mandate integrity (consent capture, OTP, agent IDs) and employer remittance tracking are the two control points.

## 1. Kenya

### 1.1 Legal mechanics: Co-operative Societies Act (Cap. 490) s.35

**Takeaway.** The statute does not say "by the 10th of the following month"; it says **seven days after the date of deduction**, with compound interest of **not less than 5% per month**, and lets the Commissioner for Co-operatives appoint **any person, bank or institution** holding money for the employer as a collection agent for up to twelve months. The agent becomes liable as if it were the employer if it does not pay within thirty days. The Cooperatives Bill 2024 (cl. 73) re-enacts the same text.

**Cited findings.**

- Exact text (Kenya Law revised edition, FAO Faolex copy generated 2025-10-06; section inserted by Act No. 2 of 2004, s.18):

  > **35. Failure to remit the sum deducted**
  > (1) Where an employer of a person who is a member of a co-operative society has, under the instructions of the employee, made a deduction from the employee's emoluments for remittance to the co-operative society concerned but fails to remit the deductions within seven days after the date upon which the deduction was made, the employer shall be liable to pay the sum deducted together with compound interest thereon at a rate of not less than five percent per month.
  > (2) The Commissioner may, on behalf of the society, institute legal proceedings in court for recovery of the sum owing under subsection (1) without prejudice to any other mode of recovery and such sum shall be a civil debt recoverable summarily.
  > (3) The Commissioner may, by written notice, appoint any person, bank or institution to be an agent of the society for the purposes of collection and recovery of a debt owed to the society.
  > (4) The agent shall pay the amount specified in the notice issued under subsection (3) out of any moneys which may, at any time during the twelve months following the date of the notice, be held by him for the employer or are due from him to the employer.
  > (5) Where an agent claims to be or to have become unable to comply with subsection (3) by reason of lack of moneys held by or due from him, he shall give a written notification to the Commissioner stating the reasons for his inability and the Commissioner may (a) accept the notification and cancel or amend the notice accordingly; or (b) if he is not satisfied with the reasons, reject the notification in writing.
  > (6) Where an agent fails to notify the Commissioner or the notification is rejected, it shall be presumed that the agent has sufficient moneys for the payment of the amount specified in the notice.
  > (7) Where an agent fails to pay the amount specified in the notice within thirty days from the date of service or the date on which any moneys come into his hands for or become due to him from the employer, the agent shall be liable for the amount specified in the notification as if he were the employer.
  > (8) In any proceedings for the collection or recovery of the amount specified in the notice, it shall not be a defence for the agent to claim lack of the moneys.
  > (9) This section shall apply notwithstanding that the failure under subsection (1), to remit the sum deducted may constitute an offence under some other law for which the employer has been prosecuted, or is being, or is likely to be prosecuted.
  > (10) In this section "employer" includes any person, firm or organization holding remuneration or payment for produce of a member of a co-operative society and the term "employee" includes any person who receives remuneration or payment for produce from such persons or firm or organization.

  Source: https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/ken64070.pdf (text extracted from PDF); Kenya Law consolidated text at https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/1997/12/eng@2023-12-11 (returned 403 to automated fetch; same section).
- Note s.35(10): "employer" includes anyone holding **payment for produce** - the same mechanism covers agricultural marketing check-off (coffee, milk, tea), which is why Kenyan vendor systems speak of "Salary/Coffee/milk" uploads (see 1.4).
- **Bank-account attachment in practice.** The Commissioner for Co-operative Development described attachment of employer accounts through banks under s.35 as "the most effective method", citing KSh5m recovered from Egerton University and KSh200m from the University of Nairobi (Sacco Review, 8 Jun 2021, https://saccoreview.co.ke/tight-laws-will-curb-non-remittance/). A second Sacco Review piece (9 Aug 2021) states the Commissioner "can direct banks to transfer funds deducted from employee salaries but not remitted within seven days" (https://saccoreview.co.ke/saccos-mull-ways-to-address-non-remittance-of-funds/).
- **Court use.** *Egerton University Sacco Society Ltd v Egerton University & KCB*, High Court Nakuru, 15/16 Jan 2020: the SACCO sought ~KSh434.4m of deductions unremitted since Nov 2017 and orders directing KCB to transfer funds from named university accounts; the application was declined (summary via https://sheriahub.com/cases/ke/disputant/Egerton+University+Sacco+Society/1; judgment at https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/judgment/kehc/2020/8786/eng@2020-01-16, 403 to automated fetch). *Mwonya v Egerton University*, ELRC Nakuru, 5 Feb 2025: employee recovered KSh75,000 of unremitted SACCO deductions; the court noted such sums are civil debt recoverable under s.35(2) (same sheriahub index). *[unverified: grounds for the 2020 refusal; read the judgment before relying on it.]*
- **Cooperatives Bill 2024.** Clause 73(1) repeats s.35(1) verbatim but extends to "remuneration or payment for produce" and keeps the 7-day / >=5%-per-month compound interest rule; the Commissioner may appoint "any person, bank, or institution" as collection agent. Passed by the National Assembly with amendments 3 Dec 2024; First Reading in the Senate 12 Feb 2025 (Parliament Bills Digest, https://parliament.go.ke/sites/default/files/2025-03/Bills%20Digest%20-%20Cooperatives%20Bill,%202024.pdf: "Employers who deduct member contributions but fail to remit them are liable for repayment with penalties and the Cooperative Commissioner can take legal action or appoint collection agents"). The CS Co-operatives in Nov 2023 put unreported non-remittances at ~KSh4bn (https://saccoreview.co.ke/govt-to-punish-employers-not-remitting-employees-deductions-to-saccos/).
- **Deduction cap.** Employment Act 2007 s.19(3): total deductions "shall not exceed two-thirds of such wages" (https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2007/11/eng@2024-04-26; summary https://www.sheriaplex.com/kenya-acts/1433-section-19-of-employment-act-cap-226-deduction-of-wages). Kenyan payroll guides describe this as net pay not falling below one-third of basic.

**Gaps.** No statutory "by the 10th" rule was found; the 10th-of-month convention, if it exists, is contractual (SACCO-employer check-off agreements) *[unverified]*. The Senate outcome on the Cooperatives Bill 2024 after Feb 2025 was not confirmed. Interest actually awarded in reported judgments was not extracted.

### 1.3 Legislative and payroll reforms (2025-2026)

**Takeaway.** Two tracks: (i) the SACCO Societies (Amendment) Bill 2025 (introduced 10 Jun 2025) builds a Central Liquidity Facility / shared-services tier with **daily electronic reporting to SASRA**, penalties up to KSh3m / 5 years, and operationalises the Deposit Guarantee Fund (KSh100,000 protected); (ii) the National Treasury is designing a **centralised government payroll that remits SACCO deductions directly to the SACCO**, removing the employer intermediary.

**Cited findings.**
- SACCO Societies (Amendment) Bill 2025: CK Advocates note, 20 Jul 2026, https://ckadvocates.co.ke/2026/07/20/sacco-societies-amendment-bill-2025-structural-reforms-set-to-reshape-the-sacco-sector/ (CLF formed by 30+ licensed SACCOs; may "provide shared payment platforms, issue payment instruments"; must "provide daily liquidity and performance reports to SASRA"). Kenya News Agency on the reform agenda "making digital reporting mandatory": https://www.kenyanews.go.ke/sacco-reforms-to-strengthen-financial-stability-and-protect-members/. The SACCO Societies Act consolidated to 20 Jun 2025: https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2008/14/eng@2025-06-20.
- Treasury direct-remittance plan: Sacco Review, 30 Sep 2026, https://saccoreview.co.ke/sacco-deductions-to-skip-employers-in-treasury-payroll-plan/ ("the employer would no longer act as the financial intermediary for that portion of an employee's salary"); Business Daily, "Treasury payroll reforms to end sacco remittance delays", https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/companies/treasury-payroll-reforms-to-end-sacco-remittance-delays-5614634 (employers "would receive only the amounts due for payment to employees, while sacco deductions would flow directly to the societies"). No implementation date given.

**Gaps.** The Bill's "digital returns" clause text was not read directly (only summarised by a law firm); whether the Bill specifically addresses employer check-off returns is *unverified*. Which payroll system (IFMIS/GHRIS/UHR) will carry the direct remittance is not stated.

### 1.4 Monthly process, data exchange and core-system features

**Takeaway.** Public documentation is thin, but the pattern is consistent: SACCO issues a per-employer **deduction list** (member no., payroll no., savings amount, loan instalments by product); employer payroll deducts and sends back a **schedule** (Excel/CSV, often by email or portal) plus a **single bank credit** (EFT/RTGS/cheque; Co-op Bank dominates SACCO cheque and PesaLink processing); the SACCO **uploads the schedule**, the system **auto-distributes** each line to deposits/loan/ledger accounts, generates **one receipt per batch** (multi-member receipting), and parks exceptions. Open-source Sawazi documents the most explicit matching/variance model.

**Cited findings.**
- **Sawazi (Pesara), open source, Kenya-focused**: "check-off reconciliation ... schedule vs remittance per employer and period: short, over, missing, unscheduled and unidentifiable payroll lines". Imports: `POST /institutions/{id}/import/checkoff_schedule` and `.../import/checkoff_remittance`, both keyed by `employer` and `period=YYYY-MM`; `POST /institutions/{id}/checkoff/reconcile`. Matching keys: payroll number, member number, national ID, then name-only lines "matched through search"; "uncertain payments go to suspense with a suggested member and the reason". Sample output: collection rates 94.5% and 98.3% for two employers "with every short, missing and unidentified line listed". https://github.com/essendisidney/sawazi
- **Vendor feature language (Kenya)**: Ecomtech SACCO CBS lists "Bulk automated uploads facility. Example Salary/Coffee/milk etc." and bank reconciliation (https://www.ecomtech.co.ke/sacco-core-banking-software-cbs/). Coretec CoopMIS: "multiple receipting - raise one receipt and use it to distribute funds to more than one member's accounts"; "transfer of unposted entries from the statement to the journal for reconciliation" (https://coretecafrica.com/core-banking-systems-in-kenya/). Other vendors describe "payroll check-off deductions from different employers/income centers through system imports with automated distribution to respective member's accounts (savings/deposits, loans, and ledgers)" (search aggregate incl. https://apex-softwares.com/industry-credit-unions/, https://softeyesolutions.com/saccosoftware.html).
- **Kwara** public site: SASRA-compliant reporting, USSD *341#, mobile loans and repayments; no public description of a check-off module (https://kwara.com). **Fintech Group** (FinSacco/FINFINANCIALS): single member view, M-Pesa/Airtel integration, salary processing & bulk payments (Business Daily, https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/technology/fintech-s-new-application-to-benefit-sacco-members--1996760). **Craft Silicon, Fintech, CoreTec** were the three vendors short-listed in the WOCCU/FSD Kenya SACCO automation evaluation (https://www.fsdkenya.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/aws/Archive%20data%20FSD/10-09-22_SACCO_automation_report.pdf).
- **Payment rails**: Co-op Bank processed cheques for 92 DT-SACCOs in 2025 (Family Bank 22), PesaLink for 21, ATMs for 110; agency-banking transactions KSh37.07bn (TechTrends KE, 30 Sep 2026, https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/09/30/sacco-payments-kenya-banks-fintechs/). Co-op Bank's **Sacco Link** switch connected 619 SACCOs' FOSAs to ATM/mobile/POS/internet channels by Dec 2024 (Business Daily, 9 May 2025, https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/companies/co-op-bank-links-619-saccos-for-front-office-services-5034914). It is a channel switch, not a check-off clearing house.
- **Sacco Central Kenya (SCK)**, secondary cooperative est. 2018: phase-1 scope = shared core banking, inter-SACCO payments / direct NPS participation, inter-SACCO lending; 2026 milestones = requirements, architecture, pilots (FSD Kenya, https://www.fsdkenya.org/blogs-publications/the-rise-of-shared-services-for-savings-and-credit-co-operative-organisations-saccos-in-kenya/).
- **Member-initiated channels** that bypass the employer: M-Pesa paybill with member number as account reference (e.g. Mwalimu National paybill 541900), standing orders, and **FOSA salary routing** - members redirect their whole salary to the SACCO's FOSA so the SACCO "deduct[s] its share before disbursing" (Sacco Review Aug 2021; paybill directories https://paybillke.com/guides/top-saccos-in-kenya-2026).
- Member-facing "check-off loan" products require the latest payslip and ID and target county, police, TSC and public servants (Kuza Sacco, https://www.kuzasacco.com/home/checkoff-loan/).

**Gaps.** No public check-off schedule template or employer portal spec was found for any Kenyan SACCO or vendor (Kwara, Craft Silicon, Fintech, Coretec, Fern) - this would need vendor demos or SACCO employer-relations teams. Whether employers remit via M-Pesa paybill at scale is *unverified*; evidence points to bank EFT/cheque for employer bulk remittance and paybill for individual members. Sacco Central is not yet live as a shared switch.

## 2. Ghana

**Takeaway.** Government payroll deductions for credit unions, welfare associations, insurers and lenders run through CAGD's **Third Party Reference System (TPRS)**, launched 2014 and upgraded with an **affordability engine, mandate PIN/number sent to the employee's phone, and photo-ID on the GoG e-payslip portal**. The documented failure is the reverse of Kenya's: CAGD deducts but **remits late to lenders (3 months outstanding in Oct 2026)**, pushing banks to threaten to stop public-sector lending.

**Cited findings.**
- TPRS "allows Third Party Institutions (TPIs) to directly deduct loan repayments, Insurance premiums, welfare contributions and credit union deductions from salaries of Government of Ghana (GOG) employees at source"; the upgrade addresses "fraudulent and impersonation issues, wrongful deductions from TPIs as well as affordability issues" (CAGD, https://www.cagd.gov.gh/cagd-sets-dates-for-nationwide-sensitization-exercise-on-the-new-tprs-check-details/; post-upgrade review https://www.cagd.gov.gh/tprs-post-upgrade-implementation-review-held-in-a-4-day-meeting-by-the-cagd-with-stakeholders/).
- Consent and security: "No association can deduct salaries ... until the individuals legally authorize"; "automatic generation of the Mandate Pin and Mandate Number. The system sends these numbers to your phone whenever you carry out a transaction with a TPI"; employees upload photos to the GoG e-payslip platform instead of guarantors (YEN, https://yen.com.gh/188849-tprs-ghana-usefulness-accessibility-facilities-features.html). Origin 2014, teacher-protection framing: https://educationgh.wordpress.com/2014/09/21/third-party-reference-system-tprs-to-help-protect-teachers-salaries-cagd/.
- Affordability = net salary minus all existing deductions; existing "cooperative society dues, union fees" reduce it (e-payslip guides, https://gogpayslip-gh.com/gog-epayslip-loan-affordability/) *[secondary source]*.
- Credit union sector: CUA Ltd supervises credit unions on behalf of Bank of Ghana (https://knowledgehub.ghamfin.org/ghana-co-operative-credit-unions-association-cua-limited/). CAGD staff have their own credit union (CAGECCU, https://cageccu.com/about-cageccu/).
- Failure: Ghana Association of Banks (CEO John Awuah) threatened to suspend loans to CAGD-paid workers; banks "awaiting three months of outstanding remittances as of October"; repayments had already been deducted from salaries (Citi Newsroom 8 Oct 2026, https://www.citinewsroom.com/2026/10/banks-threaten-to-suspend-loans-to-public-sector-workers-over-delayed-repayments/; MyJoyOnline https://www.myjoyonline.com/banks-to-suspend-lending-to-public-sector-workers-under-controller-and-accountant-generals-payroll/; earlier 2024 episode GhanaWeb https://www.ghanaweb.com/GhanaHomePage/business/Banks-threaten-to-stop-loans-to-public-sector-workers-over-unpaid-deductions-2055805). Teachers also demanded release of mutual-fund and welfare dues held by CAGD (Modern Ghana, https://www.modernghana.com/news/1298451/release-our-mutual-funds-welfare-dues-now-teach.html).

**Gaps.** No CAGD circular specifying a percentage cap (e.g. 50% take-home) was found; the "affordability" rule is computed by the system rather than published as a fixed percentage *[unverified]*. e-zwich payroll's role in third-party deductions was not researched. TPRS file formats are not public.

## 3. South Africa

**Takeaway.** Government payroll (PERSAL) third-party deductions are governed by **Treasury Regulations Chapter 23** (origin: Notice 3098 of 2000, which banned "discretionary deductions" after ~497,000 state employees were found carrying microloan deductions, avg 2.1 loans each). Remaining deductions flow through **Q LINK** as "Service Provider" under the Office of the Accountant-General, with **deduction codes**, an **affordability enquiry / reservation facility**, **voice-recorded or electronic mandates**, agent-ID in every SEPDI transaction, and a **40% cap on discretionary deductions**. Emoluments attachment orders were reformed in 2016-2017 (judicial oversight, **25% cap**). SARB's 2024 draft directive would restrict all private-sector payroll deductions to five categories and require annual reporting.

**Cited findings.**
- Treasury Regulations on Government Payroll Deductions (Notice 3098 of 2000, under PFMA s.76): defines "benefit", "collective agreement", "State", "statutory" and "discretionary" deductions on Persal; item 3(1): no one operating Persal "may ... process a discretionary deduction"; item 3(2): responsible official must certify each deduction is due; item 3(3) adds EAO documentation checks (https://www.treasury.gov.za/legislation/pfma/regulations/gazette_21534.pdf). Press notes: https://www.treasury.gov.za/comm_media/press/2000/2000062901.pdf and https://www.treasury.gov.za/comm_media/press/2000/2000080201.pdf.
- SARB/NT joint consultation paper (c.2018): regulations "cap discretionary deductions at 40% of the state employee's salary"; 2000 context "about 497 000 state employees had non-statutory deductions ... vast majority ... repayments on microloans ... average of 2.1 loans"; 2015-16 EAO review: "legitimacy of about 121 487 EAOs, with an outstanding balance amounting to R1.9 billion, is under investigation ... highest number of occurrences of EAOs per employee was 22 ... over R190 million has been saved for employees" (https://www.resbank.co.za/content/dam/sarb/what-we-do/payments-and-settlements/consultation-documents/Joint%20Consultation%20Paper-%20Regulatory%20proposals%20on%20payroll%20deductions.pdf).
- ASISA Standard on Government Payroll Deductions (18 Jul 2024): "Affordability Enquiry" checks "whether a specific deduction ... breaks any of the Affordability Rules (including net take home pay)" per Treasury Regs Ch.23; "Deduction Code" issued by the OAG; "Reservation" and "Cancellation" of a reserved amount; VR (voice recorded) mandates; "identification number of sales agents ... must be included in the Standard Electronic Payroll Deductions Interface (SEPDI) transactions ... file based, through XML ... or API"; "registration ... of each sales agent on the Q LINK Active Directory is a prerequisite"; benefit payments: "reconciliation of payments will use the DCH unique reference and not bank details"; fraud-flagged intermediaries blocked and reported to all code holders (https://www.asisa.org.za/media/kjbhmmis/asisa-standard-on-payroll-deductions-final-18jul24.pdf; 2020 version https://www.asisa.org.za/media/dgcjmf5f/20200715_asisa-standard_government-payroll-deductions.pdf).
- SARB Draft Directive on payroll deductions (31 Jul 2024): payroll deduction defined as withholding "immediately before the salary/earnings are paid into the employee's bank account"; permitted only for (i) law, (ii) court order, (iii) collective agreement, (iv) arbitration award, (v) employer-employee relationship with "prior written consent"; third-party deductions otherwise need SARB approval "under exceptional circumstances"; annual report to SARB by 30 November listing types, beneficiaries, volumes and values; effective six months after publication; rationale includes preferential treatment vs DebiCheck/EFT collections, employer admin burden, over-indebtedness (https://www.resbank.co.za/content/dam/sarb/what-we-do/payments-and-settlements/regulation-oversight-and-supervision/document-for-comments/Draft%20Payroll%20Deductions%20Directive%20-%2031%20July%202024.pdf).
- EAO reform: *University of Stellenbosch Legal Aid Clinic* (CC 2016) and Courts of Law Amendment Act 2017: EAO only by a magistrate, only in debtor's jurisdiction, after assessing ability to pay, **max 25% of gross salary** (Wikipedia summary https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emoluments_attachment_orders_in_South_Africa; LSSA submission https://www.lssa.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/LSSA-Comments-on-Courts-of-Law-Amendment-Bill-2016-2-March-2017.pdf; NCR Circular 6 of 2023 https://www.ncr.org.za/documents/Circulars/Circular%206of%202023-%20Emoulument%20Attachment%20Orders.pdf; PER 2023 article https://www.saflii.org/za/journals/PER/2023/19.html).
- Failure: SADTU (19 Sep 2024) - Rand Mutual Assurance with EUSA loaded PERSAL deductions on 8,808 KZN educators "without their knowledge and consent"; demanded restitution with interest, criminal proceedings and Treasury disclosure of mandates (https://www.sadtu.org.za/2024/sadtu-condemns-the-unlawful-deductions-from-the-salaries-of-educators-by-a-company-working-with-pseudo-union-eusa/).

**Gaps.** Chapter 23 current text (post-2002 amendments) not read directly; the 40% figure is from SARB's paper, not the regulation text. Q LINK's file layouts (SEPDI) are proprietary. Status of the SARB directive after consultation is *unverified*.

## 4. India

**Takeaway.** Two layers: the **Payment of Wages Act 1936 s.7(2)(j)** permits deductions "for payments to co-operative societies approved by the appropriate Government", and **s.7(3)** raises the total deduction cap from 50% to **75% of wages** when co-op deductions are included. **State Co-operative Societies Acts** (Delhi s.52, similar in Gujarat, Karnataka, Goa, Maharashtra) make the employer's duty statutory once the member signs an agreement and the society serves a written requisition; a defaulting employer is liable "as arrears of land revenue", ranking equally with wages. Government drawing officers follow treasury handbook procedures.

**Cited findings.**
- Payment of Wages Act s.7(2)(j): deductions "for payments to co-operative societies approved by the appropriate Government ... subject to such conditions as the appropriate Government may impose"; s.7(3): total deductions "in cases where such deductions are wholly or partly made for payments to co-operative societies under clause (j) ... seventy-five per cent of such wages, and in any other case, fifty per cent" (https://indiankanoon.org/doc/392051/; official text https://labour.gov.in/sites/default/files/the_payment_of_wages_act-1936-english.pdf).
- Delhi Co-operative Societies Act 2003 s.52 (as summarised from the Act text): member "may execute an agreement in favour of the co-operative society providing that his employer shall be competent to deduct from the salary or wages ... and to pay the amount so deducted to the co-operative society"; on written requisition the employer "shall ... make the deduction ... and pay the amount so deducted ... as if it were a part of the salary or wages payable on the day as required under the Payment of Wages Act, 1936"; on failure "the co-operative society shall be entitled to recover any such amount from the employer as arrears of land revenue and the amount so due from the employer shall rank in priority ... equal to that of the wages in arrears" (https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/13605/1/dcs_act,_2003.pdf; https://indiankanoon.org/doc/191999120/). Parallel provisions: Gujarat 1961 Act, Karnataka 1959 Act, Goa 2001 Act (indiacode links in search; not quoted).
- UP Financial Handbook Vol. V para 39: recoveries "where such Acts impose a statutory obligation on the Government to make such deductions"; DDO deducts "from net pay after drawing the full gross amount, then remitting funds to the society minus remittance charges"; amounts "treated in the accounts in the same way as salary paid"; "The society issues receipts directly to affected individuals" (https://budget.up.nic.in/Fin_H_Book/volume5/part1/039.html).
- Employee cooperative credit societies are a recognised category (e.g. West Bengal State Co-op Bank page, https://www.wbstcb.com/pages/employees_cooperative_credit_society).

**Gaps.** No source found describing chit funds collected via employer payroll deduction; chit subscriptions appear to be paid directly by subscribers *[unverified]*. Mumbai/Maharashtra salary-earners' societies (large sector) not specifically researched.

## 5. UK and US

### 5.1 UK credit-union payroll deduction

**Takeaway.** No statutory check-off duty; schemes are contractual between credit union and employer ("payroll partners"). Mechanics: employee authorises amount; employer deducts; employer sends **one monthly data file plus one BACS payment**; CU posts to member accounts. Take-up is the weak point (c.5% opt-in), hence the MaPS/Financial Inclusion Centre work on auto-enrolment style nudges.

**Cited findings.**
- Leeds Credit Union: "Ongoing management is hassle-free via a simple file transfer of monthly data and a single monthly BACS payment. There are no set-up costs"; partners include Leeds City Council, Wakefield Council, LB Barking & Dagenham, NHS Trusts, housing associations (https://leedscreditunion.co.uk/payroll-savings-the-benefits-to-employers-and-employees/; https://leedscreditunion.co.uk/savings/save-through-your-pay/).
- MaPS-funded "Getting Workforces Saving: Payroll Schemes with Credit Unions" (Financial Inclusion Centre, Feb 2021): 70% of payroll members saved every month; 78% of payroll savers save regularly vs 55% non-payroll CU members and 47% non-members; 96% would recommend; 62% of non-savers unaware of the option; opt-in sign-up ~5% (summaries: https://inclusioncentre.co.uk/our-work/payroll-savings; https://www.alltogethermoney.coop/news/new-research-demonstrates-effectiveness-of-workplace-savings-schemes/; report PDF https://maps.org.uk/content/dam/maps-corporate/en/work-with-us/financial-wellbeing-in-the-workplace/getting-workforces-saving-payroll-schemes-with-credit-unions-final-report-february-2021.pdf).
- Other CU scheme pages: https://www.partnerscreditunion.co.uk/payroll-deduction, https://bdmoney.co.uk/payroll-deduction/.

### 5.2 UK Payroll Giving (design analogue for a regulated intermediary)

**Takeaway.** HMRC-approved **Payroll Giving Agencies** sit between employer and charities under the Charitable Deductions (Approved Schemes) Regulations 1986 (SI 1986/2211, amended 2000). The timing chain - employer to agency **within 14 days of the end of the tax month**, agency to charity **within 35 days** when donor/charity are known (**60 days** otherwise) - is a clean statutory SLA model for a check-off aggregator.

**Cited findings.**
- "Employers must pass donations over to the Payroll Giving Agency within 14 days of the end of the tax month"; agencies "must pay donations to a charity within 35 days of receipt where the agency already knows the donor and charity ... and within 60 days in all other cases" (Charity Tax Group, https://www.charitytaxgroup.org.uk/tax/employment-taxes/payroll-giving/; APGO, https://www.payrollgivingorgs.co.uk/payroll-giving-explained/; CIOF, https://ciof.org.uk/library/types-of-fundraising/what-is-payroll-giving-a-complete-guide-for-charities-employees-and-employers/). The 2000 Amendment Regulations (SI 2000/759) introduced the 60-day rule (Accountancy Age, https://www.accountancyage.com/2000/05/02/16-march-2000-improvement-of-payroll-giving-scheme/). SI 1986/2211 at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1986/2211 (image-only PDF; regulation text not extracted).
- Agencies reconcile each receipt "with the relevant employee and organisation" before paying (APGO, above) - i.e. the agency, not the employer, owns identification and the 35/60-day clock starts at receipt.

### 5.3 US credit-union payroll deduction

**Takeaway.** Two distinct rails: **"payroll deduction"** (employer relationship; CU receives a payroll file and a lump sum, must **balance file to funds** before posting; returns/modifications negotiated bilaterally) vs **ACH** (member-initiated split direct deposit; "only what is transmitted is posted", no balancing). Modern US CUs push members toward ACH split deposit precisely to avoid reconciliation.

**Cited findings.**
- CU*BASE (CU*Answers core) help: payroll deduction "Requires balancing against a payroll file ... to ensure that all funds received are posted to member accounts properly, and that what is posted equals funds received"; "Transmission of payroll can be via any method agreed upon between the company and the credit union (printed list, magnetic tape, diskette, etc.)"; "Returns and modifications are coordinated between credit union and company"; "Timing of posting is controlled by the credit union". ACH: "does not require manual account balancing; only what is transmitted is posted" (https://help.cbxcore.com/cubase/ach_vs_payroll_deduction.htm; https://help.cubase.org/cubase/understanding_payroll_deduction.htm).
- Member forms list distribution across share/loan accounts (e.g. NC SECU https://www.ncsecu.org/accounts/services/payroll-deduction; Gale CU https://www.galecu.net/products-and-services/payroll-deduction-transfers).
- State of California SCO Deduction Program Handbook for employee organisations (deduction codes, file layouts) exists but was not retrievable (403): https://www.sco.ca.gov/Files-PPSD/deduction_program_handbook_1119.pdf.

### 5.4 Remittance data standards usable as design patterns

**Takeaway.** Three proven ways to carry per-employee remittance detail with the money: (a) **NACHA CCD+ one 80-char addenda** with a delimited `DED` segment (one employee per payment); (b) **NACHA CTX** carrying an X12 820 with up to 9,999 addenda lines (many employees per payment); (c) **ISO 20022 pain.001 -> pacs.008 -> camt.054** with `RmtInf` either `Ustrd` (140 chars) or `Strd` (up to 999 occurrences; `RfrdDocInf`, `RfrdDocAmt`, `CdtrRefInf` with ISO 11649 check-digit references). The US child-support `DED` convention is the closest existing analogue to a check-off line.

**Cited findings.**
- NACHA/OCSE *User Guide for Electronic Child Support Payments* v9.0 (24 Feb 2017): DED segment in the 80-position Payment Related Information field: `DED*application identifier*case identifier*pay date*payment amount*non-custodial parent ssn*medical support indicator*non-custodial parent name*FIPS code*employment termination indicator\`. Field grid: DED01 Application Identifier `CS` (M, 2); DED02 Case Identifier (M, AN 1/20); DED03 Pay Date YYMMDD (M); DED04 Payment Amount $$$$$$$$CC (M, N2 1/10); DED05 SSN (M, 9); DED06 Medical Support Y/N/W (M, 1); DED07 Name (O, 1/10); DED08 FIPS (O, 5/7); DED09 Employment Termination Indicator `Y` (O) - "The payment amount field may contain zero when this field is used." Sample: `DED*CS*ZC146*951024*13547*975348431*N*SMITH,HAR*19000*Y\`. Pre-go-live: "you must contact the state and perform case reconciliation so they have a chance to correct any case data"; the Case Identifier "always refers to the identification number of the case in the state receiving" (i.e. the receiver's key, not the payer's). CTX alternative carries X12 820 with one `705DED*...` addenda per employee (https://www.nacha.org/system/files/2023-03/Child-Support-User-Guide-Feb-2017.pdf). CCD vs CTX limits: 80 chars vs "9,999 lines of 80 characters" (https://achgenie.com/nacha-file-format/nacha-payment-format-ctx-or-ccd/; https://bankxlsx.com/blog/what-is-a-nacha-addenda-record). TXP convention for tax payments: https://www.nacha.org/system/files/2023-06/TXP-Banking-Convention-Revised-12-12.pdf.
- ISO 20022: `RmtInf/Ustrd` 140 chars per occurrence; `Strd` fields `RfrdDocInf`, `RfrdDocAmt`, `CdtrRefInf`; same "Structured" grouping shared by pain.001, pacs.008, camt.054 "so that data can pass without alteration or truncation"; EPC ERI guidance: max 999 structured occurrences; ISO 11649 creditor reference = "RF" + 2 check digits + up to 21 alphanumerics (EPC092-19, https://www.europeanpaymentscouncil.eu/sites/default/files/kb/file/2019-07/EPC092-19V1.0%20Guidance%20On%20Usage%20Of%20Extended%20Remittance%20Information%20(ERI).pdf; X9 IR 01-2022 https://eact.eu/Core/Images/EACT_PDFs/ISO-20022-Remittance-Content-Market-Guide-February-2022.pdf; https://x9ware.com/iso-20022-remittance-information/; Goldman camt.054 https://developer.gs.com/docs/services/transaction-banking/camt54-advices/).
- UK BACS: Standard 18 file; payment reference 18 characters; employer payroll exports support "Bank Payments CSV ... Standard CSV (BACS) which includes the BACS Cross Ref" (Staffology help, https://help.staffology.co.uk/payroll/administration/settings/edit-employer-detials/bank-account/bank-account.htm) *[AUDDIS applies to Direct Debit mandates, i.e. member-initiated collection, not employer check-off; not researched further]*.
- South Africa SARS PAYE employer reconciliation BRS (EMP501, employee-level certificate files reconciled against monthly EMP201 payments) is a mature "schedule vs remittance" spec worth reading for field design: https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Docs/PAYE/BRS/SARS_PAYE_BRS-PAYE-Employer-Reconciliation_V20-0-1.pdf; guide https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Ops/Guides/EMP-GEN-02-G01-A-Guide-to-the-Employer-Reconciliation-Process-External-Guide.pdf *[not read in detail]*.

**Gaps.** SI 1986/2211 regulation numbers for the 14/35/60-day rules not quoted (image PDF). No public US CU payroll-file layout was retrieved. Kenyan bank EFT files (KBA/PesaLink) remittance-field limits not researched.

## 6. Reconciliation algorithm patterns (synthesis)

**Takeaway.** Across the sources the working model is the same; the following is a synthesis *[inference from the cited systems, not a single source]*.

1. **Entities**: Employer (with deduction code / employer ID, payroll calendar, contact, bank account(s) used to remit); Member-Employer link (payroll number, start/end dates); Deduction Instruction (member, product, amount, start/stop, mandate evidence); Expected Schedule per employer-period (generated from instructions); Employer Schedule (uploaded); Remittance (bank credit with reference/advice); Reconciliation Run (employer-period); Variance lines; Suspense items; Employer Ledger (receivable from employer).
2. **Match keys, in order**: payroll number -> member number -> national ID -> normalised name (fuzzy, flagged "unidentifiable/uncertain" and routed to suspense with suggested member) (Sawazi; US DED uses case ID + SSN + pay date; ASISA/Q LINK uses a unique DCH reference rather than bank details).
3. **Matching unit** = employer x period x member x product; amount tolerance zero by default, configurable rounding tolerance *[inference]*.
4. **Variance classes**: short, over, missing (expected/scheduled but not remitted), unscheduled (remitted but not expected: new joiner, re-activation, employer error), unidentifiable; plus derived signals: **leaver** (missing with termination flag or three consecutive misses *[inference]*; US DED09 gives an explicit indicator with zero amount), **new joiner** (unscheduled with valid instruction).
5. **Three-way balance** before posting: sum(schedule) vs bank credit vs sum(expected). CU*BASE: "what is posted equals funds received". Post only balanced lines; hold the remainder in an employer suspense/clearing account; auto-receipt one batch receipt that distributes to many members (Coretec "multiple receipting").
6. **Partial remittances**: allocate by rule (loan instalments first, then savings - Kenyan SACCOs push FOSA with "loan repayments as first charge"; Sacco Review 2021) or pro-rata; keep the unpaid balance on the **employer ledger**, not as member arrears, so the member's loan is not reported delinquent for an employer's default (SASRA's stated harm is exactly that loans "appear in default"; Sacco Review 30 Sep 2026).
7. **Ageing** from the pay date: 0-7 days (statutory grace in Kenya), 8-30, 31-60, 61-90, 90+; accrue statutory interest (Kenya >=5%/month compound) as a receivable memo; trigger demand letter at day 8, Commissioner referral / agent-notice at day 30 *[inference mapped onto s.35 timelines]*.
8. **Employer scorecard**: remittance rate (Sawazi "collection rate"), days-late distribution, variance counts by class, members affected, value outstanding, savings/loan split (SASRA shape), trend vs prior periods; publishable per employer category.
9. **Dispute workflow**: employer-level ticket per variance batch; states open -> queried with employer -> employer response -> adjusted/written-off/escalated; "Returns and modifications are coordinated between credit union and company" (CU*BASE); member-level statement line shows "deducted by employer, not yet received" so members can self-verify (Zimbabwe ARTUZ complaint shows what happens when payslips are withheld).
10. **Mandate integrity controls** (from SA/Ghana): capture consent evidence (e-mandate, OTP/PIN to employee phone, voice recording), agent identity on every instruction, affordability check against net pay before activation, reservation/cancellation of a deduction slot, fraud-flag sharing across code holders.
