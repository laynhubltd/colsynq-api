# Written Rules, Not Capital, Decide Pooled Ventures

Groups pool money successfully when they choose a legal wrapper that matches their headcount and control preferences, write the profit-sharing and governance rules down before the first naira moves, and keep the pooled funds under multi-signatory custody with meeting-by-meeting reporting — and they fail, almost without exception, when one of those three is missing. In Nigeria the menu of wrappers widened sharply with CAMA 2020 (which added limited partnerships and LLPs to the old business-name partnership and private company), while the Investments and Securities Act 2025 and the four 2025 tax reform acts (effective 1 January 2026) redrew the regulatory and tax boundaries: a closed group of co-owners in a private company with turnover at or below **₦100 million now pays 0% company income tax, 0% on gains and no development levy**, a registered cooperative stays income-tax-exempt on member business, a partnership or LLP is fully transparent to the partners' 0–25% personal rates, and any pool that invites the public or promises fixed returns is a collective investment scheme carrying a **₦10 million minimum fine plus ₦100,000 a day** if unregistered and **₦20 million and/or 10 years** if it is a Ponzi. Profit-sharing in every structure reduces to one appropriation sequence — pay for work first, then a return on capital, then the residual by an agreed ratio — and the choice of ratio (capital, fixed deed share, patronage volume, waterfall promote or contribution-tracked slices) changes individual outcomes far more than the amount contributed, as the worked figures below show. The informal models that most Nigerians actually use (ajo/esusu/adashe, thrift and credit societies, investment clubs) run on social collateral and deliver zero-to-negative cash returns in exchange for credit access and discipline; they lose money through collector absconding and member default (each cited by roughly 35% of Lagos traders) rather than through bad business economics. The documented successes — Chevron staff's 57-hectare Nicon Town estate, immigration and teachers' housing cooperatives, Mondragón — share a common bond, a titled asset, elected officers and a multi-year horizon; the documented failures — more than 90% of agri-crowdfunding platforms, MMM, MBA Forex's ₦171 billion, CBEX's ₦1.3 trillion — share strangers recruited by marketing, a fixed short-tenor return above any plausible source, and no asset the investor could seize. For an analyst or accountant advising a group, the practical rule is to pick the wrapper by four variables (headcount, active-versus-passive split, liability appetite, exit mechanics), choose the venture by capital tier and supervision capacity using the Treasury-bill yield as the hurdle, and treat the deed, the bank mandate and the monthly reporting pack as the real risk controls.

## CAMA 2020 and ISA 2025 define Nigeria's eight wrappers

A group deciding how to hold pooled money is choosing among eight legal positions, and the decision turns on who bears liability, whether control follows capital or heads, how many people can join, and how fast and cheaply the vehicle can be created. At one end sits the general partnership, registered with CAC as an ordinary business name: it is capped at **20 partners**, has no separate legal personality or perpetual succession, and exposes every partner's private property to creditors ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/shareholders/1430552/registering-business-partnerships-in-nigeria-general-partnerships-limited-partnerships-and-limited-liability-partnerships-in-nigeria)). It is the cheapest formal option — under the CAC fee schedule effective 1 September 2025, name reservation costs ₦1,000 and business-name registration ₦20,000 ([SplashDict](https://splashdict.com/blog/cac/understanding-the-new-2025-cac-service-fees/)) — but registration "records the name; it does not create a separate legal person and it does not limit liability" ([LegalDoc.ng](https://legaldoc.ng/glossary/partnership-deed)). The notes flag that the primary CAC fee PDF returned HTTP 403, so every 2025 fee figure here comes from secondary summaries and should be checked against cac.gov.ng before being relied on.

CAMA 2020, enacted 7 August 2020, introduced two structures Nigeria never had: the limited partnership (Part D, s.795 ff.) and the limited liability partnership (Part C, s.746 ff.), with CAC opening registration on its portal in 2021 ([DLA Piper Africa](https://www.dlapiperafrica.com/en/nigeria/insights/2021/cac-commences-registration-of-limited-liability-partnerships-and-limited-partnerships-in-nigeria.html)). The LP mirrors the global private-fund design — one or more general partners with unlimited liability and management control, plus limited partners whose liability is capped at "the amount so contributed or agreed to be contributed" — but it keeps the **20-person ceiling**, and a limited partner who takes part in management becomes liable "as though he were a general partner" ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1142692/registration-of-limited-partnerships-in-nigeria-under-cama-2020)). Registration costs ₦30,000 under the 2025 schedule ([SplashDict](https://splashdict.com/blog/cac/understanding-the-new-2025-cac-service-fees/)), and the LP is tax-transparent: "LPs are not taxed but the partners are taxed as individuals on profits from the Partnership" ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1142692/registration-of-limited-partnerships-in-nigeria-under-cama-2020)). The LLP, by contrast, is a body corporate with perpetual succession, **no cap on partners**, liability limited to the agreed contribution, and the ability to sue and be sued in its own name; it needs at least two partners and two "designated partners" responsible for compliance, one of whom must be resident in Nigeria ([Andersen Nigeria](https://ng.andersen.com/a-review-of-limited-liability-partnerships-under-the-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020/); [Pavestones Legal](https://pavestoneslegal.com/procedure-for-registration-of-a-limited-liability-partnership-in-nigeria/)). Like companies, LLPs must disclose persons holding 5% or more interest or control ([Starlion Legal](https://starlionlegal.wordpress.com/2020/09/05/50-key-points-on-the-new-companies-and-allied-matters-act-cama-2020/)). For a professional-services or operating partnership where all members work and want limited liability without share-capital formalities, the LLP is the natural fit; its constitution is the LLP agreement, which governs profit shares and transfers of partners' economic rights.

The private company limited by shares remains the default pooled-capital vehicle. CAMA 2020 permits a single shareholder (s.18(2)), caps private-company membership at **50** excluding employees (s.22), and lets the articles restrict transfers, require pre-emptive offers to existing members, and bar any sale of more than 50% of the shares to an outsider unless that buyer offers to purchase everyone's shares on the same terms ([LawGlobal Hub, s.22](https://www.lawglobalhub.com/section-22-companies-and-allied-matters-act-2020/); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/opinion/article/single-member-companies-and-the-restriction-to-convert/)). Authorised share capital was abolished in favour of a **₦100,000 minimum issued share capital** for private companies, all of which must be issued ([Aluko & Oyebode](https://www.aluko-oyebode.com/insights/minimum-issued-share-capital-requirement-in-the-cama-2020/)); CAC charges ₦10,000 per ₦1 million of share capital plus 0.75% stamp duty ([SplashDict](https://splashdict.com/blog/cac/understanding-the-new-2025-cac-service-fees/); [Fbanabena & Co](https://www.fbanabena.com/cac-registration/cac-registration-fees/)), so a group can incorporate at the minimum for well under ₦50,000 in statutory fees and raise capital later under s.127. CAC's stated timeline is 24 hours; practitioners report 2–10 business days ([Lawzana](https://lawzana.com/articles/nigeria/cac-registration-in-nigeria-step-by-step-guide-2026-881)). A "small company" — private, turnover ≤ ₦120 million, net assets ≤ ₦60 million, no alien or government members, directors holding at least 51% — may have a single director, needs no company secretary and is exempt from audit (ss.394–402) ([Starlion Legal](https://starlionlegal.wordpress.com/2020/09/05/50-key-points-on-the-new-companies-and-allied-matters-act-cama-2020/)). The shareholders' agreement is where the group's real rules live: it is a private contract, not filed at CAC, that "must expressly declare that it will supersede the provisions of CAMA and the company's articles of association in the event of a conflict", binds future buyers through a deed of accession, and cannot override mandatory CAMA provisions such as director-removal thresholds ([IBA Guide – Nigeria](https://www.ibanet.org/medias/iba-guide-shareholders-agreements-nigeria.pdf)).

The cooperative society is the only wrapper where control is deliberately decoupled from capital. Registered by each state's Director of Cooperatives under the Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act, a primary society needs at least ten qualified persons (six for industrial societies), must include "Co-operative" in its name with "Limited" as the last word, and must file bye-laws ([LawGlobal Hub](https://www.lawglobalhub.com/nigerian-co-operative-societies-act-ng/)); state registrars apply higher floors in practice — 20 members for savings and credit, 50 for agricultural ([Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/strategy/procedure-for-registration-of-cooperative-society-in-nigeria)). Each member has one vote (s.25), no member may hold more than one-fifth of share capital (s.27), and at least a quarter of net profit must go to an indivisible reserve before any distribution (s.34) ([Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)). Cooperatives are outside CAMA, file returns with the Director instead of CAC, and are exempt from company income tax on their member activities ([Pavestones Legal](https://pavestoneslegal.com/establishing-a-cooperative-society-for-investment-purposes-in-nigeria/)); the cost is slow, state-by-state registration — roughly ₦5,000–₦25,000 in fees but two to three months plus a monitoring period and, in Lagos, a trustee induction course ([Pavestones Legal](https://pavestoneslegal.com/registering-a-cooperative-society-in-lagos-state/); [Smartteller](https://smartteller.medium.com/a-step-by-step-guide-to-registering-a-cooperative-society-in-lagos-state-nigeria-e1843630a9e7)). Nigeria has over **370,000 registered cooperatives with more than 30 million members** ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2023/04/26/over-30-million-nigerians-belong-to-cooperative-societies/)), and the sector has mobilised over ₦1.8 trillion in savings and disbursed more than ₦1.3 trillion in loans ([Leadership](https://leadership.ng/cooperative-sector-mobilises-n1-8trn-in-savings/)).

Deal-by-deal pooling — the syndicate or SPV — is well developed globally and still a grey area in Nigeria. In the United States a lead forms a Delaware LLC or a series of a master LLC, sells interests under Rule 506(b) (up to 35 sophisticated non-accredited investors, unlimited accredited) and stays under the Investment Company Act's **100-beneficial-owner cap (250 for a qualifying venture fund under $12 million)**; AngelList charges about $8,000 set-up plus roughly $2,000 state filing per vehicle, with a $80,000 minimum raise, and the lead typically takes 20% carry on profits above cost ([Angel Investors Network](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/capital-raising/spv-setup-costs-what-angel-investors-actually-pay-in-2026); [Repool](https://repool.com/fundpedia/section-3c1/); [Allocations](https://www.allocations.com/insights/delaware-series-llc-for-spvs)). In Nigeria the ISA 2025 defines a collective investment scheme (s.150) to cover any arrangement "in whatever form" by which the public or qualified investors are invited to invest in a portfolio, presumes PE and VC funds to be CIS, and permits LP-form schemes under s.151 ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/venture-capital/1638148/nigerias-investments-and-securities-act-2025-what-is-changing-for-private-capital-players); [UUBO](https://uubo.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/UPDATED-DETAILED-ANALYSIS-OF-ISA-2025-WHAT-IS-CHANGING-FOR-PE-AND-VC-FUNDS-1.pdf)). No source found quantifies when a private pool crosses into a registrable CIS; the practical reading is that a closed circle of co-owners who all hold shares and decide collectively is outside s.150, while anything that advertises, manages third-party money for a fee or promises returns is inside it. For a single-asset purchase among a handful of acquaintances, a private company holding title with a shareholders' agreement setting the waterfall is the pragmatic Nigerian SPV. Investment clubs commonly register as partnerships, companies or cooperatives; "most small clubs don't require SEC registration", but larger pools or public solicitation may trigger it ([Lexpraxis](https://lexpraxisng.com/how-to-start-an-investment-club-in-nigeria/)). Contractual joint ventures without a new entity make sense only where each party already carries its own limited liability, and an unregistered group is at law an unincorporated association or general partnership whose members are personally — and often jointly and severally — liable and cannot hold land or a bank account in the group's name ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=cd347d14-58c3-45e1-80e9-dfcacbbe50bc); [Wagenmaker & Oberly](https://www.wagenmakerlaw.com/blog/should-we-incorporate)).

The UK and US comparators show the same design space with different defaults. A UK LLP and a private limited company both register at Companies House for the same £100 online fee; the LLP needs two members, the company can be formed by one ([Binderr](https://binderr.com/marketplace/uk/cost-of-setting-up-a-uk-company); [The Accountancy Partnership](https://www.theaccountancy.co.uk/limited-company/whats-difference-llp-limited-company-7698.html)). In the US a general partnership gives every partner management rights and unlimited liability, an LP splits managing general partners from passive limited partners who forfeit their shield if they manage, and the LLP is in effect "a limited partnership without a general partner" ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/legal/learn/general-partnership-vs-limited-partnership); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_liability_partnership)). Nigeria's post-2020 menu is therefore structurally complete; what remains distinctive is the 20-person cap on both partnership forms, the 50-member cap on private companies, and the cooperative's statutory one-fifth shareholding limit, which together determine that any pool above about 50 contributors must be a cooperative or an SEC-registered scheme.

## Ajo, chamas and thrift societies run on social collateral

Formal wrappers describe how a minority of pooled-capital groups are organised; the majority of Nigerians who pool money do so through rotating or accumulating savings associations. EFInA's 2023 Access to Finance survey found that **46% of Nigerian adults used some informal financial service**, up from 33% in 2020, with 10% relying on informal services only; savings groups, thrift, village associations and cooperatives were the main providers, with a sharp rise in village associations in the South East ([EFInA A2F 2023](https://efina.org.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/A2F-2023-Event-Day-Presentation-Version4-1.pdf)). Kenya's FinAccess 2024 reports informal-group membership at **30.7% of adults (about 8.7 million people)** even as formal inclusion reached 84.8%, which shows these groups complement rather than substitute for banks ([CBK/KNBS/FSD Kenya FinAccess 2024](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/2024-FINACCESS-HOUSEHOLD-SURVEY-MAIN-REPORT.pdf)).

The ROSCA — esusu among the Yoruba, isusu or etoto among the Igbo, adashi among the Hausa, and known as ajo in its urban collector form — follows Ardener's 1964 definition of "an association formed upon a core of participants who agree to make regular contributions to a fund which is given, in whole or in part, to each contributor in rotation" ([Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/frbp/assets/community-development/discussion-papers/discussionpaper-ROSCAs.pdf); [Global Informality Project](https://www.in-formality.com/wiki/index.php?title=Esusu_%28Nigeria%29)). Members contribute a fixed equal sum daily, weekly or monthly; rotation order is "usually decided by means of a ballot or by consensus", with need-based agreement and a lottery as tie-break; and the organiser normally takes the first pot as compensation for administering the group ([Global Informality Project](https://www.in-formality.com/wiki/index.php?title=Esusu_%28Nigeria%29); [Philadelphia Fed](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/frbp/assets/community-development/discussion-papers/discussionpaper-ROSCAs.pdf)). The economics are entirely in the rotation order: the first recipient "is effectively receiving an interest-free loan", everyone else "begin[s] the ROSCA as lenders, not borrowers", and in bidding variants the auction discount is the implicit interest, redistributed to later recipients as a dividend ([Philadelphia Fed](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/frbp/assets/community-development/discussion-papers/discussionpaper-ROSCAs.pdf); [Klonner & Rai](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~webfac/emiguel/e271_s06/klonner.pdf)). A 40-member bidding ROSCA in Philadelphia with $1,000 monthly contributions produced ex-post borrowing rates of 2.7–7.4% a year for rounds two to seven — between bank CD rates and prime loan rates — while the organiser took the full $40,000 at 0% ([Philadelphia Fed](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/frbp/assets/community-development/discussion-papers/discussionpaper-ROSCAs.pdf)). In the urban ajo variant a professional collector, the alajo, is "paid a small commission for this service", typically "one of the rounds of the total contribution" ([Global Informality Project](https://www.in-formality.com/wiki/index.php?title=Esusu_%28Nigeria%29)). Applied to a 12-round monthly cycle that is a 1/12 ≈ 8.3% fee on a zero-interest deposit; on a 31-day daily cycle it is 1/31 ≈ 3.2%, exactly the Ghanaian susu collector's charge ([AfroTools](https://afrotools.com/blog/susu-savings-ghana/)). The cash return to an ajo saver is therefore negative, which is consistent with the Lagos market survey of 1,200 traders finding that members join primarily for credit access (43.5%) and savings discipline (39.0%) rather than yield, with 51.7% contributing weekly and 24.9% monthly ([Adeyemi et al., 2025](https://www.gojamss.net/journal/index.php/ASMJ/article/viewFile/1285/1297)).

The quantitative core of ROSCA risk comes from the one rigorous default series available, Shriram Chits in Tamil Nadu: total default of **1.6%**, but early-half recipients defaulted at **1.74% against 0.54% for late-half recipients** before a 1993 bid ceiling — adverse selection in which those willing to pay most to receive the pot first are the least able to keep paying — and the organiser accordingly demands three cosigners from early recipients and takes a 5–6% commission per round ([Klonner & Rai](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~webfac/emiguel/e271_s06/klonner.pdf)). That is why strong groups insist on guarantors for early positions and why the Lagos survey found non-payment of dues (35%) as prevalent as collector misappropriation (35%), with "exit frauds" where the collector disappears mid-cycle at 30%; 82% of traders said deviant practices were common and 74.6% reported significant losses ([Adeyemi et al., 2025](https://www.gojamss.net/journal/index.php/ASMJ/article/viewFile/1285/1297)). Enforcement is social — "an oath of allegiance and mutual trust" — and government "does not have any role directly to protect informal savings schemes" ([Global Informality Project](https://www.in-formality.com/wiki/index.php?title=Esusu_%28Nigeria%29); [VOA News](https://www.voanews.com/a/nigerians-turn-to-community-savings-amid-financial-struggles-/7890615.html)).

Accumulating associations keep the pot and lend it. The CARE/VSLA design, the global reference, uses groups of 15–25 members who buy one to five shares per meeting at a group-set price, lend up to three times a member's savings for up to three months at 5–20% (commonly 10%) per month, keep a separate interest-free social fund, store cash in a box "secured with three padlocks, with three group members each holding one of the keys", and elect a five-member committee ([CARE VSLA 101](https://www.care.org/our-work/economic-growth/savings-groups/vsla-101/)). At the year-end share-out the record-keeper totals all shares, the money counters count all cash, total money ÷ total shares gives the new share value, and each member receives share value × shares held — so all interest and fines are distributed in proportion to savings, the informal equivalent of a unit-trust NAV ([VSLA.net](https://www.vsla.net/the-vsla-methodology/)). A randomised evaluation across 561 clusters in Ghana, Malawi and Uganda found 68% of members borrowed at a median 10% rate, savings rose 34% against comparison, business ownership rose 6%, but there was no detectable effect on average consumption — the welfare gain is smoothing and enterprise formation, not yield ([IPA](https://poverty-action.org/study/impact-savings-groups-lives-rural-poor-ghana-malawi-and-uganda)). Kenyan table banking applies the same logic monthly at 5–10% per month, and the Joyful Women Organisation has grown from 60 women in 2009 to **over 240,000 members revolving KES 3.4 billion** ([Huduma Global](https://hudumaglobal.com/blog/understanding-chamas-kenya-investment-groups-merry-go-rounds-collective-saving); [JoyWo](https://joywo.org/)). Nigeria's registered equivalent is the cooperative thrift and credit society, where practitioner guidance reports monthly contributions of ₦2,000–₦10,000, loans "as low as 1% monthly" with fellow members as guarantors, and annual dividends of 5–15% of savings ([KudiCompass](https://kudicompass.com/how-to-join-cooperative-society-nigeria/)) — a return at or below Nigerian T-bill yields, so again the appeal is credit, not yield.

Investment clubs solve the problem of members joining at different times with unit valuation: net worth ÷ units outstanding gives the unit value on each valuation date, contributions buy units at that price and withdrawals redeem at it, so a club whose portfolio rises from $300 to $400 re-prices its 30 units from $10 to $13.33 before accepting new money ([ICLUBcentral](https://www.iclub.com/clubs/unit_value.asp)). Kenya's chama ladder — merry-go-round, then table banking, then a share-based investment chama, then a registered SACCO or company — shows where this leads: an estimated 300,000 chamas hold about KES 300 billion, graduation into real estate and businesses is common, and formal registration is available at county level under the Community Groups Registration Act 2022 for KES 1,000 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chama_(investment)); [Huduma Global](https://hudumaglobal.com/blog/understanding-chamas-kenya-investment-groups-merry-go-rounds-collective-saving)). The governance lessons are specific: FSD Kenya puts theft or embezzlement at **13% of chamas** and "most chamas collapse within the first two years", with poor records, weak leader selection and absent contribution-deadline rules as the drivers ([Money254 citing FSD Kenya/KAIG](https://www.money254.co.ke/post/chama-revolution-what-successful-chamas-know-do-why-many-fail)); FinAccess 2024 adds that 28.9% of chama users reported unexpected charges ([FinAccess 2024](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/2024-FINACCESS-HOUSEHOLD-SURVEY-MAIN-REPORT.pdf)); and the single most reliable red flag in the case files is a group that stops holding physical meetings and tells members to pay the treasurer directly ([Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/counties/nairobi/-chama-treasurer-charged-with-defrauding-members-sh70-000-4286232)).

Digitisation in Nigeria runs on two tracks. Collector-led platforms keep the alajo but give them point-of-sale devices, receipts and escrowed accounts in CBN-licensed, NDIC-insured microfinance banks: AjoCard operates under a CBN microfinance licence; LibertyPay issued 150,000 digital ajo cards in late 2023 and projects about ₦5 billion in monthly group savings; the Alajo App had collected ₦373 million by April 2023 over USSD ([AjoCard](https://ajocard.com/); [Technext](https://technext24.com/2025/07/29/fintech-reinventing-alajo-model-nigeria/)). Consumer apps eliminate the pooled pot altogether: PiggyVest's Group Target Savings lets members save towards a shared goal "while keeping your funds in your own secure, private account" at up to 12% a year, and PiggyVest crossed 6 million users and paid out ₦1.3 trillion in 2025 ([PiggyVest](https://blog.piggyvest.com/save/piggyvest-target-group-savings/); [Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/news/piggyvest-2025-payouts/)). These are commitment devices rather than ROSCAs — no lump-sum rotation, no credit — and because CBN licenses the deposit-taking entity rather than the product, a "digital ajo" is only as safe as the institution actually holding the money; under BOFIA 2020 it is an offence to carry on banking business without a CBN licence, and the Bank warned in April 2022 against "unlicensed or illegal financial operators, who lure and defraud…by offering extraordinary returns" ([Daily Trust](https://dailytrust.com/early-detection-of-unregistered-financial-institutions-in-nigeria-mechanisms-gaps-and-global-lessons/)).

## Contributions are booked at agreed fair value, never at effort

Every structure begins with the same accounting question: what did each person put in, and what is it worth? Cash is credited to the contributor's capital account at face value — "a contribution will be a credit entry in the capital account and a debit entry in the bank account" — and non-cash assets are recorded "at the fair market value, not book value or tax basis", net of any liabilities the business assumes, with equipment appraised at current market value, real estate by professional appraisal, receivables tested for collectibility and client relationships generally refused as capital ([ACCA FA2](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/foundation-level-study-resources/fa2/fa2-technical-articles/accounting-for-partnerships.html); [LeanLaw](https://www.leanlaw.co/blog/the-financial-mechanics-of-partner-capital-contributions-how-theyre-valued-paid-and-recorded/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership_accounting)). Standard partnership bookkeeping credits nothing for unpaid labour. Where a capital interest is granted for services, US tax rules treat "the FMV of an interest in partnership capital transferred to a partner as payment for services" as a guaranteed payment taxable as ordinary income on receipt, whereas a profits-only interest is generally not taxed on grant ([IRS Publication 541](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p541)); the practical valuation rule for sweat equity is "what the work would cost to buy in the market" ([Equity Matrix](https://equitymatrix.io/blog/sweat-equity-valuation)).

Member loans are liabilities, not capital, and this distinction matters at every later stage. Interest on a partner's loan is a business expense above the line, not an appropriation of profit, and the loan ranks ahead of capital on winding up ([ACCA FA2](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/foundation-level-study-resources/fa2/fa2-technical-articles/accounting-for-partnerships.html)). The default statutory rate is low: under the Partnership Act 1890 s.24 a partner who advances money beyond agreed capital earns 5% a year, while no partner is entitled to interest on capital before profits are ascertained, and the Lagos State Partnership Law s.23 mirrors this — equal shares in capital and profits absent agreement, no interest on capital, 5% on advances ([Partnership Act 1890 s.24](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1890/act/39/section/24/enacted/en/html); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=1f0ca982-779b-4c17-bcef-2788c265dc0c)). At Nigerian market rates a 5% default badly under-compensates a lending member, so the deed must fix the rate expressly. Most firms run two accounts per partner: a fixed capital account recording the contribution base on which interest is computed, and a fluctuating current account carrying salaries, interest, residual profit and drawings, which can go negative if draws exceed earnings ([ACCA FA2](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/foundation-level-study-resources/fa2/fa2-technical-articles/accounting-for-partnerships.html); [LeanLaw](https://www.leanlaw.co/blog/the-financial-mechanics-of-partner-capital-contributions-how-theyre-valued-paid-and-recorded/)).

An illustrative opening journal for a five-member Nigerian venture (the arithmetic is the research team's, applying the sourced rules) makes the mechanics concrete. Member A contributes ₦5,000,000 cash; B a truck agreed at ₦3,000,000 subject to an ₦800,000 vehicle loan the business assumes; C a shop fit-out valued at ₦1,500,000; D ₦2,000,000 cash plus a ₦1,000,000 member loan at 10%; E services only. The entries are Dr Bank 7,000,000, Dr Truck 3,000,000, Dr Fixtures 1,500,000; Cr Vehicle loan 800,000, Cr Member loan (D) 1,000,000; Cr Capital A 5,000,000, B 2,200,000, C 1,500,000, D 2,000,000, E nil — total capital ₦10,700,000. E's reward must come through a salary step in the appropriation account or a vesting or dynamic-equity arrangement, and D's ₦100,000 annual loan interest is paid as an expense before any profit is shared. The three numbers a deed should record for every in-kind contribution are the agreed fair value, who valued it, and any liabilities assumed, because disputes almost always trace back to an unwritten valuation. Where members cannot fund their contribution up front, law-firm practice offers templates: bank loans at prime plus 1–3% over five to ten years, zero-interest firm loans deducted from distributions, graduated contributions over three to five years, or profit-based funding; return of capital on exit typically runs over one to five years net of offsets ([LeanLaw](https://www.leanlaw.co/blog/the-financial-mechanics-of-partner-capital-contributions-how-theyre-valued-paid-and-recorded/)).

Startups increasingly replace fixed splits with contribution-tracked equity. The Slicing Pie model treats unpaid time, unreimbursed expenses, equipment, IP and relationships as "at-risk contributions" valued at fair market and converted to slices, with ownership = individual slices ÷ all slices until the pie freezes at breakeven or funding ([Slicing Pie](https://slicingpie.com/learn-slicing-pie-model/)). Multipliers reward risk: unpaid work at 2× fair hourly rate, at-risk cash at 4×, equipment at 1× resale value, IP at 1–2× development cost; a $150,000 developer's hourly rate is $75, so 20 unpaid hours earn $3,000 in slices ([Equity Matrix](https://equitymatrix.io/blog/slicing-pie-guide)). In the worked example, a developer, a business lead contributing $30,000 cash and an advisor start month one at 15.4%, 81.0% and 3.6%, and after six months of steady work shift to 38.8%, 52.1% and 9.1% — "consistent work compounds over time" ([Equity Matrix](https://equitymatrix.io/blog/slicing-pie-guide)). The evidence for why this matters is Wasserman's finding that **73% of founding teams split equity within the first month**, before anyone knows who will contribute what, and that equal splits "feel fair because they avoid a hard talk" but breed resentment when contributions diverge ([Culta](https://culta.ai/blog/cofounder-equity-salary-split)). The standard protection for sweat-equity holders in a fixed-split structure is a four-year vest with a one-year cliff — 25% at twelve months, the rest monthly — with unvested shares reverting on departure ([Carta](https://carta.com/learn/equity/stock-options/vesting/)).

## Six sharing methods, one appropriation sequence, very different outcomes

All profit-sharing methods are variations on a single appropriation order: "first any salaries, then any interest on capital, and finally the remaining profit in the profit-sharing ratio"; salaries and interest on capital are not P&L expenses but appropriations, and interest on capital exists "as a means of rewarding partners for investing funds in the partnership as opposed to alternative investments" ([ACCA F3](https://www.accaglobal.com/us/en/student/exam-support-resources/fundamentals-exams-study-resources/f3/technical-articles/working-partnership.html)). Interest charged on drawings is credited back to the appropriation account, penalising members who take cash early ([ACCA FA2](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/foundation-level-study-resources/fa2/fa2-technical-articles/accounting-for-partnerships.html)). The methods differ only in which steps exist and what ratio governs the residual.

Applied to the five-member venture above, with net profit of ₦2,140,000 after D's loan interest, the choice of method moves individual outcomes by hundreds of thousands of naira. Under pure pro-rata to capital, A receives ₦1,000,000 (46.7%), B ₦440,000, C ₦300,000, D ₦400,000 and E, the full-time manager, nothing — which is why capital-ratio sharing is unworkable where one member contributes labour. Under a salary-plus-interest-plus-equal-residual deed (E salary ₦600,000; 10% interest on capital = ₦1,070,000; residual ₦470,000 split five ways), the totals become A ₦594,000, B ₦314,000, C ₦244,000, D ₦294,000 and E ₦694,000. Same profit, radically different result: the deed, not the capital, decides. A full year's books under the second method, with drawings of ₦400,000, ₦200,000, ₦100,000, ₦200,000 and ₦500,000 and 5% interest on drawings (₦70,000 in total, which lifts the divisible residual to ₦540,000 or ₦108,000 each), produce the following current accounts (research team's arithmetic applying the ACCA mechanics):

| ₦ | A | B | C | D | E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salary | – | – | – | – | 600,000 |
| Interest on capital (10%) | 500,000 | 220,000 | 150,000 | 200,000 | – |
| Residual (equal) | 108,000 | 108,000 | 108,000 | 108,000 | 108,000 |
| Less drawings | (400,000) | (200,000) | (100,000) | (200,000) | (500,000) |
| Less interest on drawings (5%) | (20,000) | (10,000) | (5,000) | (10,000) | (25,000) |
| Closing current account | 188,000 | 118,000 | 153,000 | 98,000 | 183,000 |

Total credited to partners is ₦2,210,000 (salary 600,000 + interest 1,070,000 + residual 540,000), total drawings plus interest on drawings ₦1,470,000, net increase in current accounts ₦740,000; balance-sheet equity is capital ₦10,700,000 plus current ₦740,000, with D's ₦1,000,000 loan shown separately under liabilities. Default rules apply where the deed is silent: "all partners are entitled to share equally in the capital and profits of the business" and equally in losses, with no salary and equal management say regardless of effort ([Partnership Act 1890 s.24](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1890/act/39/section/24/enacted/en/html); [LegalDoc.ng](https://legaldoc.ng/glossary/partnership-deed)).

Syndicates and real-estate deals use the same structure with return of capital and a sponsor catch-up inserted before the residual split: return of capital, then a preferred return to the limited partners, then a GP catch-up, then the residual at the promote ratio, commonly 80/20 or 70/30 ([Agora](https://agorareal.com/learn/commercial-real-estate-waterfall-model/)). In Agora's worked example LP equity of $4,000,000 returns $6,400,000 after five years, a profit of $2,400,000; an 8% simple cumulative preferred return absorbs $1,600,000, leaving $800,000. Without a catch-up, the $800,000 splits 80/20 — LP $640,000, GP $160,000, so the sponsor's promote is only 6.7% of profit. With a full catch-up to 20% of total profit, the GP first takes $400,000, the remaining $400,000 splits 80/20, and the GP ends at $480,000 — exactly 20% of profit — while the LP multiple falls from 1.56× to 1.48× ([Agora](https://agorareal.com/learn/commercial-real-estate-waterfall-model/)). The catch-up algebra generalises: with promote p and preferred return Pref, the catch-up C = p × Pref ÷ (1 − p), which gives 0.2 × 1,600,000 ÷ 0.8 = $400,000. Multi-hurdle IRR waterfalls layer tiers (100% to LPs to an 8% IRR, then 80/20 to 12%, then 70/30), and the European-versus-American distinction decides whether carry is paid deal by deal or only after fund-wide capital and preferred return are repaid, with a clawback forcing the GP to return excess carry ([Agora](https://agorareal.com/learn/commercial-real-estate-waterfall-model/)). Angel SPVs simplify this to a 20% carry on profits above cost: an LP who invests $5,000 and receives $100,000 pays carry of 20% × ($100,000 − $5,000) = $19,000 and nets $81,000; at vehicle level a $1 million raise exiting at $10 million yields $1.8 million carry and $8.2 million to investors ([AngelList](https://help.angellist.com/hc/en-us/articles/360047684032-How-does-carried-interest-carry-work-for-SPVs)). AngelList adds 5% carry on capital sourced from its own network, so platform LPs effectively pay about 25% ([Angel Investors Network](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/angel-investing/angellist-syndicates-rolling-funds-spv-explained-2026)).

Cooperatives invert the logic: capital earns a capped return and the residual follows usage. ICA Principle 3 states that "members usually receive limited compensation, if any, on capital subscribed" and allocate surplus to reserves, to "benefiting members in proportion to their transactions with the cooperative", and to approved activities ([ICA](https://ica.coop/en/cooperatives/cooperative-identity)). Nigerian law hard-codes the sequence. Under s.34 of the Co-operative Societies Act, "at least one fourth of the net profits…shall be paid into a fund to be called the 'reserve fund'"; with the Director's approval up to 10% of the remainder may go to an education fund; no dividend or bonus may be paid until the committee approves the distribution, and wilful breach is an offence; s.57 defines "bonus" as surplus "divided among its members in proportion to the volume of business done by them with the society" and "dividend" as profit shared "in proportion to the share capital held by them", with dividend rates capped by regulation — bye-laws typically at 5% ([Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/996640/establishing-a-cooperative-society-for-investment-purposes-in-nigeria)). The ASUU Ojo cooperative's registered bye-laws show the sequence in practice: at least 25% to reserve, 2.5% to education, honoraria to the committee capped at 7.5% of surplus and approved by the general meeting, and dividend on savings, shares and loans "not less than 50% of the net surplus" ([ASUU Ojo bye-laws](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)). The US patronage model works the same way: a cooperative declaring $0.03 per bushel paid half in cash allocates $3,000 to a member who sold 100,000 bushels — $1,500 now and $1,500 as retained allocated equity — with at least 20% of a qualified refund payable in cash and the whole allocation taxable to the member in the year allocated ([Iowa State Extension](https://www.extension.iastate.edu/agdm/crops/html/a2-80.html); [USDA CIR 44-2](https://www.rd.usda.gov/files/cir44-2.pdf)). Mondragón, the world's largest worker cooperative at €11.2 billion revenue and 70,085 employees in 2024, requires an initial member contribution of roughly a year's minimum salary (85% to the member's individual capital account, 15% to collective reserve) and splits profits roughly 60% reinvested, 30% to members' capital accounts and 10% to community ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondragon_Corporation); [ILSR](https://www.ilsr.org/wp-content/uploads/files/images/mondragon.pdf)).

Losses follow the profit ratio by default, and appropriations can turn a small profit into a shared loss: when salaries and interest exceed profit, "the amount to be shared between the partners will be a loss" debited to current accounts in the profit-sharing ratio ([ACCA FA2](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/foundation-level-study-resources/fa2/fa2-technical-articles/accounting-for-partnerships.html)). In the five-member venture, a year with a ₦300,000 trading loss after E's ₦600,000 salary and ₦1,070,000 interest on capital produces a divisible loss of ₦1,970,000 — members must understand that salaries and interest are still credited in a loss year unless the deed makes them contingent on profit. A deficit capital account is a debt the member owes the firm, and if the member cannot pay it on dissolution the English rule in Garner v Murray applies absent agreement: "the deficiency of insolvent partner will be borne by solvent partners in proportion to their capital accounts and not in accordance with the profit sharing ratio" — so with equal-profit partners A ₦60,000 Cr, B ₦40,000 Cr and C ₦20,000 Dr insolvent, A bears ₦12,000 and B ₦8,000 ([Testbook](https://testbook.com/ugc-net-commerce/garner-vs-murray-rule)). No Nigerian case applying the rule was found, so the deed should state the loss ratio, whether deficits must be restored in cash and by when, and which dissolution rule applies. Allocation and distribution are separate decisions: "a partnership can allocate profits to a partner for the year without making a matching cash distribution", producing phantom income, which is why well-drafted agreements require tax distributions sufficient to cover tax on allocated profit and set an order of cash use — reserves, debt, tax distributions, then discretionary payout ([Philly Small Business Lawyer](https://phillysmallbusinesslawyer.com/business-success-tips/how-to-handle-distributions-allocations-and-profitloss-division-in-partnerships/); [Hawkins Ash CPAs](https://www.hawkinsash.cpa/navigating-tax-complexities-craft-partnership-agreements-and-llc-operating-agreements-with-precision/)).

Membership changes carry their own arithmetic. Admitting a partner uses either the goodwill method (recognise implied goodwill, credit old partners in the old ratio, write it off in the new) or the bonus method (no goodwill; the gap between cash paid and book capital shifts between old and new partners). With existing capital of 65,000 shared 70:30 and a newcomer taking 20% for 30,000, the goodwill method implies a firm value of 150,000 and goodwill of 55,000 credited to the old partners, while the bonus method credits the newcomer only 19,000 and gives the 11,000 excess to the old partners as a bonus ([Double Entry Bookkeeping](https://www.double-entry-bookkeeping.com/partnership/admission-of-a-new-partner/)). The choice affects later exits because the goodwill method inflates total capital and therefore any book-value buyout. Buy-sell clauses fix price by fixed value updated annually, formula (a multiple of EBITDA or revenue), book value, or independent appraisal, with triggers of "death, disability, retirement, and divorce" and funding through life insurance or promissory notes; many agreements "use a formula as the default and allow an independent appraisal if the owners disagree by more than a set percentage" ([The Tax Adviser](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2020/feb/buy-sell-agreement-value-business-interest/); [Virtue CPAs](https://virtuecpas.com/business-valuation-partner-buyouts/)). Nigerian cooperatives restrict share transfers (s.28) and require every member to nominate a successor who inherits or is paid out the value of the interest less sums due ([Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf); [ASUU Ojo bye-laws](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)).

## Governance works as three tiers, two signatures and a monthly pack

Every source that explains why co-owned businesses fail lands on the same cause. Wasserman's data attribute 65% of high-potential startup failures to "problems between the people at the top" — though the figure traces partly to a 1989 survey of 49 venture capitalists about 96 troubled companies ([CNN Money](https://money.cnn.com/2014/02/24/smallbusiness/startups-entrepreneur-cofounder/); [Cofounder Clarity](https://cofounderclarity.com/founder-therapy-blog/the-truth-behind-8-common-myths-on-cofounder-conflict)). Nigeria's clearest case is Pivo, a fintech that raised a $2 million seed in November 2022 on 98% loan repayment and 400% transaction growth, then collapsed by December 2023 after the CEO "appointed her brother as company secretary without consulting" her co-founder: "what it destroyed was the assumption that major decisions would be made jointly" ([PlanetWeb.ng](https://planetweb.ng/co-founder-conflicts-in-nigerian-startups/); [WeeTracker](https://weetracker.com/2023/12/06/why-pivo-shutdown/)). A diaspora founder's 50-50 Congo venture with no contract ended after 18 months and $15,000 in dissolution fees ([Nohemie Co](https://nohemieco.substack.com/p/diaspora-playbook-why-partnerships)). The formation sequence that follows from the evidence is: align on purpose and horizon, fix contributions and equity with vesting, pick the wrapper, sign the deed or shareholders' agreement or bye-laws before money moves, open a bank account under a written mandate, then register and diarise the filings — CAC annual returns within 42 days of the AGM for companies and by 30 June for business names ([SmartSMS](https://smartsmssolutions.com/resources/blog/ng/ng-02-hub-cac-annual-returns)).

Decision rights in real agreements run on three tiers. Day-to-day matters go to the manager or management committee; ordinary matters pass by simple majority; a scheduled list of reserved matters — borrowing above a limit, leases, large asset purchases, litigation, guarantees, changing the business, admitting members, amending the agreement, dissolution — needs two-thirds, 75% or unanimity ([PastPaperHero](https://www.pastpaperhero.com/resources/sqe1-flk1-partnership-governance-common-provisions-in-partnership-agreements); [Aaron Hall](https://aaronhall.com/supermajority-vote-requirements-in-llc-agreements/)). The voting basis follows the wrapper: in a Nigerian company an ordinary resolution is a simple majority of votes cast and a special resolution needs "at least three-fourths of the votes cast" on 21 days' notice (CAMA s.258) ([LawGlobal Hub](https://www.lawglobalhub.com/section-258-companies-and-allied-matters-act-cama-2020/)); in a cooperative each member has one vote with the chairman holding a casting vote, and registered bye-laws fix quorum at a quarter of members, special resolutions at three-quarters on 21 days' notice, and secret ballots on demand of five members ([Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act s.25](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf); [ASUU Ojo bye-laws](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)). Because 75% aligns with both CAMA special resolutions and cooperative special resolutions, one threshold can serve whichever wrapper the group chooses. Nigerian shareholders' agreements typically reserve "taking on debt above a specified threshold, making acquisitions, changing the nature of the business, or approving related-party transactions", and protect minorities through board-nomination rights, quorum rules requiring a minority nominee, and vetoes ([IBA Guide – Nigeria](https://www.ibanet.org/medias/iba-guide-shareholders-agreements-nigeria.pdf); [Enebeli & Partners](https://goenebeli.com/why-every-nigerian-business-needs-a-shareholders-agreement-before-the-first-deal/)). Capital-weighted voting protects large funders; per-head voting protects the many small ones; groups with wide contribution disparity should choose deliberately.

Roles separate a governing body from an executive and an independent check. The registered Nigerian cooperative template is directly reusable in any wrapper: the general body is "the ultimate authority"; a management committee of seven to nine elected for two-year terms with a two-term cap runs day-to-day affairs; and a three-person supervisory committee of non-committee members "shall meet at least once every three months to make…an examination of the affairs of the society, which shall include an audit of its books and an inspection of the sureties, cash account, loans and stock of goods", reporting in writing after each examination and submitting an annual audit to the AGM ([ASUU Ojo bye-laws](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf); [NDU Workers Cooperative bye-laws](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf)). Committee members "stand in a fiduciary relationship towards the Society", may not let personal interests conflict with duties, and the society may not contract with a committee member or connected person ([ASUU Ojo bye-laws](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)). Paying active members is handled by separating pay for work from return on capital — a committee honorarium capped at 7.5% of surplus and approved at the general meeting in the cooperative case, or "modest monthly salaries reflecting workload disparity, reserving equity splits for profit distribution" in the founder case ([ASUU Ojo bye-laws](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf); [Nohemie Co](https://nohemieco.substack.com/p/diaspora-playbook-why-partnerships)).

Bank mandates and financial controls are where the evidence is most prescriptive. The ACFE's 2024 study puts the median fraud loss at $145,000 globally and **$141,000 for organisations under 100 employees**, with owner-executive perpetrators causing $500,000 median losses, asset misappropriation in 89% of cases, lack of controls or override in 51%, and tips uncovering 43% of frauds ([Porte Brown summary of ACFE 2024](https://www.portebrown.com/newsblog-archive/acfe-publishes-new-fraud-study); [Schneider Downs](https://schneiderdowns.com/our-thoughts-on/spot-and-stop-long-lasting-fraud/)). Registered bye-laws answer with specific rules: "the Society shall operate a Trustee Bank Account. Before money is withdrawn from the Bank, the cheques or withdrawal slip shall be signed by at least two of the Trustees"; "all cash accrued or left over must be paid into the Cooperative Bank Account within 48 hours"; no unofficial books; employees give two guarantors; and the supervisory checklist asks whether every receipt has a receipt, every payment a voucher signed by the President, whether any cheques are signed in blank, whether the cashbook is balanced daily, weekly and monthly, and whether members' balances are compiled monthly and agree to the control account ([ASUU Ojo bye-laws](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf); [NDU bye-laws](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf)). The segregation-of-duties principle is that "no single person controls all three parts of a transaction: authorizing it, holding the related asset (cash or the checkbook), and recording it", with owners receiving bank statements unopened and reviewing cleared-cheque images monthly ([Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/05/15/segregation-of-duties-small-business-three-employees-internal-controls-fraud-prevention-compensating-controls-guide); [US DOJ/UST](https://justice.gov/sites/default/files/ust-regions/legacy/2011/07/13/sb_internal_control_info.pdf)). The lock-out risk — a partner who "unilaterally decides to change account access credentials…without the consent or knowledge of the other partner" — is prevented by requiring two signatories from different camps above a threshold and prohibiting any sole signatory ([Aaron Hall](https://aaronhall.com/can-you-be-locked-out/)). The minimum control stack for a 3–30 member venture therefore is: a business account only; three named signatories, any two to sign, with a petty-cash single-signature ceiling and a committee-resolution tier above a set naira limit; a voucher for every payment approved by a non-signatory; cash banked within 48 hours and no blank cheques; a monthly bank statement, reconciliation and members' balance report circulated to all members; a quarterly supervisory inspection; an annual external audit approved at the AGM; read-only accounting access for members; and a confidential tip channel.

Reporting rhythm and information rights are written, not assumed. Bye-laws require the treasurer to present quarterly financial reports, the supervisory committee to inspect quarterly, members' balances to be compiled monthly, and the AGM to be held "within the first or second quarters of the succeeding year after the Annual Statement of Accounts have been prepared" on 21 days' notice to receive the committee's report and audited accounts, "decide upon the disposal of the surplus", elect officers and appoint the auditor ([ASUU Ojo bye-laws](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf); [NDU bye-laws](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf)). Statute backs this: accounts "shall be audited at the end of the financial year" by an approved auditor with power to summon any officer or member and require production of books, cash or securities (s.36), the bye-laws must be open for inspection (s.13), and one-third of members can compel the Director to hold an inquiry into "the constitution, working and financial state" of the society (s.37) ([Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf)). A Kenyan court case shows the alternative: a 33% shareholder "removed…as director without notice, denied…access to company records and funds" obtained a court-ordered independent valuation and buy-out ([Business Daily Africa](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/companies/court-orders-buy-out-in-fight-over-firm-founded-on-friendship-5604604)). Writing information rights — inspection on seven days' notice, receipt of the monthly pack — into the deed makes exclusion a breach rather than a grievance.

Disputes escalate through negotiation, mediation and arbitration, with deadlock-breakers for ownership stalemates. The catalogue includes the casting vote, an independent expert choosing among pre-agreed options, and buy-sell devices — Russian roulette ("one party sets a price for a half interest; the other party chooses to buy or sell at that price"), Texas shoot-out sealed bids, and the deterrence approach where the initiator "must either buy all shares at 125% of fair market value or sell theirs at 75%" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadlock_provision)). Shotgun clauses are "rapid and definitive" but disadvantage "the cash-poor member every time" and the less-informed passive owner ([WebLegal](https://weblegal.net/article/llc-operating-agreement-deadlock-buyout-clauses); [Business Divorce Law Report](https://www.thebusinessdivorcelawyer.com/shotgun-buy-sell-agreements-solution-to-deadlock/)), so they suit two roughly equal, equally liquid owners rather than a 3–30 member group. Nigerian cooperatives must refer any dispute "to the Director for settlement by arbitration" (s.49), and Nigerian shareholders' agreements may provide for arbitration seated outside Nigeria, with the Federal High Court holding first-instance jurisdiction over CAMA matters and minorities able to sue for unfairly prejudicial conduct ([Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf); [IBA Guide – Nigeria](https://www.ibanet.org/medias/iba-guide-shareholders-agreements-nigeria.pdf)). Non-performing members are handled by expulsion for defined grounds — insolvency, persistent breach, dishonesty, permanent incapacity, or in cooperatives "repeated failure to make thrift savings" — after a written show-cause notice, confirmed by the general meeting, with the expelled member paid "the sum of his credit less any sum due" ([Guildhall Chambers](https://www.guildhallchambers.co.uk/uploadedFiles/ExclusionsfromPartnerships_KG.pdf); [ASUU Ojo bye-laws](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)). Partners generally cannot run a competing side business without consent, and deeds should require disclosure of personal profits from partnership opportunities ([Gross Law Group](https://grosslaw.com/common-causes-of-partnership-disputes-and-how-to-resolve-them-legally/); [MHCO Law](https://mhcolaw.com/insights/key-clauses-every-partnership-deed-must-include/163)).

Exit and wind-up follow a fixed order. Nigerian private companies already carry statutory pre-emption — s.142(1) CAMA "restricts shareholders from transferring shares to a third party without first offering said shares to existing members" — and board approval of transfers; shareholders' agreements add permitted transfers to family and affiliates, tag-along so minorities join a majority sale on the same terms, drag-along so a 75% majority can compel a sale, and a valuation formula or independent valuer ([IBA Guide – Nigeria](https://www.ibanet.org/medias/iba-guide-shareholders-agreements-nigeria.pdf); [Turtons](https://www.turtons.com/blog/tag-along-drag-along-and-other-exit-provisions-in-a-shareholders-agreement)). Cooperative bye-laws impose lock-ins — no withdrawal until three years' membership, then three months' notice — and keep past members liable for debts existing at exit for one to two years ([NDU bye-laws](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf); [ASUU Ojo bye-laws](https://asuuojocoop.com/downloads/bye-laws.pdf)). On liquidation the universal partnership order is "outside creditors first, then partners' loans, then their capital, then their profits", and "if the assets fall short, partners may have to contribute more rather than receive anything" ([Business LibreTexts](https://biz.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Accounting/Financial_Accounting_(OpenStax)/15:_Partnership_Accounting/15.05:_Discuss_and_Record_Entries_for_the_Dissolution_of_a_Partnership)). Cooperative dissolution is different in kind: the Director cancels registration on application of three-quarters of members, a liquidator fixes creditor priority, share capital is repaid after liabilities, and the remainder is not shared — it is "placed by the Director of Cooperatives in the Bank until a new Cooperative Society with a similar area of operations is registered", because the reserve fund "is indivisible and no member is entitled to claim a specified share in it" ([Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act ss.38–41](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf); [NDU bye-laws](https://nducoop.com/uploads/Bye-Laws.pdf)). A group that wants residual value to flow back to members on wind-up should not choose a cooperative.

## Nigeria's 2026 tax reform makes the small company the default wrapper

The four tax reform acts signed on 26 June 2025 and effective 1 January 2026 changed the arithmetic of wrapper choice. A company with gross turnover of **₦100 million or less and fixed assets not exceeding ₦250 million pays 0% company income tax**, 0% on capital gains and no development levy; any company above either threshold pays **30% CIT plus a 4% development levy** that replaces the tertiary education, NASENI, NITDA and police trust fund levies, and the old 0.5% minimum tax is abolished ([PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income); [Baker Tilly Nigeria](https://www.bakertilly.ng/insights/nigerias-2025-tax-reform-acts-explained); [Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/guide/nigerian-tax-reform-acts/)). The notes flag a conflict: EY's June 2025 alert on signing described the small-company threshold as ₦50 million ([EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/nigeria-tax-act-2025-has-been-signed-highlights)), but PwC's May 2026 summary and every 2026 practitioner source use ₦100 million, and the 2026 sources list only 0% and 30% bands with no intermediate 20% medium-company rate; ₦100 million is treated as operative here but should be verified against the gazette. Dividends and interest bear 10% withholding, and under s.19 NTA no further tax is charged on dividends beyond the WHT deducted at source; the old excess-dividend tax no longer applies to dividends paid from already-taxed retained earnings ([PwC WWTS withholding](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/withholding-taxes); [taxngr.com](https://taxngr.com/wht-on-dividends-in-nigeria/); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/tax/an-overview-of-the-major-changes-introduced-by-the-finance-act-to-nigeria's-tax-regime)).

Partnerships and LLPs are transparent. Partners are assessed as individuals on their share of partnership income, and the NTA expressly folds into a partner's taxable gains "any remuneration, interest on capital, leave passages or other benefits charged to the partnership accounts for that partner" plus their share of computed profit; for an LLP all profits are deemed distributed and taxed proportionately in the partners' hands ([SSRN, Ademoroti](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/SSRN_ID2965515_code1836193.pdf?abstractid=2965515&mirid=1); [Nigeria Tax Act 2025 gazette, per search summary](https://irs.gm.gov.ng/docs/national/NIGERIA_TAX_ACT_2025.pdf)). Re-labelling drawings as salary or interest on capital therefore yields no arbitrage inside a Nigerian partnership. The new personal income tax bands are 0% on the first ₦800,000, 15% to ₦3 million, 18% to ₦12 million, 21% to ₦25 million, 23% to ₦50 million and **25% above ₦50 million**, with the consolidated relief allowance replaced by rent relief of 20% of rent capped at ₦500,000 ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/capital-gains-tax/1726922/understanding-personal-income-tax-under-the-nigerian-tax-act-2025)). Cooperatives registered under state law are exempt from income tax, but "the exemption does not extend to income from trade or business carried on for profit", so a cooperative that trades with the public or runs a commercial venture must segregate and tax that stream ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/business-economy/article/eight-organisations-exempt-from-companies-income-tax-in-nigeria/); [Lexpraxis](https://lexpraxisng.com/nigerias-new-tax-laws-what-you-should-know/)). Distributions from SEC-authorised collective investment schemes are exempt in unitholders' hands under s.162(1)(b) NTA ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-tax/1819958/an-overview-of-the-tax-treatment-of-collective-investment-schemes-under-the-nigeria-tax-act-2025-and-the-nigeria-tax-administration-act-2025)).

Capital gains moved from a flat 10% into the income-tax base: companies' gains are taxed as profits at 30% (0% for small companies), individuals' gains within the 0–25% bands, rollover relief on business-asset reinvestment is gone, and gains on Nigerian shares are exempt only where aggregate proceeds in any 12 months are below ₦150 million and the gain does not exceed ₦10 million ([EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/nigeria-tax-act-2025-has-been-signed-highlights); [Mondaq CGT](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/capital-gains-tax/1751296/capital-gains-tax-under-the-nigeria-tax-act-2025-no-longer-business-as-usual)). A group that buys and sells investee stakes above those thresholds now pays 30% or up to 25% on gains rather than 10%. VAT stays at 7.5% with input VAT on services and capital assets now fully recoverable; practitioner guides report the registration threshold rising to ₦50 million and small companies relieved from charging VAT, though the two thresholds are not reconciled in any primary source found ([Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.ng/insights/nigerias-2025-tax-reform-acts-explained); [Pronalytics](https://pronalytics.ng/blog/vat-registration-nigeria-2026-guide)). Stamping deadlines fell from 40 to 30 days, ad valorem conveyance duty is now confined to real property and mineral assets so share transfers appear to fall outside it, unspecified agreements bear a flat ₦1,000, and unstamped instruments are inadmissible in evidence ([Andersen Nigeria](https://ng.andersen.com/signed-sealed-and-taxed-stamp-duty-compliance-under-the-nigeria-tax-act-2025/); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/tax/nigeria-tax-reforms-for-non-residents-expatriate-foreign-company-guide)).

The planning calculus follows from these rates (research team's arithmetic from the cited bands). ₦10 million of profit in a small company distributed as dividend costs **₦1 million (10%)** in total. The same ₦10 million earned through a partnership by one partner with no other income costs about ₦1.59 million — nil on ₦800,000, 15% on ₦2.2 million (₦330,000), 18% on ₦7 million (₦1.26 million) — roughly 16%. Above the small-company thresholds the company route costs 30% plus 4% at entity level and then 10% WHT on the remainder, about **40% of pre-tax profit if fully distributed**, against a maximum 25% through a partnership — so the wrapper decision flips at scale. Salary to an active member is deductible to the company but taxed at the member's PIT rate and attracts pension contributions of 18% of emoluments (employer 10%, employee 8%), NHF of 2.5% and employee compensation of 1% ([PwC WWTS other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/other-taxes)), so dividends at a flat 10% dominate for all but very low earners. Because WHT on dividends is final and small-company CIT is nil, there is no tax reason to delay distributions in a small company; one strong year that pushes turnover past ₦100 million switches the whole year's profit to 30%. Loss carry-forward is indefinite for companies ([Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/tax/an-overview-of-the-major-changes-introduced-by-the-finance-act-to-nigeria's-tax-regime)).

Compliance touches up to five regulators. The Nigeria Revenue Service replaces FIRS; every taxable person needs a TIN; failure to file attracts ₦100,000 for the first month and ₦50,000 for each subsequent month; records must be kept for at least six years; and the small-company exemption still requires a TIN, proper records and nil returns ([Banwo & Ighodalo](https://www.banwo-ighodalo.com/grey-matter/nigerias-new-tax-regime-a-taxpayers-guide-to-compliance-offences-penalties-enforcement-and-dispute-resolution/); [Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/guide/nigerian-tax-reform-acts/)). WHT is remitted by the 21st of the month following payment ([PwC WWTS withholding](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/withholding-taxes)). CAC annual returns fall due within 42 days of the AGM, and since 1 January 2024 the Commission applies penalties against both company and directors personally, so inactive pooled-capital companies that skip returns expose the members who sit as directors ([EBC Consults](https://www.ebconsults.ng/cac-annual-returns-nigeria/); [Njunkim Legal](https://njunkimlegal.com/how-to-file-your-annual-returns-with-the-cac-and-what-happens-if-you-dont/)). Note that the CAMA audit exemption (turnover ≤ ₦120 million, net assets ≤ ₦60 million, under 50 employees) and the NTA tax exemption (turnover ≤ ₦100 million, fixed assets ≤ ₦250 million) use different thresholds and bases, so a company can be audit-exempt but tax-paying or the reverse, and both tests should be documented annually ([Matog Consulting](https://www.matogconsulting.com/audit-exemptions-for-small-companies-in-nigeria-who-qualifies/)). The SEC perimeter is the ISA 2025: an unregistered fund faces a minimum ₦10 million fine plus ₦100,000 a day; promoters of a "prohibited scheme" face not less than ₦20 million and/or 10 years (one outlet reports ₦40 million; the Act's text could not be accessed to resolve this); and s.169 penalises misuse of investor funds at ₦50 million or four times the gain plus restitution ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/venture-capital/1638148/nigerias-investments-and-securities-act-2025-what-is-changing-for-private-capital-players); [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/04/01/isa-2025-ponzi-schemes-promoters-in-nigeria-now-face-10-years-jail-term-sec-dg/); [Mondaq ISA updates](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/securities/1700288/the-investment-and-securities-act-2025-navigating-the-key-updates)). The CBN perimeter is BOFIA 2020 — unlicensed banking carries up to five years or a fine of not less than ₦50 million or twice the deposits — and CBN's microfinance policy requires any financial cooperative reaching **₦20 million in assets or 2,000 members** to transform into a licensed institution ([KMO Legal](https://www.kmo.legal/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/AN-OVERVIEW-OF-THE-BOFIA-2020.x45260.pdf); [CBN Revised Microfinance Policy 2011](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/OUT/2011/PRESSRELEASE/GVD/REVISED%20MICROFINANCE%20POLICY%20JULY%2012%202011.PDF)); whether that trigger has been updated since 2011 could not be confirmed. State cooperative registrars require proper books, annual audit and audited returns, with suspension of the committee as the sanction for default ([Lagos Department of Cooperative Services](https://mccti.lagosstate.gov.ng/departments/cooperatives)).

Opening the pool to strangers online has two lawful routes. Under the SEC's January 2021 Crowdfunding Rules only Nigerian-incorporated MSMEs with a two-year track record may raise through SEC-registered portals, capped at **₦50 million (micro), ₦70 million (small) or ₦100 million (medium) in 12 months**, retail investors may not commit more than 10% of annual income a year, offers run at most 90 days, and businesses "intending to use proceeds to lend or invest in other companies" are excluded — which rules out crowdfunding for a "we pool and invest in other businesses" club entirely and pushes it to CIS registration ([ǼLEX](https://aelex.com/the-nigerian-securities-and-exchange-commission-crowdfunding-rules/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/securities/1138928/crowdfunding-for-small-to-medium-scale-businesses-an-analysis-under-the-nigerian-law)). The comparators converge on the same logic: US Regulation Crowdfunding caps raises at $5 million per 12 months with non-accredited investors limited to the greater of $2,500 or 5% of income or net worth below $124,000, and 10% above it ([SEC Investor Bulletin](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/updated-11)); the UK FCA restricts direct-offer promotions of unlisted securities and P2P loans to certified high-net-worth, sophisticated or "restricted" investors who pledge not to put more than 10% of net investible assets into such products ([FCA PS19/14](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps19-14.pdf)). On entity taxation, a US partnership or multi-member LLC files Form 1065 and issues K-1s by 15 March, an S corporation passes through but must pay shareholder-employees reasonable compensation first, and a Subchapter T cooperative deducts patronage dividends paid within 8.5 months of year end ([IRS Publication 541](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p541); [IRS Form 1120-S instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120s); [IRS Form 1120-C instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120c)); UK partnerships and LLPs are transparent via the SA800 return, companies pay 19–25% corporation tax from April 2026 with dividends then taxed at 10.75–39.35%, and community interest companies carry an asset lock and a 35% dividend cap ([Gravita](https://www.gravita.com/insights/partnership-tax-returns-and-your-sa800-obligations/); [PwC WWTS UK](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/united-kingdom/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income); [Taxfly](https://www.taxfly.co.uk/guides/dividend-tax-2026-27-rates-and-allowance); [Office of the Regulator of CICs](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69b14f4c187a6dea7823316e/13-783-community-interest-companies-information-pack.pdf)). Nigeria's 0% small-company regime is more generous than either comparator, and its cooperative exemption is broader than Subchapter T, which only defers tax to the patron.

## Titled assets and a common bond separate the successes from the frauds

What groups actually invest in clusters into three capital tiers, and the evidence on returns separates sharply into documented figures and promotional claims. Under about ₦2 million a group can run a POS outlet (₦100,000–₦650,000 all-in, earning ₦5,000–₦12,000 a day with break-even in one to two months), a 500-bird broiler batch (₦1.5–2.5 million, six-to-eight-week cycle, feed at 70% of cost) or a 500-fish catfish pond (₦700,000–₦900,000, roughly ₦400,000 profit on ₦1.2 million in five months) ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2023/11/24/pos-business-how-operators-stay-afloat-amidst-rising-competition/); [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/ask-legit/1709817-how-cost-start-a-chicken-farm-nigeria-everything-budget-for/); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/business-economy/article/how-to-profit-from-catfish-farming/)). An academic study of Delta State broiler operators recorded average net profit of ₦928,720 per cycle and **₦2.89 returned per naira invested** ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348827505_FINANCIAL_BENEFITS_ANALYSIS_OF_BROILER_CHICKEN_FARM_OPERATORS_IN_DELTA_STATE_NIGERIA)). Between ₦2 million and ₦20 million the options widen to a layer farm (₦3.5–4 million for 500 birds, 18–19 weeks to first egg, more profitable than broilers per cycle), a tricycle fleet under hire purchase (₦1.3 million per unit, 30% deposit, ₦5,000–₦10,000 daily remittance, net ₦85,000–₦210,000 a month per unit, 18-month term) or a titled peri-urban plot ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Costs-and-returns-to-broiler-and-layer-enterprises-for-one-production-cycle_tbl3_337056128); [Turnet Finance](https://turnetfinance.ng/keke-tricycle-bike-business-in-nigeria/)). Above ₦40 million sit filling stations (₦40–80 million for a mini station, ₦150–400 million for a large one), a Chicken Republic franchise (about ₦250 million plus a $25,000 fee, 5% royalty and 4% levies, 18–36 month payback, 20% EBITDA margin, full-time owner-operator required), short-let blocks and cooperative estate land ([Prioni](https://www.prioni.com.ng/cost-of-building-a-filling-station-in-nigeria/); [FBDS Nigeria](https://fbdsnigeria.com/directory/chicken-republic/)). A 20-member group at ₦250,000 each reaches the first tier comfortably and the second with staggered contributions; the third needs 100-plus members or institutional co-funding, which is why it is dominated by staff cooperatives.

The gap between documented and advertised returns is itself a risk signal. The highest advertised figures — short-let net yields of 24–32%, Keble's "up to 30%" on naira property, land appreciation of 18–35% — are all unaudited realtor or platform claims, while the sober figures — T-bills at 15–20%, the academic broiler study — are documented ([Nigeria Real Estate Blog](https://nigeriarealestateblog.com/lagos-short-let-yields-q1-2026/); [TechCabal on Keble](https://techcabal.com/2025/01/17/keble-making-global-real-estate-accessible-to-africans-for-just-10/)). Independent Airbnb trackers disagree on Lagos occupancy by a factor of nearly two (75% versus roughly 43% over the same window), and utilities alone add ₦250,000–₦700,000 a month per unit ([Airbtics](https://airbtics.com/annual-airbnb-revenue-in-lagos-nigeria/); [AirROI](https://www.airroi.com/airbnb-data/nigeria/lagos/lagos); [PropComms Africa](https://propcommsafrica.com/the-2026-lagos-shortlet-reality-check-why-data-beats-hype-in-a-%E2%82%A6285-billion-market/)). Land in transition zones carries omo-onile extortion and sale of untitled or double-allocated plots as the dominant fraud, with one Ibeju-Lekki company taking payment from over 500 subscribers in 2021 and allocating no plot to any of them years later ([Omonile Lawyer](https://omonilelawyer.com/ibeju-lekki-real-estate-company-scams-over-500-people-without-allocating-a-single-plot-to-any-buyer/)). Operating models converge on two templates: fixed remittance (the keke or danfo driver pays a daily sum and the owner bears repairs; the outgrower sells at market price after input deduction, as in Olam's 35,000-farmer rice scheme with 48-hour payment and Babban Gona's 200,000-smallholder franchise reporting 98–99% repayment) or pro-rata unit ownership with a manager or platform fee (0.5% of assets at Risevest, 5% royalty plus 4% levies at Chicken Republic) ([Daily Trust](https://dailytrust.com/how-out-grower-scheme-is-boosting-farmers-fortunes/); [Babban Gona](https://babbangona.com/); [TradingBrokers](https://tradingbrokers.com/risevest-review/)). SEC-registered group tooling now exists: Cowa pools groups of up to 50 members into mutual funds or T-bills via ARM and Meristem with pro-rata distribution, and banks pool customer money to meet the CBN's ₦50 million primary-market T-bill minimum ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/10/04/investment-platforms-in-nigeria/); [CSL Stockbrokers](https://www.cslstockbrokers.com/blog-details.php?slug=how-to-invest-in-treasury-bills-in-nigeria-a-comprehensive-guide-for-you)).

The case record divides cleanly. Every Nigerian success has members with a common employer or profession, a tangible titled asset, and a horizon of five to twenty years. The Chevron Employees Multipurpose Cooperative Society bought most of the plots in Nicon Town, Ikate-Lekki, in 2001 and completed the **57-hectare, 431-plot estate in 2007**, then developed the 14-hectare Melrose Park Estate ([OwnAHome](https://ownahome.ng/blog/what-is-so-special-about-nicon-town-estate-lekki); [Estate Intel](https://estateintel.com/insights/real-estate-developments-owned-by-cooperative-societies-in-nigeria)). The Nigeria Immigration Service staff cooperative commissioned 150 bungalows at Gwagwalada and started a 1,000-unit scheme at Kuje ([Leadership](https://leadership.ng/nis-unveils-nationwide-housing-drive-inaugurates-new-staff-estates/)); the Oluyole teachers' union cooperative commissioned a housing estate in Ibadan ([Tribune](https://tribuneonlineng.com/oluyole-lg-boss-commissions-teachers-housing-estate/)). Kenya's Mwalimu National Sacco, founded by teachers in 1974, has over 60,000 members, core capital above KES 5 billion, an 871-unit housing plan and a KES 1.5 billion tower ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/markets/mwalimu-sacco-plans-sh2-9bn-housing-estate-2075478)). The Lagos Angel Network, with 110-plus members writing $25,000–$250,000 cheques, has invested over $5 million across 100-plus startups, though no exit data exist ([Ellty](https://www.ellty.com/investors/lagos-angel-investors)). Mondragón's Fagor bankruptcy in 2013 — €1.1 billion of debt, 5,600 jobs at risk — shows how a cooperative federation socialises failure: 1,710 of 1,800 members were redeployed to other cooperatives and the rest covered by internal unemployment insurance, something no Nigerian structure replicates ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fagor); [Reasons to be Cheerful](https://reasonstobecheerful.world/mondragon-cooperatives-spain/)).

Every Nigerian failure has strangers recruited by marketing, a promised fixed return over one to nine months, and no asset the investor could seize. Of the agritech crowdfunding cohort — Farmcrowdy, ThriveAgric, Farmsponsor, Agropartnerships, Porkmoney, Agrorite, HoCorn, PayFarmer — "more than 90 percent" defaulted, with defaults accelerating in 2021 as the SEC rules took effect ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/backpage/article/in-nigeria-agriculture-became-fraud-tech-was-the-enabler/)). ThriveAgric's insurance covered on-farm mortality only and excluded COVID losses; subscribers faced 12–24 month delays before the company, under an interim CEO, repaid all crowdfunders in 2021 ([Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/2020/10/06/thrive-agric-defaulting-insurance/); [Technext](https://technext24.com/news/thrive-agric-completes-payment-of-investment-returns-to-its-crowdfunders/)). Crowdyvest, inheriting Farmcrowdy's book, owed customers **₦7.7 billion** by November 2023 and proposed converting ₦5.7 billion of that into a 35% equity stake ([Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/2023/11/03/crowdyvest-to-convert-customer-debt-to-equity)). The same business model thus ended in full repayment or billions unpaid depending on balance sheet, governance response and backers — the model itself, retail money funding seasonal cycles with insurance that excludes systemic risk, is fragile. The outright frauds scale from MMM (30% a month, 3 million participants, ₦18 billion lost, December 2016) through MBA Forex (15% every 30 days, **₦171.1 billion from 125,397 investors**, founder wanted by the EFCC) and Famzhi Interbiz (up to 30% a month, ₦30 billion, "posed as a cooperative") to CBEX (100% in 30 days, launched July 2024, collapsed April 2025, about **₦1.3 trillion** lost on forged regulatory certificates) ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/from-mmm-to-cbex-the-chronicles-of-nigerians-ponzi-relationship/); [FIJ](https://fij.ng/article/mba-capitals-maxwell-odum-made-n171bn-from-investors-now-hes-nowhere-to-be-found/); [Daily Trust](https://dailytrust.com/how-thousands-of-nigerians-lost-n1-3trn-to-scam/)). NDIC put cumulative Ponzi losses at ₦911.45 billion over 23 years and SEC at ₦300.2 billion for recent schemes; the figures are not reconciled and should be read as orders of magnitude ([Technext](https://technext24.com/2025/10/30/ponzi-scheme-nigerians-lost-n300-2b-sec/)). The Famzhi disguise worked because state cooperative registration carries legitimacy but little prudential supervision — a cooperative that recruits non-members publicly for fixed returns has crossed into SEC territory. The operative test is structural: a legitimate ROSCA, thrift society or club's payouts are bounded by what members put in plus interest they paid each other or disclosed market returns; no member pays 30% a month to borrow from a thrift society, so a group that pays 30% a month cannot be one.

## A four-variable test picks the wrapper, a T-bill hurdle picks the venture

The decision framework that falls out of the evidence has two halves. The wrapper is chosen by four variables: headcount, the split between active and passive members, liability appetite, and the exit and return mechanics the group needs. Two to five people all working in a service or professional business should form an LLP — all limited liability, a flexible profit split via the LLP agreement, perpetual succession and no share-capital formalities. A small team that will raise equity now or later, and wants votes proportional to money, should form a private company with a shareholders' agreement layered over the model articles and s.22's statutory transfer protections, staying audit-exempt while small and under the 50-member cap. One to three managers with up to about seventeen passive money partners map onto the CAMA limited partnership, which mirrors the global GP/LP fund design and is tax-transparent, but the 20-partner cap and the loss of the liability shield on any management participation make it unsuitable for hands-on groups, and ISA 2025's presumption that funds are collective investment schemes means any LP that looks like a fund likely needs SEC registration under s.151 ([Mondaq LP](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/corporate-and-company-law/1142692/registration-of-limited-partnerships-in-nigeria-under-cama-2020); [UUBO](https://uubo.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/UPDATED-DETAILED-ANALYSIS-OF-ISA-2025-WHAT-IS-CHANGING-FOR-PE-AND-VC-FUNDS-1.pdf)). Ten to fifty-plus people saving monthly and lending or investing for members belong in a thrift and credit or multipurpose cooperative — tax-exempt, limited liability, cheap, with a statutory supervisory committee and Director arbitration — provided they accept one-member-one-vote, the 20% share cap, a 25% indivisible reserve, and that residual value on dissolution does not come back to them. Three to fifteen acquaintances buying one building or doing one deal should use an SPV private company holding the asset with a waterfall in the shareholders' agreement, which keeps them outside public-offer rules and gives a clean exit by selling shares or the asset. Anyone raising from strangers or online has only the SEC-registered crowdfunding portal (within the ₦50–100 million caps and never for on-lending or re-investing) or a registered CIS. Short projects between existing companies suit a contractual JV; individuals should not be parties. A friends' club that never registers is at law an unincorporated association or general partnership with every member personally liable and no capacity to hold title or a bank account in the group's name. The 2026 tax regime then layers a second filter: below ₦100 million turnover the company wrapper is close to tax-neutral at entity level with a flat 10% final dividend tax, so it beats a partnership for most groups; above the thresholds, the roughly 40% combined company burden on distributed profit makes the single-layer partnership or LLP at a maximum 25% cheaper.

The venture is chosen by capital tier, supervision capacity, time, risk, liquidity and governance. Under ₦5 million the realistic options are a POS outlet, one broiler or catfish cycle, one used tricycle, or pooled T-bills and money-market funds; ₦5–50 million opens a layer farm, a fleet of three to ten tricycles under hire purchase, a titled plot or two, or a short-let unit; above ₦50 million a filling station, franchise, estate land or multiple short-lets become possible but only with a hired professional manager and a formal cooperative or company. If nobody in the group can inspect a farm, a fleet or a property weekly, the group should hold passive instruments or use a regulated portal — operator-dependent businesses failed in the evidence precisely when supervision was outsourced to a platform. Time commitment scales from a monthly meeting for passive instruments, through weekly collection and inspection for fleets, POS and short-lets, to a full-time manager plus an audit committee for farms, franchises and filling stations; Chicken Republic requires a hands-on owner-operator and is not a passive investment ([FBDS Nigeria](https://fbdsnigeria.com/directory/chicken-republic/)). The risk hurdle is the T-bill yield of 15–20%: anything promising more than roughly twice that a year without a seizable asset is speculative, and anything promising monthly double-digit returns matches the MMM, MBA Forex and CBEX pattern exactly. Liquidity runs from days (money-market funds, T-bills, dollar apps) through months (selling a tricycle or POS business) to the full cycle (farm batches) and years (land and estates), so a group whose members may need to exit should hold a liquid tranche and set a withdrawal notice period in the rules. Payback ranks POS and broilers (weeks to months) ahead of catfish and tricycle hire-purchase (five to eighteen months), layers and short-lets (one to three years), and land and estates (three to ten-plus years).

Governance is the non-negotiable layer regardless of wrapper or venture, because it is the feature shared by CEMCS, NIMCOS, Mwalimu and Mondragón and absent from every failure case. The group should have a common bond (employer, profession, congregation or market), written rules signed before money moves, elected officers with term limits, a dual-signatory bank account in a licensed institution with monthly statements to all members, individual capital accounts in the Mondragón style so each member's stake is always visible, a three-person supervisory committee inspecting quarterly, an annual external audit, guarantors for anyone taking an early ROSCA position or a loan, and a written valuation and buyout formula with instalment payment and insurance for death triggers. Before investing through any platform, the group should verify SEC registration directly on the Commission's portal rather than trusting a certificate, since CBEX forged its regulatory documents ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/04/16/cbex-collapse-sparks-calls-for-stronger-government-regulation-in-digital-asset-trading-in-nigeria/)). Two gaps in the evidence deserve the analyst's attention: no Nigerian regulator or academic study quantifies default or loss rates for ajo, esusu or cooperatives by cause (the 13% embezzlement figure is Kenyan, and the one rigorous default series is Indian), and no SEC or CBN document addresses how informal investment clubs or digital ROSCAs should structure themselves — the regulatory basis is inferred from licensing categories and general warnings.

## Conclusion

The most useful reframing for an analyst is that pooled-capital ventures are governance products wrapped around an asset, not asset products wrapped in a group. The Shriram Chits data show that a well-screened, guarantor-backed ROSCA runs at about 1.5% default — better than consumer credit — while Lagos traders report 35% misappropriation and 35% non-payment in groups with no custody or enforcement design; the same agri-crowdfunding model ended in full repayment at one company and ₦7.7 billion of unpaid debt at another. The variable that explains these gaps is never the return on broilers or land but who holds the money, who can sign, who inspects, and what happens on exit. Nigeria's 2026 reforms sharpen this by making the small private company nearly free of entity tax and by criminalising the public-facing pool, so the economically sensible path for a closed group — a Ltd or LLP with a tight shareholders' or partnership agreement and cooperative-style controls, or a registered cooperative where heads should outvote capital — is now also the tax-efficient and legally safest one. The informal sector will not disappear into these wrappers; EFInA and FinAccess both show informal group use rising alongside formal inclusion, which means the real opportunity for accountants, fintechs and regulators is to layer custody, records and unit-based accounting onto existing groups rather than to replace them.

Two implications follow. First, the profit-sharing deed should be modelled before it is signed: the five-member example shows the same ₦2.14 million producing ₦0 or ₦694,000 for the working member depending on whether a salary step exists, and the waterfall example shows a catch-up clause tripling the sponsor's take from 6.7% to 20% of profit at the limited partners' expense — these are decisions to be priced, not boilerplate to be copied. Second, the ISA 2025 perimeter is still unquantified: no threshold tells a Nigerian club when it has become a collective investment scheme, and until SEC issues guidance for private clubs and syndicates, the safe design is one in which every contributor is a registered co-owner with a vote, nobody is paid a fee to manage other people's money, and nobody outside the group is ever invited in.
