# Cooperative housing estate: workflows

Nine stages. The gate at each stage is what separates the completed estates (Nicon Town, Woodhill, NIMCOS Gwagwalada) from the stalled ones (Apo-Tafyi, Idu-Sabo, Koroduma). Money may be collected from members only at stages 1, 5 and 7.

### Stage 1. Demand and mandate

1. Survey members: how many want a plot or unit, at what price band, with what monthly capacity. *Gate:* enough demand at a price members can pay within one-third of salary.
2. General meeting approves the project, the delivery model (land-only, turnkey, JV or FMBN scheme), the budget ceiling and the committee. *Gate:* 75% or the bye-law threshold.
3. Members pay a refundable expression-of-interest deposit into a ring-fenced account. *Gate:* deposit is small, refundable and never used to pay a vendor.

### Stage 2. Land search and due diligence

1. Shortlist land by location band, price and title type (government allocation, C of O, gazetted excision, Governor's Consent).
2. Registry search at the Lands Bureau; charting at the Surveyor-General (committed, acquisition or free land); litigation search; physical inspection; verify the vendor's authority (family head and principal members, or the allottee's title).
3. Reject land under acquisition, "excision in progress" without a gazette, Area Council papers not yet regularised, or any land with competing claims. *Gate:* a written due-diligence report signed by the lawyer and surveyor.

### Stage 3. Acquisition and master title

1. Negotiate price; agree community settlement in writing with a receipt.
2. Pay the vendor from escrow against the executed deed of assignment and documents, or by lender-controlled disbursement. *Gate:* no payment direct from the society account to an unverified vendor.
3. Perimeter survey and beaconing; lodge the survey plan.
4. Stamp the deed within 30 days; apply for Governor's Consent (about 3% of value plus legal; 3–12 months); register the deed. For the FCT: pay the R of O bill within 21 days and start development within two years.
5. Enter ground rent and Land Use Charge into the society's calendar. *Gate:* master title perfected or consent lodged before any member sale proceeds.

### Stage 4. Layout and approvals

1. Town planner prepares the layout (plot sizes, roads, drainage, open space, unit mix) and the design code.
2. Obtain layout approval from the planning authority (3–24 months); environmental assessment where required. *Gate:* layout approval exists before any plot is allocated.
3. Quantity surveyor costs infrastructure and units; the budget is restated with a contingency of at least 10%.

### Stage 5. Financing and pricing

1. Set the member price: land plus infrastructure plus fees plus contingency plus the stated administrative margin; publish the cost build-up.
2. Choose finance: member instalments only; FMBN Cooperative Housing Development Loan (requires perfected title, NHF-contributing members); bank or DFI project loan; or JV with a developer on an agreed unit split. *Gate:* general meeting approves borrowing and the security given.
3. Open the project escrow or lender-controlled account; agree the instalment schedule tied to milestones and the refund and default rules. *Gate:* subscription agreement signed by every subscriber before the first instalment.
4. Collect instalments into the project account only.

### Stage 6. Procurement and construction

1. Tender infrastructure and construction; evaluate on track record, bond capacity, price and REDAN or LASRERA status; the general meeting or committee awards within the budget.
2. Sign a fixed-price or stated-escalation contract with a performance bond, retention and milestone certification by the quantity surveyor and engineer.
3. Obtain building permits (6–14 weeks) before construction. *Gate:* no construction without permits; no payment without a certified milestone.
4. Build infrastructure (roads, drainage, water, power, fence) and units in phases matched to collections; the committee reports progress and cost against budget to members quarterly.
5. Handle overruns under the contract and the subscription agreement's price-review clause; any member price change goes to the general meeting.

### Stage 7. Allocation

1. Allocation only when the member has paid the stated share and the plot or unit is serviced or complete. *Gate:* layout approval and master title in place.
2. Allocate by the published method: ballot, seniority, payment order or a combination; minutes and witnesses recorded.
3. Issue the allocation letter stating plot or unit, price paid, balance, build-by period, design code, perfection obligations and resale conditions.
4. Collect the balance and the member's share of perfection costs.

### Stage 8. Member title

1. Prepare each member's deed of sub-lease or sub-assignment under the master title; stamp within 30 days.
2. Apply for consent and registration for each member deed (ad valorem fees apply again); for flats, a long sub-lease plus management arrangement, since no strata title law exists.
3. Deliver the registered deed; record it in the estate register. *Gate:* the society does not treat the project as complete until every allottee holds a registered deed.

### Stage 9. Handover and estate management

1. Hand over infrastructure and common areas to a residents' association or facility manager; set the service charge.
2. Transfer or retain responsibility for ground rent and Land Use Charge on common areas; enforce the build-by period and design code.
3. Close the project account; present final accounts and the cooperative's surplus or loss on the estate to the AGM.

### Exceptions

- **Subscriber default**: notice, then forfeiture or reallocation under the agreement, with refund less the stated charge within the stated period.
- **Vendor or title failure**: stop collections; pursue the vendor; report to members and the Director within 30 days.
- **Developer default**: call the bond; terminate; re-tender; report.
- **Revocation notice**: pay or appeal within the notice period; no revocation is valid without written notice (Olabanji v Minister FCT, 2025).

# Cooperative housing estate: business rules

### Land and title

- The society acquires only land with a government allocation, a C of O, a gazetted excision or a consented deed; never land under acquisition, "excision in progress" or unregularised Area Council papers.
- Due diligence is three searches (Lands Registry, Surveyor-General charting, litigation) plus physical inspection and vendor-authority verification, written up and signed before any offer.
- Deeds are stamped within 30 days; Governor's Consent is applied for immediately; the perfected master title is the condition for member sales.
- FCT: pay the R of O bill within 21 days of offer; develop within two years; ground rent is paid in advance every 1 January.
- Lagos: layout approval before any allocation; building permits before any construction; Land Use Charge paid annually, with the 100% surcharge on undeveloped land budgeted.
- The title and carrying-cost calendar (ground rent, Land Use Charge, consent fees, C-of-O fees) is a budget line funded before mobilisation.

### Money collection (control)

- No member payment reaches a vendor, developer or contractor except from escrow or a lender-controlled account against an executed deed or a certified milestone.
- Expression-of-interest deposits are refundable and ring-fenced; substantive instalments start only after the master title is perfected or lodged for consent and the subscription agreement is signed.
- Project funds are kept in a separate project account, never commingled with thrift savings; refunds are paid within the stated period from that account.
- The committee may not sign payments alone: two of three signatories, with the quantity surveyor's certificate attached for construction payments.

### Pricing and finance

- The member price is published as a cost build-up: land, survey and legal, consent and registration, infrastructure, construction, professional fees, finance cost, contingency of at least 10%, and the stated administrative margin.
- Infrastructure is budgeted at full cost (20–30% of project cost); land and survey alone are never treated as the project budget.
- Borrowing and the security given are approved by the general meeting; FMBN CHDL terms (up to ₦500m, 10%, 24 months, 12-month moratorium) and capitalised interest are shown in the price.
- A price-review clause states who bears cost overruns from materials, FX or delay, and how members are notified and consulted.
- Instalments are matched to milestones; the schedule respects the one-third deduction cap where paid by payroll.

### Procurement

- Developers and contractors are selected by tender on track record, bond capacity and price; REDAN membership or LASRERA registration is a screen, not a guarantee.
- Contracts are fixed price or stated escalation, with a performance bond, retention and milestone certification; penalties bind the developer as well as the subscriber.
- No payment without a certified milestone; no construction without permits.

### Allocation and member agreements

- The allocation method (ballot, seniority, payment order) is approved by the general meeting and published before collections start; allocations are minuted and witnessed.
- Every subscriber signs a subscription agreement covering price and build-up, instalment schedule, refund and default terms, allocation method, build-by period and design code, perfection costs, resale right of first refusal to the society, inheritance by nominee, and the dispute route.
- Allocation letters state the plot or unit, amount paid, balance, build-by period and perfection obligations.
- Members build to the design code within the build-by period; undeveloped plots after the period are subject to the agreement's remedy.

### Member title

- Each allottee receives a deed of sub-lease or sub-assignment under the master title, stamped within 30 days and lodged for consent and registration; the society tracks every deed to registration.
- Flats are held by long sub-lease with a management arrangement; the society or a management company remains title-holder for common areas and budgets their charges.
- The project is complete only when every allottee holds a registered deed.

### Legal form and tax

- Transactions are with members; any sale of surplus plots to non-members requires general meeting approval, LASRERA registration in Lagos, segregated accounts, and tax on the gain (NTA s.163 exemption does not cover trading).
- The estate's accounts are separate from thrift accounts and presented to the AGM with the annual return to the Director.
- Stamp duty, consent CGT, registration and VAT on construction contracts are budgeted per transaction, not absorbed unplanned.

### Exit, resale and inheritance

- A subscriber may withdraw before allocation with a refund less the stated charge within the stated period; after allocation, resale is subject to the society's right of first refusal.
- On death, the allocation passes to the registered nominee or estate, subject to sums due.
- Appreciation accrues to the allottee on resale unless the general meeting adopts a price cap or buy-back formula to preserve affordability for later members.

### Reporting (control)

- Quarterly to members: collections, spend against budget, milestone status, title and approval status, arrears.
- Annually to the AGM and the Director: estate accounts, units allocated and titled, outstanding carrying costs, disputes.
- Within 30 days to members and the Director: any vendor, title, developer or revocation failure.
